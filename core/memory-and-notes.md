# Memory and notes

An employee who starts from zero every morning is a temp, not a hire. This is how an employee in this hub accumulates knowledge instead of repeating itself.

## The four files every employee keeps

```
notes/
  learnings.md    durable lessons - what worked, what failed, and the rule you now follow
  contacts.md     the people, vendors, buyers, partners and their context
  journal/        one file per month: YYYY-MM.md - what happened, dated
  decisions.md    choices made and why, so they are not re-litigated
```

Seed them at creation. Keep them short. A note nobody re-reads is a note that should not exist.

## When to write

- **After every task that taught something** - one line in the journal: date, task, outcome, what you would do differently.
- **When a rule emerges** - promote it to `learnings.md` as a rule, not a story. "Bulk liquidation protects retail price better than discounting" is a rule. "We talked about discounts once" is not.
- **When a person or vendor appears twice** - add them to `contacts.md` with the context that makes them useful next time.
- **When a decision is contested** - `decisions.md`, with the reason and the date. This is what stops the same argument next quarter.

## How to read

At the **start** of any task: read `learnings.md` and the current month's journal. Two minutes, and it is the difference between a repeat mistake and an improvement.

At the **end**: write the line. Before you finish the reply, not after.

## What a good note looks like

- One fact per line, dated where it matters.
- Written for a stranger: no "as discussed", no pronouns pointing at a conversation nobody can see.
- Numbers with units and dates. "₹48,000 of dead stock at cost, as of 31 Oct" beats "a lot of stock".
- No secrets. No customer personal data beyond what the job needs. No API keys, ever.

## Contacts

The most valuable thing a long-serving employee has is a phone book. Record: who they are, what they are good for, how they were last useful, and when. When a task needs a printer, a bulk buyer, a CA, a photographer - the employee should already know two.

## Getting better, measurably

Once a month, read the journal and answer: what did I get wrong more than once? Write the fix as a rule in `learnings.md`, and if it is a craft lesson, add it to the relevant `references/` file. That is how a skill in this hub improves instead of ageing.
