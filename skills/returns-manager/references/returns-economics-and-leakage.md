# Returns economics and leakage

Returns are a cost centre with a revenue side: recovered stock. This file prices both.

## The cost of one return

| Line | Typical range (INR) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Reverse freight | 90-250 | Per kg and per pin code; higher for furniture and large items |
| Forward freight written off | 60-180 | Only where the sale is fully reversed |
| Payment-gateway fee | 1.5-2.5% of the amount | Usually not returned on refund |
| Repack and QC labour | 25-60 | Per unit |
| Refurbishment | 0-400 | Depends on grade |
| Markdown on resale | 10-60% of price | The real cost, and the one nobody counts |
| Customer service time | 10-40 minutes | Multiply by the loaded hourly cost |
| **Total** | **₹250-900 per unit** | On a ₹900 product this is often the entire margin |

The rule that follows: **a 10% return rate on a 35% gross margin is roughly a 7-point margin loss.**
Put that sentence in front of the founder once, and the returns conversation changes from support to
commercial.

## The recovery side

| Disposition | Recovered | Where it goes | Watch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Restock as new | 100% of cost | Normal stock | Only grade A |
| Open-box sale | 75-90% of price | Own site, a separate listing | Clearly labelled, or a second return |
| Second-hand or refurb | 40-70% | Marketplace, local resale | Warranty position stated |
| Return to vendor | Per contract | Vendor credit | RTV terms are usually in the supply agreement |
| Spare parts | 20-50% | Service operation | Only where a service exists |
| Scrap | 0-5% | Scrap buyer | Record the write-off |

A monthly recovery rate below 60% of returned cost is a signal that grading is late, disposition is
unowned, or the refurb channel does not exist.

## Fraud and abuse patterns

| Pattern | Signal | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Empty box | Weight at pickup below the packed weight; repeated by the same customer | Hold the refund, request the unboxing video, escalate to a human; claim where the channel allows |
| Wardrobing | Purchase, wear, return within the window; tags removed or reattached | Policy on condition, enforced consistently |
| Serial returner | Above the threshold in a rolling 12 months | Flag, do not accuse; a human decides the account treatment |
| Item swap | A different or older unit returned | Serial or IMEI check at QC |
| Address abuse | Repeated RTOs to one pin code | COD restriction, address verification |

Thresholds are a business decision, not a moral one: state the number, apply it consistently, and
never let a flag become an accusation. In India a public accusation that turns out wrong is a
consumer-forum complaint with a cost attached.

## The weekly number

Four figures, one page: return rate by channel and by SKU, cost per return, recovery rate, and refund
cycle time. Add the top three reasons and the SKU with the worst rate. That page is the whole
management system; anything more is a dashboard nobody opens.

## Anti-patterns

- **Counting only reverse freight.** The markdown on resale is usually the largest line.
- **Measuring returns as a percentage of orders** when the question is value: 6% of orders can be 14%
  of revenue.
- **No recovery target.** Stock sits in a bin, which is a 100% write-off with extra steps.
- **Treating a serial returner as a service failure.** It is a policy decision, made once, in writing.
- **Comparing your rate to an industry number.** Channel, category and price band change it entirely.
- **Ignoring the SKU that funds the rest.** One bad SKU can be half the total cost.
