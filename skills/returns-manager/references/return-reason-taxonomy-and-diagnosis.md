# Return reasons and diagnosis

The reason code is the most valuable field in the whole returns log, and the one most often reduced to
"customer does not want". This file is how to read it properly.

## The eight reasons that cover almost everything

| Reason | What it really means | Who pays return freight | Typical share, apparel | Typical share, electronics |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Size or fit | The chart, the photo or the expectation was wrong | Business | 40-55% | 3-5% |
| Not as described | Colour, material, quantity or feature differs from the listing | Business | 12-18% | 10-15% |
| Damaged in transit | Packaging failed or the courier did | Business, then claim the courier | 5-8% | 8-12% |
| Defective / DOA | Product quality or a dead unit | Business | 4-6% | 15-25% |
| Wrong item sent | Pick error | Business | 3-5% | 3-5% |
| Changed mind | Genuine buyer's remorse | Customer, if policy says so | 12-20% | 5-8% |
| Late delivery | Promise broken | Business | 4-8% | 3-5% |
| Integrity concern | Empty box, used item, weight mismatch | Neither until proven | 1-3% | 2-6% |

The percentages are orientation, not benchmarks. What matters is the mix inside one business over
time: a shift from "changed mind" to "not as described" is a listing problem, and a shift from "size"
to "defective" is a supplier problem.

## Diagnosis: read four cuts, in this order

1. **By SKU.** One SKU at three times the category rate is a product or fit problem. Ten SKUs at the
   same rate is a listing, packaging or courier problem.
2. **By size or variant.** If 80% of returns on a shirt are size M and L, the chart is wrong, not the
   customer. Compare the returned sizes against the sizes sold: a size that returns above its share of
   sales is a chart error.
3. **By channel.** Marketplace returns run higher than your own store because the buyer sees less of
   the brand and more of the grid. A channel 8 points above the others is a content problem on that
   channel.
4. **By courier and pin code cluster.** Damage concentrated in one courier or one route is a
   packaging or handling fix with a claim attached.

## The five causes and the fix each one points to

| Cause | Evidence | Fix | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Size chart wrong | Return concentration in two sizes | Measure the actual garment, publish flat measurements, add model height and size worn | Content and merchandising |
| Photos misleading | "Not as described" on colour | Shoot in daylight, add a colour-true swatch, show the product at scale | Creative |
| Packaging fails | Damage claims by courier | Double-wall, corner protection, a fragile label, a test drop | Packaging |
| Pick error | Wrong item, wrong variant | Barcode scan at pick, variant photo on the pick list | Warehouse |
| Promise too aggressive | Late delivery returns | Honest dispatch and delivery estimate by pin code | Operations |

## Reading a single return properly

Ask four questions before the disposition: what did the customer say, what did the listing show, what
does the timeline say (delivered when, requested when), and what does the history say (first return or
fifth). The answers decide whether this is a service recovery, a policy application or an integrity
flag.

## Anti-patterns

- **One reason code for everything.** "Customer not satisfied" makes the whole log unreadable.
- **Letting the marketplace's codes replace yours.** Map them to your eight, or the read is impossible.
- **Judging a SKU on a month.** Small numbers swing; three months or 30 returns, whichever is later.
- **Fixing the customer instead of the chart.** The chart is the cheaper fix and it stops the next
  hundred returns.
- **Reading returns only when they spike.** By then the season is over and the stock is bought.
- **Blaming the courier for damage without a packaging test.** Half of "damaged in transit" is
  packaging that was never going to survive a hub.
