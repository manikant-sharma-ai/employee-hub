# Marketplace returns and claims

On a marketplace you do not own the return. You own the evidence, the window and the claim - and the
window is the part that costs money when it closes.

## Who decides what

| Decision | Who | What you control |
|---|---|---|
| Return acceptance | Marketplace policy | Your reason codes and your response time |
| Refund | Marketplace | Nothing, once approved |
| Reverse pickup | Marketplace logistics | Nothing, except the escalation |
| Unit disposition | Marketplace, or you if it is returned to you | Whether you opt for return-to-seller |
| Claim | You, inside the window | The file and its quality |

Your leverage is entirely in three places: the reason code you map to, the evidence you upload, and
whether you file inside the window.

## The claim types and their windows

| Claim | When it applies | Evidence that wins | Typical window |
|---|---|---|---|
| SAFE-T / buyer-return abuse | Empty box, used item, wrong item returned | Unboxing video, weight record, QC photos, order history | 7-15 days from the return event |
| Lost in transit | Forward shipment not delivered | AWB trace, courier proof | Per marketplace, often 30 days |
| Damaged in transit | Item reached the buyer damaged | Buyer photos, packaging photos, weight | Per marketplace |
| Reimbursement for a marketplace-caused cancellation | Out of stock after order, logistics failure | Order and inventory records | Per marketplace |
| Weight or dimension dispute | Charge correction by the marketplace | Packed weight and dimension proof | Per invoice cycle |

Windows move. Check the current policy in the seller panel before filing, and record the date checked.

## The claim file that gets paid

1. **Order and return IDs** in the title of the claim, not in the body.
2. **The evidence, in the order the reviewer reads it**: what was shipped, what came back, what
   differs.
3. **Weight and dimension proof** - the packed weight, the return weight, and where each was recorded.
4. **Photos with a scale** and, for electronics, the serial or IMEI.
5. **The policy clause** the claim rests on, quoted.
6. **One claim per event.** Bundling three issues into one claim gets one rejected.

## Operating rhythm

- **Daily**: new return requests, reason codes mapped, response inside the SLA.
- **Weekly**: claim windows closing in the next seven days, filed before they close.
- **Monthly**: claim recovery rate - claimed, approved, rejected, appealed, recovered - as a percentage
  of the value of returns. Anything below 40% means the evidence discipline is weak.

## Anti-patterns

- **Filing after the window.** The most common and most expensive mistake; the money is simply gone.
- **A generic claim with no weight or photo.** Rejected, and the appeal has less to work with.
- **Accepting a reason code that shifts liability to you.** Map the buyer's words to the correct code.
- **No unboxing discipline at the warehouse.** The video taken on receipt is the only evidence that
  survives an appeal.
- **Treating a rejected claim as final.** One appeal with a better file is worth the ten minutes.
- **Assuming the marketplace policy matches your own.** It does not, and the stricter one governs.

## The seller panel, weekly

Twenty minutes a week, in this order:

1. **New return requests** - reason code mapped, response inside the SLA.
2. **Claims expiring in seven days** - filed with evidence before the window closes.
3. **Rejected claims** - one appeal each, with a better file, before the appeal window shuts.
4. **Charge corrections** - weight and dimension disputes, with the packed weight proof.
5. **Return-to-origin rate by courier** - the pin codes where delivery keeps failing.
6. **Buyer ratings and late-dispatch flags** - the two metrics that restrict participation.

The panel is a queue with deadlines. Anything not read weekly is money that has already been lost.
