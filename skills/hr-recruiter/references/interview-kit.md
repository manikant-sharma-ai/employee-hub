# Interview kit

An unstructured interview measures how much the interviewer liked the conversation. A structured one measures whether the candidate has done the thing the role requires.

## The loop, with each stage owning different criteria

| Stage | Duration | Owns | Who |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Screen call | 15-20 min | Must-haves at a surface level, practicals | Recruiter |
| 2. Craft / work sample | 45-60 min | Can they do the actual work | Hiring manager |
| 3. Judgement and scenarios | 45 min | Decisions under ambiguity | Peer or senior operator |
| 4. Working style and values | 30 min | How they behave when it is hard | Someone they will not report to |
| 5. Close | 20 min | Questions, expectations, the hard parts again | Founder or hiring manager |

Rules:

- **Every criterion is owned by exactly one stage.** Two stages testing "communication" waste an hour.
- **Every stage has a written rubric**, filled in before the discussion, not after.
- **The same questions, in the same order**, for every candidate at that stage.
- **No stage is a vibe check.** If a stage cannot produce a score, remove it.

## The work sample, which is the strongest signal

| Role type | Sample | Time to complete |
|---|---|---|
| Sales | Write the first touch to a real (anonymised) prospect from the ICP | 30 min |
| Operations | Here is a week of dispatch data with 14 discrepancies - find the cause | 45 min |
| Accounts | Reconcile a 40-line bank extract against a 38-line ledger | 45 min |
| Support | Answer three real customer messages, one angry | 30 min |
| Engineering | A small, realistic task with a stated time box | 90 min max |
| Design | Redo one screen from a real product with the brief | 60 min |
| Recruiting or HR | Screen four anonymised CVs against a scorecard | 40 min |

Rules: **pay for anything over two hours**, never use unpaid work that has commercial value, and give the same brief to every candidate. The debrief is about the reasoning, not the polish.

## Question patterns that produce evidence

| Pattern | Example | What it avoids |
|---|---|---|
| Past-specific | "Tell me about the last discrepancy you investigated. What did you do first?" | Hypotheticals that produce rehearsed answers |
| Follow the number | "You said 99% accuracy. What was it before, and what changed it?" | Borrowed credit |
| Failure-forward | "What is something you got wrong in the last year, and what did you change?" | Performed perfection |
| Scenario with a constraint | "Two of your three loaders call in sick on the day of a big dispatch. Walk me through the next two hours." | Abstract "how would you handle" |
| Reverse | "What would your last supervisor say is the thing you need to improve?" | Self-awareness, honestly scored |
| Trade-off | "Faster dispatch or lower cost - which would you protect, and when would you switch?" | Values in the abstract |

## What a strong answer contains

For each question, write this before the interviews, not after.

| Element | Example - "a carrier disputes a loading error" |
|---|---|
| First move | Secures the evidence (photos, seal number, weight slip) before anyone speaks |
| Second move | Separates the commercial conversation from the fact-finding |
| Names the constraint | Does not promise a credit without authority |
| Follows up | Writes it down, tells the customer a time |
| Recognises the pattern | Asks whether it has happened with this carrier before |

## Scoring

| Score | Definition |
|---|---|
| 3 | Exceeds - would teach the team something |
| 2 | Meets - has done it, can explain the reasoning |
| 1 | Partial - adjacent, would need support |
| 0 | No evidence |
| -1 | Evidence of a harmful pattern |

Decision rule, agreed before the loop: **all criteria at 2 or above, and no -1 on any must-have.** Anything else is a discussion, not an average - averaging hides a fatal gap behind three strong scores.

## The debrief

1. **Scores collected before anyone speaks.** Written, independent.
2. **Disagreements surfaced first**, not averaged away.
3. **Evidence quoted, not impressions** - "she said X" rather than "she seemed sharp".
4. **The one open question** named, and whether it can be closed with a reference or a second conversation.
5. **Decision and reason recorded**, including for the candidates rejected.

## Anti-patterns

- **"Tell me about yourself" as the opening question.** It measures rehearsal, and it eats ten minutes.
- **Brainteasers and riddles.** They measure tolerance for riddles.
- **An interview panel of five.** It produces one dominant voice and four abstentions.
- **Questions about family, marriage, caste, religion or age.** Never, in any framing, however friendly.
- **Changing the questions for the candidate you like.** That is how a process becomes a preference.
- **Debriefing by averaging scores.** A fatal gap is not offset by enthusiasm.
