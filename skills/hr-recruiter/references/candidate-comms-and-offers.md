# Candidate communication and offers

The candidate experience is the employer brand, and in a small market it is the whole of it. Every applicant talks to three others.

## The four messages you will write most

| Message | When | Must contain |
|---|---|---|
| Advance | Within 2 working days of the stage | What happens next, who, when, and what to prepare |
| Decline | Within 5 working days of the decision | A real reason, one line, respectfully put |
| Scheduling | Same day as the decision | Two specific slots, the duration, the format, who they will meet |
| Silence recovery | 4-5 days after no reply | A low-pressure, specific re-open |

## The decline that does not damage

> Subject: your application for <role>
>
> Hi <name>,
>
> Thank you for applying for the operations manager role, and for the time you put into the dispatch case.
>
> We are going ahead with candidates who have run multi-godown dispatch at this scale, which was the main gap in your application. That is a specific requirement of this role rather than a judgement on your work.
>
> I will keep your details for the supervisor role we expect to open in the next quarter, if you would like that - just reply and I will note it.
>
> <name>

Rules: a reason that is about the role, not the person; no false hope; no "we'll keep your CV on file" unless it is true; the candidate's name spelled correctly.

## Silence recovery

> Subject: still interested?
>
> Hi <name> - you were going to come back to me about the Tuesday slot and I have not heard, which usually means the role or the timing has changed on your side.
>
> No pressure either way. If you are still interested, reply with one of: Tuesday 4pm, Wednesday 11am, or a time that suits you. If you have taken something else, tell me and I will close the file - genuinely no hard feelings.
>
> <name>

Why it works: it names the likely reason, removes the obligation to explain, and gives a four-word way to answer.

## CTC structure, presented so it can be compared

| Component | Amount | Guaranteed? | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fixed pay (monthly x 12) | ₹ | Yes | The number to compare on |
| Variable / incentive | ₹ | Conditional | State the condition and the historic payout |
| Statutory bonus | ₹ | Per statute | Do not present it as a benefit |
| PF (employer) | ₹ | Yes | Part of CTC, not take-home |
| Gratuity provision | ₹ | Accrues | Part of CTC, paid on exit |
| Insurance | ₹ | Yes | Health cover amount and who is covered |
| Allowances (conveyance, telephone) | ₹ | Yes, if fixed | Say whether they are reimbursed or fixed |
| **Total CTC** | ₹ | | |
| **Monthly take-home, approximate** | ₹ | | The number the candidate actually needs |

Rule: always show take-home. A ₹9 LPA CTC and a ₹9.6 LPA CTC can differ by ₹4,000 a month in hand because of structure, and a candidate who discovers that after joining leaves.

## The offer conversation

1. **Confirm interest before discussing the number** - "if the number works, is there anything else that would stop you joining?"
2. **State the number and the structure together**, never the number alone.
3. **Give the start date options** and the notice-period reality.
4. **Ask for a decision date**, and hold it.
5. **Put it in writing the same day** - a verbal offer that waits a week is a lost candidate.

## Counter-offers and negotiation

| Situation | Response |
|---|---|
| "I have another offer at X" | Ask for the structure, not just the number; compare like for like |
| Counter-offer from their current employer | Ask what the counter-offer changes about the reason they were leaving; it rarely fixes it |
| Asking above the band | Say the band and what would justify the top of it - never invent room |
| Negotiating the title | Trade it for something the business needs; do not give a title the role does not support |
| Delay because of a notice period | Confirm the buy-out policy and who pays; put it in writing |

Never negotiate against yourself. One clear number, one clear reason, one decision date.

## Anti-patterns

- **Ghosting after the final round.** The most damaging single behaviour in hiring, and it is remembered for years.
- **"We'll keep your CV on file"** when there is no file. Say what is true.
- **Sending a decline from a no-reply address.** It reads as a system, because it is one.
- **Quoting CTC without take-home.** It creates a resignation in month two.
- **Promising a start date the business has not approved.** The candidate resigns, then waits, then leaves.
- **Discussing one candidate's offer with another.** Word travels in a small industry.
- **Changing the offer after acceptance.** It is legal in most cases and destroys the relationship in all of them.
