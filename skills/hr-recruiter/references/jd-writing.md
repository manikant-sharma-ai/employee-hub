# JD writing

A JD has two jobs: get the right people to apply, and get the wrong people not to. Most JDs fail the second and do not do the first.

## The structure that works

| Section | Length | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Title | 2-5 words | The role as the market names it, plus seniority. Not "Growth Ninja" |
| First line | 1-2 sentences | What the person will own, and why it matters now |
| The company, honestly | 3-4 lines | What you do, size, stage, where the money comes from |
| In your first year | 3-5 bullets | Outcomes with numbers, straight from the scorecard |
| What you must have done before | 3-4 bullets | Must-have competencies, stated as experience |
| Adds an advantage | 2-3 bullets | Nice-to-haves, clearly optional |
| How we work | 3-4 lines | Real practices: hours, remote policy, who you report to, how decisions are made |
| What is hard about this job | 2-4 bullets | The messy truth |
| Compensation | 1-2 lines | Band if possible, structure if not |
| Process | 3-4 lines | Stages, timeline, who you will meet |
| How to apply | 1-2 lines | Exactly what to send, and to whom |

Total: 400-700 words. Longer JDs are read by nobody and apply-rate falls.

## Titles

- Use the words candidates search: "Operations Manager", "Accounts Executive", "Field Sales Executive", "Backend Engineer".
- Add seniority honestly: Junior, Senior, Lead. If the person will be the only one, say "sole" or "first hire".
- Never use internal grades or invented titles in the public posting - nobody searches for them.

## The hard-parts section, which is the differentiator

| Honest line | What it filters out |
|---|---|
| "You will spend two hours a day on the floor, in a non-air-conditioned godown." | People who wanted a desk job |
| "The last person left because the reporting was manual. Fixing that is your first project." | People who need clean systems to start |
| "You will manage two people who have been here longer than you." | People who cannot handle that conversation |
| "Our customers call on WhatsApp, at 9pm." | People who want a 9-to-6 boundary |
| "There is no marketing budget. Outbound is the channel." | People who expect inbound leads |

A JD without this section gets applications from people who will leave in four months, and the cost of that is far higher than a longer search.

## Compensation, stated

| Option | When |
|---|---|
| Exact band | Best. Saves a screening stage and signals fairness |
| Band width | Where the number depends on experience; state both ends |
| Structure only | If the business refuses; give fixed vs variable split and the benefits |
| Nothing | Only acceptable for a very senior retained search, and even then it costs applicants |

Never write "salary as per industry standards". It is read as "we have not decided", and the candidates you most want do not apply.

## Language

- **Second person, active voice.** "You will own dispatch" beats "the candidate will be responsible for".
- **Short sentences.** Assume the reader is on a phone, between two other things.
- **No internal jargon, no abbreviations without expansion**, no "LTV/CAC" unless the role requires it.
- **Inclusive by construction** - "you" rather than "he", no gendered role nouns, no "young and energetic", no "recent graduate preferred" unless genuinely required.
- **Regional language version** where the role is a shop-floor or field role: a Marathi or Tamil version of a Nagpur or Coimbatore JD gets materially more applications from people who will actually do the job.

## What to leave out

| Leave out | Because |
|---|---|
| "Should be hardworking and passionate" | Untestable, and everyone writes it |
| "Excellent communication skills" | Means nothing without a context; say what they must explain and to whom |
| A list of 14 tools | Candidates optimise for the list instead of the job |
| "Fast-paced environment" | Read as "chaotic"; say what the pace actually is |
| A degree requirement not needed for the work | It removes good candidates for no gain |
| Photos of the office as the main content | It is not the job |

## Anti-patterns

- **Copying a JD from a similar company.** It describes their role, and candidates can tell.
- **A 12-bullet "responsibilities" list.** It is a job description from 2009; outcomes and hard parts are what people respond to.
- **Hiding the location or the working days.** It produces applications that die at the first call.
- **"Apply with your CV and a cover letter"** when nobody reads cover letters. Ask for the one thing you will actually read - a short note, a sample, a number from their past.
- **Posting the same JD on six boards with no change.** Each board has a different audience and reading register.
- **A JD that a current employee would not recognise as their own job.** Ask one of them to read it before it goes out.
