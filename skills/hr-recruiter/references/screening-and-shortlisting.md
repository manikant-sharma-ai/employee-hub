# Screening and shortlisting

Screening is not reading CVs. It is applying the same five criteria to every applicant, in the same order, with the evidence written down.

## The scoring method

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 2 | Clear evidence of the criterion, from a specific role or project |
| 1 | Partial evidence, adjacent experience, or plausible but unstated |
| 0 | No evidence |
| -1 | Evidence of the opposite (a gap that would need unlearning) |

Per candidate: score every must-have. A candidate with a 0 on any must-have goes to decline, however strong elsewhere - unless the must-have is genuinely teachable, which the scorecard has already decided.

## The pass order, cheapest filter first

| Pass | What you look at | What it removes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Must-have 1 only | The largest group, in the least time |
| 2 | Remaining must-haves | Structural mismatches |
| 3 | Evidence of the outcomes | People who did the work, not the adjacent work |
| 4 | Stability and trajectory | Job-hopping patterns, or a step down that needs explaining |
| 5 | Practical constraints - location, notice, band | Deals that die at offer stage |
| 6 | Nice-to-haves | Ranking, not filtering |

Doing the passes in this order is what makes 200 applications a two-hour job instead of two days.

## Reading a CV for evidence

| Signal | What it suggests | Caution |
|---|---|---|
| A number attached to a past role | Real ownership | Check whether the number is theirs or the team's |
| "Responsible for" | Probably a support role | Ask what they decided, not what they were near |
| A promotion inside one company | Someone who earned trust | Ask what changed |
| Three companies, 14 months each | Either contract work or a pattern | Ask directly; do not assume |
| A gap | Often caregiving, health, study or a failed venture | Never penalise without asking |
| A switch of industry | Transferable or not, depends on the mechanism | Test the mechanism, not the industry |
| Freelance or family business | Broad ownership, thin structure | Test process discipline specifically |

## Structuring the shortlist

Six is a good number: enough choice, few enough to interview this week.

| Bucket | Definition | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Advance | Scores 2 on all must-haves, evidence for at least two outcomes | Interview this week |
| Hold | One must-have at 1, or a constraint unresolved | One clarifying question by email |
| Decline | A 0 or -1 on a must-have | Written decline, reason recorded internally |

## The screening call, 15 minutes

1. **Two minutes on the role and the hard parts** - and let them react. The reaction is data.
2. **Ten minutes on evidence** - one question per must-have, asking for a specific past instance.
3. **Three minutes on practicals** - notice period, location, band, and the earliest start date.
4. **One question from them**, and note what they chose to ask. It reveals what they care about.

Record: score per criterion, one line of evidence, and the practicals. Never a paragraph of impression.

## Blind screening, where it is possible

Strip before the first pass: name, photo, gender, age, marital status, address, caste or community markers, college name where it is not job-relevant.

Record in the file that the first pass was blind. It is both fairer and more accurate - the evidence is that it produces better shortlists, not just nicer ones.

## Anti-patterns

- **Screening by college and previous employer.** It reproduces the last hire and misses the person who did the job in a smaller company.
- **Ranking by years of experience.** Ten years of the same year repeated is not seniority.
- **Judging English fluency from a CV.** A strong operator with weak written English is common; test the actual requirement.
- **Rejecting for a gap or a career break.** Ask. The reason is usually ordinary and the person is often better than the field.
- **A shortlist of 20.** It means the criteria were not used, and the interviews will be unstructured.
- **No written reason per rejection.** You cannot improve a process you cannot audit, and candidates deserve better than silence.
