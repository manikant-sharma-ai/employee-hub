# Targets, incentives and review

A target is a design decision, and most are made by adding a percentage to last year. This file is a better way,
and the review cadence that makes it real.

## Splitting a target, in order

1. **Territory potential**: outlets × realistic monthly value per outlet by class. This is the base.
2. **Current share**: what the territory actually does today, from the sales data.
3. **A realistic share gain**: two to four points a year in a mature territory, more in a new one.
4. **Coverage adjustment**: a territory at 60% coverage has room that a fully covered one does not.
5. **Seasonality**: the monthly split, from last year's shape, not an even twelfth.
6. **The sanity check**: does the rep's target imply a drop size and a call count that the beat can deliver?

If step 6 fails, the target is arithmetic that nobody can execute.

## Primary against secondary in the target

| Target type | What it drives | The failure it causes |
|---|---|---|
| Primary only | Stock movement out of your godown | Stuffing, returns, and a dead quarter |
| Secondary only | Real offtake | The distributor's stock position becomes invisible |
| Both, weighted | Movement and offtake | Needs secondary data, which needs a clause |

A workable default for a business without reliable secondary data: set the target on primary, but pay the
variable component on primary **and** a stock-cover condition - no incentive above 45 days of distributor cover.
That single condition removes most of the stuffing.

## The monthly split

| Month | Share of the year | Note |
|---|---|---|
| April-June | 22-25% | A new financial year, and often a scheme month |
| July-September | 24-26% | Monsoon slows some categories, lifts others |
| October-December | 28-32% | Festive; the quarter that decides the year |
| January-March | 22-25% | Year-end push, and the discount month |

State the shape and its source. An even twelfth is a plan that is wrong eleven months out of twelve.

## Incentive design, four rules

1. **Pay on the thing you want to happen.** If it is secondary, pay on secondary; if coverage, pay on coverage.
2. **Cap the upside at a level the business can afford** in the best month, and model it.
3. **Make the gate explicit**: stock cover, outstanding, and data submission. An incentive that ignores these
   buys the wrong behaviour.
4. **Pay on time.** A late incentive teaches the team that the number is negotiable.

| Structure | When it fits | The risk |
|---|---|---|
| Flat percentage of sales | Simple, mature territory | Rewards the big territory, not the effort |
| Slab with a gate | Most field teams | Complexity; publish the slab in one page |
| Growth over last year | A stable base | Punishes a rep who inherited a good year |
| Coverage plus offtake | New territories | Slower to earn, so watch attrition |
| Team pool | Interdependent beats | Free-riders, unless the pool is small |

## The review cadence

| Cadence | Duration | Agenda |
|---|---|---|
| Daily | 10 min | Yesterday's coverage and orders, one good visit, one question |
| Weekly | 45 min | Territory numbers against the monthly split, coverage, stock-outs, credit, one fix |
| Monthly | 90 min | Full territory read, distributor review, the target for next month, one coaching topic |
| Quarterly | Half day | Territory re-split, coverage gaps, attrition risk, the target for the quarter |

The weekly review is where a miss is still fixable. A monthly review explains a miss; a weekly one prevents it.

## Reading a miss, in three decompositions

```
Sales = Outlets covered x Strike rate x Drop size
```

| Component | If it fell | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| Outlets covered | Fewer days, more travel, absences | Rebuild the beat or the cluster |
| Strike rate | Fewer orders per visit | The call standard, the order ask, a stock or price problem |
| Drop size | Smaller orders per outlet | Range selling, a scheme, or the outlet is buying elsewhere |

Never accept "market is slow" as an explanation. It may be true; it is not a decomposition, and it does not tell
anyone what to do on Monday.

## Attrition risk, before the resignation

| Signal | Reading |
|---|---|
| Coverage falling while effort is unchanged | Disengagement, or a beat that has become impossible |
| Orders clustering in two friendly outlets | Comfort, and a shrinking universe |
| Logs arriving late or in a different format | Disengagement, or a personal problem |
| A rep's incentive falling two months running while the market is fine | A territory problem the manager has not seen |

A rep who resigns has usually been signalling for two months in the coverage data.

## Anti-patterns

- **A uniform growth percentage on every territory.** It rewards the territory with the most headroom and
  punishes the one that is already covered.
- **A target set without the beat arithmetic.** It becomes a number the rep ignores by the 15th.
- **An incentive that pays on primary with no stock-cover gate.** It buys a godown full of stock and a return.
- **A monthly review with no weekly review.** By the time the miss is visible, the month is gone.
- **Publishing individual numbers in the group.** It produces politics, not performance.
