# Targets and review cadence

A target that is not converted into a daily call number is a wish with a date on it.

## The arithmetic that must be shown

```
Monthly target            ₹18,00,000
Working days                     24
Required per day          ₹75,000
Average order value        ₹9,000
Calls needed per day          8.3
Strike rate (orders/calls)     55%
Calls to make per day         15.1
```

Fifteen calls a day is not a target, it is a capacity problem. The same arithmetic, run honestly,
tells the business which lever exists: more calls (capacity), a bigger basket (mix or pricing), or a
higher strike rate (coaching or product fit). Say which, with the number.

## Primary and secondary

| Term | Meaning | What it hides |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | What the company invoices to the dealer or distributor | Loading a godown |
| Secondary | What the dealer sells onward to retail | The actual market |
| Tertiary | What retail sells to the consumer | Rarely tracked, always the truth |

A rep can hit primary every month while secondary falls, and the business finds out when the dealer
returns stock or stops paying. Track secondary wherever possible; where it is not tracked, say so
every time primary is reported.

## Target setting

| Method | When | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Last year plus a growth % | Stable category, no new outlets | Institutionalises last year's mistake |
| Market potential bottom-up (outlets x frequency x basket) | New territory, or after a re-cut | Needs an honest outlet list |
| Capacity-based (calls x strike rate x basket) | When the team is the constraint | Becomes an excuse if the strike rate is low |
| Top-down from the company plan | Always, in the end | Disconnected from the field unless tested |

The defensible method is bottom-up, tested against capacity, then reconciled to the company number.
When the three disagree, the gap is the conversation - not a number handed down.

## The cadence

| Rhythm | What happens | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Daily | Rep's call list and the day's orders | Coverage, orders, one blocker |
| Weekly | Plan versus executed, pipeline for the next week | The next beat, one coaching point per rep |
| Fortnightly | Dealer secondary and stock cover | Loading risk, scheme decision |
| Monthly | Full territory review: coverage, strike rate, AOV, new, lost, collections | The next month's target and the one change |
| Quarterly | Territory re-cut, outlet master clean, category recalculation | A new beat, not a new target |

## The four numbers that matter

| Metric | Definition | What it diagnoses |
|---|---|---|
| Coverage | Outlets visited / outlets due per policy | Whether the plan is being run |
| Strike rate | Orders / calls | Whether the rep can sell |
| Average order value | Value / orders | Whether the basket is right |
| New and lost outlets | Opened / closed in the period | Whether the territory is growing or leaking |

Ranking on one number always misleads. A rep with 90% coverage and a 20% strike rate is visiting the
wrong outlets; a rep with 40% coverage and a 90% strike rate is under-covering a good territory.

## Reviewing a rep without demoralising them

1. Show the four numbers against the territory's median, not against the best rep.
2. Ask what is blocking, and write the answer down.
3. Pick one thing to change this week - one, not five.
4. Ride along before concluding. A rep's route tells you more in a day than a month of numbers.
5. Never compare two reps with different territories on the same target.

## Anti-patterns

- **A target with no daily arithmetic.** The rep cannot act on a monthly figure until the last week.
- **Primary reported as sales.** It is a shipment until secondary proves otherwise.
- **Changing the target mid-month** without recording why. It destroys the review's meaning.
- **Ranking reps publicly on one number.** It produces sandbagging, not performance.
- **A monthly review that is a scolding.** The output should be one change and one support action.
