# Visit log and call notes

A visit that produces no record produces no follow-up, and a follow-up that is not dated does not
happen. The log is the only asset a field team builds that compounds.

## The six fields

No more. A log that takes four minutes is a log that gets written.

| Field | Content | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Outlet and person met | Name and role - owner, manager, accountant | The person decides, not the shop |
| What was said | Two sentences, in their words | Paraphrase loses the objection |
| Order | Value, SKUs, delivery date, or "no order" with the reason | The number is the point |
| Objection or opportunity | One line: price, credit, competitor scheme, shelf space | This is what the next visit uses |
| Follow-up | One action, one owner, one date | The only field that makes the log matter |
| Next visit | Planned date | Closes the loop with the beat |

Optional but valuable: stock visible on shelf, competitor activity, display condition, a photograph.

## The two-sentence standard

> "Owner said the new 500g pack is moving but the 1kg is not, because the local wholesaler is
> undercutting by ₹12. He will take 20 cases of 500g on Friday if we hold the scheme."

Two sentences, and a follow-up is obvious. Compare:

> "Met owner. Positive. Will follow up."

The second is what most logs contain, and it is worth nothing on the next visit.

## Objection codes

Fixed codes make objections countable across a territory.

| Code | Objection | What usually resolves it |
|---|---|---|
| `PRICE` | Cheaper competitor | A value comparison, not a discount |
| `CREDIT` | Wants more credit days | The credit policy, and the founder |
| `SCHEME` | Waiting for a better scheme | Scheme calendar, in writing |
| `STOCK` | Already has stock | Stock cover check before the visit |
| `DEMAND` | Customers do not ask for it | Merchandising or a trial pack |
| `TRUST` | New brand, unknown | Reference outlet, replacement guarantee |
| `SPACE` | No shelf space | Display plan, or a category where space exists |
| `RELATIONSHIP` | Loyal to the current supplier | Time and consistency, not a discount |
| `PAYMENT` | Dispute over a past invoice | Reconcile before selling |

Counted monthly, these codes tell the business what the field is actually fighting. Forty `CREDIT`
codes is a finance problem wearing a sales costume.

## Follow-up discipline

| Rule | Reason |
|---|---|
| One follow-up per visit, maximum | Three actions is none |
| Always with a date | "Soon" is not a commitment |
| Owned by a person, not "the team" | Shared ownership is no ownership |
| Reviewed at the start of the next visit | The follow-up is the agenda |
| Closed or re-dated, never left open | An open follow-up is a broken promise with a paper trail |

## What the note is not

- Not a diary. Nobody reads a paragraph about the weather.
- Not a place for a personal remark about the owner. The log is discoverable.
- Not a sales pitch rehearsal. Record what they said, not what you said.
- Not a substitute for the order. If an order was taken, the order goes into the system the same day.

## Photographs and evidence

| What | Why | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Shelf display | Proves the merchandising claim | No customer faces, no personal data |
| Competitor scheme poster | The competitive picture, dated | Photograph the poster, not the shopkeeper |
| Damaged stock | Supports a claim or a return | Date and outlet, no names in the repo |
| Signed order or acknowledgement | Dispute evidence | Store in the business system, not in notes |

## Anti-patterns

- **A log written at 11 pm from memory.** The objection is already lost.
- **A log with no follow-up field.** The visit becomes a courtesy call.
- **Ten fields.** The rep will fill three and skip the rest, and the three will be the easy ones.
- **Free-text objections.** They cannot be counted, so the territory never learns what it is fighting.
- **No photograph standard.** Then every merchandising claim is an opinion.
