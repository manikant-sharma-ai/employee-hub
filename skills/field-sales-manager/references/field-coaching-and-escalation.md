# Field coaching and escalation

Two jobs in one file: making a rep better, and knowing when the answer is not coaching.

## Diagnose before you coach

| Symptom | Likely cause | The test | The fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low coverage, low strike rate | Wrong outlets or wrong beat | Ride along one day | Re-cut the beat |
| Low coverage, high strike rate | Territory too large | Count outlets against capacity | Split the territory |
| High coverage, low strike rate | Pitching the wrong thing, or wrong outlets | Listen to three calls | Coaching on the opening and the outlet list |
| High coverage, high strike rate, flat value | Basket too small | Check SKU mix per order | Bundle, mix, or a scheme |
| Good numbers, bad collections | Selling to anyone | Ageing by rep | Order-hold rules, not coaching |
| Falling numbers, no explanation | Personal, or a lost key outlet | A private conversation | Whatever it is, not a target reset |

The ride-along is the cheapest diagnostic in field sales and the one most often skipped. One day
tells you whether the problem is the plan, the pitch or the person.

## The coaching conversation, in four questions

1. What is working in this territory right now?
2. Which three outlets are you losing, and why?
3. What would make next week different?
4. What do you need from me?

Then one change, written down, with a date. Not five. A rep given five changes makes none.

## When the answer is escalation, not coaching

| Situation | Escalate to | Because |
|---|---|---|
| A key outlet is being poached with a better scheme | Founder | Pricing and scheme decisions are not the rep's |
| A dealer's credit has gone bad | Founder and `collections-agent` | Money, not sales |
| A distributor is pushing a competing brand | Founder | Channel strategy |
| A price objection repeats across the territory | `pricing-analyst` | It is a pricing finding, not forty objections |
| A product defect appears in the field | `quality-inspector` | It is a quality problem wearing a sales costume |
| A rep is in a personal difficulty | Founder, privately | Never in a review meeting |
| A market is closing or a road is out | The beat plan | It is a planning input |

The distinction matters: coaching a rep for a pricing problem is how a good rep leaves.

## Territory re-cut, when and how

| Trigger | Action |
|---|---|
| Coverage below 70% of policy for two months | Re-cut or add capacity |
| A new town or market opens | Assign it before it is worked informally |
| A rep leaves | Re-cut before hiring, not after |
| A distributor is added or replaced | Redraw the boundary around the channel, not the map |
| A zone's outlet count doubles | Split it before the coverage falls |

Re-cut on travel time and outlet count, not on a map's straight lines. Then ride the new territory once
before publishing it.

## The escalation ladder, written down

| Level | Who | Decides |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Rep | Order within the discount ladder, visit sequence |
| 2 | Area manager or founder | Discounts above the ladder, credit within policy, scheme participation |
| 3 | Founder only | Credit beyond policy, distributor replacement, price change, a key account |
| 4 | Outside | Legal recovery, contract termination, statutory notice |

A ladder nobody has written down becomes level 3 by default, and the founder spends the week on
decisions a rep could have made.

## Anti-patterns

- **Coaching a structural problem.** A rep cannot fix a territory that is too large.
- **Escalating everything.** A founder who decides every discount has no field team, only messengers.
- **A review meeting with no ride-along behind it.** The numbers describe the outcome, not the cause.
- **Comparing reps across different territories.** It teaches the team to want a small territory.
- **Waiting for the quarterly review** to act on a coverage collapse visible in week two.
