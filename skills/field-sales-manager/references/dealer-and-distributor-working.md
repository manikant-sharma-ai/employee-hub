# Dealer and distributor working

A distributor is not a customer. He is a business that buys your stock, sells it onward, and is paid on
terms that put your money at risk. Read him as a business, not as a relationship.

## The four numbers per dealer

| Number | Definition | Warning sign |
|---|---|---|
| Secondary sales | What he sells onward, per month | Falling while primary is flat |
| Stock cover | His stock / his monthly secondary | Above 1.5 months is loading |
| Credit days | Actual days to pay, not agreed | Actual above agreed, trending up |
| Outstanding | Amount due, and its ageing | Any bucket over 60 days growing |

If the business cannot produce secondary and stock cover, the first recommendation is to start
collecting them - monthly, in one format, from every distributor. Without them, every primary number
is a shipment and every dispute is an argument.

## Claims against secondary

The classic dispute: the dealer claims secondary sales far above what primary supports. The mechanics:

| Check | What it proves |
|---|---|
| Primary in, secondary claimed out, stock at start and end | Stock must reconcile: opening + primary - secondary = closing |
| Closing stock claim | Ask for it. A dealer with no stock count has no case and no defence |
| Scheme claims against invoices | Every scheme claim needs an invoice reference |
| Damage and expiry claims | Need photographs, batch numbers and a date |
| Credit notes issued | Compare against the scheme calendar, not the dealer's memory |

The reconciliation identity is the whole tool. If opening + primary - secondary does not equal the
closing stock he reports, one of the four is wrong, and the conversation is about which.

## Credit, without being the credit analyst

| Situation | What this role does | What it does not do |
|---|---|---|
| Dealer wants 45 days instead of 30 | Supplies the payment history and the outstanding ageing | Grant the limit |
| Outstanding crosses the agreed terms | Flags it in the visit plan before the next order | Chase it (collections-agent) |
| A dealer's cheque bounced | Records it and stops the beat from loading him | Decide the recovery step |
| A dealer wants to return slow stock | Checks the return policy and the scheme terms | Approve the return |

The field's job is to know before the order is taken. A rep who loads a dealer already at 90 days has
created a bad debt with a delivery note.

## Margins, schemes and the ladder

- **Dealer margin is a policy, not a negotiation.** If a rep can vary it, it varies.
- **Schemes have dates and conditions.** A scheme announced verbally and honoured late is a discount
  the business did not plan for.
- **Quantity schemes invite loading.** A dealer buys 500 cases to hit a slab and returns 200 after the
  scheme ends.
- **The discount ladder must be written**: what a rep may offer, what needs the area manager, what
  needs the founder. Anything above the rep's limit is a call, not a judgement.

## The distributor review, monthly

| Item | Question | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Secondary trend | Up, flat, down, and against the category | One line per distributor |
| Stock cover | Over 1.5 months? | A loading flag |
| Outstanding | Ageing by bucket | The order-hold list |
| Coverage of his own retailers | How many does he actually service? | A gap list for the rep |
| Claims | Scheme, damage, expiry | Reconciled or disputed, with the identity above |
| Competition | What is he pushing harder, and why | The scheme or margin conversation |

## When a distributor should be replaced

| Signal | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Secondary falling for three months while the market grows | Three consecutive periods |
| Stock cover above 2 months | Persistent, with no scheme exit |
| Outstanding beyond terms for 90+ days | With no payment plan honoured |
| Servicing under 50% of his own retailer list | Measured, not alleged |
| Pushing a competing brand in the same category | Documented, not suspected |

Replacement costs two quarters of coverage. Say so before recommending it, and put the transition plan
in the same page.

## Anti-patterns

- **Treating a distributor as a customer.** He is a channel with a P&L, and it can fail.
- **Loading to hit a primary target.** The stock comes back as a return, a discount or a dead
  relationship.
- **A scheme with no written conditions.** Every dispute is then decided by whoever is loudest.
- **No stock count at the dealer.** Then the reconciliation identity has a free variable and the
  argument is unwinnable.
- **Replacing a distributor without a coverage plan.** The gap is filled by a competitor, not by you.
