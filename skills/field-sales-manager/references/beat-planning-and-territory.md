# Beat planning and territory

A beat is a promise about frequency. Everything else - the route, the order, the day - exists to keep
that promise without wasting the rep's day in traffic.

## The frequency policy

Write it down per outlet category, before any plan is built. A policy that lives in a founder's head
is a policy that changes with whoever is asking.

| Category | Typical definition | Frequency | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Top 20% of outlets by volume | Weekly | The order can be lost in a fortnight |
| B | Middle 50% | Fortnightly | Enough contact to hold share, not enough to crowd A |
| C | Long tail | Monthly | Presence, not relationship |
| New | Opened in the last 60 days | Weekly for 8 weeks | The habit is set early or never |
| Dormant | No order in 90 days | One visit, then quarterly | A visit to find out why, then a decision |
| Key account / institution | Contracts, tenders | Per contract calendar | Visits are driven by the contract, not the beat |

The policy produces a required-visit count per week. If that count exceeds capacity, the policy is
wrong - not the reps.

## The capacity arithmetic

| Input | Typical Indian field reality |
|---|---|
| Working days per month | 24-26, minus one for a monthly review |
| Calls per day, urban | 8-12 for FMCG-style retail, 4-6 for a technical sale |
| Calls per day, semi-urban | 6-9, longer hops |
| Time per call | 12-25 minutes including waiting for the owner |
| Travel per hop | 15-40 minutes in a city, 45-90 minutes between towns |
| Productive selling hours | 5.5-6.5 in a day, not 9 |

Calls per day is not a motivational number, it is a travel-and-time number. A plan with 15 calls a day
across four zones is a plan that will be abandoned on day two, and the abandonment will be blamed on
the rep.

## The zone-first rule

1. **Split the territory into zones** the rep can cover in a day without crossing back.
2. **Within a zone, order by priority**, then by physical sequence on the ground.
3. **Never cross a zone twice in a day.** The second crossing is where the day disappears.
4. **Cluster by market, not by pin code.** A market with 30 outlets is one stop and 30 calls.
5. **Respect the closed day.** Markets close on a fixed weekday in most Indian towns; a beat that
   visits a closed market is a plan that produces a zero and a wasted hop.

## The weekly shape

| Day | Shape | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Highest-priority zone, or the review and the plan | Collections and orders land early in the week |
| Tuesday-Thursday | Core selling days, the two best zones | Owner availability is highest |
| Friday | Secondary towns, new outlets, or the zone with the longest hop | The week has slack for travel |
| Saturday | Half day, collections, dealer reconciliation | Dealers are usually open |
| Sunday | Closed, unless retail is the business | — |

## Territory sizing

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Coverage below 70% of policy | Too many outlets for the rep | Re-cut, or add a rep, or lower the policy for C |
| Rep always travelling | Zones not contiguous | Redraw the boundary, not the beat |
| A outlets under-visited, C over-visited | No policy, or a policy nobody enforces | Publish the policy and measure against it |
| New outlets not opening | No dedicated new-outlet time | One day a fortnight ring-fenced for new |
| Big outlets in a "far" zone | Territory drawn on a map, not on travel time | Draw it on travel time |

## The outlet master

The plan is only as good as the list. Clean it before every quarter:

| Check | Rule |
|---|---|
| Duplicates | Same name and same locality = one outlet |
| Dead outlets | No order in 180 days = dormant, not deleted |
| Missing zone | Cannot be planned; assign before the plan |
| Missing last visit | Assume unvisited and schedule |
| Outlet type | Retail, dealer, distributor, institution, project - never blank |
| Category | A/B/C from volume, recalculated quarterly |

An outlet master with 15% duplicates produces a coverage number that is wrong by 15% and a plan that
sends a rep to the same shop twice under two names.

## Anti-patterns

- **A beat built from a map, not from travel time.** The straight line is never the road.
- **Equal frequency for every outlet.** It is the fastest way to under-serve the ones that matter.
- **A plan with no closed-day check.** One wasted hop a week is 4% of the year.
- **Planning 100% of capacity.** No slack means no new outlet, no emergency collection, no dealer
  visit that was not planned.
- **A beat that never changes.** The outlet mix changes every quarter; the plan should too.
- **A rep's route designed by someone who has never done the route.** Ride along before re-cutting.
