# Measurement and coverage

PR is the easiest place in a business to lie to yourself. This file is what to
count, what to say out loud, and what a clipping is actually worth.

## The four numbers that mean something

| Metric | How to get it | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| Reply rate | replies ÷ pitches sent, per list segment | Whether the list and the pitch fit |
| Coverage rate | published pieces ÷ pitches sent | Whether the story was news |
| Message pull-through | of the three points you wanted made, how many appear | Whether the note worked |
| Referral traffic and signups | UTM-tagged links, in their analytics | Whether coverage did anything commercial |

Two vanity numbers to report only with a caveat: **AVE** (advertising value
equivalency - a made-up multiplier that no buyer uses) and **total reach** (a
publication's readership figure, not the number of people who read the piece).

## The coverage log

One row per published piece:

| Date | Outlet | Reporter | Headline | Angle taken | Messages landed (of 3) | Link/UTM | Referrals | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Fill the "angle taken" column honestly. If you pitched the pricing story and they
published a founder profile, the pitch did not work even though coverage happened.

## Message pull-through

Before the send, write the three things a reader should come away knowing. After
publication, mark each as landed, distorted or absent. Two of three is a good
result. A story that mentions the company and gets the price wrong is worse than
silence, because it will be copied.

## Reading a clipping

- **Was the number right?** A wrong figure in a national daily propagates to every
  later story and to the next investor's diligence.
- **Was the description right?** "A food company" when you are a software company
  costs you the next twelve months of positioning.
- **Was the tone neutral, positive or hostile?** Neutral with the facts right beats
  glowing with the facts wrong.
- **Did it include a link or a name someone can search?** Coverage nobody can act
  on is a scrapbook entry.

## Corrections

If a fact is wrong: one short mail to the reporter, the correct fact, the source,
and a polite request for a correction. No argument, no threat, no tweet. If the
error is serious and the outlet does not correct it, a letter to the editor. If it
is defamatory, `legal-drafter` and a lawyer, immediately, and nothing is said in
public until they have advised.

## The monthly read

Six lines, no more:

1. Pitches sent, by segment.
2. Replies, by segment.
3. Pieces published, and the angle each took.
4. Messages that landed, out of three per piece.
5. Referral sessions and signups from UTM links - or "not measured", if analytics
   is not connected.
6. The one change for next month: the list, the angle, the note or the timing.

## What coverage cannot do

- It cannot substitute for demand. A well-covered product nobody wants stays
  unwanted.
- It cannot be bought honestly. Paid placement is advertising and must be labelled.
- It cannot be repeated monthly on the same hook. The second "we launched" is not news.
- It cannot be measured in isolation from the sales conversation that follows.

## Anti-patterns

- Reporting AVE as if it were revenue.
- Counting a syndicated copy of one wire story as eight pieces of coverage.
- Claiming a "media mention" that is a paid listicle.
- Never writing down which angle was pitched, so nobody can tell what worked.
- Judging PR on a single announcement instead of a quarter of attempts.
