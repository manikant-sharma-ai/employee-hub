# News hooks and angles

Most PR fails before it is written: there is no news. This file is the test that
saves the effort, and the list of things that genuinely are news.

## The editor test

Write the sentence a reporter would say to their editor, out loud:

> "A <type of company> in <place> has done <specific thing>, which <why it is unusual>,
> and it matters to <their readers> because <consequence>."

If you cannot fill all five blanks with facts, the story is not ready. Fix the
story, not the prose.

## What is actually news

| Hook | Example | What the note must carry |
|---|---|---|
| Money | Seed, Series A, debt, grant, profitable quarter | Amount, investor names, use of funds, revenue or growth |
| First | First in a city, a state, a segment, a language | What "first" is measured against, and who verified it |
| Price | A third cheaper, no subscription, ₹99 flat | The comparable, the unit, the basis of the claim |
| Scale milestone | 10,000 customers, 100 cities, 1 lakh units | The count, the period, the source of the count |
| A named person | Senior hire from a known company, a turnaround | The person, the prior role, the mandate |
| A named customer | A marquee logo or a government body | Permission in writing, and the specific outcome |
| New capability | A regulatory approval, a patent, a certification | The number, the authority, the date |
| Contrarian number | Category is shrinking; we grew 60% | The dataset, the period, the comparison |
| Expansion | New plant, new city, new country, new jobs | Investment, jobs, timeline, local detail |
| Crisis turned around | Survived a fire, a recall, a ban, and rebuilt | Facts, dates, what changed - never spin |

## What is not news, however it is dressed

- A website redesign, a new logo, an app update, a rebrand.
- "We are excited to announce" anything with no number, no first and no customer.
- A partnership where both sides are unknown and nothing has shipped.
- A founder's opinion on the industry, with no data behind it.
- An award nobody has heard of, bought by the entrant.
- A hiring plan with no names and no date.
- A product that "will revolutionise" a category with no users yet.

If the announcement is one of these, say so plainly, then offer the two moves that
work: **make news** (ship the thing, sign the customer, publish the number) or
**make a story** (a founder essay, a data study, a customer case) and route it to
`content-writer` instead.

## Angles from one fact set

One announcement carries four to six angles. Choose per publication.

| Publication type | Wants | Angle shape |
|---|---|---|
| Business daily (ET, Mint, Business Standard, Hindu BusinessLine) | Market, money, policy | Category size, the money, the competitive implication |
| Startup media (YourStory, Inc42, Entrackr, TechCircle) | Founder, funding, traction | The founder's turn, the round, the growth number |
| Trade press | Specification, process, compliance | What it does differently, capacity, certifications |
| Regional press | Local jobs, local pride, local language | Investment in the district, employment, a local customer |
| Vertical/consumer media | The user's experience | What changes for the person using it, with a price |
| TV and radio | One visual, one line | A demonstration, a number, a person to interview |

## The "so what" ladder

Ask three times, in order, and stop when the answer is about the reader:

1. So what? - "We launched a product."
2. So what? - "It costs a third of what agencies charge."
3. So what? - "A 20-person firm can now afford what only large firms could."

The third answer is the story. The first is a notice.

## Timing

- **Budget and financial-year windows** (January-March, and the April new year) produce
  money and policy stories; announcements there compete with a wall of them.
- **Diwali, Holi, the monsoon session of Parliament and the union budget week** are
  where announcements go to die. Work around them.
- **A quiet week in the trade press is worth more than a busy week in the national press.**
- **Never announce on a Friday evening** unless the story is bad. Nobody is reading.

## Anti-patterns

- Writing the release first and looking for the hook afterwards.
- One angle for every publication, so all six pitches read the same and none fits.
- "Exclusive" offered to five outlets on the same morning.
- A hook that requires a paragraph of context before it makes sense.
- Announcing a partnership that has not shipped a single transaction.
- Using "first" without naming the set you are first in.
