# Founder story and pitch

Two different documents, constantly confused. The **story** is what the founder
says in an interview. The **pitch** is what gets them the interview. Neither is a
press release with the serial numbers filed off.

## The founder story

A reporter is not asking what the company does. They are asking why this person,
why this thing, and why now. The answers live in four beats:

| Beat | What it holds | Question that gets it |
|---|---|---|
| The turn | The moment the old plan stopped working | "What was the day you decided to do this instead?" |
| The cost | What it took - money, a job, a relationship, a year | "What did you give up, and what did it cost you?" |
| The near-death | The thing that almost ended it | "What was the week you thought it was over?" |
| The proof | The number or customer that says it worked | "When did you know it was going to work?" |

Write the answers in the founder's own words, in their own register. A story
written in the PR voice reads as a brochure, and the reporter will quote the
brochure.

**What never goes in:** a chronological CV, a list of degrees, the founding date,
"passionate about", "vision", and any sentence starting with "As a founder".

## The pitch email

Under 120 words. Five lines.

```
Subject: <the news, in 8 words> - <publication-specific hook>

Hi <first name>,

<Line 1: the news, with the number. 20 words.>
<Line 2: why your readers care, referencing something they wrote. 20 words.>
<Line 3: the offer - interview, exclusive number, demo, photos. 15 words.>

<One line on who I am and how to reach me.>

<Name>, <role>, <company>, <phone>
```

Worked example:

> Subject: Pune startup cuts GST filing to 20 minutes - your piece on compliance costs
>
> Hi Nandita,
>
> Kirti Foods' 1,400 retailers now close GST returns in about 20 minutes a month, down from three hours. Your August piece on compliance costs for small traders said the tools were priced for CA firms - this one is ₹299 a month.
>
> I can give you the founder for 20 minutes on Thursday, plus the anonymised time data across 1,400 accounts.
>
> Rahul Menon, Kirti Foods, 98xxxxxxx

## Subject lines

| Works | Dies |
|---|---|
| `₹8 crore seed for Pune cold-chain startup` | `Exciting news from our team!` |
| `Data: 1,400 retailers, GST filing time -83%` | `Partnership announcement` |
| `Follow-up: your piece on D2C margins` | `Just checking in` |
| `Exclusive: first solar cold store in Vidarbha` | `Press release - for immediate release` |

## Follow-up rules

- **One follow-up**, three working days later, adding something: a new number, a
  customer who agreed to speak, a photograph, an angle for a different section.
- **Never** "just following up", "bumping this", "any update?", or a third mail.
- **A reply saying "not for us"** gets one line of thanks and a note in
  `notes/contacts.md`. That reporter is a relationship for the next story.
- **A reply asking a question** gets an answer within the hour, with facts, even
  if the answer is "I need to confirm and will revert by 3 p.m."

## The Q&A sheet

Prepare ten questions before any interview, with an approved answer and a
spokesperson per line. Always include:

1. The number you would rather not discuss (revenue, burn, churn).
2. The competitor by name.
3. The layoff, the shutdown, the failed product.
4. The regulator, the licence, the pending case.
5. The customer who left.
6. "Why should anyone trust you?"
7. The price and whether it will rise.
8. The next round, and whether you are raising.
9. What happens if a large player copies you.
10. Something human - the first customer, the worst day.

Mark three of them **do not answer off the cuff**: refer to the founder's written
statement or to `legal-drafter` and counsel.

## Anti-patterns

- A pitch that opens with the company's founding year.
- A story that is really a product description with "I" in front of it.
- Attaching a 12-page deck to a first email.
- Sending the same pitch to a reporter who covers a different beat.
- Quoting the founder's own press release back at the journalist as the interview.
- Answering a hostile question with a slogan.
