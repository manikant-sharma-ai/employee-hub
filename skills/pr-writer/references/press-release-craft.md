# Press release craft

The press note is not the deliverable - the story is. But the note is what a
journalist checks facts against, so every line must survive being copied.

## Shape

| Order | Block | Length | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Headline | 8-14 words | The news, active verb, no colon-padded cleverness |
| 2 | Subhead | 15-25 words | The number or the detail the headline could not carry |
| 3 | Dateline | City, date | `PUNE, 12 November 2026` - the format journalists expect |
| 4 | Lede | 35-50 words | Who, what, when, where, how much, why now |
| 5 | Second para | 40-60 words | The context: the market, the problem, the alternative |
| 6 | Founder quote | 25-40 words | One idea, spoken, no adjectives stacked |
| 7 | Detail paras | 2-3 × 40 words | Product, pricing, capacity, the mechanics |
| 8 | Customer/investor quote | 20-35 words | Named, titled, specific outcome |
| 9 | Availability | 20 words | Where, when, price, how to buy or apply |
| 10 | Boilerplate | 50-70 words | The company in the third person, unchanged every time |
| 11 | Media contact | 2 lines | A name, a phone that is answered, an email |

Total: 350-500 words. Longer gets skimmed; shorter reads like a notice.

## Headline patterns that work

- **Number + change**: `Pune's Kirti Foods cuts distributor settlement time from 9 days to 2`
- **First + place**: `First solar cold store in Vidarbha opens in Yavatmal`
- **Price + comparison**: `Fabrication software at ₹4,999 a month, a tenth of imported licences`
- **Scale**: `Nashik exporter crosses 1 lakh litres a month, adds 40 jobs`
- **Named counterparty**: `X partners with Bank of Baroda to offer invoice credit at 90 days`

Headlines that do not work: questions, puns, "revolutionary", "game-changing",
"is all set to", "ushers in a new era".

## Quotes

A quote carries opinion, colour or commitment - never fact that belongs in the body.

| Bad | Why | Better |
|---|---|---|
| "We are thrilled to launch this revolutionary product." | Says nothing, could be anyone | "Our customers were spending six hours a week on this. Now it is forty minutes." |
| "This will transform the industry." | Unfalsifiable | "We expect 300 units in the first year, across Maharashtra and Gujarat." |
| "Our vision is to empower SMEs." | Vision language | "A 20-person workshop cannot afford a ₹40 lakh system. At ₹4,999 it can." |

Rules: one idea per quote, 25-40 words, attributable to a named person with a
designation, approved in writing by that person. Never invent a quote, never
"polish" one into something they did not say, and never write a quote for a
customer without their sign-off on the exact words.

## Numbers

- Every number carries a unit, a period and a basis: `₹4.2 crore in FY26, audited`,
  `1,400 orders in October`, `18% quarter on quarter, unaudited`.
- Round where the precision is false: `about 1,200` beats `1,183` if the count is
  approximate.
- Indian conventions: ₹, lakh and crore, `dd Month yyyy`. Add the international
  figure in brackets only if the outlet is international.
- Never put a number in the headline that is not in the body, sourced.

## Boilerplate

Four sentences, never changed without a decision: what the company does, who it
serves, where it operates, and one scale fact. It is the paragraph journalists
copy verbatim into the "About" line, so it is the highest-leverage 60 words in the
document.

## The review pass, in this order

1. **Names and designations** - spelled as on LinkedIn and the MCA record, titles current.
2. **Numbers** - each one against the source document, not against the last draft.
3. **Dates** - day of week correct; the dateline matches the send date.
4. **Claims** - every superlative has a basis or is deleted.
5. **Permission** - customer names, investor names and quotes cleared in writing.
6. **Length** - under 500 words; the lede under 50.
7. **Read aloud** - any sentence that cannot be said in one breath is rewritten.

## Anti-patterns

- Burying the news under two paragraphs of company history.
- Three quotes from the same founder under different headings.
- A boilerplate that describes the vision rather than the business.
- Attaching the release as a PDF that nobody opens; paste it in the mail body too.
- Sending the same note to a business daily and a consumer magazine.
- "For immediate release" with no date, no city and no contact number.
