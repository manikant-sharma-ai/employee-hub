# Verbatims and the written read

The read is one page and it is read by a founder in ninety seconds. Everything in this file serves that.

## The one-page structure

| Section | Length | Content |
|---|---|---|
| The corpus | 3 lines | Channels, volumes, period, the bias of each |
| The top three | 3 lines each | Theme, customers, share, severity, one quote |
| The single sentence | 1 line | What the founder should take away |
| The table | 10-15 rows | All themes, ranked, with the disposition and owner |
| The segment read | 3-5 lines | Who is saying it |
| The limits | 3 lines | Channels missing, sample, the silent majority |
| The decision | 2 lines | The question restated, and the answer this supports |

Anything longer goes in an appendix. A read that needs a meeting to explain is not a read.

## Choosing quotes

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| Representative over dramatic | The dramatic quote wins the argument and loses the decision |
| One per segment per theme | Shows whether it is universal or concentrated |
| Verbatim, including the errors | Cleaning the grammar cleans the evidence |
| Short - one or two sentences | The full paragraph belongs in the appendix |
| From at least two channels | Proves it is not a channel artefact |
| Chosen before the ranking is written | Otherwise you pick quotes that confirm the rank |

## Redaction, in order

1. Remove names, handles, email addresses, phone numbers, order and invoice numbers.
2. Remove company names and anything that identifies an employer.
3. Remove combinations: a city plus a plan plus a product plus a date can identify one person to their own
   account manager.
4. Replace with a segment tag: `[tier-2, 8 months, ₹2,400/mo]`.
5. Re-read as the customer. If they would recognise themselves and be uncomfortable, cut it.

The test is not "is it anonymous". It is "would this person be upset to read it in a deck".

## Writing about customers without condescending

| Write | Not |
|---|---|
| "Customers cannot find the invoice download" | "Users are confused by the UI" |
| "Delivery takes 9 days against a 4-day promise" | "Logistics needs improvement" |
| "Three customers asked for GST invoices and were sent a receipt" | "Billing issues" |
| "The app logs out mid-order on Android 12" | "Technical problems" |

The left column can be fixed by someone. The right column cannot.

## Handling Indian language feedback

- Code in the language it arrived in. A Hindi complaint translated by someone in a hurry loses the exact
  complaint.
- Quote in the original with a translation beside it, or paraphrase and mark it as a paraphrase. Never present
  a translation as a verbatim.
- Hinglish is normal, not sloppy. "Order kab tak aayega" is a delivery theme with an emotional register that
  English misses.
- Regional-language volume is usually under-counted, because those customers call rather than write. Say so in
  the limits.

## The appendix that earns its place

| Appendix | Keep when |
|---|---|
| Full theme table with all codes | Always - it is the audit trail |
| 20-40 quotes per theme | When the read will be used to write a spec |
| The coding sheet | When someone will re-run the read next quarter |
| The channel volume chart | When a channel's bias is being questioned |
| The cost estimate per theme | When a budget conversation is coming |

## The three sentences that must appear

1. **The denominator.** "Of the 214 customers who wrote to us across these three channels in Q3."
2. **The limit.** "WhatsApp and sales-call notes were not available; both would likely add delivery and pricing
   themes."
3. **The decision.** "This read answers: should we rebuild onboarding before the October launch. The answer is
   yes, and the evidence is theme 2."

## Anti-patterns

- **A forty-slide deck for a five-theme read.** The deck is the read avoiding the decision.
- **Quotes with no segment.** Then the reader assumes the quote is typical of everyone.
- **Sentiment scores with no method.** If it was not computed, do not show it.
- **"Customers want" as a summary of one theme.** Say how many, out of how many.
- **A read that lists problems and no dispositions.** That is a complaint, not work.
