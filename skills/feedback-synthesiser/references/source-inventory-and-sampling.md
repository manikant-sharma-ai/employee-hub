# Source inventory and sampling

Before a single theme is written, the business needs to know what it is standing on. The inventory is
the credibility of everything that follows.

## The inventory table

| Source | What it holds | Records in period | Received | Segments present | Bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Support tickets | The problems people bothered to report | | | plan, tenure | Over-represents the broken |
| Chat and WhatsApp | Small questions, quick complaints | | | geography | Under-represents the patient |
| Survey free text | Answers to a question you chose | | | whoever replied | Over-represents the engaged |
| App store reviews | Public, emotional, dated | | | platform | Over-represents extremes |
| Google and Zomato reviews | Local, public | | | location | Over-represents bad visits |
| Cancellation reasons | The last thing said before leaving | | | churned only | Says nothing about the happy |
| Sales call notes | Objections before the sale | | | prospects only | Pre-purchase, not post |
| NPS comments | A score plus a sentence | | | whoever answers | Skews to promoters and detractors |
| Account manager notes | The relationship, in prose | | | large accounts | Enterprise-heavy |
| Refunds and returns | Money-back reasons | | | product | Silent on service |

Ten sources and no single one is the truth. A synthesis that reads two of them says which two.

## Sampling, honestly

| Situation | What to do | What to claim |
|---|---|---|
| Under 200 records | Read all of them | "All records in the period" |
| 200-1,000 | Read all, or a random 200 plus all 1-2 star reviews | "A random 200 of 740, plus all negative reviews" |
| 1,000-10,000 | Stratified sample by source and segment, minimum 300 | "A stratified sample of 320 of 4,100" |
| Over 10,000 | Sample 500, and check the sample's segment mix against the population | "A sample of 500, segment mix within 3 points of the population" |

Stratify by the segment the decision depends on - plan, tenure, or channel. A random sample of a
support desk over-represents whoever writes in most, which is usually the smallest customers.

## Period and comparability

- **State the window in dates**, and check every source actually covers it. A review export that
  starts in March and a ticket export that starts in June cannot be compared month to month.
- **Mark the events inside the window** - a launch, an outage, a price change, a festival. Feedback
  read without the calendar produces false causality, and the founder will catch it.
- **Two periods for a trend claim.** Otherwise say "snapshot".

## The biases worth naming out loud

| Bias | Effect | How to correct or disclose |
|---|---|---|
| Only the unhappy write in | Volume over-represents problems | Weight by customer count, and use cancellation reasons for the silent |
| Only the engaged answer surveys | Themes skew to power users | Segment the readout, or sample the quiet ones separately |
| Public reviews skew to extremes | 5 and 1 stars dominate | Read the 3s; they carry the specific complaint |
| Support tickets are the loud minority | A quiet majority churns without a ticket | Pair with cancellation reasons |
| The loudest customer is the biggest | One enterprise account can look like a trend | Count records, not emotion, and split by segment |
| Recency | The last week feels like the trend | Compare to the previous period before saying "growing" |

## What "we have no data" actually means

Often the honest finding is that the business has almost no feedback: 14 reviews, 30 tickets, no
survey. That is a finding with a recommendation - and it is a better output than a confident
synthesis of 44 records presented as market truth.

| Situation | The recommendation |
|---|---|
| Under 50 records total | One question in the product or the invoice email; a review request after delivery |
| No cancellation reason captured | One required field at cancellation, five options |
| Tickets untagged | A tag taxonomy of ten, applied at close |
| Reviews unclaimed | Claim the Google Business Profile and reply to the last 20 |

## Anti-patterns

- **Reading the source that is easiest to export.** The desk export is never the whole story.
- **A sample of convenience described as a sample.** "The tickets I could find" is not a method.
- **Comparing sources with different windows** and reporting a trend.
- **Ignoring the unmatched records.** They are often the newest problem, not the noise.
- **Treating a survey question as neutral.** The question you asked decides the answers you got.
