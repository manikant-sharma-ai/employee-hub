# Taxes on a quotation

The tax lines on a quote are the part that is audited, disputed and credit-noted. Get them right once,
on the page, and the invoice follows.

## The four facts that decide the tax

1. **The HSN or SAC** for the goods or service. It sets the rate and it appears on the invoice.
2. **The GST rate** for that HSN or SAC, at the date of the quote.
3. **The place of supply** - where the goods are delivered, or where the service is performed, with the
   rules for each case.
4. **The supplier's and buyer's state**, which decide whether the charge is IGST or CGST plus SGST.

| Situation | Charge | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Supplier and delivery in the same state | CGST + SGST | Split equally, both shown separately |
| Supplier and delivery in different states | IGST | The full rate as one head |
| Goods delivered to a third-party address | Place of supply is the delivery address | Bill-to and ship-to can differ |
| Service performed at the customer's site | Usually the location of the service | Confirm the specific rule for the service |
| Export of goods or services | Zero-rated, with an LUT | Without an LUT, IGST is charged and refunded later |
| Sale to a SEZ unit | Zero-rated with the prescribed documents | Documentation decides the benefit |

## Rate and classification discipline

- Never quote a rate from memory. Look up the HSN or SAC and confirm the rate for the date.
- A product that could fall in two headings is a classification decision, and it belongs to the CA in
  writing, not to a salesperson in a hurry.
- Where the rate changes between quote and invoice, the invoice follows the law at the time of supply -
  the quote says so in its terms.

## Inclusive versus exclusive pricing

State it once, unambiguously, near the total: "All prices exclusive of GST" or "Prices inclusive of
GST". A quote that says both, or says neither, produces an argument at the invoice and, if the customer
is a business, a payment hold.

## The other charges and their treatment

| Charge | Treatment | Watch |
|---|---|---|
| Freight recovered from the customer | Part of the taxable value in most cases | A separate GTA service can have its own treatment |
| Installation | Taxable at the rate of the principal supply, or as a separate service | Depends on whether it is a composite supply |
| Discount shown on the invoice | Reduces taxable value if it meets the conditions | A post-invoice discount needs a credit note |
| Advance received | Tax point can arise on receipt | Many businesses miss this |
| TDS by the buyer (194Q, 194C, 194J) | Not a tax on the quote, but it affects cash | The customer deducts it; plan the receivable |

## E-invoicing and the B2B chain

Where the business is in the e-invoicing threshold, the tax invoice needs an IRN. The quote does not,
but the quote's customer details must be complete enough to generate one - legal name, GSTIN, address,
and the place of supply. A quote with a misspelt legal name becomes an invoice that cannot be
e-invoiced.

## Anti-patterns

- **"GST extra as applicable."** It is neither a rate nor a statement.
- **One rate for every product.** The HSN decides, not the habit.
- **CGST+SGST on an inter-state delivery.** It is the most common error and the easiest to find.
- **A quote with no GSTIN for a business buyer.** The invoice cannot be issued correctly.
- **Quoting an export without an LUT.** The IGST has to be paid and refunded.
- **Assuming a discount after the invoice needs no credit note.** It does.
