# Price lists, tiers and margin

The price list is a decision someone else made. This file is how to apply it without leaking margin.

## The tiers a small business usually needs

| Tier | Who | Basis | Typical discount from list |
|---|---|---|---|
| MRP / list | Retail walk-in, small quantity | Published price | 0% |
| Standard | Regular B2B, 1-10 units | The default commercial price | 0-8% |
| Volume | Above a stated quantity | Quantity break, stepped | 8-18% |
| Dealer | Resells, holds stock, provides service | Margin protected for their resale | 18-30% |
| Distributor | Territory, investment, targets | Volume commitment and credit | 25-40% |
| Project / institutional | One-off, negotiated, tender-linked | Scope and competition | Case by case |

A tier only works if the next one up is defined in units, not in adjectives. "Bulk order" is not a
tier.

## The volume break, done properly

Breaks should reflect cost, not generosity: packaging, freight per unit, payment risk and the cost of
serving all fall per unit as volume rises. A break that gives away more than the cost curve saves is a
permanent price cut disguised as a discount.

| Quantity | Price | Margin % | Why the break is affordable |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1-9 | List | | Full service, full freight per unit |
| 10-49 | -5% | | Carton packing, lower freight per unit |
| 50-199 | -10% | | Palletised, prepaid, lower handling |
| 200+ | -15% | | Production batch, no stocking cost |

## Applying a discount without breaking the floor

```
price after discount = list x (1 - d)
margin = (price after discount - landed cost) / price after discount
```

Check three things before any concession: the margin after the discount against the floor, the effect
on the whole order (a discount on one line, not the total), and whether the customer will treat the
discounted price as the new list price on the next order. The third is the one that compounds.

## The concessions that are not price

| Instead of a discount | What it costs | When it works |
|---|---|---|
| Extended payment terms | The cost of money for the extra days | A customer with a cash cycle, not a price problem |
| Free freight | The freight cost | Small relative to the discount requested |
| Extended warranty | Provision and service risk | Equipment where service is the worry |
| Installation or training included | A known, bounded cost | The customer's real objection is complexity |
| A larger quantity at the same price | Marginal cost only | The customer can use more |
| A staged delivery | Logistics planning | The customer's constraint is storage |

## When the floor is breached

The answer is not a number. It is a one-line recommendation: the price the customer wants, the margin
it produces, the floor, and the two or three options - reduce scope, reduce quantity of the discount,
change the payment terms, or decline. The founder decides. A salesperson who decides alone is how a
price list becomes fiction.

## Anti-patterns

- **Discounting the total instead of a line.** It hides which product is now unprofitable.
- **A discount that becomes the new list price.** The next quote starts from the concession.
- **Matching a competitor you know nothing about.** You do not know their cost or their scope.
- **Margin computed on the selling price in one place and on cost in another.** State the basis.
- **Ignoring the cost of the credit given.** 60 days at 12% is about 2% of the order.
- **A dealer price with no resale margin for the dealer.** They stop selling, quietly.
