# Promise to pay

A promise is the cheapest form of payment and the easiest to lose. This file is
how to record one, check it, and respond when it breaks.

## Getting a real promise

A real promise has four parts. Without all four it is a hope.

| Part | Question to ask | Weak answer | Real answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Amount | "How much will be released?" | "We will clear it" | "₹1,24,000, the full invoice" |
| Date | "Which date will it leave your account?" | "Soon" | "Friday the 14th" |
| Approver | "Who releases it?" | "Finance" | "Mr Rao in finance, he signs on Thursday" |
| Condition | "What has to happen first?" | "Nothing" | "The GRN has to be entered, I will do that tomorrow" |

Ask the four in one conversation, in that order. Then read them back: "So ₹1,24,000
on the 14th, once the GRN is entered tomorrow, released by Mr Rao. Correct?"

## Recording it

One row per promise in the tracker.

| Account | Invoice | Amount | Promised date | Approver | Condition | Logged on | Check on | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | | | | | |

`Check on` is the promised date itself, in the morning, not three days after.

## Checking it, on the day

1. **Morning of the promised date:** check the bank or the ledger. If it landed, close the row and send one line of thanks - it is the cheapest relationship deposit there is.
2. **If it has not landed by 4 pm:** one short message. "You mentioned the 14th for INV-2291. Has it gone out today, or has something moved?"
3. **If the answer is a new date:** record it as a second promise, and count it. Two broken promises changes the class of the account, not just the date.
4. **If there is no answer in 48 hours:** move to the next ladder rung and inform the account owner.

## When a promise breaks

| Situation | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| First broken promise, with a reason | Normal in a business with a slow approval chain | Accept, log, check the new date, no lecture |
| First broken promise, no reason | A soft no | A firm step now, not in two weeks |
| Second broken promise | The promise is a way to end the call | Founder step, and the consequence named in writing |
| Third | The money is doubtful | Provision it, stop supply, decide whether it is a legal matter |
| Part payment | Genuine intent, cash constraint | Accept, record it against the invoice, keep the balance on the ladder |
| "Cheque is ready, come and collect" | Common in Indian B2B, and a real signal | Collect it, and ask what changes next month |

## Part payments

- **Allocate to the oldest invoice first**, unless the customer specifies otherwise in writing.
- **Issue a receipt the same day**, with the allocation shown. An unallocated payment becomes next month's dispute.
- **Keep the balance on the ladder at the rung it was on**, not reset to day 1.
- **Note the reason**, because a customer paying in thirds is telling you something about their cash cycle.

## The psychology that works

- **Make the ask easy to answer.** "Has it gone out today?" is answerable in one word. "Please confirm when we can expect payment" invites silence.
- **Assume the process, not the intent.** "Is something holding it up on our side?" gets a reply; "you have not paid" gets a defence.
- **Never punish the messenger.** The person you are emailing usually does not release the payment, and they can be your best ally in getting it released.
- **Give them a reason to pay that is about them**: their credit line, their pricing, their supply. Not your cash flow, which is your problem and reads as pressure.
- **One decision per message.** Two questions in one mail gets the easier one answered.

## Anti-patterns

- **Accepting "soon" as a date.** It is not a promise, and it will be followed by another "soon".
- **Not checking on the promised day.** The customer learns the date is decorative, and every later promise is worth less.
- **Restarting the ladder after a broken promise.** It rewards the break.
- **A promise taken by phone and not written down.** It did not happen.
- **Chasing the approver's junior contact.** Ask them to help you reach the approver, never around them.
- **Thanking a customer for a payment that has not landed.** It happens, and it costs the credibility of every later reminder.
