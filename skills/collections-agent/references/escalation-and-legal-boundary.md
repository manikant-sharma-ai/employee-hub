# Escalation and the legal boundary

Collections ends where legal process begins. This file is the line, and the
sentences that must never cross it.

## The escalation ladder, after the five stages

| Step | Trigger | Who decides | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| Account hold | 30+ days, stage 4 ignored | Founder or finance head | No new orders dispatched; existing orders honoured |
| Formal notice | 45+ days, no plan | Founder, drafted by `legal-drafter` | A letter stating amount, clause, deadline, consequence |
| Recovery agent | 90+ days, notice ignored | Founder | A third party, on a written mandate, with a conduct clause |
| Legal proceedings | 120+ days, or insolvency signal | Founder with the lawyer | Civil suit, arbitration, or insolvency route |
| Write-off | Recovery cost exceeds the debt | Founder and the CA | Books adjusted, lesson recorded |

Every step needs a written decision. "We stopped supplying them" that nobody
approved is a business risk, not a collections action.

## What may be said, and what may not

| Say | Do not say |
|---|---|
| "Under clause 7 of the agreement, interest applies at 1.5% per month" | "We will charge you whatever it takes" |
| "Orders will be on hold until the account is cleared" | "You will not get a single order from us again" |
| "If payment is not received by 20 November, the matter will be referred to our lawyer" | "We will take you to court and destroy your business" |
| "This has been referred to our recovery partner" | "We will send people to your office" |
| "We will report this as a statutory default where the law requires it" | "We will get you blacklisted" |

The left column is a fact about the contract. The right column is a threat, and in
India some of those threats are independently actionable.

## The five forbidden moves

1. **Criminal complaints as a collections tactic.** A cheque-bounce notice has a
   legal procedure and a deadline; a police complaint about a commercial dispute is
   abuse of process.
2. **Contacting the customer's customers, employers or family** to pressure them.
3. **Publishing a defaulter list**, or posting about the debt on social media.
4. **Calling outside reasonable hours, repeatedly, or at a personal number** that
   was not given for business.
5. **Recovering by force, or hiring anyone who does.** In India, coercive recovery
   is a criminal exposure for the person who authorised it.

## Interest and penal charges

Only where the contract says so. Check three things before a single sentence:

- **The clause exists** in the signed agreement or the accepted order terms.
- **The rate is stated** - "18% per annum" or "1.5% per month", not "as applicable".
- **The trigger is stated** - from the due date, after a grace period, or only on
  written demand.

If any of the three is missing, the interest is not chargeable, whatever the
customer's behaviour. Route the question to `legal-drafter` and the CA.

## The MSMED route

If the business is registered as a micro or small enterprise under Udyam, the
MSMED Act gives a statutory route for delayed payments, including interest. Three
qualifications, all of which need a professional:

- Whether the registration and the goods or services qualify.
- What the statutory interest rate is, and from which date.
- Whether the buyer's written objection changes the position.

This role names the route and does not compute it. The CA computes; the lawyer
files.

## The recovery agent mandate

If a recovery route is chosen, the mandate must say:

| Term | Why |
|---|---|
| The exact invoices and amounts | Prevents overreach |
| Commission, and who bears it | Usually 10-25% of recovered value |
| Conduct rules - hours, language, no third-party contact | The brand carries the agent's behaviour |
| A single point of contact at the business | Prevents two people negotiating |
| Data handling - what may be shared | DPDP obligations travel with the mandate |
| Termination and the return of documents | The relationship may be recoverable later |

## The write-off case

Present it, do not decide it:

1. The amount, the age and the last contact.
2. What was tried, stage by stage, with dates.
3. Why recovery is unlikely - insolvency, disappearance, cost exceeding the debt.
4. The relationship value, if any, of keeping the account alive.
5. The recommendation, and the tax treatment question for the CA.

## Anti-patterns

- **Threats that are not in the contract.** They are unenforceable and they are evidence.
- **Escalating to a lawyer on day 35.** The notice costs more than most invoices.
- **A hold nobody enforces.** The customer learns the ladder is decorative.
- **Two people negotiating the same debt.** One owner, one thread, one record.
- **Writing off silently.** The lesson is lost and the same customer type repeats it.
- **Using a recovery agent without a conduct clause.** Their behaviour becomes your reputation.
