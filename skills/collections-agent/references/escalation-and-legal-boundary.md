# Escalation and the legal boundary

There is a point where a chase becomes a decision, and a point where a decision
becomes a lawyer. Both should be written down before the first overdue invoice.

## The escalation ladder

| Rung | Trigger | Who acts | What is said or done |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 30 days, no promise | Collections | Firm letter, consequence named from the terms |
| 2 | 45 days, or a second broken promise | Founder or head of sales | A relationship call, and a payment schedule offered |
| 3 | 60 days, no schedule | Founder | Credit stopped, further orders on hold, in writing |
| 4 | 75-90 days, disputed facts | Founder + CA | Reconciliation meeting, ledger vs ledger |
| 5 | 90 days, no response, value above the threshold | Founder + lawyer | Legal notice, drafted by `legal-drafter` and reviewed by a lawyer |
| 6 | 180 days, or the customer is insolvent | Founder + CA | Provision, write-off decision, or recovery proceedings |

Every rung has a threshold in rupees set by the founder, in advance. A ₹4,000
invoice never reaches rung 5, whatever the principle says.

## What this role may say, and what it may not

| May say | May not say |
|---|---|
| "Invoices unpaid after 30 days attract interest at 1.5% per month, per our terms" | "We will sue you" |
| "Further orders are on hold until the account is cleared" | "We will ruin your reputation" |
| "We will escalate this to our founder" | "Our lawyers will be in touch" (unless a lawyer is actually instructed) |
| "We will have to consider our options under the agreement" | "You will be blacklisted" |
| "Please confirm the date the payment is released" | Anything about the customer's family, home or other business |

## Interest and late fees

- **Only if the terms allow it**, in writing, on the invoice or the contract.
- **State the rate and the calculation** - "1.5% per month on ₹1,24,000 from 9 November, ₹1,860 to date".
- **Decide whether to actually charge it.** A clause invoked and never applied is decoration; a clause applied to a good customer over ₹1,860 is an expensive way to be right.
- **MSMED-registered suppliers** have a statutory position on delayed payment that a CA should confirm before it is mentioned to a customer. Name it as a question, never as a threat.

## The legal boundary

This role never:

- drafts a legal notice, a demand notice or a complaint;
- signs, serves or implies one;
- advises on the strength of a case, limitation periods or jurisdiction;
- negotiates a settlement with legal effect;
- contacts a customer's customers, employers, family or neighbours.

`legal-drafter` drafts, a lawyer reviews and serves, and the founder decides. This
role's job ends with the brief: the invoices, the ledger, the correspondence, the
promises, and the dates - a chronology a lawyer can act on in an hour.

## When the customer is in genuine trouble

- **Ask what the constraint is.** Cash, a lost client, a bank limit, a family event - the answer decides everything.
- **Offer a schedule, in writing**, with dates and amounts, and a consequence for missing one. A written schedule is worth more than a promise.
- **Consider the value of the relationship over the value of the invoice.** A customer who pays 60% now and the rest over three months is usually better than a legal process and a dead account.
- **Never accept a settlement at a discount without authority**, and never one that sets a precedent for other accounts.
- **Do not advise on insolvency or on the customer's own obligations.** That is their CA's job; yours is to protect your position and say so plainly.

## Cheques that bounce

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | Inform the customer the same day, in writing, with the bank's return memo |
| 2 | Ask for a replacement or a transfer within 48 hours |
| 3 | Record it. A bounced cheque changes the account's class immediately |
| 4 | Stop credit until cleared |
| 5 | A second bounce is a founder decision, and the statutory position is a question for the lawyer, not a conclusion for this role |

## Documenting for a future dispute

Keep, per account: the invoice, the delivery proof or GRN, the acceptance or
sign-off, the terms, every reminder with its date, every promise with its date and
the name of the person who made it, and the payment history. A dispute is won on
the record, and the record is built by the ladder that was actually followed.

## Anti-patterns

- **Naming a consequence you have not been authorised to apply.** It is discovered in one phone call.
- **A legal notice as a bluff.** Once served, it is a legal act with consequences, and the relationship is over.
- **Public shaming on social media or in a WhatsApp group.** It is defamation, and it ends the account and the referral.
- **Escalating a ₹4,000 invoice to a lawyer.** The cost exceeds the claim and the precedent is worse.
- **Continuing to supply while escalating.** The customer learns the consequence is words.
- **No chronology.** A lawyer handed a folder of emails instead of a dated list charges for the sorting.
