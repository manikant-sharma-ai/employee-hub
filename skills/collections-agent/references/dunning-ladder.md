# Dunning ladder

One sequence, built per account, escalated in steps. The point of a ladder is that
the customer can always see the next rung and knows it is coming.

## The standard ladder, for a reliable-but-slow account

| Day | Channel | What it does | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| -3 | Email, polite | Heads-up before the due date, with the invoice attached | 60 words |
| 1 | Email | "Due today" with the payment link and the reference | 70 words |
| 7 | Email + WhatsApp | Direct ask, names the days overdue, offers help | 100 words |
| 15 | Phone call | Establishes the blocker, gets a date | 5 minutes |
| 22 | Email | Confirms the date agreed on the call, in writing | 80 words |
| 30 | Email, firm | Names the consequence in the terms, states the amount and interest | 120 words |
| 45 | Founder email | The relationship conversation, one step above the process | 120 words |
| 60 | Written boundary | Credit stopped, or the account handed to a lawyer by the founder | Decision |

Compress for a small amount or a first-time late payer: -3, 3, 10, 20, 30.
Extend for a government or large-enterprise buyer whose payment cycle is genuinely
45-60 days, and say so in the terms rather than in the chase.

## What every step contains

1. **The invoice number and date.**
2. **The amount, in the currency and with the tax shown as invoiced.**
3. **The due date and the days overdue.**
4. **The payment link or the bank details, whichever they use.**
5. **One ask**, phrased as an action: "please release the payment", "confirm the date", "send the GRN number if that is what is holding it".
6. **A name and a number** they can reply to.

A reminder without the amount, the reference or the link is a reminder that
generates a question instead of a payment.

## Step-by-step wording that works

**Day -3, courtesy.** "Invoice INV-2291 for ₹1,24,000 is due on 9 November. Attached
for your records, and the payment link is below in case it helps your team."

**Day 1.** "Invoice INV-2291 for ₹1,24,000 was due today. Payment link: <link>. If
it is already in process, please ignore this - and if you can share the UTR, I will
mark it off."

**Day 7.** "INV-2291 is now 7 days past due, ₹1,24,000. Is there something on our
side holding it up - a PO number, a GRN, a mismatch in the invoice? I can fix that
today. If not, the link is here."

**Day 15, the call.** Open with the invoice, not the relationship. "I am calling
about INV-2291, ₹1,24,000, due on 9 November. Can you tell me where it is in your
process?" Then: who approves, what they need, and the date it will be released.
Write the answer down while they are speaking and read it back.

**Day 30, firm.** "INV-2291 is 30 days past due. Under our terms, invoices unpaid
after 30 days attract interest at 1.5% per month, and further orders are on hold
until the account is clear. I would rather not apply either. If there is a genuine
constraint, tell me and we will look at a schedule."

**Day 45, founder.** Two paragraphs. What is owed, what has been tried, and one
question: "Is there something about the work or the invoice that we have not
addressed?" This is the step that resolves most stuck accounts, because it moves
the conversation above the process.

## Channel rules

| Channel | Use it for | Never |
|---|---|---|
| Email | Everything, and the record | Long threads with three people copied |
| WhatsApp | A nudge to a known contact, after consent | A first contact, a group, or a document dump |
| Phone | Day 15 and anything stuck | Before 9 am, after 7 pm, more than twice a day |
| In person | Accounts worth the visit | Turning up unannounced at a home address |
| Letter | The formal step, with the founder's signature | Anything that reads as a legal notice unless it is one |

## Cadence rules

- **One touch a week, maximum**, until day 30. After that, one every ten days.
- **Never two channels on the same day** except day 7, where a WhatsApp after an unanswered email is reasonable.
- **Pause the ladder** on a reply, a promise, a dispute, or a part payment. Restart from the next rung, not the beginning.
- **Stop the ladder entirely** once the founder has taken it over or a lawyer is involved.

## Anti-patterns

- **The same template to every customer every week.** It reads as a system and invites a system's response: silence.
- **Starting at day 30.** The first 15 days are when a polite nudge still works and the relationship still has room.
- **Copying the customer's CFO on the first reminder.** It converts a process question into a complaint.
- **Interest invoked and never applied.** The customer learns the terms are decorative.
- **Chasing an invoice the customer says is disputed.** It hardens the dispute and delays the money.
- **No link, no reference, no amount.** The customer has to look it up, so they do it later, and later is next month.
