# Dunning sequence

The ladder is five messages over 45 days. Written once, sent consistently, it
collects more than any amount of improvisation - and it never depends on someone
being in a bad mood.

## The five stages

| Stage | When | Channel | Tone | Ask |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Due date + 1 | Email | Assume an oversight | "Confirm the payment date" |
| 2 | Due date + 7 | Email + WhatsApp | Polite, specific | "Pay by Friday, or tell me the date" |
| 3 | Due date + 15 | Email + phone call | Direct, still warm | "This is now 15 days late; what is blocking it?" |
| 4 | Due date + 30 | Email from the account owner or founder | Formal | "Payment by the 10th, or supply is on hold" |
| 5 | Due date + 45 | Letter or email, cc the CA | Final | "Settlement by the 20th, or the account goes to recovery" |

Nothing in stage 5 that was not implied in stage 4. Escalation surprises are how
relationships end badly.

## Stage 1 - the assumption of oversight

> Subject: Invoice INV-2041 - ₹1,84,000, due 12 November
>
> Hello <name>,
>
> Invoice INV-2041 for ₹1,84,000 was due on 12 November. I am writing in case it
> slipped through - it happens.
>
> You can pay here: <link>. If it has already been processed, ignore this and
> thank you.
>
> <sender>, <company>, <phone>

Three facts, one link, one exit. Under 80 words.

## Stage 2 - specific and dated

> Subject: Invoice INV-2041 - ₹1,84,000, now 8 days late
>
> Hello <name>,
>
> INV-2041 is now 8 days past due. Could you confirm a payment date? If the
> invoice is disputed, tell me what is wrong and I will get it checked today.
>
> Pay here: <link>. If Friday is realistic, reply with Friday and I will stop
> writing until then.

The offer to name a date is what converts this stage: most customers will commit
to something to end the sequence, and a commitment is collectable.

## Stage 3 - the call

Phone first, email after. The email records what was said:

> Subject: INV-2041 - as discussed
>
> Hello <name>,
>
> Thanks for the call. As discussed, ₹1,84,000 is due and you will confirm a date
> by Thursday. I have noted the delay is on your customer's side.
>
> If anything changes, tell me before Thursday rather than after.

Never paraphrase a promise into something better than it was.

## Stage 4 - formal, from a senior person

> Subject: INV-2041 - ₹1,84,000, 31 days past due
>
> Hello <name>,
>
> INV-2041 remains unpaid 31 days after the due date. Under our terms, accounts
> more than 30 days overdue go on hold, which means orders from 25 November will
> not be dispatched.
>
> Please pay by 20 November, or send a dated plan in writing, and I will hold the
> account open.
>
> <founder name>, <company>

The hold must be real and in the terms. A threat you do not carry out teaches the
customer that the ladder is theatre.

## Stage 5 - final

Stage 5 is drafted with `legal-drafter`, not by this role. It states the amount,
the days, the contract clause, the deadline and the consequence, and it copies the
CA. Nothing in it is a threat that is not in the contract.

## Channel rules for India

| Channel | Use | Care |
|---|---|---|
| Email | Every stage, the record | The accounts-payable contact, not the sales contact |
| WhatsApp | Stages 2-4, smaller accounts | Business account, professional tone, no stickers, no voice notes |
| Phone | Stage 3 onwards | Call the number given for business, not a personal one scraped from somewhere |
| Letter | Stage 5 | Registered post if the amount justifies it |
| In person | Large accounts, 60+ days | The account owner, not the collections person |

## Writing rules

- **The invoice number, the amount and the days, in the subject line.** It is searchable and it removes ambiguity.
- **One ask per message.** Two asks produce no answer.
- **No sarcasm, no "as per our previous mail", no escalation in capitals.**
- **No apology for asking.** The money is owed.
- **The sender is a person with a phone number**, not "the accounts team".
- **Reply-to is monitored.** A dunning address nobody reads collects nothing.

## Anti-patterns

- **A single "gentle reminder" every month.** It is a subscription, not a ladder.
- **Escalating tone while the facts stay vague.** "Kindly do the needful at the earliest" tells the customer nothing.
- **Copying the customer's CEO at stage 2.** You have used your escalation before you needed it.
- **Restarting the ladder after a promise that slipped.** The next stage is one step up, not back to stage 1.
- **Threatening a hold the operations team will not enforce.**
- **Sending from a no-reply address.** The reply is the point.
