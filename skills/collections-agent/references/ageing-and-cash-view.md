# Ageing and the cash view

The ageing report is the map. This file turns it into a queue and a forecast the
founder can act on in a week.

## The buckets, and what each one means

| Bucket | What it usually means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Not yet due | Normal | Send the day -3 courtesy, and nothing else |
| 1-30 | A process question | The polite ladder, one touch a week |
| 31-60 | A real delay, or an approval stuck | The call, then the firm step |
| 61-90 | Cash trouble, or a dispute nobody raised | Founder step, and a payment schedule |
| 91-180 | Doubtful | Provision part of it, stop credit, decide on legal |
| 180+ | Bad debt until proven otherwise | Written off or handed to a lawyer; stop spending time |

Days overdue is measured from the due date, not the invoice date. A 60-day credit
customer at day 45 is not late, and treating them as late costs the relationship.

## Classifying accounts, not just invoices

| Class | Signals | The words that work |
|---|---|---|
| Reliable, slow | Pays at 55-70 days every time, never misses | "Your usual cycle is around 60 days - this one is at 68, is everything alright?" |
| First-time late | New customer, or a first late payment | Assume a process gap; a helpful tone, a quick call |
| Disputed | A written or verbal objection to the invoice | Answer the dispute; pause the chase |
| In trouble | Part payments, broken promises, staff turnover, silence | Schedule, founder involvement, credit stop, protect the relationship |
| Gone | No contact, bounced cheques, closed office | Provision, legal decision, stop the cost of chasing |
| Strategic | A large or reference customer, slow by policy | Founder decides the trade; the ladder waits |

Two accounts in the same bucket need different letters because they are in
different classes. That is the whole job.

## The weekly cash view, one page

| Line | Amount | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Opening bank balance | | From the bank, not from memory |
| Received this week | | Confirmed in the ledger |
| Promised for next week | | With the promise date and the approver |
| Due next week, not yet promised | | The ladder's work this week |
| Overdue over 60 days | | The founder's list |
| Doubtful, should be provided for | | A percentage, with the reason |
| Expected closing | | Opening + received + high-confidence promises |

The expected closing figure is the one the founder will act on. Show the range:
"₹9.2 lakh if every promise lands, ₹6.4 lakh if only the confirmed ones do."

## DSO, computed honestly

Days sales outstanding = (receivables / revenue) x days in the period.

| Use | Formula | Watch |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly DSO | (closing receivables / month revenue) x 30 | Seasonality distorts a single month |
| Rolling 3-month | (receivables / 3-month revenue) x 90 | The one to report |
| Best possible DSO | (current receivables / revenue) x days | The floor if everyone paid on time |
| DSO against terms | DSO minus the weighted average credit period | The number that actually measures the chase |

A DSO of 55 days on 30-day terms is a 25-day problem. A DSO of 55 days on 60-day
terms is a business that is collecting well.

## Concentration, the risk nobody looks at

- **Top three customers as a share of receivables.** Above 50%, one late payment is a cash crisis.
- **One customer as a share of overdue.** Above 30%, the chase is really one conversation.
- **Ageing by customer, not just by invoice.** A customer with four invoices across three buckets is a pattern, not four events.

## The stop-credit decision

When an account crosses 60 days with no promise:

| Value of the account | What to do |
|---|---|
| Small, one-off | Stop supply, keep chasing by mail, write off at 180 |
| Repeat buyer, small | Stop new orders until cleared, keep the relationship warm |
| Large or strategic | Founder decides: a payment schedule, a credit limit, or a hard stop |
| Government or PSU | Expect 90-120 days; plan cash around it, do not chase hard |

Credit stopping is a commercial decision with a cash consequence in both
directions. The role prepares the case; a human decides.

## Anti-patterns

- **Reporting total receivables with no buckets.** A single number hides the ₹8 lakh that will never arrive.
- **Counting a promise as cash.** Promises go in the second line, not the first.
- **Chasing by bucket instead of by class.** The reliable customer at 65 days gets the letter meant for the one in trouble, and takes offence.
- **Ignoring concentration.** The average looks fine until the one big customer is late.
- **Provisioning nothing because "they always pay eventually".** The one that does not is the one that hurts.
- **Measuring DSO against a credit period nobody wrote down.** Put the terms in the invoice and in the ageing sheet.
