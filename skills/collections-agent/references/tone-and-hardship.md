# Tone and hardship

The money is owed and the person is not the enemy. This file is the register, the
phrases, and what to do when the customer genuinely cannot pay.

## The register, by rung

| Rung | Register | Example opening |
|---|---|---|
| Courtesy | Helpful, brief | "Ahead of Friday, here is the invoice and the link." |
| First ask | Direct, assumes a process gap | "INV-2291 is 7 days past due. Is something on our side holding it up?" |
| Second ask | Specific, names the days | "INV-2291 is now 21 days past due, ₹1,24,000." |
| Firm | Factual, names the consequence | "Under our terms, invoices unpaid after 30 days attract interest, and new orders are on hold." |
| Founder | Personal, one question | "Is there something about the work or the invoice we have not addressed?" |
| Boundary | Final, written, no adjectives | "This account is on credit hold until the outstanding amount is cleared." |

The register moves up one step at a time. Jumping from courtesy to boundary in
ten days reads as panic, and panic invites a negotiation.

## Words that cost you money

| Do not write | Why | Write instead |
|---|---|---|
| "As per our last mail" | Passive-aggressive, and it is a record of your frustration | "The invoice was due on 9 November" |
| "Kindly do the needful" | Meaningless, and it transfers the thinking to them | "Please release the payment by Friday" |
| "You have ignored three reminders" | An accusation, and arguable | "We have not heard back on the last two mails" |
| "Unfortunately" | Signals a concession is coming | Delete it |
| "We understand your situation" (when you do not) | Hollow, and the customer can tell | "Tell me what is holding it up and I will work with it" |
| "Final reminder" (repeated) | Trains the customer to ignore the word final | Use it once, at the boundary |
| "Dear Sir/Madam" | Anonymous, in a relationship job | Use the name of the person you have spoken to |

## The subject line

The subject decides whether the mail is opened and whether it is found later.

- Include the invoice number: "INV-2291 - payment due 9 Nov" is searchable.
- Include the amount only in a private mail, never in a group thread subject.
- Never "URGENT" in capitals, and never a red exclamation.
- For a first ask: "Invoice INV-2291 - is anything holding it up?"

## Writing to the right person

| Person | What they need | What not to do |
|---|---|---|
| The person who raises the PO | A copy of the invoice, a reminder of the reference | Ask them to approve payment - it is not their job |
| The approver | The amount, the due date, the approval reference | Send a long thread they will not read |
| The releaser or accounts team | The vendor code, the bank details, the invoice copy | Copy their boss on the first contact |
| The founder or MD | One paragraph: what is owed, how long, what has been tried | Attach nine documents |

Ask your contact: "Who releases the payment, and what do they need from us?" Most
slow payments are a missing vendor code or a GRN that nobody entered.

## When the customer cannot pay

The distinction that matters: **cannot** and **will not**.

| Signal | Cannot | Will not |
|---|---|---|
| Communication | Answers, explains, offers part payment | Silence, or a new excuse each time |
| History | Paid well until recently | A pattern of late payment from the start |
| Staff | Mentions a lost client, a bank issue, a closure | Mentions nothing, or blames your invoice |
| Behaviour | Sends something, however small | Sends nothing and asks for more supply |

For **cannot**: a written schedule, part payments accepted, supply scaled rather
than stopped, and the founder informed. This is where a business earns a customer
for a decade.

For **will not**: the ladder moves faster, credit stops, and the decision goes to
the founder with the chronology. Do not spend months being reasonable at a
customer who has decided not to pay.

## Hardship, handled with dignity

- Never ask for personal financial details. Ask what the business can commit to.
- Never discuss the customer's difficulty with anyone else, including your own team beyond the account owner.
- Never use their difficulty as leverage for a bigger concession than needed.
- Offer the schedule in writing, with dates, and keep it exactly.
- If the business fails, keep the record and let the lawyer or the CA decide the recovery route. Do not chase a director personally.

## The call script, day 15

1. "This is <name> from <company>, about INV-2291 for ₹1,24,000, due on 9 November."
2. "Can you tell me where it is in your process?"
3. Listen. Write. Do not fill the silence.
4. "What do you need from us to release it?"
5. "So it will be released on <date> by <name>. I will note that and check on the day."
6. "Thank you - and if anything changes, tell me before the date, not after."

Under four minutes. The purpose is a date and a name, not a conversation.

## Anti-patterns

- **Sarcasm, however mild.** It is remembered for years and quoted to other buyers.
- **A wall of text with the invoice buried.** The customer replies "which invoice?" and you have lost a week.
- **Apologising for asking.** "Sorry to bother you again" tells them the ask is optional.
- **Threatening in the same mail as a friendly line.** The mixed message is read as manipulation.
- **Copying everyone.** It wins the mail and loses the relationship, and the next invoice is slower.
- **Treating a hardship case and a refusal case with the same letter.** One loses a customer, the other loses the money.
