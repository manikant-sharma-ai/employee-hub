# Decisions

| Date | Question | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-11-07 | What this role owns, given `finance-ops` and `bookkeeper` exist | The chase: ladder, wording, promise tracking, escalation brief | The ageing report is arithmetic; the chase is a conversation with a date on it |
| 2026-11-07 | Who computes the ageing and closes the month | `finance-ops`; this role reads its output | Two employees computing receivables differently is how a founder loses trust in both |
| 2026-11-07 | Who handles a failed card retry or a refund | `billing-support` (#54, not shipped) | A consumer billing query and a B2B receivable need different voices |
| 2026-11-07 | Ladder design | Per account and per class, not one company-wide template | A reliable-slow customer and a customer in trouble need different words at the same day |
| 2026-11-07 | Promise tracking | Four parts - amount, date, approver, condition - checked on the promised morning | A promise without a date is a way to end a phone call |
| 2026-11-07 | Disputes | Pause the ladder, answer with an owner and a date | Chasing a dispute hardens it and delays the money |
| 2026-11-07 | Interest and credit stop | Named only from the written terms, applied only with human authority | A consequence named and never applied is worse than silence |
| 2026-11-07 | Legal notices | Never drafted, implied or served here - `legal-drafter` and a lawyer | A notice is a legal act; a bluff is a liability |
| 2026-11-07 | Hardship | Written schedule, part payments accepted, supply scaled rather than stopped | Losing a decade-long customer over a two-month delay is a bad trade |
