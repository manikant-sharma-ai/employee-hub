# The brief and the handover

Most bad work from a capable employee is a bad brief. This file is the five
things that have to be in the handover, and the four ways people skip them.

## The five things

1. **The job** - what exists at the end that does not exist now. "A 45-second reel" is a job. "Some video content" is not.
2. **The definition of done** - how the person will know it is finished. A count, a format, a size, an approval. Without it the employee guesses and guesses expensive.
3. **The access** - which connector, whose account, read or write. Naming it in the handover is what lets the preflight say "you do not have this yet" before the work starts.
4. **The budget** - money, time, or attention. "Two rounds of feedback" is a budget. Unlimited revision is not a budget, it is a trap.
5. **The consequence** - what happens if it is wrong. A wrong internal note costs an hour; a wrong label costs a print run; a wrong GST entry costs a notice. The employee calibrates its caution to this line.

## The four ways a handover is skipped

- **"You know what I mean."** The employee does not, and will produce the most average version of the most likely reading. Ask for one example of good output instead.
- **The brief that is a task list.** Ten bullets of activities, no definition of done. Convert to one outcome and a deadline.
- **The brief with two owners.** "Design and write it" - pick the first owner, and make the second's input an approval gate.
- **The brief with no reader.** Ask who consumes it. A post for customers, a deck for a distributor and a memo for the board are three different documents with the same words.

## Handover sizes

| Size | Handover |
|---|---|
| Under an hour | The five things in chat, in one message |
| A day to a week | `assets/handover-template.md`, filled, plus one example of good output |
| Longer, or money at risk | The template plus a written definition of done the person signs off, plus the review cadence |

## What comes back

A good employee returns three things: the deliverable, the list of what it could
not do and why, and the one decision it made on your behalf that you might want
to reverse. If a handover comes back without the second and third, the brief did
not ask for them - fix the brief.

## Scheduling the handover

A repeating job needs one extra line: **what the employee does when the input is
missing**. Skipping the run, sending an empty report, or asking you are three
different behaviours. Choose one in the handover, not in production.
