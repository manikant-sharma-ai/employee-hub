# Merchandising calendar and launches

A storefront with no calendar is a storefront that reacts. The calendar is what makes the home page
a decision instead of a habit.

## The year, mapped for an Indian D2C business

| Window | Why it matters | Lead time |
|---|---|---|
| Jan | Resolution and new-year ranges | 3 weeks |
| Feb-Mar | Wedding and exam season, depending on category | 4-6 weeks |
| Apr-May | Summer, and the pre-monsoon lull | 3 weeks |
| Jun-Jul | Monsoon; low-ticket, high-utility | 3 weeks |
| Aug | Raksha Bandhan, Independence Day sales | 4 weeks |
| Sep-Oct | Festive: Navratri, Durga Puja, Dussehra, Diwali | 8-10 weeks |
| Nov | Post-Diwali lull, then Black Friday and marketplace sales | 4 weeks |
| Dec | Gifting, year-end, and clearance before the new range | 3 weeks |

Build the calendar backward from the shipping cut-off, not forward from the idea. A Diwali
campaign that ships after the courier's cut-off sells nothing.

## The campaign brief, one page per campaign

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Window | Start, end, and the shipping cut-off |
| Objective | One metric, with the baseline |
| Audience | New, returning, or a segment |
| Offer | The mechanic, from the offer sheet |
| Hero products | Three to five, in stock, with the stock figure and date read |
| Home page | The exact blocks in order, top to bottom |
| Supporting | Email, WhatsApp, ads, marketplace match |
| Margin guardrail | Floor and kill condition |
| Owner | Who publishes each block, and by when |

Use `assets/collection-brief.md` for the build detail.

## The home page, in the order that sells

| Slot | Content | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hero banner | One product story, one CTA, one offer. Changes with the calendar |
| 2 | Bestsellers | Top 8-12 by revenue, in stock, sorted by what sells |
| 3 | New in | Last 30-45 days |
| 4 | Collection tiles | Three or four reasons to shop, each with a face |
| 5 | Offer block | The live offer, with the end date |
| 6 | Social proof | Reviews, press, UGC - real ones |
| 7 | Story or founder block | Why this brand exists, in 80 words |
| 8 | Email or WhatsApp capture | With a reason to subscribe |

A home page that has not changed in six weeks is telling returning visitors the brand is asleep.

## Launch sequence, T-21 to T+7

| When | Do |
|---|---|
| T-21 | Range finalised, stock confirmed, photography briefed |
| T-14 | Product pages built and hidden; copy and specs approved |
| T-10 | Collections and navigation created; internal link check |
| T-7 | Home page blocks drafted; email and WhatsApp copy written |
| T-5 | Test order end to end: payment, invoice, shipping label, delivery promise |
| T-3 | Pages published but not linked; a soft launch to a small list |
| T-1 | Hero live, email scheduled, ads approved, stock re-checked |
| T-0 | Launch; watch the first 50 orders for a defect |
| T+1 | Fix the top three issues from orders and support |
| T+7 | Read-out: conversion, AOV, stock cover, what sold out and what did not |

The T-5 test order is not optional. It is the only way to find a broken payment mode, a wrong GST
setting or a shipping rule that charges ₹400 for a ₹900 product.

## Restock and clearance, on the calendar too

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| Stock cover under 3 weeks on a hero | Restock flag, and reduce promotion |
| Stock cover over 16 weeks | Move to a bundle or a clearance collection |
| End of season | Clearance collection, and stop buying |
| Return rate above 15% on a product | Fix the page before promoting it again |
| Dead stock older than 120 days | Bundle, then clearance, then a channel outside the store |

Clearance belongs in its own collection with its own rules. Putting a clearance badge on the hero
page is how a brand teaches its customers to wait.

## Sale discipline

| Rule | Reason |
|---|---|
| A sale has a start and an end, both published | Open-ended sales are price cuts |
| Sale stock is real stock | A "sale" on three units is a broken promise |
| Match marketplace sale dates only if margin allows | Matching a 60% marketplace discount is usually a loss |
| Exclude new arrivals from the discount | Protect the price of the newest thing |
| One hero offer per window | Two offers split attention and both underperform |
| Post-sale, restore prices on a stated date | The date is part of the promise |

## Anti-patterns

| Anti-pattern | Cost |
|---|---|
| Calendar built forward from the idea | The campaign ships after the courier cut-off |
| Home page unchanged for a quarter | Returning visitors see a static store |
| Launch without a test order | Payment or shipping breaks in front of the launch traffic |
| Festive campaign with no stock check | Sold out in a day, traffic wasted, reviews lost |
| Clearance on the hero page | The list price stops being believed |
| Sale with no end date | It is a price cut, and it is permanent |
| Every campaign at once | Nothing is legible, and no result is attributable |
