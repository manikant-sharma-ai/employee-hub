# On-site search and measurement

Two disciplines that share one file because they share one question: what is the visitor actually
trying to do, and where does the store stop them.

## The diagnosis page, before any change

| Metric | Where | Healthy range for a small D2C store |
|---|---|---|
| Sessions | Analytics | Trend matters more than the number |
| Conversion rate | Analytics | 1.0-2.5%; below 0.7% is a page or an offer problem |
| Add-to-cart rate | Analytics or the funnel | 5-10% of sessions |
| Cart-to-order | Funnel | 35-60%; below 25% is checkout, shipping or COD |
| AOV | Orders | Rising with offers, or the offers are not working |
| Returning customer rate | Analytics | 20-35% is healthy at 12 months |
| Discount cost | Orders | Under 8% of gross revenue, unless a stated campaign |
| Bounce on collection pages | Analytics | Under 45% |
| Mobile share and mobile conversion | Analytics | Mobile conversion is usually half of desktop; if it is a quarter, the page is the problem |
| Search usage and null rate | Platform | 10-25% of sessions search; a null rate above 10% is lost demand |

Name the problem in one sentence: traffic, relevance, page, offer, or checkout. Then fix only that.

## The four problems, and what each looks like

| Problem | Symptoms | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Not enough traffic | Low sessions, decent conversion | `seo-specialist`, `performance-ads-buyer` |
| Wrong traffic | High sessions, low ATC, high bounce | Offer, audience, ad copy |
| Page does not close | Good ATC, poor cart-to-order, or low ATC on the hero | This role - page and proof |
| Offer gives away margin | Conversion fine, contribution falling | This role - offers and floor |
| Checkout friction | High ATC, low order, abandoned carts | Payments, shipping, COD - partly this role |

## On-site search

| What to look at | What it tells you | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Top 20 search terms | The customer's own vocabulary | Use those words in titles and collections |
| Null results | Demand with no supply, or a synonym gap | Add a synonym, or a collection |
| Searches with low click-through | Results ranked badly | Boost bestselling, in-stock items |
| Searches followed by exit | The results did not answer | A page, a size, or a stock problem |
| Brand-name searches | A returning or referral visitor | Serve them the hero collection first |

Fix in this order: **synonyms** (cheap, immediate), **ranking** (bestsellers and in-stock first),
**zero-result landing** (route to the closest collection instead of a blank page), then
**merchandising the top ten terms** with a curated result set.

## Testing without a statistics team

| Method | When | How to read it |
|---|---|---|
| **Before and after** | A structural change nobody can split-test | Compare 14 days before with 14 days after, same weekday mix |
| **A/B on the theme** | Two page variants, enough traffic | Needs roughly 100+ orders per variant to be worth reading |
| **Hold-out collection** | A new collection vs the old path | Compare conversion and AOV on the same traffic source |
| **One-variable change** | Price, offer, or delivery promise | Change one thing; otherwise nothing is attributable |

Rules: pick the metric before you start, write the baseline, decide the kill condition, and check
at 7 and 28 days. A "lift" measured on a Diwali week against a normal week is seasonality, not a
result.

| Sample size reality check | Orders per variant | What you can conclude |
|---|---|---|
| Under 50 | Nothing |
| 50-100 | Direction only; do not change the price on it |
| 100-300 | A 20%+ relative change is believable |
| 300+ | A 10% change is believable |

## The read-out, one page

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| Window and baseline | Dates, and the same period last year or the prior 28 days |
| What changed | The one or two things, precisely |
| Result | Conversion, AOV, revenue, contribution, discount cost |
| Attribution honesty | What else moved in the window - a sale, an ad push, a festive week |
| Decision | Keep, revert, or test further, with the date |
| Learning | One line, into `notes/learnings.md` |

## Attribution honesty

Small stores over-credit their own changes. Before claiming a win, check whether any of these also
happened: an ad budget change, a marketplace sale, a festive week, a price change, an out-of-stock
on a competitor, a new review wave, a courier delay. If one did, say so in the read-out. A
merchandiser whose results survive scrutiny is one who is asked again.

## Anti-patterns

| Anti-pattern | Cost |
|---|---|
| Changing five things at once | No result is attributable, and the budget is spent |
| Reading a 3-day result | Noise, and a decision made on it |
| Comparing a festive week to a normal one | Seasonality read as a lift |
| No baseline written before the change | Every result is a story |
| Ignoring mobile conversion | The majority of traffic, unexamined |
| Never looking at search terms | The cheapest source of product and copy insight |
| Claiming a win with no contribution figure | Revenue up, profit down, and nobody noticed |
