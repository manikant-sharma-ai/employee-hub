# Disclosure and compliance in India

The brand carries the exposure, not only the creator. A missing disclosure on a
paid post is the brand's problem in the regulator's eyes and in the comment
section's.

## What must be disclosed

| Situation | Disclose | Not enough |
|---|---|---|
| Paid post | #ad or #sponsored, in the first two lines | #collab, #partner, @brand mention |
| Free product, no fee | Yes - barter is a material connection | A "thank you" tag |
| Discount or affiliate code | Yes | "Use my code" alone |
| Employee posting about the employer | Yes, as an employee | — |
| Trip, hotel, event hosted by the brand | Yes | A location tag |

Rules of thumb that satisfy the ASCI influencer guidelines: the disclosure is
**upfront, unambiguous, in the same language as the post, and not hidden among
hashtags**. "#ad" in the first two lines of the caption, and the platform's own
paid-partnership label where it exists.

## Claims that are regulated, not just risky

| Category | What cannot be said | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Health and wellness | Cures, treats, prevents, doctor-recommended without a name | Drugs and Magic Remedies Act, and ASCI codes |
| Weight loss | "Lose 10 kg in a month" | Unsubstantiated and unsafe |
| Fairness and skin | "Fair", "whitening" claims | Advertising rules restrict them |
| Finance and investing | Returns, guaranteed profits | SEBI rules on investment advice |
| Education | Guaranteed rank, guaranteed placement | Consumer protection exposure |
| Food | Health claims without FSSAI-permitted wording | FSSAI labelling and claims rules |
| Alcohol and tobacco | Surrogate advertising of any kind | Statutory ban |
| Sustainability | "Eco-friendly", "100% natural" without basis | Greenwashing exposure |

The brief's **do-not-say** list exists for this, and it is written by the brand
with the product's actual substantiation in hand. If nobody can produce the
evidence for a claim, the claim does not go in the brief.

## Paperwork

| Item | Who handles | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Written brief and acceptance | You | The brief is the contract for small deals |
| Letter agreement or contract | `legal-drafter` for deals above a few lakh, or with exclusivity | Rights and liability need drafting |
| GST invoice from the creator | Creator, checked by `finance-ops` | Unregistered creators cannot charge GST |
| TDS deduction | `finance-ops` or the CA | Rates and thresholds change; do not quote them from memory |
| Barter valuation | `finance-ops` | Free product is a supply, not a favour |
| Data handling | You | No audience lists or personal data from the creator; DPDP Act applies to what you collect |

## The approval checklist

- [ ] Disclosure in the first two lines, in the post's language
- [ ] No claim outside the substantiated list
- [ ] Price, offer, code and link correct and tested
- [ ] No competitor product in frame
- [ ] Music licensed for commercial use, or platform-cleared
- [ ] Rights recorded: organic, paid, duration
- [ ] Screenshot and 48-hour numbers requested in the brief

## Anti-patterns

- **"Everyone does it without #ad".** The ones who do it are the ones in the enforcement notices.
- **Letting the creator write the claim.** They will make it bigger to make the post land.
- **Disclosure in the last line among 30 hashtags.** It does not count and reads as evasion.
- **A do-not-say list nobody reads.** Put it in the brief's body, not an annexure.
- **Quoting a TDS rate from memory.** Thresholds and sections change; the CA decides.
