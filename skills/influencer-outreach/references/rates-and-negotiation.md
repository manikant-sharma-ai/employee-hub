# Rates and negotiation

Negotiate the deal, not the number. The fee is one of six things on the table and
usually not the one that decides whether the campaign worked.

## The six levers

| Lever | Typical range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Fee | The headline number | Anchor to cost per expected action, never to follower count |
| Deliverables | 1 reel vs 1 reel + 3 stories + 1 static | Extra assets are often cheaper than a higher fee |
| Usage rights | +25-100% of fee | Paid amplification, website, 6 or 12 months |
| Exclusivity | +20-50% | Category exclusivity for 30-90 days is usually enough |
| Timeline | Discount for flexibility | A post inside a festival week costs more |
| Payment terms | 50/50 or net 15 | Full upfront is a red flag; net 45 kills goodwill |

## Working out the ceiling

```
Max cost per order      = product price x contribution margin x share you can spend on acquisition
Expected orders         = expected views x landing conversion x creator-to-brand conversion
Max fee                 = expected orders x max cost per order
```

Worked example: a ₹1,200 product with 55% margin and 30% of margin available for
acquisition gives ₹198 per order. A creator likely to deliver 60,000 views, at a
1.5% landing conversion gives about 900 visits and, at 3%, 27 orders. Ceiling:
about ₹5,300. An ask of ₹25,000 is not expensive or cheap in the abstract; it is
five times the ceiling, and that is the sentence to say.

## India benchmarks, as a starting point only

| Tier | Per reel (₹) | Per story set (₹) | CPM equivalent (₹) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nano | 1,000-5,000 | 500-2,000 | 200-600 |
| Micro | 5,000-25,000 | 2,000-8,000 | 150-450 |
| Mid | 25,000-75,000 | 8,000-25,000 | 120-350 |
| Macro | 75,000-3,00,000 | 25,000-60,000 | 100-300 |

Benchmarks are a sanity check, never the argument. The argument is the ceiling
calculation above.

## Negotiation moves that work

- **Ask for the media kit and the last three paid posts' numbers.** The rate often drops when the numbers are on the table.
- **Offer a bundle instead of a discount.** "₹30,000 for one reel plus three stories" protects their rate card and gets you more assets.
- **Trade rights for fee.** A lower fee with paid amplification rights is usually the better deal for a launch.
- **Ask what their best-performing brand deal was** and buy that format, not a new one.
- **Use a performance kicker.** Base fee plus a bonus above a redemption threshold aligns both sides and is standard now.
- **Walk away once, politely, with the reason.** Creators respect a number with a calculation behind it.

## What goes in writing, before any content

Fee and GST, deliverables and dates, revision rounds (one), rights and duration,
exclusivity window, disclosure requirement, payment trigger (live link plus
screenshot), and the underperformance clause if any. One page, sent as the brief.

## Tax and paperwork in India

- **GST**: creator services attract GST; a registered creator invoices with GST, an unregistered one cannot. Confirm the rate and the registration position with a CA.
- **TDS**: payments to creators may attract TDS, and free product may attract TDS on perquisites above the threshold. `finance-ops` or the CA decides; the tracker records what was deducted.
- **Barter**: still a taxable supply in most readings, and still requires disclosure. Treat it as a payment, not as a favour.
- **Contract**: a one-page letter agreement is enough for a ₹15,000 deal and not enough for a ₹3,00,000 one with rights. Route the big ones to `legal-drafter`.

## Anti-patterns

- **Negotiating only the fee.** You will pay the same total and own less.
- **Paying 100% upfront.** No draft approval, no recourse.
- **Accepting "we will share the numbers after posting"** as a rate justification.
- **Buying perpetual, all-platform rights by accident.** The words "in perpetuity" in a template cost more than the fee.
- **Skipping the written brief to save a day.** The revision loop costs a week.
