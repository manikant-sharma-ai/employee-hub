# Creator shortlisting

A shortlist is an argument, not a list. Every name carries the reason it is there
and the number that justifies it.

## Tiers, and what each is actually for

| Tier | Followers | Typical India rate per reel | Best used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nano | 1k-10k | ₹1,000-5,000 or barter | Local, high-trust, cheap content volume |
| Micro | 10k-50k | ₹5,000-25,000 | The workhorse tier: best cost per sale in most categories |
| Mid | 50k-200k | ₹25,000-75,000 | Reach with credibility intact |
| Macro | 200k-1M | ₹75,000-3,00,000 | Launch spikes, category credibility |
| Celebrity | 1M+ | ₹3,00,000+ | Awareness only; rarely attributable |

The rule that survives most campaigns: **spend 60-70% of the budget on micro and
mid, keep 20-30% for two macro names for credibility, and use nano for content
volume.** All-macro budgets produce a spike and no learning.

## Where to look, in order

1. **Your own customers.** The best creator for your product already bought it. Search tagged posts and reviews.
2. **Comment sections of competitors' creators.** The audience is pre-qualified; the creator next to them is not exclusive.
3. **Niche hashtags and location tags** for the city and language you need.
4. **Creator marketplaces and agencies** - Plixxo, CreatorIQ, WordofMouth, Chtrbox, and the long tail of Mumbai and Delhi agencies. Faster, and they take 15-30%.
5. **Adjacent categories** - a kitchen brand looking at home-organisation creators, not only food.

## The shortlist sheet

| Field | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Handle, name, city | Geography is the cheapest filter there is |
| Tier, followers | Sets the rate band, nothing more |
| Engagement rate | Quality signal; compare within tier only |
| Audience top cities | If 60% is outside your serviceable market, the reach is decorative |
| Audience language | Hindi, English, Hinglish, Tamil - the comment section tells you |
| Content style | Talking head, skit, tutorial, review, aesthetic |
| Brand history | Who they already promote, and whether a competitor is in there |
| Previous paid posts | Have they ever run a #ad? A creator who never does, will do it badly |
| Ask | Their quoted rate, or "not published" |
| Fit note | One line: why this audience needs this product |
| Verdict | Yes, maybe, no - with the reason |

## Fit tests, in order

1. **Does their audience already have the problem?** A fitness creator and a protein brand: yes. A fitness creator and a GST filing tool: no, whatever the numbers say.
2. **Would they plausibly use it?** A creator who has never mentioned the category will read the script.
3. **Is the price band compatible?** A luxury audience does not convert on a ₹299 product, and a value audience resists a ₹9,999 one.
4. **Can they say it in the language the buyer thinks in?** Hinglish for metro D2C, Hindi for tier-2, Tamil for Tamil Nadu - and check the comments, not the bio.

## Sizing the list

| Budget | Creators | Mix |
|---|---|---|
| ₹50,000 | 4-6 | Nano and micro only, barter plus fee |
| ₹2,00,000 | 8-12 | 70% micro, 2 mid, 1 macro |
| ₹5,00,000 | 15-20 | Micro base, 4 mid, 2 macro, content reuse |
| ₹10,00,000+ | 25+ | Add always-on micro programme plus two hero names |

Ten is a workable number for a first campaign: enough to learn, few enough to
manage personally.

## Anti-patterns

- **Shortlisting by follower count.** It is the one number the creator controls and the one that predicts least.
- **Only macro names.** They deliver reach you could buy as ads for less, with no content rights.
- **One creator per tier as a "test".** One data point is an anecdote.
- **Ignoring the serviceable market.** 200,000 views from an audience you cannot ship to is a vanity metric.
- **No barter option.** Many nano creators will work for product plus a small fee, and the content is often better.
