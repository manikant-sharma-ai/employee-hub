# Creator brief and approval

A brief that fits on one page gets followed. A brief that arrives as a 12-page
deck gets skimmed, and the disclosure gets missed.

## The one-page brief

```
Brand: <name>            Campaign: <name>            Dates: <post window>
Deliverables: <1 reel 30-45 s + 3 stories>            Fee: <₹, GST, payment trigger>

THE ONE THING
<The single sentence the audience should remember.>

THE ANGLE
<Why this creator, why this audience, in one line.>

THREE POINTS THAT MUST LAND
1. <product, price, where to buy>
2. <the proof: ingredient, test result, warranty, delivery time>
3. <the offer and the code>

SAY IT YOUR WAY
<Hooks that have worked for this product - offer 3, they can write their own.>

DO NOT SAY
<cure, guaranteed, best in India, doctor-recommended - anything unsubstantiated>

DISCLOSURE (mandatory, in the first two lines of the caption)
#ad  +  "Paid partnership with <brand>"

FORMAT
<9:16, 30-45 s, captions burned in, first 3 seconds no logo>

DEADLINE
Draft by <date> -> one round of feedback within 24 h -> live <date> -> screenshot + 48-hour numbers

RIGHTS
<Organic 12 months + paid amplification 90 days on Meta and Google>

CONTACT
<name, WhatsApp, email>
```

## Approval, in one round

| Step | Who | SLA |
|---|---|---|
| Draft received | You | — |
| Feedback sent | You | Within 24 hours |
| Revision | Creator | 48 hours |
| Final approval | You | Same day |
| Live | Creator | Inside the agreed window |
| Proof | Creator | Screenshot plus link within 2 hours of posting |
| Numbers | Creator | 48 hours after posting |

One revision round. A second round is a new deliverable and gets priced, or the
creator walks - both are acceptable outcomes, and neither happens by accident.

## What to check in the draft

- **Disclosure present, in the first two lines**, not buried after 30 hashtags.
- **No prohibited claim**, no competitor's product in frame, no music that will be muted.
- **Price and offer correct** - this is the most common error and the most expensive.
- **Code and link correct** - test it yourself before approval.
- **The hook lands in three seconds** without a logo or a "hey guys".
- **Sound works on a phone speaker**, since most viewing is silent with captions.

## Hooks that work for a paid recommendation

| Hook | Why it works |
|---|---|
| "I did not expect this to work." | Admits scepticism, buys attention |
| "This is the ₹X I spend every month and never think about." | Specific and familiar |
| "Three things I wish I knew before buying this." | Utility promise |
| "My <family member> bought this before I did." | Social proof from inside |
| "Do not buy this if..." | Disqualification earns trust |

## Anti-patterns

- **A script the creator reads word for word.** It performs badly and it is not what you paid for.
- **Twelve mandatory points.** The audience remembers one; choose it.
- **Feedback after the post is live.** That is a complaint, not an approval.
- **Disclosure left to the creator's memory.** It is the brand's exposure too.
- **Approving a draft on the phone without testing the code.** One wrong code wastes the whole fee.
- **No deadline for the screenshot.** Without proof, payment has no trigger and the tracker rots.
