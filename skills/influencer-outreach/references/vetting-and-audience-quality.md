# Vetting and audience quality

Paid follower counts are sold openly in India for a few hundred rupees per
thousand. Vetting is therefore not a nicety; it is the difference between buying
an audience and buying a number.

## Engagement rate, read within the tier

| Tier | Healthy | Suspicious | Likely purchased |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nano (1k-10k) | 5-10% | 3-5% | under 2% |
| Micro (10k-50k) | 3-7% | 1.5-3% | under 1% |
| Mid (50k-200k) | 1.5-4% | 0.8-1.5% | under 0.6% |
| Macro (200k-1M) | 1-2.5% | 0.5-1% | under 0.4% |

Engagement rate = (likes + comments + saves) / followers, averaged over the last
nine to twelve posts, excluding giveaways and reels that went viral.

## The seven checks, in ten minutes

1. **Follower growth curve.** A vertical jump of 8,000 in a week is bought. Ask for the analytics screenshot.
2. **Comment quality.** Read twenty comments. "Nice", emojis only, and identical phrases across posts are a pod or a bot.
3. **Comment language and city.** If the audience is meant to be Pune and the comments are in a language not spoken there, stop.
4. **Engagement-to-view ratio on reels.** Views without saves and shares means the algorithm pushed it, not the audience.
5. **Tagged posts and brand history.** A creator with 30 brand tags in a month is a billboard, not a recommendation.
6. **The 48-hour pattern.** Likes arriving in one burst and stopping is a pod; organic engagement decays over days.
7. **Their own sales claim.** Ask "which brand did best for you, and what did it do?" A creator who cannot answer has not been measuring.

## Audience geography

Ask for the Instagram or YouTube analytics screenshot for the last 30 days, with
top cities and countries. What to look for:

| Signal | Read |
|---|---|
| Top city matches your serviceable market | Good |
| 40%+ from a country you do not ship to | The reach is not yours |
| City mix shifted in the last month | Possible purchased or viral traffic |
| Age band matches the buyer | A 16-24 audience for a home loan product is wrong |

## Content quality, judged quickly

- **First three seconds.** Does the hook exist, or does it start with "hey guys"?
- **One idea per post.** Creators who cram five messages convert on none.
- **Their own words.** A creator who reads a script on camera will read yours.
- **Lighting and sound.** Bad sound on a phone is normal; bad sound from a ring light studio is a choice.

## Red flags that end the conversation

| Red flag | Why |
|---|---|
| Refuses an analytics screenshot | The audience is not real or not theirs |
| Offers to buy engagement for your campaign | They will, and the numbers will be fiction |
| Wants full payment upfront with no draft approval | No recourse if the content is unusable |
| Will not disclose a prior paid post | Compliance risk and a hidden competitor |
| Asks you to write the caption without the #ad | ASCI exposure lands on the brand too |
| Sells a "guaranteed 500k views" package | It is either bot traffic or a lie |

## Anti-patterns

- **Judging by the blue tick or the media kit.** Media kits are marketing documents.
- **Trusting a third-party score alone.** Tools miss pods and misread regional audiences.
- **Vetting only the shortlisted names.** Vet the whole long list; the expensive mistake is the one you did not check.
- **Confusing virality with fit.** One viral reel tells you the algorithm liked it.
- **Skipping the check because the creator is a friend of the founder.** That is how the unverifiable spend happens.
