# Account structure

Structure decides what the platform can learn. Too flat and the algorithm cannot
find the audience; too fragmented and every ad set starves below the learning
threshold.

## The learning threshold

Most platforms want roughly 50 conversions per ad set per week to optimise well.
That number, not the campaign name, decides how many ad sets you may run.

| Monthly budget | Realistic conversions a week | Ad sets to run |
|---|---|---|
| Rs 30,000 | 10-25 | 1 campaign, 1-2 ad sets |
| Rs 1,00,000 | 40-80 | 1 campaign, 2-4 ad sets |
| Rs 3,00,000 | 120-250 | 2-3 campaigns, 4-8 ad sets |
| Rs 10,00,000+ | 400+ | Multiple campaigns by objective or geography |

If the maths says two ad sets, run two. A structure of nine ad sets on a small
budget produces nine sets that never exit learning and a report nobody can read.

## The naming convention

```
<objective>_<audience>_<geo>_<creative-angle>_<yyyymmdd>
CONV_Broad-IN_Metro_Delivery-promise_261105
```

Rules: the name states what is inside, the date is the launch date, and nobody
needs a lookup sheet to read the account. Rename nothing mid-flight - the report
breaks.

## The standard build

| Level | Split by | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Campaign | Objective, then budget owner | One optimisation goal per campaign |
| Ad set | Audience or geography, not both | Two variables at once is unattributable |
| Ad | One creative variable | Hook, proof, offer or format - one at a time |

## Audiences, and their overlaps

| Audience | Use | Watch |
|---|---|---|
| Broad, with age and geo only | Let the algorithm find buyers | Needs creative variety to work |
| Interest stacks | Early stage, when the buyer is unknown | Interest targeting is shrinking as platforms automate |
| Lookalike 1% from buyers | Scale once there are 100+ buyers | Quality of the seed list decides everything |
| Retargeting, 7/14/30 day | Cheapest conversions | Frequency climbs fast; cap it |
| Customer list exclusion | Every campaign, always | Paying to re-acquire an existing customer is pure waste |
| Competitor or brand search | Small, defensive | Never let it eat the prospecting budget |

## Exclusions checklist

- Existing customers and active subscribers.
- Employees and agencies.
- The wrong geography, including the states or pin codes the business cannot deliver to.
- Anyone who converted in the last 7-30 days, depending on the purchase cycle.
- The audience of an ad set you are testing against - overlap is how two ad sets bid each other up.

## Scaling without breaking it

| Move | Rule |
|---|---|
| Raise budget | 20% every 2-3 days, not 100% overnight |
| Duplicate an ad set | Only to a genuinely different audience, not to "scale" the same one |
| Add a creative | Keeps the ad set learning; add in the same angle family first |
| Open a new geography | Separate ad set, separate budget, separate read |
| Change the offer | New campaign, not an edit - the old data is no longer comparable |

## Anti-patterns

- **One campaign, nine ad sets, Rs 40,000.** Nothing exits learning.
- **Interest stacking until the audience is 8,000 people.** Frequency burns it out in a week.
- **No exclusions.** A large share of small-account spend goes to people who already bought.
- **Renaming and restructuring mid-flight.** The report becomes unreadable and the week's data is lost.
- **Duplicating ad sets to "scale".** You have created competitors for your own budget.
