# Wasted spend and brand safety

Most small accounts waste 10-30% of spend. Finding it is usually worth more than
a week of optimisation.

## The weekly waste hunt

| Check | Where | Typical saving |
|---|---|---|
| Search terms with no relevance | Google Ads search terms report | 5-15% of spend |
| Placements on junk apps and sites | Display and video campaigns | 5-20% |
| Audience overlap bidding against itself | Audience overlap tool | 3-10% |
| Existing customers in prospecting | Exclusion lists | 5-15% |
| Duplicate conversion events | Tag diagnostics | Distorts everything, not just spend |
| Campaigns running after a stockout or offer end | The account and the store | 100% of that spend |
| Brand bidding against your own organic | Search campaigns | Usually waste, sometimes defensive |
| Broad match with no negative list | Search campaigns | 10-25% |
| Retargeting frequency above 8-10 | Frequency report | Burnout plus wasted impressions |
| Geo targeting including undeliverable areas | Ad set settings | 2-8% |

## The negative keyword discipline

- **Build the first negative list before launch**: jobs, free, cheap, wholesale, competitor names you do not want, and the wrong product categories.
- **Add negatives weekly** from the search terms report, not monthly.
- **Share one negative list across campaigns** so the same junk does not return.
- **Review broad match monthly.** It finds volume and it finds waste, in similar quantities.

## Brand safety, without over-engineering

| Risk | Control |
|---|---|
| Ads next to harmful or misleading content | Placement exclusions and content exclusions |
| Comment sections turning hostile | Turn off or moderate comments on paid social |
| Ads in a news cycle the brand should not be near | Pause during a crisis, deliberately and briefly |
| Misleading claims in the creative | The offer must match what the product does |
| Sensitive categories in targeting | No targeting by health, religion, caste, sexuality, financial distress |
| User-generated creative with no rights | Written usage permission before the ad runs |

## Landing page waste

The ad is only half the funnel. Check the page weekly:

- **Load time** - every extra second costs conversions, and mobile data in India makes it worse.
- **Message match** - the page repeats the ad's promise in the first screen.
- **Form length** - each extra field costs completions; ask only what sales will use.
- **Price and stock** - a page showing an old price is a refund and a complaint.
- **Mobile first** - most Indian D2C and B2B traffic is mobile; test on a mid-range Android phone.

## The monthly waste report

```
Spend:                 Rs <n>
Wasted spend found:    Rs <n> (<x>%)
Stopped this month:    <list>
Still to check:        <list>
Cost per verified action before/after: <n> / <n>
```

Two or three lines per item, with the number. This report is the fastest way to
show value in an account that is already "working".

## Anti-patterns

- **Optimising bids while the search terms are junk.** You are buying the wrong thing more efficiently.
- **Negative lists built once.** The junk returns with every broad match change.
- **No placement exclusions on display.** The cheapest inventory is the cheapest for a reason.
- **Leaving a campaign live after a sold-out offer.** It buys traffic for a page that cannot convert.
- **Ignoring the landing page.** The cheapest conversion win is usually on the page, not in the account.
