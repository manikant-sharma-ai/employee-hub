# Read-outs and decisions

The weekly read-out is where spend becomes a decision. One page, five decisions,
same shape every week.

## The page

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Period | Dates, and days remaining in the budget period |
| Spend | To date, projected, against budget |
| Results | Conversions, CPA, and the reconciled order count |
| Movement | What changed versus last week, and why |
| Creative | Live concepts, age, the winner, the fatigued |
| Decisions | Pause, scale, test, hold - each with the rule it follows |
| Risks | Frequency, page speed, stock, a landing page down |

## The decision rules, written down before you need them

| Situation | Rule | Action |
|---|---|---|
| CPA above ceiling for 7 days, 50+ clicks | Fatigued or wrong audience | Pause the ad, keep the ad set |
| CPA above ceiling across every ad in an ad set for 10 days | Offer, page or audience | Pause the ad set, fix the page before relaunching |
| CPA below target for 5 days, frequency under 2.5 | Headroom | Scale 20% |
| Frequency above 3 on a cold audience | Saturation | Refresh creative, widen audience, or cap spend |
| Zero conversions, spend above 1.5x target CPA | Something is broken | Check tracking and the page before pausing |
| Landing page failing a phone check | The most expensive failure | Pause the campaign the same hour |
| Stock-out on the promoted SKU | Spending to disappoint | Pause, or swap the offer |

A rule with a number and a duration can be applied by anyone. A rule like "if it
feels expensive" cannot.

## The five decisions

Every read-out answers these, in this order:

1. **What do we pause?** Named, with the rule.
2. **What do we scale?** Named, with the step size and the ceiling.
3. **What do we test next?** One concept, one audience change at most.
4. **What do we fix outside the account?** Page, price, stock, tracking.
5. **What do we need from the founder?** Budget approval, a decision on the target,
   or a stock answer.

If a week produces no decision, the account is on autopilot, and autopilot is how a
CPA drifts for a month before anyone notices.

## Reporting to a founder

- **Three numbers, not thirty.** Spend, cost per acquired customer, and orders
  collected. Everything else is appendix.
- **The number that matters is reconciled.** Say which one it is.
- **One recommendation**, with the cost of being wrong.
- **No jargon**: no "learning phase", no "CBO", no "incrementality" without a
  one-line translation.
- **Say what you do not know.** "The platform claims 210 orders; the gateway shows
  168; I am reconciling before we scale" is a sentence that builds trust.

## When a CPA blows up

In this order, and stop at the first cause found:

1. **Is the page up, fast and correct on a phone?**
2. **Is tracking firing once, with the right event and value?**
3. **Is the stock available for the promoted SKU?**
4. **Did the creative fatigue** - frequency, hook rate, CTR trend?
5. **Did the auction change** - a festival, a competitor, a platform change?
6. **Did the audience change** - an exclusion removed, a lookalike refreshed?
7. **Did the offer change** - price, discount, delivery promise?

Only after those seven is "the algorithm" a candidate explanation, and it is almost
never the answer.

## Anti-patterns

- **The daily panic.** Changing three things a day means no result can be attributed.
- **The read-out that reports platform numbers only.**
- **The decision with no rule.** "It felt expensive" is not a reason.
- **The scale without a ceiling.** The best week of the quarter is where the
  unprofitable month starts.
- **The silent pause.** A campaign stopped without a note is a lesson lost.
- **Reporting to the founder only when it is good.** Bad weeks are the ones that
  need a decision.
