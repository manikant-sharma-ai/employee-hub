# Budget pacing and bidding

Pacing is the difference between a month of even learning and a month's budget
spent by the 9th on the cheapest audience available.

## The pacing sheet

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| Monthly budget | Rs 3,00,000 |
| GST treatment | Excluded - invoices will carry 18% |
| Daily cap | Rs 10,000 |
| Spent to date | Rs 1,42,000 on day 13 |
| Pace | 13/30 of month = 43%; spend is 47% - slightly hot |
| Projected month-end | Rs 3,28,000 - over by Rs 28,000 |
| Action | Hold budgets for 3 days, shift Rs 20,000 from the weakest ad set |

Check the pace every second day. Weekly is too late to correct; hourly is noise.

## Pacing patterns

| Pattern | When to use |
|---|---|
| Even daily | Evergreen offers, steady demand |
| Front-loaded | A launch or a festive window with a short peak |
| Back-loaded | A month with a sale date, so budget lands on the offer |
| Weighted by weekday | B2B where weekends are worthless |

## Bidding, in plain terms

| Strategy | What it does | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| Lowest cost / maximise conversions | Spends the budget at the cheapest available cost | Default for a new account |
| Cost cap / target CPA | Holds the cost near a target | After 50+ conversions a week, when the target is real |
| Bid cap | Caps the auction bid | Rarely, and only with a strong data set |
| Maximise value / ROAS target | Optimises for revenue, not count | High AOV, wide margin spread |

Rules: never set a cost cap below the current cost per action and expect volume -
you will simply stop spending. Never change the bid strategy and the creative in
the same week.

## When the month runs hot

1. **Pause the weakest ad set**, not the whole campaign.
2. **Hold budgets** rather than dropping them - a 30% cut resets learning.
3. **Check for a runaway audience** - broad targeting plus a good creative can spend twice the plan in a day.
4. **Check duplicate conversion events** - an account that thinks it has twice the conversions spends twice as fast.

## When the month runs cold

- **Widen the audience** before raising the bid.
- **Add creative** - more variants give the algorithm more to spend against.
- **Check the learning phase** - a recently edited ad set may be throttled.
- **Only then raise the budget**, by 20%.

## Scaling ladder

| Step | Change | Wait |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Raise the daily cap 20% | 2-3 days |
| 2 | Duplicate the winner to a new audience | 3-5 days |
| 3 | Add a sibling creative | 3-5 days |
| 4 | Raise again 20% | 2-3 days |
| Stop | Cost per action exceeds target for 3 consecutive days | Revert one step |

## India-specific notes

- **GST on ad services is 18%**, and input credit is usually available for a registered business. Quote budgets ex-GST or say which, every time.
- **TDS applies to payments to Indian ad vendors and agencies**; the rate and section depend on the nature of the contract. That is a `finance-ops` and CA question, not an ads one.
- **Prepaid wallets** on Indian platforms mean a pacing failure can also mean a campaign that stops mid-flight for lack of balance.
- **Festive CPMs** in September-November can run 1.5-3x January. Plan the budget around the calendar, not the average.

## Anti-patterns

- **Daily budget as a monthly budget.** A Rs 10,000 daily cap on a 30-day month is Rs 3 lakh, not Rs 1 lakh.
- **Editing three times a day.** Each structural edit restarts learning.
- **Cutting 50% to fix overspend.** You lose the learning and the week.
- **Bid strategy roulette.** A new strategy every week means no strategy has been tested.
- **No pacing sheet.** The platform will spend whatever it is allowed to spend.
