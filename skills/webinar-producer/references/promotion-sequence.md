# Promotion sequence

Registration is a copy problem and a timing problem. This is the schedule, the
channel mix and the words.

## The fourteen-day shape

| When | Channel | Job of the touch | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| T-14 | Email to the list, LinkedIn post from the speaker | Announce, name the outcome, open registration | 120 words |
| T-10 | Founder's LinkedIn + WhatsApp broadcast to warm contacts | Social proof: who else is coming | 80 words |
| T-7 | Email: the problem, one story, the agenda in outcomes | Convert the undecided | 200 words |
| T-4 | WhatsApp to registered only | Confirm, add to calendar, ask for one question | 40 words |
| T-3 | Email: objection mail ("I am not technical", "will there be a recording") | Remove the reason not to come | 150 words |
| T-1 | Email + WhatsApp to registered | Reminder with the join link and the time in their zone | 50 words |
| T-1h | WhatsApp to registered | "Starts in an hour, link below" | 25 words |
| T-10m | Email to registered | Join now, we have started | 20 words |
| T+0 | Chat and social | Live lines, one quote per ten minutes | - |
| T+1d | Email, split three ways | The next step | 120 words |

Two touches a week for two weeks beats five in the last three days. The single
biggest lever on show-up rate is the hour-before WhatsApp, and the second is
adding the calendar invite at registration.

## Channel mix by audience

| Audience | Primary | Secondary | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| B2B owners, MSME | WhatsApp broadcast + email | LinkedIn from the founder | WhatsApp open rates are 60-80%; email 20-30% |
| SaaS and startup | Email + LinkedIn | Communities, Slack, WhatsApp groups | Founders forward emails; make them forwardable |
| Consumers | Instagram + WhatsApp | Email as a receipt | Instagram stories with a countdown work |
| CAs, doctors, teachers | WhatsApp groups + email | Association newsletters | Ask a member to forward; peer endorsement converts |
| Export buyers | Email + LinkedIn | Time-zone-specific reminders | Two reminders: one for their morning, one an hour before |

## The four mails that do the work

**Announce (T-14).** Subject is the outcome, not the event. "File GSTR-3B yourself
in 45 minutes" beats "Webinar on GST - 21 Nov". Body: the problem in two lines,
the promise, three bullets on what they will leave with, the date and time with
the zone, one button.

**Problem story (T-7).** One real customer, one number, one mistake they made. Then
the agenda as outcomes, not topics. Then the speaker in 40 words, no biography.
Then the button.

**Objection (T-3).** Title it with the objection: "No, you do not need to be
technical". Answer three objections in three short blocks. This mail routinely
converts better than the announce.

**Reminder (T-1).** Under 50 words. Time, zone, join link, one line on what they
will miss. No new argument - a reminder that introduces a new reason creates doubt.

## The registration page

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| Headline | The outcome, in the audience's words, under 12 words |
| Subhead | Who it is for and who it is not for |
| Three bullets | What you can do afterwards, each a verb |
| Date, time, duration | With the zone spelled out, and a calendar button |
| Speaker | One number and one credential, 40 words |
| The offer | Say plainly whether something is sold and at what price band |
| Form | Name, email, one qualifying question. Phone only if WhatsApp reminders are promised |
| Proof | Logos, a testimonial, or a count of past attendees - only if real |

Every extra form field costs 5-10% of registrations. The qualifying question is
worth more than the phone number: "What do you use today for X?" segments the
follow-up.

## Copy rules that move the numbers

- **The subject line carries the outcome, the preview text carries the time.** Most tools show both.
- **Say the duration and keep it.** "45 minutes" is a promise about respect.
- **Name the audience in the first line.** "For manufacturers under ₹10 crore turnover" makes everyone else leave and the right people stay.
- **One call to action per touch.** A mail with "register, follow us, and read the blog" gets none.
- **Write the WhatsApp message as a person would send it.** No template brackets, no "Dear Sir/Madam", one link.
- **Send B2B mail Tuesday to Thursday, 9:30-11:00 IST.** Monday is triage, Friday is gone.

## Benchmarks to plan against, not to promise

| Metric | Weak | Normal | Good |
|---|---|---|---|
| Page visit to registration | <15% | 20-30% | 35%+ |
| Email open rate | <20% | 25-35% | 40%+ |
| Registration to attendance, email only | <20% | 25-35% | 40% |
| Registration to attendance, email + WhatsApp + calendar | <30% | 40-50% | 55%+ |
| Attendance to a booked meeting | <5% | 8-15% | 20%+ |
| Recording views per attendee, first 30 days | <0.5 | 1-2 | 3+ |

Quote these as planning ranges and label them as such. Your own history beats any
benchmark, which is why the journal line matters.

## Anti-patterns

- **Announcing eleven days out and then going quiet.** Two weeks of silence is a reminder to forget.
- **The same mail to the list and to registrants.** Registrants need logistics, the list needs the argument.
- **A "last chance" mail for a session with unlimited seats.** It works once and costs trust every time after.
- **Buying a list to hit a registration target.** One spam complaint can cost the sending domain.
- **Registering people yourself from a business-card pile.** Consent is per person, not per event.
