# Run of show

The session is won or lost in the plan, not on the day. This file is the promise,
the format and the minute-by-minute.

## The promise

One sentence, in the audience's words, with a measurable outcome.

| Bad | Why | Better |
|---|---|---|
| "All about GST for MSMEs" | A topic, not an outcome | "File your first GSTR-3B without your CA, in 45 minutes" |
| "Scaling your D2C brand" | Nobody can tell if they got it | "Cut your return rate from 18% to under 10% with three packaging changes" |
| "AI for business" | Attracts everyone, converts none | "Write your product descriptions in 20 minutes a week, in your own voice" |

Test the promise on one person in the target audience before you build anything.
If they ask "is it free?", the promise did not land.

## Format by job

| Job | Format | Length | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sell a considered purchase | Live demo plus Q&A | 45 min + 15 | The demo answers the objection the page cannot |
| Build authority | Founder teaching a method | 40 min | No offer, or one soft one at the end |
| Launch a product | Launch session with a live offer | 30 min + 15 | Urgency is real, so it can be used honestly |
| Fill a pipeline for high-ticket services | Workshop with homework | 60-90 min | Effort filters intent; small rooms, high close rate |
| Serve existing customers | Onboarding or advanced training | 30 min | Deflection and expansion in one room |
| Test a topic | Panel with two guests | 45 min | Cheap to produce, tells you if the topic has demand |

## The slot

| Audience | Best slot (IST) | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| B2B owners and managers | Tue-Thu, 11:00 or 16:00 | Monday morning, Friday afternoon, month-end |
| Consumers, side businesses | Tue-Thu, 20:00-21:00 | Weekend evenings in festival season |
| Students and job-seekers | Weekday 18:00, Sunday 11:00 | Exam weeks, board exam months |
| NRI or export audience | 18:30-19:30 IST (covers Gulf and UK morning) | Anything after 21:00 IST |
| Doctors, CAs, teachers | After clinic or school hours, 19:00 | Their filing or exam season |

Check the calendar before fixing: Diwali, Holi, Eid, Christmas week, IPL finals,
board exams (Feb-Apr), and the last week of the financial year. A B2B webinar in
the last week of March competes with every closing in the country.

## The minute-by-minute, for a 45-minute session

| Minute | Segment | Who | Opening words | Screen |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| -10 | Room opens, holding slide, chat welcome | Host | "Say your city and your biggest question in the chat" | Title + one promise line |
| 0:00 | Cold open, no throat-clearing | Speaker | "In the next 40 minutes I will show you X. Here is what we will not cover." | Agenda in outcomes |
| 0:03 | Credibility in 60 seconds | Speaker | "Why listen to me: one number and one mistake" | Two facts, no life story |
| 0:05 | The problem, in their words | Speaker | Three quotes from real customers | Problem slide |
| 0:12 | Method, part one | Speaker | "Step one, and the mistake most people make here" | Diagram |
| 0:22 | Demo or worked example | Speaker + demo owner | "Watch the screen, I will narrate" | Live product or a sheet |
| 0:32 | Proof | Speaker | One customer case with numbers | Before/after |
| 0:36 | The offer, announced as an offer | Speaker | "For the next ten minutes this is the thing we sell" | Offer slide with price |
| 0:40 | Q&A, triaged | Host | "Three questions I can answer now, the rest by mail tomorrow" | Question slide |
| 0:50 | One action, one date | Host | "The link is in the chat and in the mail you get in an hour" | Next-step slide |
| 0:55 | Close, thank, stop talking | Host | "Recording goes out tomorrow. Bye." | Contact + replay note |

A 45-minute promise with a 55-minute delivery is normal. A 45-minute promise that
runs 75 loses the room at minute 50, which is exactly where the offer is.

## Failure scripts

| What breaks | What you say, and do |
|---|---|
| Demo crashes | "The demo is not cooperating. I will describe it and send a recording of it tomorrow." Then keep teaching. Never debug live for more than 90 seconds |
| Speaker is late | Host runs a two-minute poll and a "what do you want answered" round; the clock still starts on time |
| Room is silent | "I can see 40 of you. Type 1 if this is your first time, 2 if you have tried this before." Silence is a prompt problem |
| Hostile question in chat | "That is a fair challenge and it deserves a proper answer - I will come back to it at the end." Then do |
| Only 8 people attend | Deliver it as a workshop, not a broadcast. Small rooms convert better; say so afterwards |
| Someone asks for a refund or a policy exception | Park it. "Not something I can decide live - I will come back to you today." |

## Rehearsal, once, with a timer

- Run the full session with the real slides, the real demo and the real clock.
- Cut whatever overruns. The usual culprit is the credibility segment and the problem segment, in that order.
- Say the offer out loud once before the day. The first time a founder says the price live is not the day of the session.

## Anti-patterns

- **The agenda that is a table of contents.** "Introduction, background, market, then the method" spends 20 of 45 minutes earning the right to start.
- **Two speakers, no host.** Nobody watches the chat, so the Q&A dies and the offer lands to silence.
- **The offer at minute 44 of 45.** No time to ask a question, so nobody converts.
- **Reading slides aloud.** The slides are the notes; the talking is the product.
- **A 90-minute session with a free audience.** Attention for a free hour is about 45 minutes; plan two sessions instead.
