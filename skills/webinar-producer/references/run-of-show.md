# Run of show

A webinar is a broadcast with one clock. The run of show is that clock written
down, with an owner against every minute, so that a dropped connection, a late
guest or a rambling answer costs a segment instead of the offer.

## The shape of 60 minutes

| Minutes | Segment | Owner | Purpose | Ends with |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| -15 to 0 | Room open | producer | Music, slide with the promise, chat prompt | "We start at 7:00" |
| 0-2 | Opening 90 seconds | host | Promise, credibility, agenda, the outcome | "By 7:45 you will have X" |
| 2-5 | Credibility | host | Who we are, one proof number, one client type | "That is the lens for today" |
| 5-20 | Teaching block 1 | speaker | The problem, named better than the audience can name it | "That is why most of you are stuck" |
| 20-23 | Interaction | producer | Poll or chat prompt, read three answers aloud | "Half of you said X - that is the problem" |
| 23-38 | Teaching block 2 | speaker | The method, with one worked example | "Here is what it looks like done" |
| 38-43 | The offer | host | What it is, who it is for, what it costs, what happens next | "The link is in the chat" |
| 43-52 | Q&A | host + speaker | Objections answered in public | "One more question" |
| 52-57 | Close | host | Repeat the offer, the deadline, the next step | "Do it now, it takes two minutes" |
| 57-60 | Buffer | producer | Nothing scheduled | Hard stop |

Rules that hold this shape:

- **Content is 35-40 minutes of a 60-minute slot.** A speaker who plans 55 minutes
  of content will run 70 and skip the offer.
- **The offer transition is a segment, not a mood.** It starts at a fixed minute.
- **Every segment ends with a sentence that hands over.** "That is why most of you
  are stuck" is a handover; "so, yeah, moving on" is not.
- **Q&A is capped at nine minutes** and the host takes three seeded questions
  before opening the floor.
- **The last three minutes are unscheduled** so a technical failure does not eat
  the close.

## Segment lengths by session type

| Session | Teaching | Demo | Offer | Q&A | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30-minute lunch session | 15 | 0 | 5 | 7 | 30 |
| 45-minute workshop | 25 | 5 | 5 | 8 | 45 |
| 60-minute standard | 33 | 5 | 5 | 9 | 60 |
| 90-minute masterclass | 55 | 10 | 8 | 12 | 90 |

Anything under 30 minutes is a demo, not a webinar, and should be sold as one.
Anything over 90 minutes needs a break and an agenda sent in advance, or
attendance collapses after minute 50.

## Roles, and what happens when one is missing

| Role | Does | If missing |
|---|---|---|
| Host | Opens, transitions, closes, watches the clock | The speaker runs long and the offer is skipped |
| Speaker | Teaches the two blocks | The session is a sales pitch and the room leaves |
| Producer | Platform, recording, chat, polls, let people in | The first five minutes are spent on audio |
| Chat moderator | Answers logistics, drops links, flags objections | Questions get lost and the same one is asked four times |
| Closer | Works the follow-up queue after | The room goes cold in 48 hours |

One person can hold host and closer. Nobody should hold speaker and producer.

## The rehearsal

Twenty minutes, on the real platform, at least 48 hours before:

1. Speaker joins from the machine they will use, on the network they will use.
2. Screen share, the actual deck, the actual demo login - not a screenshot.
3. Audio in both directions, and the recording started and stopped once.
4. The producer practices letting someone in, muting, and starting a poll.
5. The host reads the opening 90 seconds out loud, once.

If the demo cannot be rehearsed, it is a recording with a voiceover. A live demo
that fails in front of 200 people costs more than a recorded one that cannot.

## Anti-patterns

- **Agenda sent as a list of topics.** "What we will cover" is not an agenda; the
  agenda is the four things they will be able to do afterwards.
- **Guest speaker with no rehearsal.** Guest no-shows are the single most common
  cause of a dead session; have a 20-minute fill segment ready.
- **Panel of four with no moderator script.** Four people and no clock is 60
  minutes of throat-clearing.
- **Q&A opened to the floor at minute 20.** It becomes a support queue and the
  teaching never resumes.
- **Recording started after the opening.** The opening is the part that sells the
  replay.
- **No hard stop.** The platform stays open, three people stay on, and the
  producer loses 40 minutes of their evening.
