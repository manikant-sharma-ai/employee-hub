# Live ops

The room is a job, not a broadcast. Someone has to work it while the speaker talks.

## Roles, even in a team of two

| Role | Owns | Never does |
|---|---|---|
| Speaker | The teaching and the offer | Reads the chat, answers logistics |
| Host | Time, chat, Q&A triage, the offer handoff | Teaches, or defends the price |
| Tech | Recording, screen share, waiting room, the join-link problem | Talks to the audience |
| Closer (optional) | DMs the three hottest questions | Pitches in the public chat |

With one person: the speaker stops at minute 22 and 36 to read two chat questions
aloud. That is the minimum viable room.

## Triage, in three buckets

Every question in the chat or Q&A gets one of three labels within 60 seconds.

| Bucket | Test | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Answer now | Applies to most of the room, answerable in 40 seconds | Read it, name the asker, answer, move on |
| Answer later | Specific to one business, needs their numbers | "I will answer that one by mail tomorrow" - and log it |
| Take to a call | Money, contract, compliance, or "can you do this for us" | "That is a 15-minute conversation - drop your email and we will send a slot" |

A question answered in the chat but not logged is a lead lost. The log is a sheet
with: time, name, email if given, question, bucket, owner, follow-up date.

## Chat craft

- Greet by name for the first five arrivals. It changes the tone of the room.
- Seed two questions from the host at the start so the chat is not empty: "Type where you are joining from."
- Answer in the chat within a minute, even if the answer is "good question, coming at minute 30".
- Never argue in the chat. "Let us take that offline" and a direct message.
- Kill links from anyone else, including partners, unless agreed in advance.

## Polls earn their place

Two polls maximum, each with a job.

1. **Segmentation poll at minute 5** - how they do the thing today. This decides which follow-up mail they get and which case study you mention.
2. **Blocker poll at minute 30** - what stops them. This becomes the offer framing and the next webinar's topic.

Report the poll results in the follow-up. People like seeing their own room's answer,
and it proves the session was live rather than recorded.

## The offer handoff

- The speaker announces the offer at the planned minute, in the planned words.
- The host puts the link in the chat once, and again at the close. Not four times.
- The closer answers price questions in DMs, never in the public chat.
- If the offer is a call, the call-to-action is a calendar link, not "reply to this mail".
- Say the deadline only if it is real.

## Recording and consent

- Announce recording in the first 30 seconds and put it on the holding slide.
- Ask attendees to keep questions free of personal data - names of their customers, ID numbers, bank details.
- If a guest appears, get written permission by mail before the session, and keep it.
- The recording is not published until you have checked it for a leaked screen, a customer name, or a price you did not mean to say.

## After the room closes, same day

1. Download the attendance report and the Q&A export.
2. Reconcile: registrations, attendees, peak concurrent, average watch time, drop-off minute.
3. Log every question with its bucket and owner.
4. Note the drop-off point. A cliff at minute 12 is a problem segment; a cliff at minute 34 is the offer arriving too early or too late.
5. Send the recording to attendees the next working morning, with the offer and the next step.

## Anti-patterns

- **The host who is also the speaker and also the tech.** One broken link and the room is gone.
- **Reading every question in order.** The room's attention is spent on the first three; pick the three that apply to most.
- **Answering a pricing objection publicly with a discount.** It becomes the price for everyone watching.
- **Leaving the recording link open to all.** A paid session's replay is the product.
- **No attendance report downloaded on the day.** Platforms change retention windows and the file is the follow-up list.
