# Follow-up and pipeline

The session is the top of the funnel. This file is everything after the room
closes, and it is where most webinars lose the value they paid for.

## Three audiences, three mails, within 24 hours

| Segment | Who | Subject | Body | Next step |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Attended, stayed to the end | Peak attendance minus drop-offs | "The three things you asked for" | Recording, the deck, the two answers they asked for, the offer | Book a call, or the offer link |
| Attended, left early | Watch time under 50% | "You left before the part you asked about" | The one segment they missed, timecoded, plus the recording | Reply with a question |
| Registered, no-show | Not in the attendance report | "We saved the recording for you" | One-line reason to watch, the timecode to start at, the offer | Watch, then book |
| Never registered, engaged on social | Optional, consent-based | Public post, not a mail | The best two minutes as a clip | Follow for the next one |

Never send "thanks for attending" to a no-show. It is the fastest way to be marked
as spam, and it tells the person you did not check.

## The mail that converts, in order

1. **The answer they asked for.** If 30 people asked about input credit, the first paragraph answers it. That is the reason the mail gets opened at all.
2. **The timecode.** "The pricing section is at 31:20" - it respects their time and gets the offer watched.
3. **The recording and the deck.** One link each. No gating on the first follow-up.
4. **The next step, with a date.** "If you want this done for you, here is a 20-minute slot on Thursday or Friday."
5. **One line of social proof.** A customer, a number, a city.

Length: under 150 words. It is a follow-up, not a second webinar.

## Attendance-to-meeting, the only number that matters

| Step | Definition | Weak | Good |
|---|---|---|---|
| Meeting booked | A calendar event with a named person | <5% of attendees | 15-20% |
| Meeting held | Showed up to the call | 60% of booked | 80% |
| Qualified | Has the problem, the budget, the authority | 40% of held | 60% |
| Closed within 90 days | Signed | - | Depends on the price |

Track these as a chain and report the weakest link, not the total. A 12% book rate
with 50% no-shows is a scheduling problem, not a demand problem.

## Segment the follow-up with what the room told you

- **Poll 1 (how they do it today)** - "nothing" gets the beginner path, "software" gets the advanced case study.
- **Poll 2 (biggest blocker)** - cost gets ROI framing, approval gets a one-pager to forward to the boss, skill gets the workshop invite.
- **The qualifying question on the form** - use the exact words they wrote in the first line of the mail. It reads as attention, because it is.
- **Chat questions** - a person who asked a specific question gets a specific answer, personally, before any bulk mail.

## What to do with the three hottest leads, same day

1. Name them from the Q&A log - the ones who asked about price, timeline or "can you do this for us".
2. Send a personal mail, no template, referencing their question.
3. Offer two specific slots.
4. Log them in the CRM with the source "webinar <date> <topic>".

A personal mail to three people beats a perfect sequence to three hundred, in the
first 24 hours.

## The replay page

- The recording, embedded, with a chapter list built from the run of show.
- The offer, with the same terms as the live session, and an honest line about whether the price changes.
- The three questions from the Q&A that most people asked, answered in text - it is what search traffic will find.
- A form for the next session, so the replay builds the next room.

## When there is no pipeline after the session

Diagnose in this order, and say which one it is:

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Good attendance, no meetings | The offer was vague, or the audience was wrong | Name the offer earlier; check who registered against who you sell to |
| Good attendance, meetings booked, none held | No confirmation, no reminder, no agenda | Two reminders and a one-line agenda per meeting |
| Registrations high, attendance low | Promotion started late, no WhatsApp, no calendar invite | Move the sequence to T-14 and add WhatsApp |
| Attendance high, wrong people | The topic attracted curiosity, not need | Narrow the promise and the qualifying question |
| No registrations at all | The promise is a topic, or the list is cold | Rewrite the promise, test it on five people |

## Anti-patterns

- **One bulk "thanks for joining" mail to everyone.** It wastes the only moment when the room remembers you.
- **Waiting for the edited recording before following up.** The follow-up is worth more than the edit; send the raw cut.
- **Gating the recording behind a form for people who already attended.** They gave you their details once.
- **Adding every attendee to the newsletter without a tick.** Consent for the session is not consent for the list.
- **Reporting registrations as the result.** It is the input, and it is the number that flatters.
