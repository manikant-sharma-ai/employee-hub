# Live delivery and hosting

The room is decided in the first 90 seconds and lost in three places: a slow
start, an unscripted offer, and a Q&A that turns into an argument. All three are
written in advance.

## The opening 90 seconds

Say these four things, in this order, in under 90 seconds:

1. **The promise** - "In the next 45 minutes I will show you how to cut your
   customer-acquisition cost without cutting spend."
2. **The credibility** - one number: "We have run this for 60 D2C brands, and the
   median reduction was 22%."
3. **The agenda** - three items, not seven.
4. **The reward for staying** - "At 7:40 I will show the exact template we use,
   and you can take it whether or not you work with us."

Then stop talking about yourself. The most common opening failure is four minutes
of company history before the first useful sentence.

## Holding the room

| Minute | Risk | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| 0-2 | Late joiners, silence, "can you hear me" | Producer confirms audio in chat; host starts on time regardless |
| 8-12 | Attention dip | Name a specific pain in the audience's words |
| 20 | Drop-off begins | Poll, read three answers, use one to pivot |
| 30-35 | Speaker fatigue, slide reading | One story with a number in it, told in 60 seconds |
| 38 | The offer arrives and people leave | Say "I am going to make an offer in a minute, and it is fine to leave" - honesty keeps more people |
| 43-52 | Q&A drift | Three seeded questions first, then the floor |

## The offer transition

Written, rehearsed, and delivered at the fixed minute:

> "That is the method. If you want us to run it with you: we do a 90-day
> engagement for ₹X, we take four clients a quarter, and the next start date is
> 12 December. The link is in the chat. If that is not for you, the template is
> yours either way - the recording goes out tonight."

Four properties: the price, the constraint, the date, the exit. A webinar offer
without a price produces curiosity, not calls.

## Q&A: the four hard ones

| Question type | What is really being asked | Answer shape |
|---|---|---|
| "Will this work for my industry?" | Is this relevant to me | Name one client in their industry and one difference |
| "How is this different from X?" | Justify the price | One axis of difference, one number, no competitor-bashing |
| "What does it cost?" | They are interested | Say the number. Deflection costs more than the number |
| Hostile or off-topic | They want the room's attention | "That is a fair concern - the short answer is X. Let me take it in writing after" |

Never argue in public. Never let a participant be humiliated. The producer takes
the hostile question into a direct message while the host moves on.

## Chat and polls as instruments, not decoration

- **One poll at minute 20**, three options, results read aloud. It creates a
  segment for the follow-up: "you said your problem was X".
- **One chat prompt at minute 5** - "where are you joining from and what is your
  biggest issue with this?" It warms the room and gives the host material.
- **Chat is moderated, not ignored.** Unanswered logistics questions in chat are
  the main cause of "the webinar was not useful" feedback.
- **Do not read the chat aloud line by line.** Three answers, then move.

## Technical failure playbook

| Failure | First move | Second move |
|---|---|---|
| Speaker audio dead | Producer posts in chat, host fills 60 seconds | Switch to phone audio, keep going |
| Screen share fails | Speaker describes, producer shares the deck | Send the deck link in chat |
| Demo breaks | "I will send this as a recording" and move on | Never debug live for more than 60 seconds |
| Platform down | Email the room with a new link within 10 minutes | Reschedule inside 48 hours, same promise |
| Host drops | Speaker takes the clock; producer promotes the co-host | Close on time regardless |

## Anti-patterns

- **"We will wait two minutes for people to join."** You lose the people who
  arrived on time, and they are the ones who buy.
- **Reading slides.** The deck is a backdrop; the teaching is spoken.
- **A panel with no clock.** Four speakers, no moderator, no offer.
- **Ending with "any other questions?"** It invites silence. End with the close.
- **Not saying the recording will be sent.** Attendance drops when people think
  they must stay to get the replay.
- **Running over.** Every minute past the hour costs the close and the next
  session's attendance.
