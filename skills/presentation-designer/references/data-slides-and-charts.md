# Data slides and charts

A chart on a slide is an argument, not a record. The record belongs in the appendix. This file is how to make a number land in three seconds from the back of a room.

## Choose the form from the comparison

| The comparison | The chart | Never |
|---|---|---|
| Change over time | Line, 2-4 series maximum | Pie chart |
| Ranking | Horizontal bar, sorted | Vertical bar with rotated labels |
| Composition of a whole | Stacked bar, or a single 100% bar | 3D pie |
| Two variables against each other | Scatter, if n is over 30 | Line with two axes |
| Actual vs target | Bullet or bar with a target line | Gauge |
| A single number that matters | The number, at 120pt, with a basis line | Any chart |

If the chart needs a legend with six entries, it is a table.

## Chart hygiene

- **One message per chart, in the title.** "Repeat orders fell after the July price change" is the title; the axes are not.
- **Delete the gridlines, the box, the 3D, the shadow and the gradient.** Each one is a distraction from the comparison.
- **Label the data directly** rather than with a legend, wherever there are three or fewer series.
- **Start the y-axis at zero for bars.** For lines, a non-zero start is allowed if it is labelled and the point is the movement, not the level.
- **Highlight one series in colour and grey the rest.** The audience's eye follows the only coloured thing.
- **Round the numbers on the chart, keep the precision in the appendix.** "₹4.2 cr" is read; "₹4,19,37,412" is not.
- **Name the unit and the period** on the slide, every time: "₹ crore, FY26, management accounts, unaudited".

## The Indian number conventions

- Use ₹ and the crore/lakh convention for a domestic audience: ₹4.2 crore, ₹18 lakh. Use millions only if the audience is international.
- Do not mix systems on one slide. ₹4.2 cr next to $520k next to 0.52M is three currencies of confusion.
- State whether the figure is inclusive or exclusive of GST when it is a price.
- Percentages: one decimal place at most. Two is noise; none can hide a real move.

## Financial slides

An investor or board audience looks for four things, in this order:

1. **The trend** - revenue, gross margin, burn, over at least six periods. Three points is a line, not a trend.
2. **The driver** - what makes the trend move: volume, price, mix, retention.
3. **The basis** - actual, forecast or assumption, stated on the slide.
4. **The bridge** - last period to this period, itemised: volume, price, new customers, churn, cost.

A bridge chart is the single most persuasive slide in a board pack, because it shows you know why the number moved.

| Bridge item | Effect |
|---|---|
| Opening revenue | The base |
| Volume growth | + |
| Price change | + or - |
| New customers | + |
| Churn | - |
| Closing revenue | Must equal the reported figure, to the rupee |

If the bridge does not reconcile, do not show it. A bridge that is plugged is worse than no bridge.

## Tables

- Six rows, four columns maximum on a live slide.
- Right-align numbers, left-align text, and put units in the header.
- Bold the row or column the audience should look at.
- A table with 20 rows is a spreadsheet screenshot; move it to the appendix and show the top five.

## Honesty rules

- **Never change the axis to make a story.** Someone in the room will notice, and everything else on the deck becomes suspect.
- **Never show a cumulative line without saying it is cumulative.** Cumulative charts always look like growth.
- **Never show a percentage on fewer than 30 observations** without a note. 2 of 5 is not 40%.
- **Never compare a partial period to a full one.** Q3-to-date against full Q2 is the most common accidental lie in board packs.
- **Label the source on the slide**: "Tally, extracted 2 Nov 2026" is enough, and it ends the argument about whose number it is.
- **Show the miss.** A deck that only shows what worked gets a harder second meeting.

## Anti-patterns

- **A dashboard screenshot pasted onto a slide.** Unreadable, and it implies nobody looked at it.
- **A pie chart with seven slices** where the smallest is 3%.
- **Two y-axes** on a line chart, making any two series look correlated.
- **"Revenue" with no period and no unit**, then a question in the room that nobody can answer.
- **A chart built from a number typed into the deck** rather than linked to the model, so the deck and the model disagree by the second revision.
