# Reviewing a deck

The pre-send pass. Twenty minutes here saves the meeting, and it is the step that gets skipped when the deck is finished at 2am.

## The seven checks, in order

1. **The title run.** Read only the slide titles in sequence. Do they argue? Is there a turn? Is the ask in there?
2. **The decision sentence.** Say it out loud. If it does not match what the titles say, one of them is wrong.
3. **The number reconciliation.** Every figure on a slide traces to a source: the model, the accounts, or a stated assumption. Any number that cannot be traced gets removed or labelled.
4. **The basis lines.** Every number has period, unit and actual/forecast. Missing basis on any slide is a stop.
5. **The claim check.** Every comparative claim ("market leader", "fastest", "cheapest") has a source or is deleted. Every customer name has permission.
6. **The read-cold test.** Open the deck on a phone, from slide one, with no presenter. Where does it stop making sense?
7. **The grey print test.** Print one slide in greyscale. Is the highlighted series still distinguishable?

## The red-flag list

Anything on this list blocks the send:

- A number on one slide that contradicts a number on another.
- A slide with no title, or a title that is a label.
- More than 25 words of body text on a live slide.
- A chart without a title claim, or with two y-axes.
- The ask missing, or buried in the appendix.
- A forecast presented as an actual.
- A competitor named with an unsourced claim.
- A customer logo without written permission.
- A slide that only exists because it existed last quarter.
- "TBD", "XX", "[name]" anywhere.

## Reviewing someone else's deck

- **Give the arc verdict first, then the detail.** "The argument arrives on slide 22; move it to slide 6" is worth more than forty small notes.
- **Name the one slide to cut first.** Usually the history or the team.
- **Do not redesign it.** Point at the problem - "this chart has two y-axes and implies a link that is not there" - and hand the fix to the owner or to `graphic-designer`.
- **Ask what the room already believes.** A deck written for a sceptical room and one written for an aligned room are different decks.
- **Limit to five notes.** Ten notes get ignored; five get fixed.

## The final gate before it leaves

| Gate | Question |
|---|---|
| Audience | Is this the right deck type for this room? |
| Time | Does it fit the slot with two minutes spare? |
| Numbers | Does everything reconcile and carry a basis? |
| Claims | Is every claim sourced or removed? |
| Permission | Are names, logos and quotes cleared? |
| Format | Does it work printed, projected and read on a phone? |
| Ask | Is the decision unmistakable? |

## Anti-patterns

- **Polishing slide 40 while the arc is broken.** Order of work is arc, then titles, then evidence, then design.
- **A review that is a list of font complaints.** Design is the last 5%; the argument is the other 95%.
- **Approving a deck nobody rehearsed.**
- **Adding a slide to answer one person's question from the last meeting.** Put it in the appendix, or the deck grows every quarter.
- **Sending the deck before the ask is agreed with the person who has to give it.** The presenter must be able to say the ask without reading it.
