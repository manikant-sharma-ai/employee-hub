# Slide-by-slide craft

Every slide has one job: make the title believable. This file is the mechanics - titles, body, density, the ask.

## Titles are sentences

| Weak title | Why | Strong title |
|---|---|---|
| Financials | A label, not a claim | Revenue tripled while margin fell 9 points |
| Our product | Says nothing | One dashboard replaces the four spreadsheets the team runs today |
| Market opportunity | Generic | The addressable market is 14,000 workshops, not 400,000 |
| Team | A directory | Two of the three founders have run this playbook before |

Test: read only the titles, in order. If they form a coherent argument, the deck works. If they read like a table of contents, it does not.

## The body proves the title

Three acceptable body shapes:

1. **One number, large** - the figure the title claims, with a basis line underneath.
2. **One chart** - the comparison that makes the claim visible, with a one-line takeaway.
3. **Three to five short lines or a table** - a list of things the claim depends on, each under 12 words.

Everything else is an appendix slide or a document.

## Density budget

| Element | Limit | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Words in the body | 25 | Beyond that the audience reads instead of listening |
| Bullets | 5 | Six is a list, not a slide |
| Words per bullet | 12 | A bullet is a phrase, not a sentence |
| Charts per slide | 1 | Two charts means the audience reads neither |
| Font size, body | 28pt minimum | 24pt is invisible from row 8 |
| Font size, footnote | 20pt minimum | The basis line must be readable or it is decoration |
| Colours, meaningful | 3 | One for the point, one for the comparison, one for everything else |

## Layout patterns worth reusing

- **Title + one big number + basis line.** The most useful slide in business.
- **Title + chart + takeaway sentence under it.** The takeaway is what the presenter says, written so the deck survives being read cold.
- **Title + two-column comparison** - before/after, us/them, option A/option B, with the same criteria on both sides.
- **Title + three boxes**, each a pillar with a number attached. Use sparingly; three boxes is the most overused slide in the world.
- **Title + a single quote**, with the name and role under it. Powerful once per deck, unbearable twice.
- **Title + table**, when the audience needs to compare more than three things on more than two criteria. Tables are read, so keep them under 6 rows.

## Section breaks

A title-only slide with three words: "What it costs". It resets attention and costs one second. Use every 4-6 slides in a deck over 20 minutes.

## The ask slide

Four lines, in this order:

1. **What is being asked** - "Approve ₹1.2 crore of capex."
2. **What it buys** - "A 6,000 sq ft leased warehouse in Hoskote, racking and a 3-person team."
3. **What happens if approved** - "Live by 1 March, capacity for 40,000 orders a quarter."
4. **What happens next** - "Signature today; PO to the landlord by Friday."

No ask slide has ever been rejected for being too clear.

## Text that must never appear

- "Lorem ipsum", "TBD", "XXX", "[insert number]".
- "Confidential" on a slide that is already marked confidential in the footer.
- A competitor's logo used as a comparison without a source.
- A screenshot of a spreadsheet with real customer names in it.
- An arrow labelled "synergy".

## Anti-patterns

- **The wall of text moved from the document into 18pt.** That is a document with worse margins.
- **Animations that reveal the argument one bullet at a time**, forcing the audience to wait for the presenter. Use them only when the reveal is the point.
- **An icon for every bullet.** Icons do not add meaning; they add furniture.
- **A photo background behind body text.** Half the room cannot read it.
- **A slide with no title**, because "it's visual". Every slide makes a claim, even the visual one.
- **Numbering slides "1/62"** in a live deck. It tells the room how long they will be there.
