# Narrative arc

A presentation is an argument delivered in public. The audience is not reading; they are deciding whether to keep listening. The arc is what keeps them listening and what makes the decision easy at the end.

## The six beats

| Beat | Job | Usually 1 slide | The test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Situation | The world as the audience already believes it | 1 | Would they nod at this without explanation? |
| Complication | The thing that changed, or the gap that will not close | 1-2 | Does it create tension the audience wants resolved? |
| Question | The decision this forces | 1 | Is it a question they would actually ask? |
| Answer | Your recommendation, stated plainly | 1 | Could they repeat it after one hearing? |
| Evidence | The three to five things that make the answer credible | 3-6 | Is each one a fact, not a feeling? |
| Ask | The specific decision, with amount and next step | 1 | Is it a yes-or-no they can give today? |

The order matters and it is not the order people naturally write in. Most first drafts are all evidence and no complication - a pile of facts that leaves the audience asking "so what".

## The decision sentence

Write this before anything else, and keep it at the top of the outline:

> By the end of this deck, **<audience>** will **<approve / fund / hire / sign>** **<specific thing>**, because **<the one reason they will believe>**.

Examples:

- Weak: "Present the Q3 results to the board." (No decision.)
- Strong: "By the end of this deck, the board will approve ₹1.2 crore for a second Bengaluru warehouse, because the current one hits capacity in March and we are turning away 60 orders a week."

If the sentence has three "because" clauses, you have three decks.

## Finding the complication

The complication is the engine. Four reliable sources:

1. **The number that moved the wrong way** - margin, churn, days-to-close.
2. **The constraint that arrives on a date** - capacity, runway, a contract ending, a regulation starting.
3. **The competitor doing the thing you decided not to do** - and being right about it.
4. **The customer behaviour that contradicts the plan** - they buy the small pack, they do not use the feature you built.

A deck with no complication is an update. Updates are fine, but they should be one page, not a meeting.

## Structure patterns that work

| Pattern | Shape | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Situation-complication-resolution | As above | Board, investor, internal proposal |
| What / so what / now what | Three beats, repeated per section | All-hands, training, review |
| The objection-first deck | Open with the strongest reason to say no, then dismantle it | A sceptical room, a second pitch |
| Before / after / bridge | Current state, future state, the three moves between | Transformation, systems, reorg |
| The three options | Option A, B, C against the same criteria, then a recommendation | Decisions where the audience wants agency |

## Pacing

- **One beat per slide, and never two beats on one slide.** The complication and the answer on the same slide means the audience reads the answer first and never feels the problem.
- **The turn lands at about 30% of the time.** If the recommendation arrives at minute 12 of 15, the room has already formed a view.
- **Section breaks every 4-6 slides** for anything over 20 minutes - a title-only slide that says where we are.
- **The ask is never the last slide of a long deck.** Put it before the appendix so it is not a surprise during questions.

## Anti-patterns

- **Starting with the company history.** Nobody has ever approved a budget because of a founding story.
- **A "thank you" slide as the last slide.** The last slide should be the ask or the one sentence you want remembered.
- **The agenda slide as the first slide.** Replace it with the complication; an agenda tells the room the deck is going to be long.
- **Three recommendations.** One, with the alternatives named as rejected and why.
- **Building the arc around the org chart** instead of around the audience's decision.
- **Reusing last quarter's arc with new numbers.** The complication changes; check it.
