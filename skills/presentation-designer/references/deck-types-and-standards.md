# Deck types and standards

Each deck answers a different question for a different room. Building the wrong type is the commonest reason a good deck fails.

## The five types

| Type | The question it answers | Length | Ends with |
|---|---|---|---|
| Investor pitch | Why should we fund this, and why now? | 10-14 main slides | The raise, the use, the milestones |
| Board pack | Are we on track, and what needs a decision? | 12-20 + appendix | The two or three decisions |
| Sales deck | Why this, why us, why now - read without us? | 8-12 | The next step, with a date |
| All-hands | Where are we, what changes, what do I do? | 8-12 | What each person does on Monday |
| Internal proposal | Should we do this, and what does it cost? | 6-10 | The approval and the owner |

## Investor pitch

Required, in roughly this order: the problem in the buyer's words, the solution as a demonstration, the market with a bottom-up number, traction with a trend, the business model with unit economics, competition framed on the axis that matters, the team, the ask with use of funds and milestones.

| Standard | Detail |
|---|---|
| Market size | Bottom-up: number of buyers x price x realistic share. A top-down "1% of a $50B market" is a credibility loss |
| Traction | Six periods minimum, with the metric that proves retention |
| Unit economics | Contribution margin per unit, CAC, payback in months |
| Competition | A 2x2 on axes the buyer cares about, not on "features" |
| Ask | Amount, use split into three lines, what it buys in 18 months |

## Board pack

- **One page of headline numbers** first, against plan, with the variance explained in a sentence.
- **The bridge** from last period to this.
- **The three things that went wrong**, before the board asks.
- **Cash and runway**, with the assumption stated.
- **The decisions required**, each with the recommendation and the alternative.
- **Appendix** holding the detail nobody will read live but everyone will ask for.

## Sales deck

It will be read without a presenter, often forwarded to someone who was not in the room. So:

- Every slide carries enough context to stand alone.
- The customer's name and the numbers they agreed to be quoted on, with the date of approval.
- One case study, told properly, not five told in a line each.
- Pricing either present or explicitly "on request" - a deck that hides the price invites the wrong meeting.
- The last slide is a next step with a date, not "thank you".

## All-hands

- What changed since last time, in numbers people recognise.
- What we are doing about the one thing that is not working.
- What each team does next, in their own language.
- No slide that requires financial literacy to parse, unless the audience has it.

## Time and slide budget

| Slot | Slides | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 5 minutes | 3-4 | One argument, one piece of evidence, the ask |
| 10 minutes | 7-8 | The arc, compressed |
| 15 minutes | 10-11 | The standard pitch |
| 30 minutes | 14-18 | Room for the evidence and a discussion slide |
| 45+ minutes | 18-24 plus appendix | Anything longer is two sessions |

One and a half minutes per slide, and the last two minutes reserved for the ask and the first question. A deck that fills the whole slot has no time to be agreed with.

## Format decisions

- **Live or cold?** Live allows fewer words and a presenter; cold requires context lines.
- **Printed?** Then the deck must work in greyscale - check it, because half the board packs in India are printed.
- **Emailed as PDF?** Then the appendix matters more than the animation.
- **Projected on a bad projector in a small room?** Contrast matters more than subtlety; test at 60% brightness.

## Anti-patterns

- **One deck for every audience.** The board does not want the sales story; the investor does not want the operating detail.
- **A 90-slide "master deck" trimmed by hiding slides.** Hidden slides get shown. Build the version for the room.
- **The appendix used as a dumping ground for numbers the deck contradicts.**
- **A pitch deck with no ask** because "we'll discuss it live". It never gets discussed.
- **An all-hands deck written for the leadership team** rather than for the person in the third row who wants to know if their project survived.
