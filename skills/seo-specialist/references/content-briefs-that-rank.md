# Content briefs that rank

A brief is not an outline. An outline says what order the headings go in; a brief says what the page must do to deserve the position, and what would make it worse than the page currently in position one.

## What goes in a brief

| Block | Content | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Query and intent | The primary query, the intent, the cluster of secondary queries | SERP + Search Console |
| Audience | One sentence: who is reading, what they already know, what they are deciding | Sales calls, support tickets |
| The promise | What the page will let them do or decide by the end | Your judgement, agreed with the writer |
| Section order | H2s in the order the searcher needs them, each with the question it answers | PAA box, top results, gaps |
| Coverage list | Entities, terms, comparisons and numbers that must appear | Top five results, cross-checked |
| Internal links | 3-5 target pages with the anchor to use | Site crawl |
| Word-count band | Taken from the current top five, not from a rule | SERP |
| Proof needed | The numbers or examples the writer must get from the business | The person |
| What to leave out | The tangents the current top results go down and the searcher does not need | Your judgement |

## Deriving the section order

1. Read the People Also Ask box - each question is a section.
2. Read the top three results' H2s - the overlap is the table stakes, the difference is the gap.
3. Read the reviews or comments where available - the objection the content does not address is the section that wins.
4. Order the sections in the sequence a buyer would ask them, not in the sequence the writer finds convenient: what it is, what it costs, whether it applies to me, how to do it, what goes wrong, what to do next.

## Word count, honestly

| SERP average for the query | What it means | Target |
|---|---|---|
| 400-700 words | The query wants a quick answer | 600-900, with the answer in the first 60 words |
| 900-1,500 words | A practical guide | 1,200-1,800, sectioned for scanning |
| 2,000+ words | A reference page, usually competing on depth | Match the depth, not the padding |

Length is a consequence of coverage, never a target. A 2,000-word page that repeats itself ranks below a 900-word page that answers.

## The brief that a writer can actually use

Weak brief: "Write 1,500 words about GST filing, include keywords: GST filing, GST return, GSTR-3B, GST for small business. Make it SEO friendly."

Strong brief, same topic:

- **Query**: `GST filing for small business` (4,400/mo, informational-commercial), secondary `GSTR-3B due date`, `GST filing charges`.
- **Audience**: an owner of a 5-20 person trading business who has a CA and does not know what the CA does.
- **Promise**: by the end they know which returns apply to them, what the CA needs each month, and what it should cost.
- **Sections**: which returns apply to a trading business → the monthly calendar with dates → the five documents the CA will ask for → what it costs in 2026 and why quotes vary → the three mistakes that produce a notice → when to switch from a CA to software.
- **Coverage**: GSTR-1, GSTR-3B, GSTR-9, composition scheme threshold, late fee per day, e-invoice threshold. Name each; do not define the ones the reader will not meet.
- **Leave out**: history of GST, the constitutional amendment, anything about exports.
- **Links**: to /services/gst-filing (anchor: "GST filing support"), to /blog/invoice-format (anchor: "invoice format that passes GST scrutiny").
- **Proof**: the ₹1,500-4,000/month range from the business's own client base, the notice example from a real (anonymised) case.
- **Length**: 1,400-1,800 words.

## Handover rules

- **The brief goes to `content-writer`; the draft comes back for review.** Do not write the article.
- **Review against the brief, not against taste.** Did it answer each section's question, name each required entity, place each link?
- **One revision round is normal, three is a broken brief.** If the draft missed the point twice, the brief was vague - fix the brief.
- **Update the brief after publication** with what the page actually did, so the next brief in the cluster is better.

## Anti-patterns

- **Keyword-stuffed outlines.** "H2: GST filing for small business in India 2026 best" is not a heading a human writes or reads.
- **Copying the top result's structure exactly.** You will produce a slightly worse version of a page that already ranks.
- **Briefing a topic with no query behind it** because it fits the content calendar. That is brand content; it belongs to `content-writer` or `brand-strategist`, not to search.
- **Setting a word count before reading the SERP.**
- **Forgetting the internal links.** The most common reason a good article gets no traffic is that nothing links to it.
