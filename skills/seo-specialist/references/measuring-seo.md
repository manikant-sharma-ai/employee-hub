# Measuring SEO

Search work is slow, cumulative and easy to lie about. This file is how to report it in a way that survives a sceptical founder and a bad month.

## The four numbers

| Metric | Where it comes from | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| Impressions | Search Console, Performance, by query | Whether the page is eligible for the query at all |
| Clicks and CTR | Search Console, by query and page | Whether the title and description earn the click |
| Average position | Search Console, by query | Whether the page is moving, and where the ceiling is |
| Conversions from organic | Analytics, or the CRM's source field | Whether any of it became money |

Read them in that order. Impressions rising with clicks flat means the title is the problem. Clicks rising with conversions flat means the page or the offer is the problem. Nothing moving at all usually means the query was never winnable.

## The four-week rule

- **Do not judge a change before four weeks.** Crawling, indexing and re-ranking take time; a two-day panic produces a revert that destroys the learning.
- **Do not judge on one keyword.** Judge on the cluster's total impressions and clicks.
- **Do not judge in a festival month or a financial-year-end month** without noting it. Seasonality is larger than most on-page changes.
- **Do judge a technical fix faster** - an indexing fix shows up in days, a content change in weeks.

## The monthly report, in one page

1. **Cluster table** - cluster, queries, impressions this month vs last, clicks, average position, and the change.
2. **What changed** - the dated list of changes made, so movement can be attributed.
3. **The three pages that moved most, up and down**, with the likely reason.
4. **The queries at position 5-20** - the next month's work list.
5. **What was not measured** - the connectors missing, the conversions not tracked.
6. **One recommendation**, not seven.

## Attribution honesty

- **Organic search is a multi-touch channel.** A visitor who searched, read and then bought a week later via a WhatsApp message will not appear in organic conversions. Say so rather than claiming zero.
- **Direct traffic is often organic.** Brand searches and dark-social shares land in "direct". Do not credit organic with it, and do not let anyone else either.
- **"Not provided" exists** in analytics; Search Console is the query-level truth.
- **A ranking is not revenue.** Report the ranking, then separately report whether enquiries moved, and label the link between them as a hypothesis unless it is measured.

## What you cannot do without a connector

| Missing | What you cannot claim |
|---|---|
| Search Console | Any current position, impression or click number. You can read the SERP, but not their standing in it |
| Analytics | That a visit did or did not convert |
| CMS | That a title, description or heading change is live |
| A crawl tool | That a page is indexed, or that internal links exist as described |

Say which of these is missing before the numbers, not after.

## Setting the review date

Every deliverable carries a review date in the form `review on <date>, judge on <metric>`. Example: "Review on 15 Dec 2026; judge on cluster impressions and enquiries from /services/kitchen."

A deliverable without a review date becomes an opinion by the following month.

## Anti-patterns

- **Reporting position changes to one decimal place.** Position 8.3 to 8.1 is noise.
- **A dashboard nobody opens.** One page a month, with a recommendation, beats a live dashboard.
- **Comparing this month to last month without last year.** Seasonality in India is large enough to invert the story.
- **Claiming credit for a lift that coincided with a brand campaign or a festival.** Note the confound.
- **Never reporting a drop.** The month a page loses 40% is the month the report matters most.
