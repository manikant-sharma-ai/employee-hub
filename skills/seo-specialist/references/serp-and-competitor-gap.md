# SERP and competitor gap

Two questions, and they are different: can this site win this query at all, and if not this query, which adjacent one can it win? This file is the arithmetic behind both.

## Reading a results page properly

Record this for the primary query before recommending anything:

| Field | What to note |
|---|---|
| Positions 1-3 | Type of page, domain, whether it is a marketplace or a brand |
| Positions 4-10 | Type of page, whether any are small sites |
| SERP features | Snippet, PAA, local pack, images, video, shopping, top stories |
| Ad density | How many ads above the first organic result |
| Freshness | Dates on the top three |
| Language | English, Hinglish, regional script |

If positions 1-6 are marketplaces, directories and brands, a 20-page site is not entering this SERP this year, and saying so is the most valuable sentence in the report.

## The winnability arithmetic

You need three numbers per query, and rough is fine:

1. **Referring domains of the weakest page in the top five.** That is the bar.
2. **This site's referring domains.** If the gap is more than 10x, the query needs authority, not content.
3. **Content depth of the top five** - do they answer the query completely? A thin top five is the opening.

| Situation | Read |
|---|---|
| Weakest top-five page is thin and low-authority | Win it with a better page in one quarter |
| Top five are all strong brands, all thin | Win it with depth, slowly, if the intent is valuable |
| Top five are all marketplaces | Do not compete; consider being present on the marketplaces instead |
| Top five are all one competitor's pages | A niche they own; attack the sub-query, not the head term |

## Gap analysis that produces decisions

Four gaps worth naming, in order of return:

1. **Query gap** - a query with demand where this site has no page. The cheapest new page you can write.
2. **Coverage gap** - this site has the page, and it answers half the questions in the PAA box. A refresh, not a new page.
3. **Format gap** - the SERP wants a comparison table, a calculator or a video, and this site has an essay. Format is often the whole difference.
4. **Depth gap** - this site has the page and it is thinner than the weakest result ranking. A rewrite with the missing sections.

## Cannibalisation: the quiet killer

Two pages targeting the same query split internal links, split signals, and alternate in the rankings, which looks like instability and is actually self-competition.

Detect it:

- Search Console, filter by query, look for two or more URLs receiving impressions for the same query.
- Site search or a crawl for two pages with near-identical titles or H1s.
- A blog post and a service page both written around the same phrase.

Fix it, in this order of preference:

1. **Merge** the weaker into the stronger and redirect, keeping the better content.
2. **Differentiate** - narrow each page to a distinct sub-intent and re-title both.
3. **Canonicalise** the weaker to the stronger if the content genuinely duplicates and merging is not possible.

Never leave it: two half-ranking pages look identical to Google to having none.

## Refresh before you publish

Before briefing a new page, check whether the query is already served by something on the site. Refreshing a page at position 8 usually beats publishing a new one at position 60, because the existing page already has history, links and crawl attention.

A refresh means: add the missing sections, update the numbers and dates, fix the title and description, add internal links in, and re-publish with a visible update date.

## When search is the wrong answer

Say it plainly when:

- The market searches on a marketplace, not on Google - many B2B categories in India run on IndiaMART and GeM, and a blog will not reach that buyer.
- The buying decision is made in a WhatsApp group, a trade association or a referral network.
- The category has under 200 monthly searches in total, in which case the page exists for credibility, not traffic - and should be briefed as such.

## Anti-patterns

- **Reporting a "content gap" from a tool's list without opening the SERP.** Tools compare strings; buyers compare answers.
- **Targeting a competitor's brand name as a strategy.** Trademark risk, poor conversion, and usually a legal letter.
- **Publishing five pages to attack one SERP.** One strong page beats five weak ones and does not split the signal.
- **Ignoring the local pack.** For any "near me" or city query in India, the map result sits above everything you can influence with on-page work.
- **Judging a SERP once.** Check it again at the start of any refresh; intent drifts.
