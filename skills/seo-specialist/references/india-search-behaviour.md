# India search behaviour

The same query behaves differently here than in the English-language SEO playbooks. This file is the local reality that decides whether a page is written for the person who is actually searching.

## Language and script

| Query form | Example | What it means for the page |
|---|---|---|
| English | `modular kitchen price bangalore` | Write in English, price-led, city-named |
| Hinglish, romanised | `kitchen ka price kya hai` | Answer in Hinglish in the body; do not translate the whole page |
| Devanagari or regional script | `रसोई का दाम` / `சமையலறை விலை` | A separate page in that language, not a machine-translated variant of the English one |
| Mixed | `GST return kaise bhare` | The FAQ section carries the Hinglish phrasing; the main body stays in the business's register |

Rules that follow:

- **Match the register, do not translate it.** A Hinglish query answered in formal Hindi reads like a government form.
- **One page per language, with its own URL and its own keywords.** Machine translation without local review produces pages that rank for nothing and read badly to a buyer.
- **Spelling variants are real demand.** `bangalore` and `bengaluru`, `cochin` and `kochi`, `gurgaon` and `gurugram` - cover both in the body, not both in the title.

## The intent shapes that dominate Indian search

- **Price-first.** A large share of commercial queries carry `price`, `cost`, `rate`, `per sq ft`, `charges`. A page without a number loses to one with a range.
- **"Near me" and city-modified.** Local intent is strong and the map pack takes the top of the page. Google Business Profile completeness matters more than a blog post for these.
- **"Best X in <city>" listicles.** Review sites and aggregators dominate. Compete only with a genuinely better comparison page.
- **"How to" in the buyer's own words.** Often phrased as a problem, not a topic: `GST notice aaya hai kya karein`.
- **Comparison queries** - `vs`, `or`, `which is better`. High value, low volume, easy to win with a real table.
- **WhatsApp-shaped queries** - `whatsapp par order`, `whatsapp business catalogue`. Signals that the buying conversation happens in chat, which changes the call to action.

## Local search: the part that outranks content

For any business with a physical presence or a service area:

- **Google Business Profile** - category, service area, hours, photos, and a weekly post. The local pack is decided mostly here.
- **Reviews** - volume, recency and replies. A page of 40 unanswered reviews converts worse than 15 answered ones.
- **NAP consistency** - name, address, phone identical across the site, the profile and the directories.
- **City pages that are genuinely different** - not the same page with the city swapped. If the content is identical, do not publish the page.
- **JustDial, IndiaMART, Sulekha, trade directories** - presence there often beats an organic listing for local service queries, and it is a decision to make consciously, not to ignore.

## Seasonality to plan around

| Window | Effect |
|---|---|
| Jan-Feb | Planning and new-year budget queries; B2B research peaks |
| Mar-Apr | Financial year end - tax, filing, closing queries spike |
| Aug-Oct | Festival season - gifting, décor, apparel, electronics; ad costs rise and organic is the cheaper channel |
| Oct-Nov | Diwali-led demand, then a dip; B2B slows |
| Nov-Dec | Wedding season in many regions; venue, apparel, jewellery queries |

Plan refreshes two months before the window, not during it. A page published in October does not rank for Diwali.

## Mobile-first realities

- Most of the traffic is on a phone, often on a mid-range Android device on a 4G connection.
- Page weight and image size decide whether the page is read at all - hand the technical fix to `seo-technical`, but flag it.
- The call to action must work without an app install: a phone number, a WhatsApp link, a form with three fields.
- Tables must be readable at 360px width, or they will not be read.

## Anti-patterns

- **A single English page for a pan-India market and calling it localisation.**
- **Publishing 40 city pages from one template.** Thin duplicates, and they compete with each other.
- **Ignoring the map pack because it is "not SEO".** For local intent it is most of the click.
- **Machine-translating a page into six languages with no native review.** It ranks nowhere and damages trust where it is read.
- **Assuming tool volume is right for long-tail Indian queries.** Cross-check with Search Console impressions before building a page around a number.
