# On-page fixes

On-page work is the cheapest part of SEO and the most frequently neglected. Every item here is checkable in under a minute per page, and every one of them has a correct answer that does not depend on opinion.

## The twelve-point checklist

| # | Element | Rule | Common failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title tag | Under 60 characters, primary query early, one reason to click | Brand name first, query last, truncated at 60 |
| 2 | Meta description | 140-155 characters, states the outcome and the proof | Auto-generated from the first paragraph |
| 3 | H1 | One per page, matches the query's promise, not the brand's slogan | Two H1s, or an H1 that says "Welcome" |
| 4 | Heading order | H2s are the sections of the answer, H3s nest under them | Headings chosen for font size |
| 5 | First 60 words | Answer the query's literal question | "In today's fast-paced world..." |
| 6 | Body coverage | Every sub-question in the PAA box is answered | 300 words that restate the title |
| 7 | Internal links | 2-4 links in, from pages with traffic; 2-3 links out, to the money page | Orphan pages, or links only in the footer |
| 8 | Anchor text | Describes the destination | "click here", "read more" |
| 9 | Image alt text | Describes the image; not a keyword dump | Empty alt, or the filename |
| 10 | URL | Short, readable, stable | `/2024/03/untitled-2-final-v3/` |
| 11 | Canonical | Self-referencing on the canonical version | Missing, or pointing at the homepage |
| 12 | Schema | Product, FAQ, LocalBusiness, Article - only where the content matches | Markup describing things that are not on the page |

## Title tags: the formula that survives a rewrite

Three working shapes:

- `Primary query - specific qualifier | Brand` → `Modular kitchen cost in Bengaluru - 2026 price guide | Kitchen Co`
- `How to <outcome> without <the feared cost>` → `How to file GSTR-3B without an accountant`
- `Best <category> for <buyer> in <place>` → `Best CRM for small manufacturers in Coimbatore`

Rules that are not negotiable:

- The query appears in the first 35 characters, because that is what survives a mobile truncation.
- No two pages share a title. Run a site-wide duplicate check before writing any new one.
- No year in the title unless the page is genuinely maintained; a 2023 title in 2026 signals abandonment.
- No "Home", no "Welcome", no "Official Site" unless the brand is being impersonated.

## Descriptions: the only ad copy most sites ever write

A description does not affect ranking and does affect the click. It has one job: give a reason to choose this result over the nine below it.

| Weak | Why | Strong |
|---|---|---|
| "We are a leading provider of modular kitchen solutions." | Every competitor says it | "See 12 real kitchens with prices from ₹1.8 lakh, installed in 21 days in Bengaluru." |
| "Contact us for more information." | Asks the searcher to do work | "Get a costed layout in 48 hours - no showroom visit needed." |

## Internal links: the part everyone skips

- **Link from pages that already have traffic.** A link from a page with 2,000 monthly visits is worth more than fifty from pages with none.
- **Link into the new page within a week of publishing.** An unlinked page is an orphan and may not be crawled for months.
- **Three to five contextual links per page**, in the body, with descriptive anchors.
- **Link up to the money page** from every informational page that could plausibly lead to a purchase. This is the whole commercial point of a blog.
- **Never sitewide-footer a link and call it internal linking.** It is a template, not a recommendation.

## Anti-patterns

- **Changing twenty things on one page in one day.** You learn nothing, and if traffic drops you cannot tell why.
- **Keyword stuffing the title with three phrases.** One query, one title.
- **Rewriting every title on the site in a batch because a tool flagged them.** Flag first, prioritise by impressions, then change in two-week waves.
- **Adding FAQ schema for questions that are not on the page.** It is a policy violation, not a trick.
- **Hiding text in white, in a div, or behind a click that a crawler cannot make.** Old tactics, still penalised.
- **Ignoring the mobile rendering.** The majority of Indian search traffic is mobile; check the page on a phone before signing it off.
