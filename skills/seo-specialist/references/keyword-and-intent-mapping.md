# Keyword and intent mapping

The query is a promise the searcher made to themselves. The page either keeps it or is bounced. Everything in this file exists to make you read the promise correctly before anyone writes a word.

## The four intents

| Intent | What the searcher wants | SERP signal | Page that wins |
|---|---|---|---|
| Informational | To understand something | Featured snippet, People Also Ask, long guides, YouTube | Guide with clear sections and a direct answer in the first 60 words |
| Navigational | A specific site or brand | The brand's own pages dominate | The brand's own page, correctly titled |
| Commercial | To compare before deciding | Listicles, "best X", review sites, comparison tables | Comparison page with criteria, prices where possible, and a recommendation |
| Transactional | To buy, book or download now | Product pages, marketplace listings, local pack, ads everywhere | The product or service page, with price, availability and one action |

The intent is decided by the results page, not by the words. "Custom furniture" looks informational; if the results page is nine furniture stores and a local pack, it is commercial and a guide will not rank.

## Reading the promise off the SERP

Open the query in a private window with location set to the market. Record:

1. **What kind of pages rank** - guides, product pages, marketplaces, forums, videos. The mix is the answer.
2. **Who ranks** - brands, directories, marketplaces, small blogs. If positions 1-6 are marketplaces and directories, a small site is not winning this query today.
3. **The SERP features** - snippet, PAA, local pack, shopping, video, images, sitelinks. A feature occupying the top of the page changes what a position is worth.
4. **The freshness** - dates on the top results. A SERP full of 2026 pages will not reward a 2022 page.
5. **The language and script** of the top results - English, Hinglish, Devanagari, Tamil. Match the market, not the office.

## From seed to cluster

1. **Seed** - five phrases the buyer would actually type, taken from sales calls, support tickets and Search Console queries, not from imagination.
2. **Expand** - modifier sets: `how to`, `best`, `price`, `near me`, `vs`, `for small business`, `in <city>`, `without <objection>`.
3. **Group by intent, not by string similarity.** "GST filing software" and "GST return last date" share words and share nothing else.
4. **One page per cluster.** A cluster is a set of queries that a single page could satisfy in one sitting. If two queries need two different answers, they are two pages.
5. **Name the cluster** with the query that has the best combination of volume and intent, and write it at the top of the brief.

## Scoring winnability

Do not use a single "difficulty" number from a tool and call it a decision. Score three things:

| Factor | Question | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Demand | Does this query get searched in the market, at this location, in this language? | High |
| Intent value | If they land here, can they become a customer from this page? | High |
| Winnability | Can this site be in the top five in two quarters? Compare referring domains and content depth of positions 1-5 against this site | High |

A query scoring high on all three is the target. High demand and high value with low winnability is a project for next year, not this quarter. High winnability with no value is a traffic vanity metric - and it is what most keyword lists are made of.

## Where the best queries actually come from

In order of value per hour spent:

1. **Search Console, position 5-20, high impressions, low clicks.** Already indexed, already relevant, one improvement from traffic. This is where 80% of early wins live.
2. **Search Console, zero clicks, growing impressions.** The page is appearing for a query it was not written for - a rewrite or a new page decision.
3. **Sales call language.** The exact phrasing a buyer used on a call, if it also has volume.
4. **Support tickets and WhatsApp questions.** Repeated questions are repeated searches.
5. **Competitor pages that rank and are thin.** A gap you can beat on quality rather than authority.
6. **Tool keyword exports.** Last, because volume estimates for Indian long-tail queries are frequently wrong by an order of magnitude.

## Anti-patterns

- **Building a list of 400 keywords and calling it a strategy.** A strategy is eight clusters, a page each, and a sequence.
- **Targeting a query whose top results are all marketplaces.** You will spend a quarter learning that Amazon wins.
- **Choosing a head term because it looks impressive in a report.** "Furniture" is not a business.
- **Ignoring zero-volume long-tail queries.** In India, tool volume for Hinglish and city-modified queries is often reported as zero and searched daily.
- **Mapping intent once and never re-checking.** SERPs change intent over time as Google learns what satisfies the query.
