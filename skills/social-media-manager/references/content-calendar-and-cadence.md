# The calendar and the cadence

A calendar is a promise about the next four weeks. Build it before the week, not
during it, or the account becomes whatever was in the founder's head that morning.

## The shape of a month

| Layer | How many | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Pillars | 2-3 per month | The themes the account is about. "What the product is made of", "who wears it", "how to care for it". |
| Posts per week | 3-5 | Below 3 the account looks dormant; above 5 the quality falls before the reach rises. |
| Formats per week | At least 2 | One video, one static or carousel. A feed of only posters reads as an advertisement. |
| Series | 1 | A repeating slot people can expect: "Monday care tip", "customer of the week". Series build return visits. |
| Campaign | 1 | Tied to an offer or a date. Everything else supports it. |

## The one-goal rule

Pick one measurable goal for the month and say what a good month looks like:
"20 enquiries from Instagram DMs", "300 saves on the care posts", "40 applications".
Followers are a vanity goal unless the business sells attention.

Then check each slot against it: does this post move that number? If not, cut the
slot. A calendar of eight posts where two move the goal is worse than three posts
that all do.

## Building it in one sitting

1. **Dates first.** Festivals, paydays, sale dates, product drops, school holidays. Mark the dead days too - a Sunday evening launch is wasted.
2. **Then pillars.** Two or three themes, in the customer's words, not the brand's.
3. **Then the material.** Match existing assets to pillars. Anything without an asset becomes a shoot or a founder voice note, listed as a task.
4. **Then the hooks.** Write the first visible line for each slot before writing the caption. If the hook is boring, the slot is not ready.
5. **Then the approval.** One approver, one deadline, two rounds maximum.

## Cadence that survives

- **Batch one day a week.** Produce all the week's assets and copy in one session; daily production is how accounts die.
- **Keep a two-week buffer.** A buffer is what makes a sick day or a launch not break the account.
- **Have a filler set.** Three evergreen posts (care instructions, sizing, a customer story) that can fill a slot without thought.
- **Never schedule the whole month at once.** The last week of a month planned 30 days earlier is always off - prices move, offers change.

## What to do when there is nothing to post

The honest sequence: ask for a shoot list, ask the founder for three voice notes
about what customers keep asking, ask for the last ten support questions. The
answers are the content. Filler quotes about "believing in yourself" cost more
credibility than a quiet week.

## Anti-patterns

- A calendar with no owner for the assets.
- Seven slots a week and one person approving at 11pm.
- A "content bank" of 40 generic posts that never get used.
- Copying a competitor's calendar. Their customers are not yours.
- Scheduling festival posts a year in advance and getting the dates wrong.
