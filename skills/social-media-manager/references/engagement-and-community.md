# Engagement and community

The replies are where the account earns or loses the business. Most accounts plan
the posts and improvise the comments; that is backwards.

## Write the rules before the replies

| Message type | Who answers | Window | Never |
|---|---|---|---|
| Price question | Social, with the published price | Same day | Inventing a discount |
| "Is it available?" | Social, with the real stock answer | Same day | Promising a delivery date |
| Complaint about an order | support-agent, moved to DM | Within 2 hours | Arguing publicly |
| Abuse, threat | Hide or block per rule, log it | Immediately | Replying in kind |
| Legal threat, refund demand | Escalate to the owner | Immediately | Any commitment |
| Genuine praise | Reply once, warmly, briefly | Same day | Turning it into a testimonial without permission |
| Spam, bot | Delete | - | Engaging |

## Reply craft

- **Answer the question in the first line.** "Yes, ₹499 for the 500ml" beats "Thank you so much for reaching out to us, we truly value..."
- **One question back at most.** A reply that asks three questions gets none answered.
- **Use the customer's word for the product**, not the catalogue name, unless the catalogue name is the point.
- **No template smell.** Vary the opening; identical replies on every comment read as a bot and get flagged.
- **Own the mistake plainly.** "You are right, that was our error, here is what we are doing" ends threads; a defensive reply extends them.

## DM triage

Run DMs twice a day, in batches, with the same order: complaints, money questions,
order status, everything else. Anything that needs an order lookup is support-agent's
job, and it needs the order ID - ask for it in one message, not three.

## Community, not audience

- **Ask, do not broadcast.** One question per week in stories produces more signal than a month of posts.
- **Name regulars.** A customer who comments weekly is worth more than a follower who never does.
- **UGC with permission only.** Ask in writing; keep the reply as the record.
- **Never buy followers, engagement pods or comment farms.** The numbers stop meaning anything and the platform's reach model punishes it.

## What to log

Daily: volume by type, anything escalated, anything that needed a policy decision.
Weekly: the three questions that keep repeating - those become posts, FAQs and
eventually product fixes. That list is the most valuable thing this role produces
for the rest of the business.

## Anti-patterns

- Replying only to praise.
- Hiding a legitimate complaint to keep the feed clean. The customer screenshots it.
- A "social media executive" with no authority to answer a price question, so every DM waits a day.
- Answering a refund demand with "our team will get back to you" and no date.
