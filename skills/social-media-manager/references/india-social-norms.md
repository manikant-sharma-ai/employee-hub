# India-specific social norms

What reads as normal to a customer in India, and what reads as foreign, careless
or offensive. Not rules of law - rules of reading the room.

## Language

- **Hinglish is a register, not laziness.** "Aaj ka offer - sirf 24 hours" works for a metro D2C brand and fails for a B2B exporter. Match the account's existing voice; do not introduce a register the audience has never seen from you.
- **Script matters.** Devanagari, Tamil, Bengali or Telugu script signals respect to some audiences and signals "mass market" to others. Look at how the brand writes its packaging first.
- **Do not machine-translate a caption and post it.** See `translator-localiser` for the craft; a wrong idiom is remembered longer than a good offer.
- **English-only is a choice.** For tier-2 and tier-3 audiences it costs reach. Say so when the brief assumes otherwise.

## Festival and date calendar

- **Check the date for this year.** Diwali, Holi, Eid, Onam, Pongal, Ganesh Chaturthi, Navratri, Baisakhi, Christmas move against the Gregorian calendar. Last year's calendar is wrong this year.
- **Regional festivals are not national.** Onam is not a pan-India moment; Pongal is not Diwali. A "pan-India festival post" often reads as a brand that does not know its market.
- **Muhurat and auspicious timing** matter for jewellery, property, vehicles and weddings. Post before, not during.
- **National days and observances**: Republic Day, Independence Day and Gandhi Jayanti carry tone rules - avoid discount-led creative on these days.

## Money and offer conventions

- **₹ before the number, no decimals on round prices.** "₹499", not "499 INR" or "Rs. 499/-".
- **Lakh and crore, not millions.** Your customer says "1 lakh", not "100K".
- **"MRP" and strikethrough pricing** carry legal meaning under Indian consumer rules - the struck price must be a real price the product was sold at.
- **Cash on delivery, free delivery thresholds and return windows** are the questions that decide the sale. Put them in the caption or the first reply.
- **EMI, UPI and COD** mention converts better than a percentage-off badge for considered purchases.

## Tone and boundaries

- **Family-forward, not irony-forward.** Sarcasm and self-deprecation land badly with a broad Indian audience; warmth lands.
- **Be careful with religious imagery, deities, and national symbols.** Never on a product or an offer.
- **Skin tone, fairness, body commentary** - never, in any wording, even as a joke.
- **Regional pride** is safe to celebrate; regional stereotypes are not.
- **Political positioning** is a business decision, not a social media one. Escalate it.

## WhatsApp

- **The default channel for real customer conversation.** Status is personal, broadcast lists need opt-in, and template messages need Meta approval - plan a week.
- **Never add a customer to a broadcast list because they bought once.** Consent first, and an easy exit in every message.

## Anti-patterns

- A "Happy Diwali" post with the wrong date, or an image of the wrong festival.
- Using a foreign festival as a sale hook without local meaning.
- English caption, Hindi comments, no one answering in Hindi.
- A price in dollars on an Indian account.
