# Analytics and reporting

The month's read is the deliverable that keeps the job. Without it, the account is
a cost centre and the first budget cut closes it.

## The four numbers, and what each actually means

| Metric | Reads as | Misleads when |
|---|---|---|
| Reach | How many feeds saw it | It rises with paid spend and says nothing about intent |
| Saves / shares | The strongest organic signal | Rare on B2B accounts; do not judge a LinkedIn page by saves |
| Profile visits / link clicks | Intent to act | A high click with no enquiry means the destination is wrong |
| Enquiries / DMs / leads | The business outcome | Only if someone is actually logging them |

Impressions and follower count belong in an appendix, if anywhere.

## The monthly read, one page

1. **What went out**: posts published against planned, and the reason for any gap.
2. **Top three and bottom three**, with the hook and the format, not just the topic.
3. **One pattern** that repeats across the winners - a format, a hook type, a day.
4. **The cost**, if there was any paid spend, and cost per enquiry.
5. **One change for next month**, and the number it should move.

## Reading the platform's own data honestly

- **Reach is not unique people.** Never add reach across posts to claim an audience.
- **Watch time and completion rate** are the real reel metrics; likes follow them.
- **Follower growth is a lagging indicator.** Post-level performance tells you what to do next week.
- **Check the drop-off graph**, not the average. It shows the second where people left.
- **Screenshot the numbers** when you report them; platform dashboards change and the number becomes unarguable.

## Attribution for a small business

Pixels lie and last-click lies more. The honest method for a business under about
₹50 lakh turnover: one question at intake ("how did you find us?"), a UTM on every
link, and a WhatsApp/DM log. Three sources that agree are worth more than a
dashboard nobody trusts.

## What to do with a bad month

Do not explain it away. Say the number, name the likely cause, and state the test
for next month. An owner forgives a bad month with a diagnosis; they do not forgive
a report that hides it.

## Anti-patterns

- A 20-slide report nobody reads.
- Reporting reach as if it were sales.
- Comparing this month to a festival month without saying so.
- Claiming growth from a boosted post without subtracting the spend.
- No log of enquiries, so the channel's value is a feeling.
