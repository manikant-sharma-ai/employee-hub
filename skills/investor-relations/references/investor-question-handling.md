# Investor question handling

Investors ask the same fifteen questions, in the same order, for years. The answer
should be written once, from the same numbers, and kept current - not composed
afresh at 11 pm before a call.

## The question bank

| Question | What they are really testing | The answer that works |
|---|---|---|
| "What is your runway?" | Whether you will need money before the next milestone | Cash ÷ trailing three-month burn, in months, with the milestone date |
| "How is growth?" | Whether the engine works | Absolute revenue, MoM and YoY, and the driver |
| "What is churn?" | Whether the revenue is real | Logo churn and revenue churn, separately, with the definition |
| "What is CAC and payback?" | Whether growth is efficient | Blended and channel-level, with the payback months |
| "Who is the competition?" | Whether you understand the market | Two real names, the axis of difference, no dismissal |
| "What is the biggest risk?" | Whether you are honest | The real one, with the mitigation and the date |
| "Why is the burn up?" | Whether spending is controlled | The line items, and what they buy |
| "When do you break even?" | Whether the plan is credible | A quarter, with the two assumptions that decide it |
| "What would you do with more money?" | Whether the next round is planned | Two priorities, ranked, with what each unlocks |
| "How is the team?" | Key-person risk | Named roles, gaps, and the hiring plan with dates |
| "Are you talking to other investors?" | Round dynamics | The truth, at the level you are willing to be quoted |
| "What has surprised you?" | Self-awareness | A real surprise and what changed because of it |
| "What can I do to help?" | Engagement | One specific, answerable ask |
| "How is <big customer>?" | Concentration risk | The share of revenue, the contract term, the renewal date |
| "When will you raise next?" | Planning | A window and the milestone that triggers it, not a promise |

## The one-page answer sheet

Maintain a single document, updated monthly, with the answer to each question in
three lines: the number, the source, the caveat. Two investors must never get
different figures for the same question in the same month.

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| Question | Runway |
| Number | 17 months |
| Source | Cash ₹2.04 crore (bank, 31 Oct) ÷ ₹12 lakh average net burn (Aug-Oct) |
| Caveat | Excludes the ₹40 lakh receivable due in December |

## Answering well

- **Lead with the number.** Context after; investors interrupt a preamble.
- **Name the definition** the first time a metric appears, then use it consistently.
- **Separate what you know from what you think.** "Revenue is ₹42.6 lakh; I expect ₹50 lakh in December because two contracts are signed" is two different claims.
- **If you do not know, say so and give a date.** "I do not have the channel-level CAC to hand; I will send it by Friday." Then send it.
- **Never answer a question about another investor.** "That is theirs to share" is a complete answer.
- **Never speculate about valuation or exit.** Route it: "That is a conversation for when we raise; today the focus is X."

## The awkward questions

| Question | The trap | The way through |
|---|---|---|
| "Are you going to run out of money?" | Reassurance without arithmetic | The runway number, the plan, the date it is reviewed |
| "Why did <senior person> leave?" | Blame or spin | The fact, the coverage, the replacement timeline |
| "Is this the same metric as last quarter?" | A definition changed quietly | Say the change, restate the old number on the new definition |
| "Why is this month worse than you said?" | Defensiveness | The cause, the impact on the plan, the correction |
| "Would you sell at this price?" | A commitment | "We are building; if that changes, you will hear it from me first." |

## The question log

| Date | Investor | Question | Answer given | Source | Owed by | Owed date |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

The log does three jobs: it keeps answers consistent, it makes the follow-up
visible, and it becomes the FAQ for the next round.

## Consistency across the base

- **One metrics workbook**, one definition per metric, versioned.
- **One answer sheet**, updated before the first call of the month.
- **One log**, so a promise made on a call is not lost.
- **A monthly check**: three random investors, same question, same answer.

## Anti-patterns

- **Different numbers to different investors.** It is discovered, usually in a group chat.
- **Answering a metric you cannot source.** "Approximately" is remembered as a fact.
- **Deflecting the risk question.** The investor already knows; deflection confirms the worry.
- **A 40-slide data dump in reply to one question.** Answer the question.
- **Promising a follow-up you do not send.** It becomes the only thing they remember.
- **Discussing one investor's position with another.**
