# Bad news and governance

The test of an investor relationship is not the good month. It is the month
something breaks and the founder tells them first.

## The rule

**Material news reaches the lead investor before it reaches anyone else, in
writing, with a plan.** Material means: a lost key customer, a departure of a
senior person, a missed milestone by more than a month, a cash problem, a legal
notice, a security incident, a regulatory action, or a founder's own departure.

## The note, in four paragraphs

1. **The fact, in the first sentence.** "We lost our largest customer, 31% of
   revenue, effective 30 November."
2. **What happened, without euphemism.** Two or three sentences of cause.
3. **What we are doing, with dates.** The plan, the owner, the checkpoints.
4. **What it does to the plan and the runway.** The revised number, and whether
   the fundraise timing moves.

Under 250 words. The lead will call; the note exists so the call starts from the
facts rather than from the discovery.

## Timing

| Event | Tell the lead within | Tell all investors within |
|---|---|---|
| Lost key customer | 24 hours of knowing | The monthly update |
| Senior departure | 24 hours | The monthly update |
| Missed milestone over a month | 48 hours | The monthly update |
| Cash below 6 months | Immediately | Immediately, with the plan |
| Legal notice received | Immediately, and the lawyer the same day | As the lawyer advises |
| Security or data incident | Immediately | As the lawyer and the privacy position require |
| Founder health or availability | As soon as it affects the business | With the founder's consent |

## What not to do

- **Wait for the monthly update** because it is only two weeks away. Two weeks of silence after bad news is remembered as concealment.
- **Bury it in paragraph six** of an otherwise cheerful note. It reads as spin, and it is worse than the news.
- **Over-promise the recovery.** "We will replace the revenue by January" is a second piece of bad news waiting.
- **Blame a customer, an employee or a market.** Investors read blame as an absence of ownership.
- **Tell one investor and not the others.** Word travels, and the exclusion is the injury.

## Governance basics this role keeps straight

| Item | Cadence | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Board meeting | Quarterly | Founder, with `board-reporter` |
| Shareholder resolutions | On event | Company secretary |
| Statutory filings (AOC-4, MGT-7, DIR-3 KYC) | Annual, with penalties for delay | Company secretary and CA |
| Register of members | On every transfer or issue | Company secretary |
| ESOP grants and board approvals | On grant | Founder and CS |
| Related-party disclosures | On event | Founder and CA |
| Investor consent rights | On the events the SHA names | Founder, with the lawyer |

This role does not file anything and does not opine. It keeps the calendar, asks
the question, and makes sure the person who files knows the date.

## Consent rights, in plain language

The SHA usually names a list of things a company cannot do without investor
consent: a new round, a debt above a limit, a related-party transaction, a change
of business, a sale of assets, a founder's share transfer. Know the list, and
check it before the founder agrees to anything in principle - not after.

## When an investor behaves badly

- **A demand outside the SHA**: answer in writing, restate what the document says, copy the founder.
- **Pressure for information beyond their rights**: give what the SHA and the law require, and say what you are not giving.
- **A public comment about the company**: route to the founder and `pr-writer`; do not respond in kind.
- **A conflict of interest** - an investor backing a competitor: the founder decides, in writing, and the board is informed.

## The annual letter

Once a year, a longer note: what the company set out to do, what happened, what it
learned, and what the next year is for. It is the one piece of writing that
survives a fund's partner change, and it is read again at the next round.

## Anti-patterns

- **The surprise in a board meeting.** Everything material should already be known, in writing, before the meeting starts.
- **A verbal-only disclosure.** "I mentioned it on the call" is not a disclosure.
- **Telling the lead and assuming they will tell the rest.** They will not, and they should not have to.
- **Reporting a miss with a new metric that happens to look good.** It is the oldest move in the book and it is spotted immediately.
- **No plan attached to the bad news.** Investors can absorb bad news; they cannot absorb bad news with no owner.
