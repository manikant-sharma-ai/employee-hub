# The monthly update

Six hundred words, the same date, the same five numbers. It is the cheapest trust a
company can buy and the first thing founders stop doing when it gets hard.

## The shape

| Section | Length | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Subject line | 8-12 words | The month and the one fact worth opening for |
| One-line summary | 1 sentence | Where the business is, in plain words |
| The numbers | A table, 5-7 rows | This month, last month, and the definition |
| Last month's promises | 3-5 lines | Done, slipped, dropped - with the reason |
| What worked | 2 paragraphs | One thing, with a number |
| What did not | 1-2 paragraphs | One thing, with the reason and the fix |
| The ask | 1-3 lines, or "none this month" | Specific, and answerable |
| Close | 2 lines | Thanks, and the date of the next one |

## The numbers block

Same rows every month. Definitions do not change.

| Metric | This month | Last month | Definition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue (recognised) | | | Invoiced and delivered, net of returns |
| MRR or monthly run rate | | | Recurring only, excluding one-time |
| Gross margin | | | After delivery cost, before overheads |
| Net burn | | | Cash out minus cash in, excluding fundraise |
| Cash in bank | | | Closing balance, reconciled |
| Runway | | | Cash divided by the 3-month average net burn |
| <Operating metric 1> | | | e.g. orders, active accounts, seats used |
| <Operating metric 2> | | | e.g. CAC, churn, utilisation |

Five to seven rows. Twelve rows of vanity metrics is a report that nobody reads
and a habit that hides the one number that matters.

## Subject lines that get opened

- "October: ₹18.4 lakh revenue, 14 months runway, one miss"
- "Update - September. We lost our biggest client. Here is the plan."
- "October update: two new hires, revenue flat, asking for one intro"

The subject carries a number or a fact. "Monthly Update - October 2026" is filed
unread, and after three of them the investor stops opening any.

## Writing the misses

The paragraph that decides whether the update is believed.

1. **The fact.** "We planned to sign 12 new accounts; we signed 7."
2. **The reason, without euphemism.** "Our onboarding takes 11 days and two of the five churned in week three."
3. **What changed as a result.** "Onboarding is now a two-call process, live from 1 November."
4. **What it does to the plan.** "The quarter's revenue target moves from ₹58 lakh to ₹49 lakh."

No "challenging environment", no "headwinds", no "learning journey". Investors
have read a thousand of those and they discount the whole mail when they see one.

## The ask

| Ask | Example | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| An intro | "Two intros to D2C finance heads in Bengaluru" | Specific, answerable in one forward |
| A hire | "A senior ops person, ₹18-24 lakh, Mumbai" | Investors enjoy hiring help |
| A view | "How would you price the annual plan against the monthly?" | Invites expertise, not money |
| A customer | "Any portfolio company shipping 5,000+ orders a month" | Concrete |
| Nothing | "No ask this month - we are heads down until the launch" | Honesty, and it makes the next ask credible |

One ask. An update with four asks gets a reply about none of them.

## Cadence and format

- **Monthly, same date.** The 5th works for most Indian businesses because the previous month's books close by the 3rd-4th.
- **Email, in the body, not an attachment.** Attachments go unopened on a phone.
- **Under 600 words.** Longer is read later, and later is never.
- **BCC, or a proper mail tool.** Never a visible list of investors.
- **Archive every update in the data room.** It is the narrative record of the company, and diligence reads it in order.

## Quarterly, add one thing

Once a quarter, add a short section the monthly does not have: the strategy in three
sentences, what has been ruled out, and the one metric that would change the plan.
This is what makes the monthly numbers legible as a story rather than a series.

## Anti-patterns

- **Skipping a month because the news is bad.** The skipped month is the news.
- **A different metric set every month.** Investors stop comparing and start skimming.
- **Growth claimed without a definition.** "Revenue up 40%" against what, and recognised how?
- **A round of applause for the team.** One line of thanks is fine; a paragraph is a newsletter.
- **Sending only to the lead.** Angels talk to each other, and the one who was left out remembers.
- **A promise in the update that nobody tracks next month.** The promises section is what makes the rest credible.
