# Investor questions

Investors ask the same fifteen questions. Answering them once, well, is most of
this job.

## The fifteen, with the answer shape

| Question | The answer must contain | The trap |
|---|---|---|
| "How much runway?" | Cash, monthly net burn, months, and the assumption | Quoting the best month's burn |
| "What is revenue?" | The recognised figure and the definition | Mixing bookings and revenue |
| "Growth rate?" | The period, the base, and whether it is MoM or YoY | A percentage on a tiny base |
| "Why did burn go up?" | The specific hires or spend, and the return expected | "Investment in growth" |
| "What is churn?" | Logo and revenue churn, and the cohort | One flattering definition |
| "Who are your biggest customers?" | Concentration as a percentage, not names | Naming a customer without permission |
| "What is CAC?" | Blended and by channel, with the payback months | Blended only |
| "Who is the competition?" | Two real ones and why you win | "We have no competition" |
| "What is the hiring plan?" | Roles, timing, cost, and what each unblocks | A number of heads with no roles |
| "When is the next round?" | The milestone, the month, and the amount range | A date you cannot hold |
| "How is the team?" | Who joined, who left, and the plan | Silence on a departure |
| "What keeps you up?" | The real risk, and the mitigation | "Nothing" |
| "Can I see the cap table?" | With permission from the founder, and the register | Sending an outdated version |
| "What can I do to help?" | One specific ask | "Nothing, we are fine" |
| "Are you raising now?" | The truth, and the timeline | Ambiguity that reads as a yes |

## Answering with a number and a source

The shape is always: **the number, the definition, the source, and the caveat.**

> "Net burn was ₹4.2 lakh in October, from Zoho Books, excluding the one-time
> ₹1.8 lakh equipment purchase. That is up from ₹3.1 lakh because we hired a
> support lead mid-month. Runway is 14 months at the October rate."

Four sentences, no adjectives, and the investor can check it.

## When you do not have the number

Say so, and say when.

> "We do not track CAC by channel yet - the instrumentation goes live this month.
> Blended CAC is ₹2,400 with a 4.5-month payback. I will send the split in the
> November update."

An investor forgives a missing metric. They do not forgive a fabricated one, and
they can usually tell.

## The FAQ

Build a one-page FAQ from the questions that repeat, and keep it beside the update.

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| The question, in their words | |
| The short answer | Under 60 words |
| The number and the source | |
| The longer answer | If they want depth |
| Last updated | The date |

Ten questions is the usual set. Refresh it monthly with the numbers, and the tenth
investor gets the same answer as the first in a third of the time.

## Individual calls, when they happen

- **Read the last update and the last call note before you dial.** Nothing damages a relationship faster than being asked the same question twice.
- **Open with the one thing they care about**, which is usually the metric they asked about last time.
- **Say the bad news before they ask.** They already suspect it.
- **Write three lines within 24 hours**: what was discussed, what was promised, by when. Send them to the investor.
- **Log it in the CRM with the next date.** A relationship with no next date is a contact.

## What not to answer

| Question | Why | What to say |
|---|---|---|
| "What valuation are you thinking for the next round?" | It sets an anchor you cannot move | "We will have a view once the March numbers are in; the plan is to raise on the retention curve" |
| "Who else is investing?" | Selective disclosure, and it invites pressure | "The lead is not final; I will share the round once it is closed" |
| "What are the other investors' terms?" | Confidential between shareholders | "I cannot share another shareholder's terms" |
| "What is your co-founder's salary?" | Personal data | "I can share the total team cost" |
| "Can you send the customer contract?" | Confidential, and usually unnecessary | "I can share a redacted copy with the commercial terms" |

## Anti-patterns

- **Answering from memory in a mail.** The number is then on the record and wrong.
- **A different definition for the same metric in two answers.** It is discovered, and it costs the next round.
- **Over-answering.** A 900-word reply to "how is it going" reads as anxiety.
- **Answering a question that was not asked.** It signals what you are worried about.
- **Letting a question sit for a week.** Under 48 hours, even if the answer is "I will have this on Friday".
- **Forwarding an investor's question to another investor.** Never, for any reason.
