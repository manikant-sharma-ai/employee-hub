# Bad news and disclosure

The way a business delivers a miss decides whether its investors help in the next
one. This file is the timing, the wording and the boundary.

## The timing rule

| Event | Tell whom | When |
|---|---|---|
| Missed the month's plan by under 10% | All investors, in the update | The normal update date |
| Missed by more than 20%, or two months running | Lead first, then the update | Within 72 hours of the close |
| Runway below 9 months | Lead, then all | Within a week of knowing |
| Loss of a top-three customer | Lead | Same week |
| Key person leaving | Lead, then all | Before it is public |
| Litigation, notice, regulatory action | Lead and counsel | Same day |
| A data incident | Per the incident plan and the DPDP obligations | Immediately, with counsel |
| A bridge or down round | Lead, then all, before the market | When the decision is made, not when it is signed |

The general rule: **the people who backed you hear it from you, before they hear
it from anyone else.** Every exception costs more than the news.

## The structure of a bad-news update

1. **The fact, in one sentence.** "We missed November revenue by 22%."
2. **The cause, specific and non-defensive.** "Two renewals moved to January and one pilot was lost to a competitor on price."
3. **The impact on the plan.** "Breakeven moves from Q2 to Q3 at the current burn."
4. **What changes.** Three actions, with dates and owners.
5. **What is not changing.** The part of the plan that is on track, briefly.
6. **The ask**, if there is one. Often there is not, and saying so is better than inventing one.

Under 300 words. Bad news does not need length; it needs a plan.

## Wording

| Say | Do not say |
|---|---|
| "We missed by 22%." | "We experienced some softness." |
| "Two renewals slipped to January." | "The macro environment has been challenging." |
| "I got the hiring wrong; the plan was too optimistic." | "The talent market is difficult." |
| "Breakeven moves to Q3." | "We remain confident in the trajectory." |
| "I do not know yet; I will know by the 15th." | A guess presented as a plan |

Ownership without self-flagellation. Investors are assessing whether the founder
sees reality, not whether they feel bad about it.

## The bridge conversation

When runway is short, the conversation is not the update - it is a separate call:

- **The number**: cash, burn, the month it runs out.
- **The options**: cost cut, revenue pull-forward, bridge, a strategic conversation. Each with a cost.
- **The recommendation**, and what the founder needs from investors.
- **The timeline** for a decision.

Ask for a decision, not for sympathy. A vague bridge conversation damages the
relationship more than a clear ask that gets a no.

## What must not be said

- **A projection presented as a commitment.** "We will hit ₹1 crore in March" is a promise; label it a plan with assumptions.
- **A different story to different investors.** It is compared, quickly.
- **Anything about another investor's position.**
- **A statement about valuation or an exit** that has not been agreed with the board.
- **An assurance about a legal or tax position.** That is counsel's sentence, not this role's.

## The record

For every material disclosure, keep:

| Date | Who was told | What was said | Channel | What was promised |
|---|---|---|---|---|

This log is the defence when memory diverges. It is also the input to the next
round's disclosure schedule, which counsel will ask for.

## Legal boundary

Whether something is material, whether it must be disclosed, when, to whom, and
under which clause of the SHA, is a legal question with real consequences -
including for directors. This role prepares the facts, the timeline and the draft;
`legal-drafter` and counsel decide the obligation. Never let a founder rely on this
skill for a securities-law conclusion.

## Anti-patterns

- **Waiting for the next scheduled update** when the news is a runway event.
- **A long explanation with the fact in paragraph four.**
- **Blaming the market for a plan that was always too optimistic.**
- **Announcing a bridge to everyone at once** before the lead has been spoken to.
- **No written record.** Six months later, nobody agrees on what was said.
- **Reassurance in place of arithmetic.** "We will be fine" is the sentence investors remember when it is not.
