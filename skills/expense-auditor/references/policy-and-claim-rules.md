# Policy and claim rules

A claim is not wrong or right in the abstract. It is wrong against a written limit, an approval
matrix and a document requirement. If those do not exist, that is the first finding.

## The policy, in the six parts that matter

| Part | What it must state | The failure when it does not |
|---|---|---|
| **Who may spend** | The approval matrix by amount and category | Everyone approves everything, or nobody does |
| **What is eligible** | Categories allowed, and the ones excluded | "Business development" becomes a personal expense |
| **Limits** | Per-claim, per-day, per-category caps | A ₹40,000 dinner passes because no cap exists |
| **Documents** | Receipt, invoice with GSTIN, purpose, attendees | Claims with no proof and no way to test them |
| **Timing** | Submission window, e.g. 30 days from the date | A year of claims arrives at year-end |
| **Consequence** | What happens to a late, undocumented or over-limit claim | The policy is advisory |

A policy without an approval matrix is a wish. The matrix is the enforceable half.

## The approval matrix, as a table

| Amount (₹) | Approved by | Evidence required |
|---|---|---|
| Up to 2,000 | Self, with receipt | Receipt |
| 2,001 - 25,000 | Function head | Receipt + purpose |
| 25,001 - 1,00,000 | Founder | Receipt + purpose + quote where competitive |
| Above 1,00,000 | Founder + one other director | Three quotes or a PO |

Two rules that carry most of the control: **no one approves their own spend**, and **the
approver is above the spender in the matrix, not a peer**.

## Claim tests, in the order to run them

| Test | What it catches | False positive to expect |
|---|---|---|
| Receipt present and legible | Undocumented claims | A photo of a faded thermal receipt is fine |
| Amount matches the receipt | Inflated claims | GST included on one and excluded on the other |
| Date inside the submission window | Stale claims dumped at year-end | A trip billed late by the hotel |
| Category is eligible | Personal spend coded as business | Genuine client entertainment |
| Within the limit for that category | Over-limit claims | A limit that was raised mid-year |
| Approved by the right person | Self-approval, peer approval | A delegate during leave |
| Purpose recorded | Unexplained spend | "Meeting" is a purpose only if the attendee is named |
| Not already reimbursed | Double claims across two cycles | A resubmission after a rejection |

## Common category rules for an Indian business

| Category | Usual rule | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Local conveyance | Actuals with receipts, or a per-km rate | Two claims for one trip; cab bills with no time |
| Mileage, own vehicle | A fixed ₹/km, with a log of from-to and km | Km claimed above the distance on a map |
| Hotel | City-tier cap per night | A suite inside a "reasonable" claim |
| Meals, self | Per-diem or a cap per day | Multiple meals on the same day |
| Client entertainment | Cap per head, attendees named | Family at a client dinner |
| Telephone and internet | A fixed monthly allowance | A reimbursed personal plan already in salary |
| Gifts and hampers | Cap per recipient, festive window | Repeat gifting to one recipient |
| Training and certification | Pre-approved, exam fee only | A course unrelated to the role |
| Work-from-home setup | One-time cap, asset tagged | A second claim for the same chair |
| Statutory and professional fees | Actuals with the invoice | A CA bill split to avoid the approval tier |

## Design the fix, not just the finding

When the same breach appears three times, the policy is the problem:

| Pattern | Fix |
|---|---|
| Repeated over-limit claims by one team | Raise the limit and require pre-approval above it |
| Claims with no receipt, repeatedly | A no-receipt cap with a declaration, and the rest rejected |
| Year-end dump of claims | 30-day window, with a hard cut-off and a stated consequence |
| Self-approval in a small team | Route to the founder above a low threshold |
| Everything coded to "miscellaneous" | A closed category list, with a monthly exception report |

## Anti-patterns

| Anti-pattern | Cost |
|---|---|
| Policy in a PDF nobody has opened | Every finding becomes a debate about fairness |
| Limits with no approval matrix | The limit is the only control, and it is tested by exception |
| "Reasonable" as the only rule | Unenforceable, and the audit becomes subjective |
| Auditing only the claims submitted | The bigger leak is the spend that never came through a claim |
| Rejecting on a technicality with no fix | Compliance drops and the policy loses authority |
| Changing the policy mid-audit | Findings get re-argued on the new rule |
