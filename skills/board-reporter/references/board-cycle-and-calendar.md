# The board cycle and the calendar

A board is a calendar before it is a conversation. Fix the dates in January and the
rest of this role becomes assembly.

## The annual cycle

| Meeting | Typical rhythm | Papers due | What it must cover |
|---|---|---|---|
| Board meeting | Quarterly, plus ad hoc | 5 working days before | Performance, cash, decisions, risk, compliance |
| Audit committee | Quarterly, before the board | 7 days before | Accounts, internal audit, auditor observations, related parties |
| Nomination and remuneration | Half-yearly | 7 days before | Appointments, KMP changes, remuneration policy |
| Stakeholders' relationship | Half-yearly | 7 days before | Shareholder grievances, transfers, complaints |
| AGM | Within six months of year end | 21 clear days' notice | Adoption of accounts, auditor appointment, dividends |
| EGM | As needed | 21 clear days, or shorter with consent | Anything reserved to shareholders |
| Advisory board | Monthly or quarterly, no statute | 3 days before | Advice only - never a resolution |

## The six lines at the top of every working file

1. Entity and instrument - private limited, LLP, listed; articles, SHA, board charter.
2. Type of meeting - board, committee, AGM, EGM, advisory.
3. Date, time, venue or video link.
4. Notice period, and the clause it comes from.
5. Quorum, and the clause it comes from.
6. Reserved matters - what needs a shareholder resolution or a special resolution.

Everything downstream depends on those six lines. A private company board meeting
generally needs seven days' notice, and one-third of the board or two directors,
whichever is higher, as quorum; a general meeting generally needs 21 clear days. Read
the articles and the SHA first - they routinely set a longer notice or a larger quorum,
and the SHA often adds matters that need investor consent.

## The countdown

| T | Action | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| T-21 | Confirm the date with the chair; check the statutory calendar | You |
| T-14 | Collect agenda items from directors and management; confirm the decision list | You and the chair |
| T-10 | Issue the notice with the business to be transacted, at item level | You, on the chair's authority |
| T-8 | Chase papers from item owners; return thin papers with the template | You |
| T-5 | Circulate the pack; log the circulation time per director | You |
| T-3 | Pre-read call with the chair; brief any new director | You |
| T-1 | Confirm quorum, video links, and any declared interest received late | You |
| T | Run the agenda to time; draft minutes live | You |
| T+3 | Draft minutes to the chair | You |
| T+7 | Minutes signed or comments closed; actions registered | Chair and you |
| T+15 | Statutory forms prepared and sent to the CS | You and the CS |

## Notice: what makes it valid

- **In writing**, to every director at the address on record, with the agenda.
- **The business is stated at item level.** "Any other business" cannot carry a
  decision; an item decided under AOB is an item a dissenting director can unpick.
- **Shorter notice is possible only with the consent the instrument requires** - and
  the consent is recorded, not assumed.
- **A director who attends waives short notice for that meeting**, but the waiver does
  not fix a substantive defect in the papers.
- **Notice to directors who are travelling** goes to the address on record, on time.
  The failure to read it is theirs; the failure to send it is yours.

## Adjournment and the lost quorum

If quorum is lost mid-meeting, the meeting can only adjourn. A decision taken after
quorum is lost is the most common defect found in a due-diligence review, and it is
found years later, by someone who is not on your side. Note the time quorum was lost,
in the minute, and nothing else.

## Meeting types that are not meetings

- **Circular resolution** - for a decision that cannot wait. Signed by the required
  majority, dated, entered in the minute book at the next meeting. Never for a matter
  reserved to a meeting by the instrument, never for a related-party item without the
  interested director excluded.
- **Committee meeting** - decisions within the delegated authority only; anything
  outside it goes to the board with a recommendation.
- **Advisory board** - advice. It produces recommendations, never resolutions, and its
  minutes say so on the first line.

## Calendar hygiene

- One calendar, one owner, every date on it - board, committee, AGM, filings,
  insurance renewals, statutory audit, and the dates the auditor needs.
- Every entry carries three dates: notice, pack, meeting. A date with no pack date is
  a date that will slip.
- Recurring entries are reviewed every quarter against the instrument; an amendment to
  the articles resets the calendar.

## Anti-patterns

| Anti-pattern | What it costs |
|---|---|
| Board dates fixed one meeting at a time | Every pack is late, every decision is rushed |
| Notice sent with a bare agenda | Directors arrive unprepared and defer the item |
| "AOB" used for a real decision | The decision is challengeable, and the dissent is unrecorded |
| Quorum assumed from attendance at the last meeting | A decision taken without quorum, discovered at diligence |
| Committee deciding outside its delegation | The decision is void and must be retaken |
| Circular resolution used for a related-party matter | A defect that a statutory auditor will qualify |
