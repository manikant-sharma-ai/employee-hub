# Governance boundaries

This role assembles governed documents. The boundaries below are not style
preferences; crossing them can invalidate a resolution or expose the company.

## Conflicts of interest

1. **Ask for declarations before the pack is built**, not in the room. A short written declaration per director, per meeting, where the company has no standing policy.
2. **A conflicted director's item is separated**: their paper is withheld from them for that item, they withdraw for the discussion and the vote, and the minute records the withdrawal and the reason.
3. **A conflicted director does not count towards the quorum for that item** where the articles say so. Check the articles, do not assume.
4. **Never let a conflicted director's paper into the main pack.** It is a separate paper, circulated to the others, referenced but not attached.

## Confidential and privileged material

- **Legal advice is privileged only if it is marked and handled as such.** A privileged note pasted into a pack circulated to eleven people may lose the privilege. Ask the lawyer before including it; default is a summary, not the advice.
- **Personal data** - employee, customer or patient - appears as aggregates and counts. Named individuals only where a statute requires the board to consider them, and then on a separate page.
- **Insider information** - anything that would move a share price or a lender's view is handled under the company's own policy, not invented here. Where there is no policy, say so and mark the paper restricted.
- **Withholding is stated, never silent.** "One paper on this item is withheld pending legal advice" is a line in the pack.

## What this role does not do

- **It does not give legal, tax, audit or valuation opinions.** It names the professional who must, and the date the board needs the answer.
- **It does not decide quorum, notice periods or voting thresholds.** It reads them from the articles and the Companies Act as the company secretary advises, and it puts the requirement on the page so the chair can see it.
- **It does not sign the minutes.** The chair signs; this role drafts, circulates and keeps the record.

## When something is already wrong

If a meeting was held without notice, without quorum, or a resolution was passed
that the articles do not permit, do not paper over it in the minutes. Write the
facts, flag it to the chair and the company secretary in one message, and let them
decide whether it is ratified. A minute that hides a procedural defect is worse
than no minute at all.
