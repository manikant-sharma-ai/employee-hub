# Action tracking and follow-through

The board's memory is this register. Everything else in the pack is a snapshot; the
register is the only page that shows whether the company does what its board decided.

## The register

| Field | Rule |
|---|---|
| ID | `YYYY-MM-n`, consecutive, never reused |
| Action | A verb and a deliverable - "circulate the revised credit policy", not "credit policy" |
| Source | Meeting date and item number |
| Owner | One named person. Never a department, never "the team" |
| Due | A date. Not "next quarter" |
| Evidence of closure | The artefact that proves it - document, number, filing reference |
| Status | Open, closed, superseded, dropped (with reason) |
| Slippage | Count of date extensions. Two is a pattern, three is a decision |

## The two-page rule

Page two of every pack carries the open actions, oldest first, with the owner's status
line in their own words. Not your summary - their line, edited for length. A board that
reads the owner's own words asks a different question than one that reads your
paraphrase.

## What counts as closed

| Action | Closed when |
|---|---|
| "Appoint the CFO" | Appointment letter signed, board resolution passed, form filed |
| "Fix debtor days" | The metric is inside the stated range for two consecutive months |
| "Circulate the revised policy" | The document is in the folder and the circulation is logged |
| "Complete the bank renewal" | Sanction letter received and the mandate updated at the bank |

Anything else is "in progress with evidence", which is a status, not a closure.

## Slippage

- **First slip:** note the new date and the reason, in the register, in one line.
- **Second slip:** the owner presents at the meeting - two lines on what changed and
  what they need. No new date without a changed plan.
- **Third slip:** the board decides: re-scope, reassign, or drop. An action that slips
  three times is usually a decision the board has not actually made.

## Actions that are really decisions

Half of all "actions" are decisions in disguise: "explore the warehouse lease" means
the board has not decided whether to expand. Convert them before the register fills
with exploration:

| Written as | Actually | Do this |
|---|---|---|
| "Look into a second warehouse" | A decision to expand, deferred | Put the decision paper on the next agenda |
| "Review pricing" | A pricing decision nobody owns | Name the owner and the date, or drop it |
| "Improve collections" | A metric, not an action | "Bring debtor days under 55 by 31 March" |

## The quarterly sweep

Once a quarter, before the pack is assembled:

1. Every open action older than 90 days gets a line to its owner: still doing it,
   re-date it, or drop it.
2. Every dropped action gets a one-line reason in the register. Silent drops are how a
   board loses trust in its own record.
3. Count: opened, closed, slipped, dropped. Put the four numbers on page two. A board
   that sees 14 opened and 3 closed in a quarter changes how it assigns work.

## The closure evidence folder

One subfolder per meeting, one file per closed action, named by action ID. This is what
an auditor asks for and what a new CS needs in week one. It takes two minutes per action
and saves a week of reconstruction.

## Anti-patterns

| Anti-pattern | Cost |
|---|---|
| Action owned by "management" | Nobody does it, and nobody can be asked |
| Due date "ongoing" | The action is a permanent fixture on page two |
| Status written by the reporter, not the owner | The board is told what it wants to hear |
| Register reset at each meeting | History lost; slippage invisible |
| Actions closed by assertion | The evidence folder is empty at diligence |
| Twenty open actions | Nothing is prioritised; the board stops reading page two |
