# Numbers and provenance

A board pack is only as good as the worst number in it. One figure that cannot be
traced back costs more trust than ten good pages earn.

## The provenance line

Every table and every figure in the pack carries four things, in a footer under
the table or a note beside the figure:

```
Source: Zoho Books, P&L for Sep 2026, pulled 14 Oct 2026 by finance-ops
Basis: management accounts, unaudited, GST-reconciled to 30 Sep
Definition: revenue net of returns and discounts, excluding inter-company
Status: provisional - September bank reconciliation open
```

If any of the four is missing, the number comes out of the pack. Not into a
footnote, not marked "TBC" - out.

## The three statuses

- **Audited** - signed off by the auditor. Safe to quote to a lender or an investor.
- **Management** - produced by the finance function, reconciled to the books, not audited. The normal basis for a board pack; say so once, on page one, not on every table.
- **Provisional** - a number that will move. Always carries the reason and the expected date it will settle.

Mixing the three in one table without a status column is the commonest way a pack
misleads a board without anyone lying.

## Checks worth running on every pack

1. **Does the total tie to the source?** Re-add the column. It takes a minute and it catches the paste error.
2. **Does last month's actual match what was reported last month?** A restatement is legitimate; a silent restatement is not. Note it and say why.
3. **Is the budget the one the board approved?** Not the revised budget nobody minuted.
4. **Is the comparison period the same length?** A 5-week month against a 4-week month has produced more false alarms than any other error in management reporting.
5. **Does the chart axis start at zero where the board will assume it does?** Truncated axes on a board slide are read as a trick, even when they are not.
6. **Is any figure a forecast sitting next to an actual?** Then it needs a column that says so.

## When a number is wrong after the pack is out

Say it at the top of the next meeting, in one line, with the corrected figure and
the cause. A board forgives a corrected number. It does not forgive discovering
one in a later meeting.
