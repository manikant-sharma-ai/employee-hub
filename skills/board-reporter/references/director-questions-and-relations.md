# Director questions and relations

Directors decide on what they are given, and what they are given is what you wrote.
This file is about the information asymmetry: the gap between what the company knows
and what the board has been told.

## The six questions every board asks

Prepare the answer before circulation, not in the room.

| Question | Where the answer lives |
|---|---|
| "What is our cash position and how many months does it last?" | Cash page, reconciled to the bank, with the runway definition |
| "Why did the number move against plan?" | Variance column with a one-line driver, not a chart |
| "Who is accountable for this, and since when?" | Action register, owner and first-due date |
| "What is the downside if this goes wrong?" | Risk line on the decision paper, with the early warning |
| "What would make us reverse this?" | The reversal condition, stated when the decision is taken |
| "What are we not being told?" | The risk page, and the one line in the pre-read call |

## The pre-read call

Fifteen minutes with the chair, three days before circulation.

1. What is contested - and who will contest it.
2. What is thin - which paper has no recommendation or no number.
3. What the chair wants the outcome to be, so the paper can be written to be decided
   rather than argued.
4. What has changed since the last meeting that is not yet in a paper.

Never use this call to build a majority before the meeting. Briefing the chair is
preparation; briefing three directors separately to pre-agree a vote is a governance
problem with your name on it.

## A new director's first pack

| Item | Why |
|---|---|
| The instrument - articles, SHA, board charter | Their authority and their constraints |
| The last four sets of minutes | What the board has already decided |
| The open action register | What is outstanding and who owns it |
| The last audited accounts and the current MIS | The numbers in their own context |
| The cap table and the shareholder map | Who they answer to |
| The risk register and the compliance calendar | Where the company is exposed |
| The three things that keep the founder awake | The real agenda |
| A named person to call | Induction is a relationship, not a folder |

## Conflicts of interest

- Ask for declarations at the start of every financial year and at the start of every
  meeting. A register that is only updated when someone remembers is a register that is
  wrong.
- A director with an interest in an item declares it, is counted for quorum, does not
  vote, and the minute records the declaration and the abstention.
- Related-party transactions need the paper, the disclosure, the approval of the
  disinterested directors, and often a shareholder resolution. The CS confirms which.
- Never draft a minute that hides an interest to make a transaction look clean. That
  minute is the evidence against the company.

## Information asymmetry, managed honestly

- **Bad news goes in the pack, not in the corridor.** A director who learns something
  after the meeting stops trusting the pack, and then stops reading it.
- **A number that is not ready is labelled, not omitted.** "Collections data is
  provisional; the reconciled figure comes with the auditor's report" is a sentence a
  board accepts.
- **The one-page risk section is written by someone who is not selling anything.** If
  every risk is "monitored", the page is decoration.
- **Management's recommendation is separate from the board's decision.** Say which is
  which in the paper; directors need to know what they are being advised to do.

## Board evaluation, once a year

Five questions, answered by each director in writing, summarised without attribution:

1. Did the papers let you decide, or did you decide despite them?
2. Was any item decided without enough information? Which?
3. Which decision from the last year would you take differently now?
4. What is the board spending time on that management should own?
5. What is management not bringing to the board that it should?

The summary goes to the chair. The individual answers do not.

## Anti-patterns

| Anti-pattern | Cost |
|---|---|
| A director hears bad news after the meeting | The pack is discounted from then on |
| Papers written to persuade rather than to decide | Directors argue the framing instead of the decision |
| No induction for a new director | Six months of silence, then an opinion formed elsewhere |
| Conflict register updated once a year | A related-party item passes with a vote that should not have counted |
| Pre-agreeing a vote in side calls | The decision is defensible but the process is not |
| Every risk "monitored" | The risk page is never read again |
