# Packaging: title and thumbnail as one decision

Packaging is the entire business of a YouTube channel. The video is only seen by the people the packaging earned.

## The pair rule

A title and a thumbnail are one unit with two jobs: the thumbnail carries the *situation*, the title carries the
*specific promise*. Neither repeats the other, and either alone is unfinished.

| Thumbnail shows | Title says | Together |
|---|---|---|
| A warehouse floor, one pallet circled in red | "Why 40% of your stock is dead capital" | Situation plus stake |
| A GST notice on a desk, hand holding a pen | "The one line that gets a notice withdrawn" | Fear plus specific |
| Two identical invoices, one stamped | "We fixed a ₹9 lakh billing error in 20 minutes" | Proof plus number |

## Title shapes that work for business content

| Shape | Example | When it wins |
|---|---|---|
| The specific number | "How we cut freight cost 31% in one quarter" | The audience is operators; numbers signal proof |
| The named mistake | "The GST mistake that costs ₹1 lakh a year" | Search demand exists and the fear is real |
| The contrarian | "Stop chasing new customers. Fix this instead." | A known belief is being challenged with evidence |
| The how-to with a constraint | "Cold email that works with no sales team" | The constraint is the viewer's own |
| The case | "How a Surat textile exporter won his first GeM tender" | Story plus specificity; strong for suggested |

Under 60 characters where possible; the first three words must carry the query for search-led videos. Front-load
the number or the noun the viewer typed.

## Thumbnail rules for a small team

- **Three elements maximum**: a subject, a stake, and at most three words. More is a poster.
- **Legible at 120 pixels wide** - check it on a phone, not a laptop.
- **One focal point and a face where honest.** Human faces lift CTR on most channels, but only a real expression
  from a real person in the video.
- **Contrast against YouTube's white and dark themes** - a mid-grey design disappears in both.
- **Consistent template, variable content.** The template builds recognition across the library; the variation
  earns the click.
- **Text must not repeat the title's words.** The thumbnail says *which* situation; the title says *what about
  it*.

## Reading the click-through rate honestly

| CTR | Traffic source | What it usually means |
|---|---|---|
| 2-3% | Browse | Packaging is not earning the impression |
| 4-6% | Browse | Healthy for a small business channel |
| 6-10% | Suggested | The video is being recommended to a warm audience |
| 3-5% | Search | Normal; the query is doing the work, not the packaging |
| Under 2% | Anywhere | Either the topic is wrong or the thumbnail is unreadable |

CTR always travels with impressions. A 9% CTR on 900 impressions is not a win; a 4% CTR on 200,000 is.

## A/B testing when there is no A/B tool

1. **Alternate by video**, not by day: one packaging style per video, four videos per style, then compare.
2. **Hold the topic constant.** Testing thumbnails on four unrelated topics measures the topics.
3. **Judge at 14 days** and on CTR at a comparable impression level, not on views.
4. **Record the result in the video log** with the thumbnail as published, or the lesson is lost by next quarter.

## Anti-patterns

- A title written by the person who made the video, describing what they made.
- A thumbnail designed before the title exists, then a title bent to fit the picture.
- Emoji, all caps and arrows used as a substitute for a stake.
- Changing both title and thumbnail on a live video at once, which makes the result unreadable.
- Packaging that promises a number the video never gives. The retention curve punishes it within a minute.
