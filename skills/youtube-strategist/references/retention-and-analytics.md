# Retention and analytics: reading the curve

The retention graph is the only honest review a video gets. It is also routinely misread.

## The three numbers, in order

1. **Impressions** - was the video offered to anyone.
2. **Click-through rate** - did the packaging earn the click. Below ~4% on browse, fix packaging before content.
3. **Average view duration and the curve** - did the video keep the promise the packaging made.

Diagnose in that order. Content advice given to a packaging problem wastes a month.

## Curve shapes and what they mean

| Shape | What it looks like | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cliff at 0-15s | Drops 40% immediately | The intro did not confirm the click; a logo, a greeting, a long setup | Open on the promise, in the first sentence |
| Steady slope | Loses 2-4% every 30s | Pacing, repetition, no new information per minute | Cut a third of the length, add a reason to stay every 60s |
| Shelf then cliff | Flat, then a sudden drop at 3:40 | A segment that does not belong: an ad read, a tangent, a guest | Move or cut the segment; check the same timestamp on other videos |
| Rising tail | Retention climbs at the end | A payoff the audience stayed for; often a demo or a result | Move the payoff earlier and tease it at the start |
| Flat and low | 20% held throughout | The video is being shown to the wrong audience | Packaging promised something the topic did not deliver |

## The first thirty seconds

Four things must happen, in this order, and nothing else:

1. **Confirm the click** - the exact thing the title promised, stated in the first sentence.
2. **State the stake** - what it costs the viewer to keep doing it wrong.
3. **Prove the credential** - one line on why this channel can answer it. Not a biography.
4. **Open a loop** - what they will know at the end that they do not know now.

Anything before those four - a channel intro, a subscribe request, a sponsor read, "welcome back" - is a tax on
every viewer.

## Reading by traffic source

| Source | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| Browse | The packaging worked on a cold audience; judge CTR and 30-second retention |
| Suggested | Another video's audience accepted this one; judge the first 30 seconds hardest |
| Search | The query matched; judge whether the video answered it, by comments and by retention shape |
| External | A post or an email drove it; retention is meaningless here, the click was social |
| Shorts feed | Loop and swipe-away rates matter, not the retention curve |

## Benchmarks to distrust

- **"Average view duration should be 50%"** - it depends entirely on length and format. A 6-minute explainer and a
  40-minute podcast have nothing in common.
- **Other channels' CTR** - they have different audiences, topics and thumbnail styles.
- **The absolute subscriber count** - subscribers per thousand views is the number that says whether the audience
  is becoming a following.

## The video log

One row per video, and it is the memory of the channel: date, title, thumbnail description, topic, series, length,
traffic source split, impressions, CTR, average view duration, subscribers gained, and one line of what was learned.
Without it, every month's read starts from zero and the same mistake is made twice.

## Anti-patterns

- Judging a video at 48 hours. The first day is the subscribers; the month is the algorithm.
- Comparing a search video's CTR to a browse video's, and concluding the search video is worse.
- Rewriting the video's content because the thumbnail was unreadable.
- Reading the curve as an average when the drop is one timestamp - look at the dip, not the line.
