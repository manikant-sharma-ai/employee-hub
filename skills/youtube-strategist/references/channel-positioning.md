# Channel positioning: the promise a viewer can repeat

A channel grows when a viewer can say what it is for in one sentence and come back for the next one. Everything
else - titles, thumbnails, series, cadence - is downstream of that sentence.

## The one-sentence promise

Fill this in, and refuse to publish until it is filled:

> *"This channel helps <named viewer> do <specific job> without <the thing they hate about it>."*

| Weak | Why | Strong |
|---|---|---|
| "Business tips for entrepreneurs" | No viewer, no job, no constraint | "GST and invoicing for Indian D2C sellers who do their own books" |
| "We share what we learn" | A diary, not a channel | "How a ₹2-crore manufacturer wins government tenders, step by step" |
| "Marketing, sales and growth" | Three channels in one | "Cold email that gets replies for B2B founders with no sales team" |

The test: take the last ten titles. If six could belong to a different channel, the promise does not exist yet and
no packaging will fix it.

## Naming the viewer

Write the viewer as a person with a job, not a demographic: *"the founder who is also the accountant"*, *"the ops
manager who inherited a warehouse"*, *"the CA's client who just crossed ₹1 crore"*. A viewer you can picture
produces titles you can write; a demographic produces titles nobody clicks.

## What the channel refuses to do

Three refusals make the promise real. Typical ones: no reaction content, no news commentary, no videos about
tools the business does not use, no guest interviews, nothing that cannot be demonstrated on camera. Write them
down and use them to reject ideas in the first minute, which is the cheapest place to reject them.

## Browse versus search: which game this channel plays

| Signal | Browse and suggested | Search |
|---|---|---|
| Where views come from | Home feed, suggested, Shorts feed | YouTube search, Google |
| What wins | Packaging, retention, session time | The exact query, the title's first words |
| Topic choice | Broad, emotionally legible, current | Specific, high-intent, evergreen |
| Ceiling | Very high, and it decays fast | Lower, and it compounds |
| Failure mode | Impressions with no clicks | Views from people who wanted something else |

Most business channels should be search-led at the start, because the demand already exists and the audience is
small but commercial, and browse-led only once there is a body of work that suggestions can pull from. Decide
which game this quarter is being played, and judge the videos by that game's numbers only.

## The competitor set

Pick three channels the viewer already watches, in the same language and market. For each, write what the channel
promises, how it packages, how long its videos run, and what it does not cover. The gap that appears in all three
is the opening. Do not benchmark a channel ten times your size for rates - benchmark it for format.

## Positioning for an Indian business channel

- **Language and code-switching** decide the audience more than any topic. Hinglish reaches a far larger SMB
  audience than English and a far smaller enterprise one; choose and stay consistent.
- **The next step is part of the promise.** A channel with no path to an enquiry, a WhatsApp group, a course or a
  product grows an audience the business cannot use, and the founder notices in month four.
- **Regional demand is real.** A channel in Tamil or Marathi on a narrow business topic often outgrows the
  national English one, at a tenth of the competition.

## Anti-patterns

- Rebranding the channel before the promise is written. The banner is not the problem.
- Adding a second content pillar because the first is slow. Two unclear channels perform worse than one clear one.
- Copying a global creator's format without their market, language or production budget.
- Letting the founder's interests set the topics while the viewer's searches go unanswered.
