# Search and discovery: how a video actually gets found

Two engines feed a channel: people typing a query, and the platform deciding a video might hold someone's
attention. The craft differs for each.

## YouTube search is not Google

| | YouTube search | Google web |
|---|---|---|
| The query | Conversational, often a task in progress | Often informational, often a question |
| What ranks | Watch time and satisfaction on that query | Links, relevance, page experience |
| Title's job | Carry the query in the first words | Carry the query in the title tag and H1 |
| The winning video | Answers the query and keeps the viewer | Answers the query on the page |
| Longevity | Years, if the query is stable | Years, with maintenance |

A video can rank on YouTube for a query nobody writes an article about, and vice versa. They are separate jobs,
and `seo-specialist` owns the second.

## Finding demand before commissioning

1. **YouTube's own suggestions** - type the seed phrase and read the autocomplete; those are real searches.
2. **Google Trends, filtered to YouTube search** - direction and seasonality, not volume.
3. **The channel's own search terms report** in Studio - the queries viewers used to find this channel. The most
   underused screen in the product.
4. **Comments and support tickets** - the questions people ask in their own words, which is the phrasing to use.
5. **Competitor videos with high views and poor retention** - demand exists and the supply is weak; the best
   opening a small channel gets.

## Query-first titles

For a search-led video, the first three words should be the thing typed. *"GST refund for exporters: the 3
documents that decide it"* beats *"The one thing exporters always miss about GST refunds"*, which is a better
browse title and a worse search one.

## Shorts: what they do and do not do

| Shorts do | Shorts do not |
|---|---|
| Reach people who do not know the channel | Build a business audience on their own |
| Test a hook cheaply | Convert to enquiries without a long-form destination |
| Feed the suggested system with watch time | Carry nuance, numbers or a process |
| Bring subscribers who may never watch long form | Replace the library |

The honest pattern for a business channel: Shorts as the top of the funnel and long form as the thing the funnel
points at, with each Short ending on a reason to watch the long version. A channel that only does Shorts grows a
number that does not pay for anything.

## What does not matter much

- **Tags.** Near-useless for discovery; useful only for common misspellings.
- **Hashtags.** Three at most, and only when the topic is genuinely a hashtag topic.
- **Description keyword stuffing.** The first two lines matter because they are visible; the rest is for humans.
- **Upload time.** Matters far less than the first hour's retention and the day of the week for the audience.
- **Subscriber count for its own sake.** A thousand right viewers beat fifty thousand wrong ones.

## Localising for India

- **Search behaviour is language-mixed.** Many users type Hinglish into an English interface; include the phrasing
  they use in the title and the first line of the description.
- **Device reality is mobile and data-conscious.** A 40-minute video competes with a 6-minute one for the same
  commute.
- **Regional search is less crowded.** A Tamil or Bengali version of a proven English topic often outranks the
  original.
- **WhatsApp is the distribution layer.** The channel's real second channel is a share into a group; design one
  moment per video that is worth forwarding.

## Anti-patterns

- Choosing topics from what the team finds interesting, then searching for a query that fits afterwards.
- Chasing a trending query with no connection to the channel's promise, which brings an audience that never returns.
- Treating Shorts views as a growth number without asking what happened to those viewers next.
- Building a keyword list in a spreadsheet and never checking it against what the platform actually suggests.
