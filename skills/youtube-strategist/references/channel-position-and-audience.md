# Channel position and audience

A channel grows when one audience keeps getting one kind of thing. That is the whole strategy, and almost every
stuck channel is stuck because it is serving three audiences badly instead of one audience well.

## The one sentence

Write it and keep it visible: **"This channel is for <a specific person> who wants <a specific outcome>, and every
video gives them <the thing they get>."**

Test it against the last twenty titles. If more than three of them serve somebody else, the channel has no
promise and the subscriber count is measuring the confusion.

## Browse against search

Two different businesses live on the same platform:

| | Search-led | Browse-led |
|---|---|---|
| The viewer | Has a question, types it | Is being shown things, chooses |
| Won by | Answering the query better than the result above | A thumbnail and title that create a click |
| The metric that matters | Views per query, and the ranking position | Click-through, and retention after the click |
| The failure | Correct video nobody searches for | Clickable video nobody finishes |
| Typical channel | How-to, repair, software, exam prep | Entertainment, story, personality, reaction |

Most small business channels are search-led and pretend to be browse-led, or the reverse. Decide which game you
are playing this quarter, and judge the videos against that game's metric.

## The audience, concretely

Not "small business owners". Instead: "the owner of a 5-40 person Indian manufacturer who runs dispatch on
WhatsApp and Excel, and is being asked for GST invoices by a large buyer". That person has a query, a browseable
fear, and a language. Every idea in the plan should be recognisably for them.

## What a view is worth

Before optimising for views, write down what a viewer is worth: the share who visit the site, the share of those
who enquire, the value of an enquiry. A channel with 2,000 views a month and four enquiries is a better business
than one with 200,000 views and none - and only one of them survives a quarter.

## The promise audit

Once a quarter, take the ten best and ten worst videos by watch time and ask of each: does this keep the promise
or break it? A video that over-performed while breaking the promise brings the wrong subscriber, who does not
return, which is why the channel's next video under-performs and nobody understands why.
