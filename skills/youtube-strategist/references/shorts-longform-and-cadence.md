# Shorts, long form and cadence

Format is a capacity decision before it is a creative one. The right mix is the one the team can hold for twelve
months without a drop.

## What each format is for

| | Shorts | Long form |
|---|---|---|
| Job | Reach new people, cheaply | Convert attention into trust and into a customer |
| Cost | Low - one idea, one shoot | High - script, shoot, edit, thumbnail |
| Discovery | Feed, unpredictable | Search, browse, suggested |
| Retention metric | Percentage viewed, loops | AVD, percentage viewed, subscribers per view |
| The trap | Views that never become anything | Videos nobody sees because nobody knows the channel |

A business channel usually needs long form for the sales it wants and Shorts for the audience it does not yet
have. The proportion is decided by who films and how often, not by a growth-hacking article.

## Cadence that survives

1. **Count the real capacity.** Who films, how many hours a week, who edits, how long one edit takes. Two videos a
   month that ship beat eight that do not.
2. **Batch.** Two shoot days a month producing four long videos and eight Shorts is a system; one video a week
   shot individually is a hobby with deadlines.
3. **Write the busy-week fallback** before the busy week: which format is dropped first, and what is published
   instead. A cadence with no fallback breaks in the first festival season and never restarts.
4. **Protect one anchor slot.** The same day, the same time, the same kind of video. The audience learns it and
   the team plans around it.

## Repurposing, and where the boundary is

- **Long form to Shorts**: three moments that stand alone, each with its own hook. `video-editor` cuts them.
- **Shorts to long form**: a Short that over-performs is evidence of demand; make the long version and link the
  two.
- **Video to other platforms**: the cut is `video-editor`, the cross-platform calendar is
  `social-media-manager`. This role supplies the idea and the packaging, not the schedule.

## The 90-day test

At the end of a quarter, answer three questions with numbers: did click-through improve against the baseline, did
percentage viewed at thirty seconds improve, and did the cadence hold? If the cadence broke, the plan was wrong
regardless of the numbers - fix the plan before fixing the videos.

## When not to make video at all

If the audience does not watch video, or the business cannot sustain four videos a quarter, the honest answer is a
smaller commitment: one search-led video a month that answers the question buyers actually ask, and the rest of
the effort in the channel where the buying happens. Saying that out loud is part of this role.
