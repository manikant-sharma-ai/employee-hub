# The library: making videos feed each other

A channel with forty unrelated videos is forty lottery tickets. A channel with a series is an asset that compounds.

## The three kinds of video, and their jobs

| Kind | Job | Cadence | Judged on |
|---|---|---|---|
| Series episode | Builds the following; the reason to subscribe | Weekly or fortnightly | Subscribers per view, return viewers |
| Evergreen search asset | Answers a query for years; the compounding floor | One or two a month | Views at 12 months, search share |
| Timely | Rides a moment: a budget, a rule change, a festival | As it happens | First-72-hour views, and whether it fed the library |

A channel that only makes timely videos has spikes and no floor. A channel that only makes evergreen videos grows
slowly and never becomes a habit. Most business channels need all three, in roughly that priority.

## Series design

A series needs a repeatable promise and a repeatable shape:

- **A name a viewer can search** - "Tender Tuesdays", "The ₹1 crore books", "Warehouse maths".
- **A fixed length band** - 6-10 minutes, or 20-30. Variation inside the band, not across it.
- **A recurring structure** - the problem, the number, the fix, the next question.
- **A numbered or dated identity** in the title where the series is episodic, so the viewer knows where they are.
- **A decision on when it ends.** Twelve episodes and a review beats an indefinite commitment nobody can keep.

## Playlists that do work

| Playlist type | Purpose | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| The series | Sequential watching | Ordered, oldest first, updated the day an episode ships |
| The problem set | Everything on one query | Named after the search phrase, not the internal topic |
| The starter | For a new viewer | Five videos, ordered, the best packaging first |
| The proof | Case studies and results | For the sales conversation and the end screen |

Playlists are the cheapest retention tool on the platform and the one most often left empty.

## End screens and the handoff

Every video points somewhere: the next episode in the series, or the playlist for the query, or the one video that
converts. One destination per video, chosen before publication. A video that ends on a blank card wastes the most
attentive moment in the session.

## The back catalogue

- **Audit it twice a year**: which videos still get search views, which are dead, which have a wrong price or an
  outdated rule in them.
- **Update rather than delete.** A re-shot title, thumbnail and pinned comment can revive a video with 40,000
  lifetime views for an hour's work.
- **Retire what is wrong.** A video with an outdated tax rule actively costs the business credibility; unlist it
  and say why in the log.
- **Point the new at the old.** A new video on a topic should link the older one where it goes deeper, and the
  older one's pinned comment should point at the new one.

## Cadence: the honest number

| Team | Sustainable long form | Plus |
|---|---|---|
| Founder alone, phone camera | 2 a month | Shorts cut from the same footage |
| Founder plus editor | 1 a week | 3-4 Shorts a week |
| Two-person content team | 2 a week | Daily Shorts, one live a month |

Choose the row that is true, and publish at that rate for six months. The channel that posts weekly for a year
beats the one that posts daily for six weeks, in every case that has ever been measured.

## Anti-patterns

- Starting a second series before the first has twelve episodes.
- A playlist named after an internal project rather than the viewer's query.
- Episodes that assume the viewer saw the previous one, in a feed where most did not.
- Deleting a video because it underperformed in week one. It is a search asset; judge it in month six.
