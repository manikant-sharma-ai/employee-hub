# Research design

Research that starts from a topic produces a document. Research that starts from a decision produces an answer. This file is how you convert one into the other in the first ten minutes.

## The conversion

| Topic (unanswerable) | Decision (answerable) |
|---|---|
| "Research the EV charging market" | "Should we open two charging bays in Pune in Q1 for ₹40 lakh?" |
| "Look into our competitors" | "Which two competitors will take the next 20 enterprise deals, and on what argument?" |
| "Study customer sentiment" | "Why did the March cohort stop using the app in week three, and is it fixable in a sprint?" |
| "Explore the packaging industry" | "Can we sell direct to exporters at 18% gross margin without a distributor?" |

The conversion needs four things: **an actor** (who acts), **an action** (what they would do), **an amount** (what it costs or is worth), and **a date** (when the decision is made). Missing any one, ask.

## Scoping by the value of the decision

Depth should track what the decision is worth. Say which bracket you are in, out loud.

| Decision value | Effort | Evidence bar |
|---|---|---|
| Under ₹50,000 | 1-2 hours | Two sources, one of them primary. Recommendation with caveats |
| ₹50,000 - ₹5 lakh | Half a day to a day | Three sources triangulated, plus one conversation with someone who has done it |
| ₹5 lakh - ₹50 lakh | Two to five days | Primary sources, 3-5 buyer or expert conversations, a written model with assumptions |
| Above ₹50 lakh | A week or more, often external help | Primary data, filed accounts, regulatory text, pre-mortem, a second reviewer |

If the client wants a ₹50 lakh decision researched in two hours, say what that buys: a directional answer with named gaps. Do not silently deliver a thin memo that looks thorough.

## The five sentences to write before gathering

1. **The decision:** who decides what, by when, at what cost.
2. **The options on the table**, including "do nothing". If "do nothing" is not an option, someone is rationalising.
3. **What would change the answer:** three to five observable conditions. "If the top three buyers are on annual contracts, this is a no."
4. **What I already believe** and how confident I am. Writing this down first is what makes disconfirmation possible later - you cannot disprove a belief you never stated.
5. **What I cannot get**, and how much it matters.

Sentences 3 and 4 are the ones that separate research from advocacy. Write them.

## Framing tools, used sparingly

| Frame | When it helps | When it misleads |
|---|---|---|
| **Jobs to be done** | Understanding why buyers switch | When you use it to justify a product nobody asked for |
| **Porter's five forces** | Structural attractiveness of a category | It is static; it says nothing about timing or execution |
| **TAM / SAM / SOM** | Sanity-checking an ambition | Top-down sizing of a market that does not exist yet |
| **Unit economics first** | Almost always | Never - if the unit economics do not work at scale, market size is irrelevant |
| **Pre-mortem** | Before committing | As a substitute for evidence |
| **2x2** | Forcing a trade-off into the open | Hiding the third variable that actually decides it |

Rule: **one frame per memo**, chosen because it answers the question, not because it is impressive. Two frameworks in one page means neither was needed.

## Bottom-up before top-down

Top-down sizing - "the market is $4 billion, we need 1%" - is the most common way a memo looks rigorous and says nothing.

Bottom-up: **how many buyers, how often do they buy, at what price, and what share could we plausibly win in 24 months.**

```
Buyers:     1,850 exporters in Coimbatore district (source, date)
Frequency:  2 orders a year (from 4 interviews, stated as an assumption)
Price:      ₹85,000 average order (their own price list)
Reachable:  ~30% we can actually reach with 2 salespeople
Realistic:  8% share of the reachable base in year one
Year one:   1,850 x 0.30 x 0.08 x 2 x 85,000 = ₹7.5 crore
```

Every line is checkable and arguable. That is the point - a founder can push back on the 8%, and the model improves.

## Sampling and evidence for qualitative questions

- **Five conversations reveal most of the pattern** for a narrow B2B question. Fifteen is usually enough for a category. Thirty is a survey.
- **Recruit against the buying criteria**, not convenience. Five friends of the founder are not a sample.
- **Ask about the last time, not opinions.** "Walk me through the last time you bought packaging" beats "what do you look for in a supplier".
- **Write down who you did not talk to.** The gap is often the finding.

## Anti-patterns

- Starting with a search instead of a question. Two hours of browsing and no decision question is the default failure mode.
- Sizing the market before checking whether the unit economics can ever work.
- Researching everything the client mentioned rather than the one thing they will decide.
- No stated prior belief, so no disconfirmation, so the memo confirms whatever the founder already wanted.
- Confusing a long memo with a thorough one. Length is not rigour; triangulation is.
- Asking ten questions before starting. One question, then begin.
- Delivering findings with no recommendation because "the client should decide". They asked you because they wanted a view.
