# Competitor and market mapping

A competitor list is not research. A map of who wins which buyer, on which argument, at which price, is. This file is how to build the second one.

## First, define the market

Write these four lines before listing anybody:

```
Buyer:    who actually pays (role, company size, geography)
Job:      what they are trying to get done
Substitute: what they use today if we do not exist (often "nothing" or "a spreadsheet")
Boundary: what we are explicitly not counting
```

A competitor set is only meaningful against a defined buyer and job. "Our competitors" usually means three companies the founder fears, not the five that actually take the deal.

## The four kinds of competitor

| Kind | What they are | Why they matter |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct** | Same buyer, same job, similar product | The obvious set, and usually the smallest threat |
| **Indirect** | Same job, different product | Often the real competition |
| **Substitute** | The buyer does it themselves, badly | The default winner in most Indian SMB categories |
| **Non-consumption** | The buyer does not solve the problem at all | The largest "competitor" and the real growth question |

Include all four in the map. A memo with only direct competitors overstates the market and understates the difficulty.

## The competitor matrix

Six to eight columns, no more. Rows are competitors; the last row is the client.

| Column | What goes in it | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Positioning | The one sentence they lead with | Their homepage, verbatim |
| Who they win | The buyer segment they are built for | Their pricing page, case studies, job ads |
| Price | Actual published price, or the range from a quote | Price list, a real quote, a marketplace listing |
| Proof | The strongest evidence they offer | Case studies, certifications, client logos |
| Weakness | Where they lose, in the buyer's words | Reviews, churn complaints, ex-employee accounts, your interviews |
| Reach | How the buyer finds them | Search, channel partners, field sales, marketplace |

Rules:

- **Quote their positioning verbatim.** Paraphrasing turns their weakness into your assumption.
- **Get real prices.** Published list, a mystery-shop enquiry (only with confirmation), a marketplace listing. A guessed price column makes the whole matrix decoration.
- **The weakness column is the memo.** Everything else is available on their website.
- **Reviews are the cheapest primary research in existence.** Read the 1-3 star reviews on Google, G2, Trustpilot, Amazon, Play Store. That is where the buyer states the failure in their own words.

## Positioning map

A 2x2 is useful only if both axes are the ones the buyer actually trades off. Common ones that work:

- Price vs speed of delivery
- Breadth of catalogue vs depth of expertise
- Self-serve vs done-for-you
- Local presence vs national reach

Plot competitors by evidence, not by impression. If you cannot justify a placement with a source, leave it off. An empty quadrant is not an opportunity - it is usually empty because nobody can make money there. Say which.

## Market sizing that survives scrutiny

**Bottom-up, always.** See `research-design.md` for the arithmetic pattern. Then sanity-check:

| Check | Question |
|---|---|
| **Top-down reconciliation** | Does the bottom-up number sit within 3x of a credible top-down estimate? If not, one of them is wrong |
| **Capacity check** | Could the supply side even serve that number? (beds, trucks, technicians, licences) |
| **Willingness to pay** | Is there evidence anyone pays this price today, or is it all modelled? |
| **Share realism** | Is the assumed share achievable with the sales capacity they have? |
| **Adjacency** | Is the "market" actually several markets stapled together? |

## Demand signals in India, ranked

| Signal | Strength | Caveat |
|---|---|---|
| Existing paid demand on a marketplace (IndiaMART enquiries, Amazon reviews count, GeM bid history) | Strong | Enquiry volume includes tyre-kickers |
| Competitors hiring for the role you would need | Medium-strong | Hiring lags demand |
| Trade body membership and expo exhibitor lists | Medium | Lists are public and often stale |
| Search volume | Medium | Small absolute numbers are noise |
| GST registrations in the category | Medium | Does not indicate buying |
| A consultant's report | Weak | Definitions vary wildly |
| "Everyone I asked said they would buy" | Very weak | Stated intent is not demand |

## Buyer interviews, done properly

- **Recruit against the buying criteria**, not convenience.
- **Ask about the last purchase**, in sequence: what triggered it, who was involved, what they compared, what they paid, what nearly stopped them.
- **Ask what they did instead**, before and after.
- **Never pitch.** A pitched interview produces politeness, and politeness is not data.
- **Five interviews** for a narrow B2B question; write down the disagreements, not just the consensus.

## Anti-patterns

- A competitor matrix built entirely from homepages. Every claim in it is marketing copy.
- Sizing the market top-down and stopping there.
- Counting the whole category as addressable when 80% of it buys through distributors you cannot reach.
- Assuming an empty quadrant is opportunity.
- Listing 20 competitors. The decision needs the three that take the deal.
- Ignoring the substitute - "they do it in Excel" - which is usually the real competitor.
- Treating a funded competitor's pricing as the market price. It is often a subsidy.
