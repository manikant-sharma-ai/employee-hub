# Company and people diligence

The question is always the same: **is this entity what it says it is, and can it do what it is promising?** This file is the checklist, ordered by how often it catches something.

## The ten-minute screen

Before any deep work, run these. Most problems surface here.

| Check | Where | Red flag |
|---|---|---|
| Does the legal entity exist, and since when | MCA21 company/LLP search; Udyam for MSMEs | Incorporated three months ago but claims ten years of experience |
| Who are the directors, and are they directors elsewhere | MCA21 director details | Fifteen shell companies, or a struck-off history |
| Is it active or struck off / dormant | MCA21 status | "Strike off" or repeated "under process" filings |
| GST registration and filing status | GST portal, GSTIN validation | GSTIN invalid, or registration in a different state than claimed |
| Registered address | MCA21, GST, Google Street View | A residential flat, a virtual office, or nothing there |
| Domain age and site quality | WHOIS, the site itself | Domain six months old, template site, no address, no names |
| Reviews and complaints | Google, Play Store, consumer forums | Complaints about non-delivery, not about quality |
| Court and regulatory record | e-courts, NCLT, SEBI orders, RBI penalties | Repeated recovery suits or a regulator order |

Checks against government registers must be done live, in this session, and cited with the date. A register checked last year is not a check.

## Financial reality

| What to ask for | What it tells you | What it does not |
|---|---|---|
| Audited financials, 2-3 years | Revenue, profit, net worth, borrowing | Cash flow, unless you read the cash flow statement |
| GST returns summary | Declared turnover, consistency | Profitability |
| Bank statements, 6 months | Whether money actually moves | Future ability |
| Ageing of receivables | Whether their customers pay | Whether they will pay you |
| Top-5 customer concentration | Fragility | Anything about their pipeline |

Rules:

- **Turnover on the GST portal should roughly match the audited revenue.** A large gap is either misreporting or a different entity.
- **Net worth negative plus a large advance request is the classic failure pattern.**
- **A company that cannot produce an audited statement is a company you deal with on delivery, not on advance.**
- **Related-party transactions** in the filings tell you whether the "revenue" is from the promoter's other company.

## People

- **Directors and promoters:** other directorships, prior struck-off companies, any disqualification under the Companies Act.
- **Key person dependency:** is the whole business one person? Ask what happens if they are unavailable for a month.
- **Reference calls:** two, not one, and never the references they chose. Ask for a customer who stopped buying.
- **Public footprint:** LinkedIn history, conference talks, filings as a director elsewhere. Inconsistency between the pitch and the record is the finding.
- **Ex-employees** are the most informative source and the hardest to find. Ask in the trade.

## Capability checks

| Claim | How to test it |
|---|---|
| "We manufacture" | Factory address, a video call walk-through, GST registration at the plant, a site visit for anything material |
| "We have 200 clients" | Ask for three logos and permission to call one |
| "ISO certified" | Ask for the certificate number and check the certification body's register |
| "We are the largest" | Ask for the basis. There usually is none |
| "We have done this for a bank" | Ask which bank, which project, and who signed |

## The advance-payment question

The most common place an Indian SMB loses money. Before any advance:

1. **Verify the bank account name matches the legal entity name exactly.** A mismatch is the single strongest fraud signal there is.
2. **Prefer a small first order** over a large advance. A supplier who refuses a small paid trial is telling you something.
3. **Split the advance against milestones** with written acceptance criteria.
4. **Get it in writing**: scope, delivery date, penalty for delay, and who owns the tooling or the IP.
5. **Never send money to a personal account** for a business transaction, however normal it seems.

## Reporting diligence findings

Diligence output is not a verdict, it is a risk register.

| Finding | Severity | What it means for the decision |
|---|---|---|
| Bank account name does not match entity | Critical | Do not pay. Resolve before anything else |
| GSTIN invalid or suspended | High | No compliant invoice; input credit at risk |
| Entity incorporated recently with large claims | Medium | Verify capability directly, do not rely on track record |
| Negative net worth, large advance requested | High | Deal on delivery or not at all |
| Excellent record, one weak reference | Low | Proceed, with a milestone structure |

Say what you checked, when, and what you could not check. "I could not verify the factory because it needs a site visit" is a finding, not a failure.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating a good website as evidence of a real business.
- Checking a register once and citing it a year later.
- Asking only the references the supplier provided.
- Reporting a clean screen as "no risk". It means "no risk found in ten minutes", which is a different sentence.
- Doing diligence after the advance is paid.
- Confusing a big brand client logo with a big contract.
- Presenting suspicion as fact. Write "the registered address is a residential flat" and let it speak.
