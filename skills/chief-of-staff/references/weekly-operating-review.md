# The weekly operating review

Forty-five minutes, one page, four numbers, three decisions. It replaces the
status meeting, the founder's inbox scan and the monthly panic.

## The four numbers

Choose them by one test: if this number moves, does the business change? Four is
the maximum. More than four and the review becomes a dashboard reading.

| Slot | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Money in | Revenue or collected cash, this week | Rs 8.4 lakh collected, target 9.0 |
| Money kept | Gross margin or contribution | 38%, target 40% |
| Pipeline or demand | New qualified conversations, orders or signups | 41 new enquiries, 12 qualified |
| The one operational constraint | The thing currently limiting growth | Dispatch backlog: 96 orders, 3 days old |

Every number carries: value, target, last week's value, and one line of reason if
it is off. A number with no target is a fact, not a metric.

## The one-pager

```
WEEK <n> - <date range>
1. The numbers            value / target / last week / why
2. Commitments            closed n | slipped n | new n
3. Decisions needed       <question> - <options> - <deadline>
4. Three priorities       <priority> - <owner> - <what done looks like>
5. The one risk           <what could break the quarter>
```

One page. If it does not fit, the review is trying to do too much.

## The 45 minutes

| Minutes | Item | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 0-10 | The numbers, against target | The one number that is the problem this week |
| 10-20 | Commitments: closed, slipped, escalated | A date for every slipped item, or a decision to drop it |
| 20-35 | Decisions needed | A decision, an owner, a reversal condition |
| 35-42 | Three priorities for the week | Owner per priority, and what "done" looks like |
| 42-45 | The one risk | Who watches it, and the trigger to act |

## The cadence around it

| Rhythm | When | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Daily stand-up, 10 min | Every morning, small team | Blocks only - no status |
| Weekly review, 45 min | Same day and time, every week | Decisions and priorities |
| Monthly business review, 90 min | First week of the month | Trend, cash, hiring, one deep topic |
| Quarterly planning, half day | Before the quarter starts | Three to five priorities with numbers |

## Making the numbers trustworthy

- **One source per number**, named on the sheet. If two systems disagree, the review says which one it uses and why.
- **Same definition every week.** A metric that changes definition cannot show a trend.
- **Say when a number is estimated.** An estimated number that looks precise is worse than an honest gap.
- **The week's number is not the month's.** Weekly revenue is noisy; look at the four-week average before reacting.

## Anti-patterns

- **Fourteen metrics.** Everyone reads their own and nobody sees the business.
- **A review that reports and never decides.** It becomes a meeting people skip.
- **A moving slot.** Tuesday this week, Thursday next week, gone by the third.
- **Ninety minutes.** Length is where reviews go to die; the constraint is what makes them decisive.
- **No owner per priority.** Three priorities with no names is a wish list.
- **The founder presenting everything.** The review is where owners report; the founder decides.
