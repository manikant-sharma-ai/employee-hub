# Commitment tracking

Every business runs on a list of things people said they would do. The list is
almost never written down, which is why the same three items have been "in
progress" since March.

## The tracker

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| Commitment | "Send the revised proposal to Tata Elxsi" |
| Owner | Priya |
| Promised to | Founder / client / team |
| Date | 12 November |
| Status | Open, done, slipped, dropped |
| Last touched | 11 November |
| Blocker | Waiting on pricing from finance |
| Times slipped | 1 |

One sheet, filtered views by owner. Visible to the team - a commitment nobody can
see is a commitment nobody keeps.

## The closure rule

A commitment is closed only when the **thing exists** - the document sent, the
invoice raised, the fix deployed - not when the person says it is nearly done.
"Sent" means an email exists. "Fixed" means it was checked.

## The escalation ladder

| Signal | Action |
|---|---|
| Date passes, no update | Ask the owner directly, in writing, same day |
| Slipped once | New date agreed in the review, in the tracker, with the reason |
| Slipped twice | The blocker gets an owner and a date of its own |
| Slipped three times | It becomes a decision: commit to a date now, or drop it publicly |
| Untouched for 7 days | Named in the review, with the owner present |

The third slip is the important one. An item that has slipped three times is not
late - it is something nobody has decided to do, and the honest move is to stop
pretending.

## What gets tracked, and what does not

| Track | Do not track |
|---|---|
| Commitments made to a customer | Vague intentions from a brainstorm |
| Commitments that unblock someone else | Anything reversible in an hour |
| Decisions with a date | Routine recurring tasks, which belong in a checklist |
| Cross-team dependencies | Personal reminders |

A tracker with 90 items is a second inbox. Twenty to thirty live commitments is
about the ceiling for a small business.

## The weekly close

Fifteen minutes before the review:

1. Update the status of everything touched this week.
2. Mark anything past its date as slipped, not "in progress".
3. Flag items untouched for seven days.
4. Count: opened, closed, slipped. That ratio is the health of the business's word.

## Anti-patterns

- **Status by memory.** If it is not updated by the owner, it is not updated.
- **"In progress" for three weeks.** Progress has a shape; if it cannot be described, it is not happening.
- **Assigning in a meeting without acceptance.** The owner agrees the date, or the date does not exist.
- **Chasing in private.** Escalation is visible; that is what makes it work.
- **Tracking everything.** The tracker becomes the thing people avoid.
