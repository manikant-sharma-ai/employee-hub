# The founder's time and delegation

The founder is the scarcest resource in a small business and the least audited one.
This file is the quarterly audit and the delegation ladder that follows it.

## The time audit, from the calendar

Two weeks of calendar, no judgement, sorted into five buckets:

| Bucket | Test | Typical share in a founder-led SMB |
|---|---|---|
| Only I can do this | A decision, a relationship, a hire, a raise, a public commitment | Should be 30-40% |
| Someone else could do this | Reporting, coordination, follow-ups, drafting | Should approach 20% |
| Nobody should do this | Meetings with no decision, status updates, rework | Should approach 0% |
| The work that moves a number | Selling, building, hiring, shipping | Should be 40%+ |
| The rest | Admin, email, firefighting | The honest leftover |

The audit is done from the calendar, not from memory. Memory reports intention.

## The four Ds

For every recurring item in the "someone else could do this" bucket:

- **Delete** - does this need to happen at all? Most weekly reports do not.
- **Decide once** - turn a recurring judgement into a written rule.
- **Delegate** - hand it to one person with the outcome and the deadline.
- **Do** - if it is genuinely founder work, put it in a protected block.

## The delegation ladder

Handing over a task badly is worse than keeping it. Five rungs, and each one takes
a cycle:

| Rung | What the founder does | What the delegate does |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Do exactly this | Shows the task, the template, the standard | Executes, asks on any deviation |
| 2. Recommend, then I decide | States the outcome and the constraints | Proposes, waits for a yes |
| 3. Decide, tell me after | Agrees the boundary and the exception | Acts, reports within a day |
| 4. Decide, report weekly | Agrees the metric | Owns the outcome, reports the number |
| 5. Own it | Reviews the metric quarterly | Runs it, hires for it, sets the standard |

Skipping from rung 1 to rung 5 is the classic failure: the founder is surprised by
a decision, the delegate is blamed for judgement they were never given.

## What only the founder can do

- Decide what the company will not do.
- Hire and fire the people who report to them.
- Set the number that everything else is measured against.
- Own the relationships that cannot be transferred: the largest customer, the
  investor, the key partner, the bank.
- Make the public commitment.

Everything else is a candidate for delegation, including pricing, hiring process,
reporting and customer escalations.

## Hiring triggers

The audit produces a hiring case when a bucket is consistently above a threshold
for two quarters. Write it as arithmetic, not as a feeling:

"Founder spends 11 hours a week on support escalations; an executive at ₹45,000 a
month covers 80% of that; the 11 hours at the founder's selling rate are worth more
than the salary if one extra deal a quarter closes." Then decide.

## Anti-patterns

- **Delegating the task and keeping the anxiety.** Checking every output at rung 3
  is rung 2 with extra steps.
- **Hiring to fix a process problem.** A support hire does not fix a product that
  generates the tickets.
- **The founder's calendar as the company's calendar.** Everything urgent gravitates
  to the founder unless someone else owns the triage.
- **The protected block that gets moved.** The first meeting that displaces it
  teaches the company it is optional.
- **The audit done once.** Time drifts back within a quarter; the audit is quarterly
  or it is theatre.
