# Follow-up enforcement

Every operating system in a small business dies the same way: the register exists,
nobody sweeps it, and by week three the entries are fiction.

## The register

| Field | Rule |
|---|---|
| Action | One verb, one object: "send the revised quote to Sharma Traders" |
| Owner | One person's name. Not a team, not "marketing" |
| Due | A date, not "next week". No date, no entry |
| Source | The meeting and date it came from, so it can be traced |
| Status | Open, done, dropped, superseded |
| Evidence | The document, the message or the number that proves it is done |

The register is a sheet until it outgrows one, and that is fine. A tool nobody
opens is worse than a sheet everybody can.

## The weekly sweep

Every Monday morning, before the review:

1. **List what is due this week**, by owner. Send each owner their own list only.
2. **List what is overdue**, with the age in days.
3. **List what has no owner**, and put it in the review as a decision: assign it or
   drop it. An unassigned action is not a task; it is a decision avoided.
4. **Send the founder's list first**, and separately. The register is credible only
   if the founder is swept like everyone else.

## The escalation ladder

| Age | What happens | Tone |
|---|---|---|
| Due today | One direct message: "due today, is it done or do we need a new date?" | Neutral |
| 2 days late | Named in the review with the age, no commentary | Factual |
| 1 week late | A conversation about whether it will happen at all; re-date once, or drop it | Direct |
| 2 weeks late | It becomes a decision: reassign, reduce scope, or cancel. The action is closed either way | Decisive |
| Repeated pattern | The person's commitments go into the review by name every week until the pattern breaks | Firm |

Never escalate by copying a growing list of people. Escalation is one owner, one
conversation, one date.

## Why commitments slip

| Cause | The fix |
|---|---|
| No date | Reject undated actions at capture |
| Too many at once | Cap open items per person at five; beyond that, choose |
| Unclear what "done" means | Write the evidence field at capture |
| Waiting on someone else | The waiting is the action: "chase X by Friday" |
| Not really agreed | Confirm in writing within 24 hours of the meeting |
| Nobody asks | The weekly sweep, which is the whole point |

## The nudge that works

Three lines, no preamble:

> "Revised quote to Sharma Traders - due Tuesday. Is it done, or what is the new
> date? If it is blocked, name the blocker and I will take it to the review."

It contains the action, the date, a binary question and a path for being stuck.
Anything longer reads as a chase, and a chase invites an excuse.

## Closing properly

An action is closed when the evidence exists - the document sent, the payment
made, the change shipped - not when someone says "done". The evidence field is the
reason the register can be trusted in month six.

Dropping is also closing. "We are not doing this" with a reason is a better
outcome than an action that ages in the register for a quarter.

## Anti-patterns

- **The chase by group message.** Public pressure produces compliance and hides
  the blocker.
- **The register that only tracks other people.** The founder's items are the ones
  that teach the system what it is for.
- **The list of forty open items.** Beyond five per person, the register is a
  storage device, not a commitment system.
- **The re-date without a conversation.** Silently moving a date is how a register
  becomes a fiction.
- **Closing on a verbal "done".** The evidence is the closure.
- **Escalating to the founder for everything.** The ladder exists so that most
  things resolve at the first rung.
