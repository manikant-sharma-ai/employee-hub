# Meeting design

A meeting is an expensive instrument: nine people for an hour is a day of work.
Most recurring meetings in a small business survive because nobody has asked
whether they should.

## The four questions before any meeting

| Question | If the answer is no |
|---|---|
| What decision or output comes out of this? | Do not hold it; send a message |
| Who must be in the room for that output? | Everyone else gets the note |
| How long does that actually take? | Halve the invite |
| What happens if we skip it this week? | Skip it |

## The standard agenda

```
Purpose (one line)
Output (the decision or the document)
Pre-read (sent 24 hours before)
1. <item> - <owner> - <minutes>
2. <item> - <owner> - <minutes>
Decisions to take
Actions: what / who / when
```

No agenda, no meeting. An agenda sent at the start of the meeting is a formality.

## The standing meetings a small business actually needs

| Meeting | Frequency | Length | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Daily stand-up | Daily | 10 min | Blocks, and who is unblocking them |
| Weekly review | Weekly | 45 min | Numbers, decisions, three priorities |
| Sales pipeline | Weekly | 30 min | Moves per deal, and the ones to kill |
| 1:1 | Fortnightly | 30 min | The person's agenda, not the manager's status |
| Monthly business review | Monthly | 90 min | Trend, cash, hiring, one deep topic |
| Quarterly planning | Quarterly | Half day | Three to five priorities with numbers |

Everything else is project-specific and should have an end date.

## Running it

- **Start on time, with the output named.** "By 11:45 we will have decided X."
- **Park the tangent, visibly.** A list called "parked" on the screen, and a decision at the end on whether it becomes an item.
- **One conversation at a time.** Side chats are a signal the meeting has too many people.
- **Decisions out loud, then written.** "So the decision is X, owner Y, by Z" - and the note repeats it.
- **End three minutes early.** The note is written in those three minutes, not at night.

## The meeting audit

Once a quarter, list every recurring meeting with its cost:

| Meeting | People | Hours a month | Output last quarter | Keep, shorten, kill |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | |

Cost is people x hours x a loaded hourly rate. A weekly one-hour meeting with six
people is roughly 26 person-hours a month - usually more than the problem costs.

## Anti-patterns

- **The status meeting.** Everyone reads what they could have written.
- **The meeting that exists because it always has.** Nobody remembers starting it.
- **The 30-minute meeting that needs 90.** The length is set by the calendar slot, not the work.
- **No note.** A meeting without a written outcome did not happen.
- **Deciding with the wrong room.** The person who will do the work is not present, so the action dies.
