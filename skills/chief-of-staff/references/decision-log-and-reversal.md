# The decision log and the reversal condition

A decision recorded only in memory gets re-argued every quarter, and the version
that survives is the one the loudest person remembers.

## The entry

| Field | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Date | When it was decided, not when it was written |
| Decision | One sentence, in the affirmative: "we will not enter Ahmedabad in FY27" |
| Owner | Who is accountable for it being carried out |
| Reason | The evidence at the time, in one or two lines |
| Reversal condition | The specific fact that would make this wrong |
| Review date | When it gets looked at again, even if nothing changed |
| Status | Active, superseded, reversed |

## The reversal condition is the point

| Bad | Good |
|---|---|
| "We will revisit if things change" | "We reopen this if monthly enquiries from Gujarat exceed 40 for two consecutive months" |
| "If the market improves" | "If the competitor's Ahmedabad store closes or their pricing drops below ours" |
| "If it does not work" | "If net contribution from the new line is negative after 90 days" |

A reversal condition must be observable, dated and specific enough that two people
would agree on the day it triggers. If it cannot be written, the decision is not
yet a decision - it is a preference.

## What goes in the log

- **Strategic choices** - markets, products, pricing, hiring plans, partnerships.
- **Expensive reversals** - anything that costs money or reputation to undo.
- **Contested decisions** - where the team disagreed, so the reasoning is preserved.
- **Decisions made under pressure** - a price cut, a client accepted, a hire rushed.

Not in the log: routine operations, anything reversible in a day, and personal
matters. A log of everything is a log nobody reads.

## Reviewing the log

Monthly, ten minutes, three questions:

1. **Which reversal conditions are close to triggering?** Those need a decision this month, not next.
2. **Which decisions have been quietly abandoned?** An unexecuted decision is a decision to not do it, and should be recorded as such.
3. **Which decisions were wrong, and what did we learn?** Write the lesson, not the blame.

## Decision quality, not decision speed

| Signal of a good decision process | Signal of a bad one |
|---|---|
| The options were written down, including "do nothing" | Only one option was discussed |
| The evidence was named, with its source | The evidence was "everyone knows" |
| The cost of being wrong was stated | Nobody asked what happens if it fails |
| The person who disagreed said so, on record | Dissent was smoothed over in the room |
| The reversal condition was written before deciding | Revisiting starts only when it hurts |

## Anti-patterns

- **The log as a scoreboard.** Used to prove someone wrong later, it stops people contributing.
- **Decisions recorded without owners.** They are minutes, not decisions.
- **No review date.** The decision is made once and never revisited, including when the world changes.
- **Retro-editing.** A log that gets rewritten to match what happened is worthless; supersede the old entry instead.
- **The log in someone's head.** If it is not in the shared sheet, it does not exist.
