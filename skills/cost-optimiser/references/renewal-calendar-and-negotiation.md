# Renewal calendar and negotiation

The cheapest saving in this whole discipline is a decision made sixty days before a renewal instead
of sixty days after it.

## The calendar

One row per subscription that auto-renews or has a term.

| Field | Why |
|---|---|
| Vendor and plan | The thing being renewed |
| Renewal date | The date the term restarts |
| Notice period | The date a cancellation or change must be notified - often 30-60 days before renewal |
| Decision date | Notice date minus 14 days - the date somebody must decide |
| Owner | Who decides |
| Current price and last change | The anchor for the negotiation |
| Target price | What the business will accept |

Set the decision date as a calendar event with the owner and the price in the title. A renewal
calendar that lives in a spreadsheet nobody opens is a spreadsheet.

## The negotiation, in five moves

1. **Know the list price and the current contract price.** A discount on list is not a discount if you
   are already below list.
2. **Get the usage data before the call.** "We have 25 seats and 16 active users" is the strongest
   sentence in a renewal conversation, and it is not an argument - it is a fact the vendor cannot
   dispute.
3. **Ask for the same product cheaper before asking for a different product.** Downgrade the plan,
   reduce the seats, remove the add-on. Then ask for the price.
4. **Have a real alternative.** The credible alternative is not a threat, it is a BATNA. If there is
   none, the negotiation is a request.
5. **Get it in writing, with the term and the price, before the renewal date.** A verbal discount that
   appears on next month's invoice at the old price is the standard outcome.

## What vendors actually concede

| Ask | Likely outcome | When |
|---|---|---|
| Reduce seats mid-term | Usually allowed at renewal, sometimes immediately | Anytime, better at renewal |
| Downgrade the plan | Allowed at renewal | At renewal |
| Waive the price increase | Often, if asked before renewal | 30-60 days before |
| Multi-year discount | 10-30% | Only if the tool is certain to stay |
| Payment terms (annual in advance to quarterly) | Rarely, and usually costs more | — |
| A credit for unused seats | Sometimes, for a mid-term reduction | With usage data |
| Removing a mandatory add-on | Sometimes, if it was bundled silently | At renewal, with the invoice in hand |

## The price-increase letter

Vendors send an increase notice 30-90 days before renewal. Three responses, in order of preference:

1. **Ask for the old price with a one-year commitment** - the most common successful ask.
2. **Downgrade the plan and keep the price flat** - same budget, fewer features.
3. **Reduce seats to the active count and accept the increase** - net saving, which is the number that
   matters.

Never accept a price increase without doing the seat audit first. The increase on 25 seats where 16
are used is often fully offset by removing nine.

## The annual-versus-monthly decision at renewal

| Situation | Decide |
|---|---|
| Used daily, core, no migration planned | Annual, with the discount, on a card that is watched |
| Under review, or usage uncertain | Monthly, and accept the premium |
| Seasonal | Monthly, or a seasonal plan if offered |
| Discount under 10% | Monthly - flexibility is worth more than 10% |
| Cash is tight this quarter | Monthly, even at a premium - this is a treasury decision, not a procurement one |

## The renewal that should not happen

Some subscriptions should simply end. The test, before renewing anything:

- Did anyone open it in the last 90 days?
- Would anyone notice if it disappeared tomorrow?
- Is there a free or included alternative already paid for?
- Is the job it does still a job the business has?

Two "no" answers and the answer is cancel, with the export done first and the data archived.

## Anti-patterns

- **Renewing because the invoice arrived.** An invoice is not a decision.
- **Cancelling on the renewal date.** The notice period already passed; the business pays for another
  year.
- **Negotiating without usage data.** The vendor has the numbers and you have an opinion.
- **Accepting a multi-year discount on a tool under review.** Lock-in on a question you have not
  answered.
- **Letting the founder's assistant own the renewal calendar.** Ownership belongs to the person who
  uses the tool, with the calendar as the enforcement.
