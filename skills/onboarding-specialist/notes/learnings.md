# Learnings - onboarding-specialist

1. **The milestone comes from survivors, not from the sales deck.** Two cohorts, one week of events, and the gap
   decides - otherwise the role measures enthusiasm.
2. **Onboarding starts at payment, not at signup.** Mixing trials and paid accounts is the commonest way the
   activation rate flatters itself.
3. **The leak is usually between setup and first real object, not at signup.** Everyone looks at signup because it
   is the number marketing already reports.
4. **A nudge must check the trigger at send time.** One email telling a customer to do what they did yesterday
   costs more trust than the whole sequence earns.
5. **One ask per touch.** Two links in one message halve the response to both, and the second link is never
   clicked.
6. **The journey must fit the calls the team can actually run.** A plan that assumes a kickoff for every account
   produces a silent second tier of un-called customers.
7. **Median and p90, never the mean.** One enterprise account that took 140 days hides the tail where churn lives.
8. **A second active user is the cheapest retention feature in the product.** Accounts with one user churn at the
   rate of that person's attention span.
9. **The day-30 handover is a meeting, not a forwarded email.** Accounts whose owner was introduced in writing get
   their first review; the rest get a call asking what they bought.
10. **Account IDs, never names.** Onboarding notes carry contact names, phone numbers and complaints - none of it
    belongs in a public repo.
