# Kickoff and success criteria

The kickoff call is the only meeting in the whole relationship where the customer will happily tell you what
"working" means, because nothing has gone wrong yet. Everything downstream is measured against what is agreed
here, so the call has one deliverable: a written definition of success with a number and a date in it.

## Before the call

- **Read what was sold.** The proposal, the quote, the last email thread. The customer bought an outcome; the
  contract lists features. Bring the outcome, not the feature list.
- **Know the activation event.** One sentence: "this account is live when ___". If sales and product disagree,
  settle it before the call, not in front of the customer.
- **Name the attendees you need.** The champion who will do the work, the decision maker who can unblock, and the
  technical or finance contact if access or money is involved. A kickoff with only the champion is a meeting that
  has to be repeated.
- **Send the agenda a day ahead** with the three questions you will ask. An agenda is a courtesy; the three
  questions are the meeting.

## The three questions

1. **"What has to be true in ninety days for this to have been worth the money?"** Their words, then your number.
2. **"Who does what, and who signs off that it is done?"** The answer names the customer-side owner of each step -
   the single most useful fact you will get all quarter.
3. **"What has to be true on your side for the date to hold?"** Data, access, approvals, a person's time. Write
   these down as their commitments, with dates, and read them back.

## Turning answers into criteria

A usable success criterion has four parts. Anything less is a wish.

| Part | Example |
|---|---|
| The action | "all dispatch invoices raised in the system, not in Excel" |
| The number | "at least 90% of October's invoices" |
| The date | "by 30 November" |
| The witness | "confirmed by their finance manager on the day-thirty call" |

## What to write in the recap

Send it the same day, in this order: what we heard success means, the four-part criterion, the milestone plan
with dates and owners, what we need from them and by when, what happens next and when. Five short sections, no
preamble. The recap is the contract for the next thirty days, and the first place a misunderstanding becomes
visible while it is still cheap.

## Common failures

- **A kickoff that becomes a product demo.** The customer learns features instead of agreeing an outcome. Cap the
  demo at ten minutes and put the three questions first.
- **Success agreed with the wrong person.** A champion can agree an outcome they cannot authorise. Ask who signs
  off, and get that person on the second call if not the first.
- **No number because "it is early".** It is never too early to agree a number; it is only early to be held to a
  wrong one, which is why the criterion has a recheck date.
- **The customer's dependencies left verbal.** Their data, their approval, their IT - each one is a dated line in
  the recap, or it becomes the reason the date slipped and nobody's fault.
