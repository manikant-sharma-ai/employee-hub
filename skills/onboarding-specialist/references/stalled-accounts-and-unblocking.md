# Stalled accounts and unblocking

An account is stalled when it has missed a dated milestone. Not when the customer is quiet - quiet is a symptom,
a missed date is the fact. The list is built from the plan, not from how anyone feels about the account.

## Classify before chasing

| Reason | How to tell | The unblock |
|---|---|---|
| Their data is not ready | The export has not arrived, or arrives malformed | Offer to take it in any format; take the first 100 rows and start |
| Their approval is pending | A named person has not signed | Go to the decision maker with a one-line ask and a date |
| Their people are busy | Meetings moved twice, champion apologetic | Shrink the ask to one 20-minute call, or do it asynchronously |
| Our setup is late | A delivery ticket is open | Chase the delivery owner; give the customer the new date yourself |
| The champion left or moved | Replies stop, a new name appears | Ask for the replacement in one line, and re-run the kickoff in 20 minutes |
| The product does not do it | A gap surfaces at first run | Say so, with the workaround and the date it will be fixed - or the scope change |
| The priority moved | Silence, and a new project mentioned | Ask the decision maker directly whether this is still a Q3 project |

## Order the list by value at risk

Chase in order of revenue times probability, not alphabetically: annual contract value, the days overdue, and
whether the customer is past a payment milestone. An account that has paid and gone quiet is a different problem
from one that has not paid and gone quiet - the second is also a `collections-agent` conversation.

## The chase

Two attempts, each specific, each with a date, then escalate.

1. **First touch** - the missed step, why it matters, two concrete options with times. To the champion, copy
   nobody.
2. **Second touch, three working days later** - the same step, the consequence of the delay stated plainly, and a
   question that can be answered in one line. Copy the decision maker if the first went unanswered.
3. **Escalation** - the account owner who sold the deal makes the call, with the value at risk and the two
   options. This is not a complaint; it is a request for a decision.

## What not to do

- **Do not send a generic "just checking in".** It teaches the customer that our messages carry no information.
- **Do not re-plan silently.** If the date moves, the plan changes in writing and the customer sees the new date.
- **Do not promise a feature to unblock a deal.** A date from the product owner, or an honest "not this quarter".
- **Do not blame the customer in writing.** State the dependency, its owner and its date. The record speaks.
- **Do not keep a stalled account on the happy list.** The report is the stalled list; a green dashboard over a
  stuck account is the failure this role exists to prevent.

## When to stop

An account stalled past twice its planned onboarding window with no response is escalated to the founder with the
value, the history and a recommendation: continue, re-scope, or refund. That is a commercial decision, and it is
made with the numbers in front of it rather than by attrition.
