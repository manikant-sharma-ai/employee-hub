# The day-30 handover

The handover is where onboarding either becomes an account relationship or quietly restarts. A handover that is a
forwarded email is the reason the new account owner calls the customer to ask what they bought.

## The activation score

One number, three components, all observable.

| Component | Weight | Measure |
|---|---|---|
| Milestone reached | 50 | Activated within the window: yes/no, and days taken |
| Depth | 30 | Core objects created against the segment's median |
| Breadth | 20 | Active users in the account against the segment's median |

Score it 0-100 and band it: **green** 70+, **amber** 40-69, **red** under 40. The band drives the first contact
date the account owner commits to, not a label on a slide.

## What the handover note carries

1. **Who bought and why** - the buyer's words at purchase, the job they said it was for, and the alternative they
   were using before.
2. **The activation score and its parts**, with the days taken.
3. **What they have actually done** - objects created, users active, integrations live, in counts.
4. **What they have not done**, and whether it is a blocker or a choice.
5. **Every human contact so far** - who, when, what was promised, what was delivered. Unkept promises are the
   first thing the account owner must fix.
6. **The open risk** - the one thing most likely to cause a renewal problem, with the evidence.
7. **The next step and its date**, agreed with the customer rather than invented.
8. **What is not known** - the connector that was missing, the question nobody asked.

## The handover meeting, fifteen minutes

- Onboarding reads the note, not the CRM screen.
- The account owner asks two questions: *what did we promise that we have not done*, and *who actually uses it*.
- The customer is introduced in writing the same day, by name, with the next contact date.
- The onboarding employee leaves the thread. Two owners is worse than a slow handover.

## Red accounts at day 30

Roughly a fifth of accounts will not be activated at handover. Three options, decided explicitly:

| Option | When | Who owns it |
|---|---|---|
| Extend onboarding by 14 days | A named blocker with a date | onboarding, with the account owner informed |
| Hand over as red | No blocker, low engagement, or the buyer has changed | account owner, first contact within 48h |
| Return to sales | Wrong fit, wrong expectation set at purchase | sales owner and the founder |

Do not extend silently. An account that has been in onboarding for 60 days has an owner problem, not an
onboarding problem.

## Anti-patterns

- A handover that is a link to a dashboard. The account owner needs the story, not the chart.
- Marking an account green because the milestone was hit by one enthusiastic user in a team of twenty.
- Handing over without the customer knowing who now owns them.
- Keeping the account "for a bit" because the relationship is good - that is how renewal dates get missed.

## What the account owner does with it in week one

Three contacts, in this order, and none of them a check-in call.

1. **A written introduction**, naming themselves, the next date and the one thing they will do - sent the day of
   handover, from their own address.
2. **The unblock**, if a promise is outstanding. Fixing a broken promise is worth more than any onboarding
   improvement in the first week.
3. **The second-user conversation**, because breadth is the component of the score that decays fastest and the one
   the onboarding role could not finish.

## Measuring the handover itself

- **Accepted without a follow-up question** - the share of handovers the account owner did not have to email back
  about. Below 80% means the note is missing something.
- **First contact inside the committed date** - the account owner's promise, tracked, because the handover is only
  as good as the contact that follows it.
- **Score at day 60 against score at day 30** - a green account that fell to amber in a month is an onboarding
  problem discovered late, and it belongs in next month's read.
