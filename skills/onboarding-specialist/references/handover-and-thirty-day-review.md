# Handover and the thirty-day review

Day thirty is where onboarding either becomes a habit or becomes a memory. The review has one purpose: transfer
the account to the people who will run it for the next year, with everything they need already written down.

## The review, in four questions

1. **Did the criterion hold?** The four-part criterion from the kickoff, the evidence, and the witness who
   confirmed it. If it did not hold, that is the first line of the review, not a footnote.
2. **What does the customer now do weekly?** The habit, its frequency, and who inside the customer does it. If
   the answer is "they log in sometimes", the account is not onboarded.
3. **What is still open, and who owns it?** Every open item with an owner and a date, including the ones that are
   ours and embarrassing.
4. **What would make them leave?** The honest risk, in one line, from what the customer said and did - not from a
   health score.

## The handover pack

One page, completed from `assets/onboarding-plan.md`:

- **Who is who** - champion, decision maker, technical, finance, and what each actually cares about.
- **What was promised and what was delivered** - including the promises that were renegotiated, and when.
- **The habit and its evidence** - what they do weekly, and how we know.
- **Open items** - owner, date, and what happens if it slips.
- **The customer's own words** about success, quoted, because it is the best brief the next employee will get.
- **The support history** - tickets raised during onboarding, what they were really about, and the two that are
  likely to recur.

## What support needs that nobody thinks to send

- The exact configuration this customer runs, because it is not the default.
- The two workarounds they were taught, because they will ask again.
- The name of the person who answers when the champion is unavailable.
- The thing that nearly went wrong, so the first ticket about it is recognised in seconds.

## What customer-success needs

The risk line, the next natural milestone (a first quarter review, a first renewal conversation, an expansion
trigger), and the promise that must not be broken in month two. Not a score; a sentence with a date.

## Closing the loop

Send the customer a two-line note on day thirty: what they can now do, who looks after them, how to reach that
person. Then write the journal line and put the account's first-quarter milestone in the calendar. The account is
now someone else's - and the handover is finished only when they say they have what they need.
