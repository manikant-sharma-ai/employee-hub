# Onboarding sequence and comms

Every message here is a draft. The named owner sends it, from their own address, in their own voice. An
onboarding sent from a no-reply address is a process; onboarding sent by a person is a relationship.

## The sequence, by day

| When | Message | Contains |
|---|---|---|
| Same working day as signature | Welcome | Who is looking after them, the three things happening this week, the kickoff invite, one question: "who else should be in the room?" |
| Day 1-2 | Agenda | The three kickoff questions, the 30 minutes, what to bring |
| Same day as kickoff | Recap | Success in their words and our number, the milestone plan, their dependencies with dates |
| Each milestone | Confirmation | What is now done, what it unlocks, the next date |
| Mid-point | Check-in | One question about the work, not about satisfaction |
| Day 25-30 | Handover | What they can now do, who supports them from here, how to raise an issue |
| Day 30 | Review | The criterion, the evidence, the open items, the next quarter's target |

## Writing rules that apply to all of them

- **One ask per message.** Two asks in one email gets one answer, usually the easier one.
- **The subject line carries the date or the decision.** "Kickoff Thursday 11am" beats "Onboarding update".
- **Say the thing in the first line.** No "hope this finds you well"; the customer is at work.
- **Name the consequence of the deadline.** "If the export is not with us by the 12th, the go-live moves to the
  26th" is respectful; "kindly revert at the earliest" is not a date.
- **Short.** Five lines, one table at most, one link. Long onboarding mail is not read, it is filed.
- **No jargon from our side.** If the customer has to ask what a term means, the term is wrong.

## The welcome

It exists to remove the fear that signing was the end of the attention. Three sentences: who owns this from our
side, what happens this week, what we need from them first. Then the invite.

## The training

Training is not a milestone; it is a support to one. Prefer one 45-minute session on the customer's own data over
three generic webinars. Record it, send the recording with the three timestamps that matter, and put the written
steps in one page. A customer who needs to watch a 90-minute recording to do step two will not do step two.

## The recap is the most valuable message

It is the only place where the customer's expectation and our plan are written side by side. Five sections:
success, plan, their dependencies, what we did, what happens next. If the customer replies "that is not what we
agreed", you have saved a month.

## Silence is data

A customer who stops replying is not busy, usually: they are stuck, or the champion changed, or the priority
moved. One specific question about a named step gets an answer far more often than a general check-in. If two
specific questions go unanswered within a week, escalate to the account owner who sold the deal - they have a
reason to be answered.
