# Timing, frequency and consent

Persistence is a virtue up to a written limit and a legal problem after it. This file sets both.

## The frequency rules

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| One touch per deal per day, maximum | Two messages in a day reads as pressure and gets the number blocked |
| Two touches per deal per week, maximum | Beyond that the reply rate falls and the complaint rate rises |
| The buyer's stated date is absolute | If they said "after the 20th", nothing before the 20th |
| A reply resets the clock | Answer within the working day; the cadence restarts from the new state |
| A "no" ends the sequence | Not a pause. The reason is coded and the deal is closed |
| A "not now" parks the deal | With a re-contact date the buyer agreed to, and nothing in between |

## India-specific timing

| Period | What to do |
|---|---|
| Diwali week | No commercial follow-ups. A greeting to existing relationships is fine; a nudge to a prospect is not |
| Holi, Eid, Christmas, Pongal, Onam, Durga Puja, Ganesh Chaturthi | Same, in the relevant region - check which applies, do not apply one to all |
| Ganesh Chaturthi and Diwali in Maharashtra, Onam in Kerala, Pongal in Tamil Nadu | Regional, not national; a pan-India calendar is wrong for both |
| 25-31 March | Real deadline pressure for many buyers; the last week is when budget decisions are made |
| 1-5 April | Nobody is available; the new financial year starts with meetings |
| Month-end and quarter-end | Finance and procurement are closing; operations are reachable |
| Wedding season (Nov-Feb in much of India) | Retail and services buyers are unavailable; B2B is normal |
| Monsoon in trade-dependent businesses | Cash is tight; a payment-terms conversation may matter more than a nudge |
| Exam season (Feb-Apr) | Anything selling to schools, students or parents is out of scope for a nudge |

Write the local pattern into `notes/learnings.md` as it is observed, with the year and the segment. A festival
calendar copied from the internet will be wrong for half the country.

## Working hours and reply windows

- **Calls**: 10:30am-12:30pm and 3pm-5:30pm on Tuesday to Thursday have the best answer rates for most B2B trades.
  Monday morning is for internal meetings; Friday afternoon is for closing the week.
- **Email**: arriving 8-9am gets read that morning; arriving after 6pm gets buried.
- **WhatsApp**: a message at 9-10pm may be read, but it is also when a buyer is with family. Use it only if that
  is how the conversation already runs.
- **Sunday**: for retail and field trades it can be the busiest day of the week for the buyer. Ask, do not assume.
- **A buyer who replies at midnight** is working; reply the next morning, never with an automated message.

## Consent and the legal line

| Channel | What is required | What this role does |
|---|---|---|
| Email to an existing business contact | A prior relationship or a lawful basis, plus a working opt-out | Honour every unsubscribe, in every future touch |
| Email to a new contact | Consent or a clear legitimate-interest basis under the DPDP Act, plus identification | Never buys a list; that is `sales-outreach` and `email-marketer` territory |
| WhatsApp one-to-one | The number was given for this purpose, or the person wrote first | One-to-one only, never a broadcast list |
| WhatsApp business API | Opt-in recorded, template approved | The owner and the platform handle it; this role does not send |
| Phone call | Reasonable hour, no repeated calls after a refusal | Never after a stated do-not-call |
| SMS or DLT | Registered headers and templates | Out of scope; route to whoever owns the SMS account |

The DPDP Act applies to a prospect's personal data as much as a customer's: collect what the follow-up needs,
keep it while the relationship is live, erase on request. A do-not-contact instruction is absolute and must be
recorded somewhere the whole team can see.

## When to stop, in plain words

Stop and record `no-decision` when all four are true:

1. The stop rule for the stage is met.
2. The last two touches got no reply, not even a one-line no.
3. No new information has appeared that would justify another touch.
4. The buyer has not named a future date.

Stop immediately, and record a do-not-contact, when the buyer says any of: stop writing, not interested, remove
me, we have a vendor, do not call again - in any language. "Not now" is not a do-not-contact; "do not contact me"
is.

## Anti-patterns

- A sequence that runs through Diwali because the automation does not know what Diwali is.
- Following up on the day the buyer said they would be travelling.
- Calling a number that answered once, at 9pm, for three weeks.
- Treating a WhatsApp "seen" as a signal and escalating to a call the same hour.
- Continuing a sequence after a "no" because the CRM stage did not change.
- Sending a festival greeting to a prospect as a disguised follow-up, then asking for the decision in the same
  message.
- Assuming one national holiday calendar applies to a buyer in another state.
