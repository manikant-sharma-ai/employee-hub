# Translation versus transcreation

Every string sits somewhere on a line between "reproduce the words" and "reproduce the effect". Choosing the wrong point is the most common localisation failure, and it is a decision, not a talent.

## The decision table

| Content type | Mode | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Legal clause, terms, privacy notice | Literal, reviewed | A nuance added or dropped is a liability |
| Statutory labels (GST, TDS, invoice, e-way bill) | Official rendering only | A wrong term is a compliance problem |
| Error messages, validation text | Literal, plain | The user needs the fact, fast |
| UI labels, buttons, menu items | Literal, then shortened to fit | Consistency beats elegance here |
| Help articles, FAQs | Faithful translation | The reader is looking for an answer, not a voice |
| Product descriptions | Faithful with idiom repair | Facts fixed, phrasing local |
| Headlines, taglines, ad copy | Transcreation | The words are the mechanism, not the message |
| Email subject lines | Transcreation | They must work in the inbox, in that language |
| Social captions, memes, jokes | Transcreation or skip | A joke that has to be explained is not a joke |
| Anything with a pun, rhyme or rhythm | Transcreation | Literal will produce nonsense |

## What transcreation actually means

You keep: the intent, the register, the claim, the emotional beat.
You change: the metaphor, the idiom, the example, the sentence order, sometimes the whole sentence.

Example - a tagline:

> EN: "Small batch. Big difference."
> Literal HI: "छोटा बैच। बड़ा अंतर।" - grammatical, and it lands like a slogan translated from English.
> Transcreated HI: "थोड़ा बनाते हैं, फ़र्क़ दिखता है।" - "we make a little, and the difference shows."

Same claim, same rhythm of contrast, no borrowed structure.

## Idiom traps

| English | Literal result | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| "Get in touch" | "संपर्क में आएं" (stiff) | "बात कीजिए" / "हमसे पूछिए" |
| "Sign up" | "साइन अप करें" | Fine in most digital contexts; keep the English in Hinglish |
| "On the house" | nonsense | "हमारी तरफ़ से मुफ़्त" |
| "No strings attached" | nonsense | "कोई शर्त नहीं" |
| "Cutting edge" | "कटिंग एज" (meaningless) | "सबसे नया" |
| "Best in class" | "क्लास में सबसे अच्छा" (wrong sense) | "अपनी श्रेणी में सबसे बेहतर" |
| "Drop us a line" | nonsense | "एक मैसेज भेज दीजिए" |
| "Delivered to your doorstep" | "दरवाज़े की सीढ़ी तक" | "आपके घर तक" |

Rule of thumb: if the literal version requires the reader to know the English idiom, transcreate.

## Preserving honesty

Transcreation is not licence to add. Two things never change:

- **A limitation stays a limitation.** "Ships in 5-7 days" does not become "जल्दी डिलीवरी".
- **A qualifier stays a qualifier.** "May help" does not become "मदद करता है".

If the transcreated line would promise more than the source, it is not transcreation, it is a new claim that nobody approved.

## When to refuse

- **The source is a draft.** Translating it twice costs more than waiting a day.
- **The claim is legally loaded** and no reviewer is available.
- **The target register does not exist for the audience** - for example a brand with no Hindi voice trying to sound like a 20-year-old on Instagram.
- **The string is a pun built on English phonetics.** Say it does not travel and propose a replacement, rather than producing a line that dies.

## Anti-patterns

- Word-by-word substitution across a whole page.
- Translating a headline literally because "it is short".
- Transcreating a legal clause to make it "read better".
- Keeping English sentence structure with Indian words - the most recognisable sign of a machine translation.
- Translating brand and product names.
- Producing an elegant line that quietly strengthens the claim.
