# Channel mix and budget

The question is never "which channel is best". It is "what does the next rupee
return, in each channel, for this segment, at this stage".

## Start with the ceiling

| Input | Where it comes from |
|---|---|
| Contribution per customer | Price minus delivery, support, payment fees, and any variable cost |
| Payback months the business can fund | Cash on hand and the founder's risk appetite |
| Maximum CAC | Contribution x payback months, adjusted for retention |
| Current spend | The ads and tools connectors, or the bank statement |

Maximum CAC is the number that ends most channel debates. If contribution is
Rs 3,000 a year and the business can wait six months, the ceiling is roughly
Rs 1,500 - and a channel returning customers at Rs 2,200 is not a growth channel
however good the ROAS looks.

## The channel table

| Channel | Strength | Fails when | Time to signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paid search | Intent already exists | Search volume is tiny or expensive | 2-4 weeks |
| Paid social | Cheap reach, strong creative leverage | Offer needs explanation; B2B with long cycles | 1-3 weeks |
| SEO and content | Compounding, low marginal cost | No patience, no publishing capacity | 3-9 months |
| Email and WhatsApp to owned lists | Highest return per rupee | List is small, cold or unconsented | 1-2 weeks |
| Outbound | Precise targeting, high deal value | Volume is needed, or the list is bad | 3-6 weeks |
| Partnerships and resellers | Borrowed trust and distribution | Partner margin is unattractive | 2-4 months |
| Marketplaces | Demand already there | Fees and price competition crush margin | 1-2 weeks |
| Referral | Cheapest good customer | No incentive, no moment of delight | 1-3 months |
| Influencer and creator | Borrowed credibility | No fit, no brief, no measurement | 2-6 weeks |
| Events and communities | Trust at scale in B2B | No follow-up process afterwards | 1-2 months |

## Allocating a fixed budget

1. **Fund the proven channel to its marginal limit** - the point where the next rupee returns below the ceiling.
2. **Reserve 20-30% for one or two challengers**, enough to reach a real sample.
3. **Keep 10% for the constraint fix** - the page, the follow-up, the onboarding step that is leaking.
4. **Never spread the remainder across five channels.** Three channels with a real sample beat six with none.

| Allocation pattern | When it is right |
|---|---|
| 70 / 20 / 10 | One channel works, one is being tested, one is fixing the funnel |
| 50 / 30 / 20 | Two channels work, testing a third |
| 40 / 20 / 20 / 20 | Early stage, no channel proven, buying information |

## Marginal return, in practice

| Spend level | What happens | What to watch |
|---|---|---|
| First Rs 50,000 | Cheapest impressions, best audiences | Return is flattering |
| Rs 50,000 to Rs 2 lakh | Frequency rises, best audiences saturate | CPA drifts up |
| Above Rs 2 lakh | You are buying worse inventory | Creative refresh becomes the lever, not budget |

If CPA rises 20% when budget rises 50%, the channel is near its limit for that
creative and audience - the answer is a new angle, not more money.

## India-specific realities

- **Marketplace versus D2C** - marketplaces bring demand and take 15-25% plus returns; D2C keeps margin and must buy every visit. Model both before choosing.
- **COD and RTO** - cash-on-delivery orders carry return-to-origin rates that can wipe out a channel's contribution. Include RTO in the cost, not in a footnote.
- **WhatsApp** - the highest-response owned channel for Indian SMBs, and it needs opt-in and template compliance. Treat it as a channel with rules, not a free channel.
- **Festive seasonality** - September to November moves CPMs and demand by multiples. A channel's January economics are not its October economics.
- **Tier 2 and 3 cities** - cheaper media, different language, different trust signals. Vernacular creative often outperforms English by a wide margin.

## Anti-patterns

- **Blended CAC.** One losing channel hides behind three good ones.
- **Average ROAS.** It ignores the marginal rupee, which is the only one you are deciding about.
- **Equal splits.** Fairness is not a strategy.
- **Killing a channel in week two.** Paid social and outbound need four weeks to produce a signal; SEO needs months.
- **No constraint budget.** Every rupee goes to demand while the leak stays open.
