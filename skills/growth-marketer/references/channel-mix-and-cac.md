# Channel mix and CAC

Channels are not a portfolio to diversify. They are a sequence: one works, you
scale it until the marginal rupee stops returning, then you add the next.

## The two numbers that decide everything

- **Contribution per order** = price − variable cost − delivery − payment fee −
  expected returns and COD failures. Not revenue. Not gross margin percentage of
  a price that includes GST you remit.
- **CAC per paying customer**, fully loaded: media spend ÷ customers acquired, not
  ÷ leads.

Then:

$$
\text{payback months} = \frac{\text{CAC}}{\text{monthly contribution per customer}}
$$

and

$$
\text{CAC ceiling} = \text{target payback months} \times \text{monthly contribution}
$$

A business that cannot fund a payback longer than three months must not buy a
channel with an eight-month payback, however good the LTV looks on a slide.

## The India channel table

| Channel | Best when | The catch |
|---|---|---|
| IndiaMART / B2B directories | Wholesale, industrial, bulk enquiries | Lead quality varies; response speed decides everything |
| Meta (Instagram and Facebook) ads | Visual, impulse, D2C, lead forms | Cheap leads, expensive customers; COD returns bite |
| Google Search ads | Existing demand, high-intent categories | Clicks get expensive fast in competitive terms |
| Google Shopping / marketplace ads | Catalogue products with price comparison | Margin compression and platform dependence |
| Amazon / Flipkart ads | Marketplace demand already exists | Fees plus ads can exceed contribution |
| WhatsApp Business broadcast | Repeat purchase, service, reorders | Opt-in and template approval; no cold lists |
| Email | Owned list, considered purchases | Deliverability work is permanent |
| SEO | Long horizon, defensible traffic | Slow; needs content that actually answers |
| Referral / word of mouth | Trust-heavy, service businesses | Needs a mechanism, not a hope |
| Distributor / reseller | Reach into segments you cannot call | Discount eats the margin; see the partnerships role |
| Events and exhibitions | High-ticket B2B, trade | Cost per lead is high but close rates are too |
| Influencer / creator | Category education, trust | Measurement is weak; rate negotiation matters more than reach |

## Saturation, and how to see it coming

A channel is saturating when, over four to six weeks:

- **CAC rises while spend rises** and creative refresh no longer resets it.
- **Frequency climbs** and CTR falls with it.
- **The audience is exhausted**: the same people see the same creative, and reach
  growth stalls while spend grows.
- **Marginal CAC exceeds the ceiling** even as blended CAC still looks fine.

The blended number hides saturation because the early, cheap spend is still in the
average. Watch marginal CAC - the cost of the last 20% of spend - and decide on
that.

## Sequencing a small budget

| Stage | Spend posture | What you are buying |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Prove one channel | Concentrated, small, 6-8 weeks | A CAC number you trust |
| 2. Fix the funnel | Same spend, better conversion | Lower CAC without more money |
| 3. Scale the winner | Increase in steps of 20% | More volume at a similar CAC |
| 4. Add a second channel | Only when the first has plateaued | A second number you trust |
| 5. Diversify | Only with margin to spare | Resilience |

Most SMBs attempt stage 4 at stage 1 and conclude that "marketing does not work".

## The margin constraint

| Contribution margin | What it permits |
|---|---|
| Above 60% | Paid acquisition at scale, longer payback, aggressive testing |
| 40-60% | Paid works if retention is good; payback under 4 months |
| 25-40% | Organic, referral and marketplace only; paid is a top-up |
| Under 25% | Paid acquisition is structurally a loss; fix price or cost first |

Say this plainly when the arithmetic says it. Recommending a channel to a business
with a 22% contribution margin is a way of spending their cash on a lesson.

## Anti-patterns

- **Five channels at ₹20,000 each.** Nothing reaches the sample or the spend needed
  to learn anything.
- **Judging a channel on its first two weeks.** Every channel has a learning phase.
- **Optimising ROAS while ignoring returns and COD failures.** The gateway settles
  the argument.
- **Adding a channel to fix a retention problem.** Churn makes every channel look
  expensive.
- **Scaling in one jump.** Tripling budget resets the learning phase and usually
  raises CAC.
- **Confusing reach with demand.** A large audience that never buys is a media
  business, not a customer base.
