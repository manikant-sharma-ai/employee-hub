# ICP and message fit

Before the channel, the segment. Before the spend, the promise. Most acquisition
problems are a message that does not match the person reading it, and no budget
fixes that.

## The ICP, written so it can be tested

| Field | Bad | Good |
|---|---|---|
| Who | "SMEs" | "Owner-run manufacturers in Coimbatore with 20-100 staff and one export customer" |
| Trigger | "They need software" | "A second export order arrives and the spreadsheet stops scaling" |
| Budget holder | "The company" | "The owner, who also does the purchasing" |
| Alternative | "Nothing" | "A part-time accountant and three spreadsheets" |
| Why us | "Better product" | "Cuts the month-end close from nine days to three without hiring" |

An ICP that cannot be tested against a list of real companies is a demographic
description, not a segment.

## The evidence hierarchy

| Evidence | Strength | How to get it |
|---|---|---|
| Recorded win-loss reasons from the last 20 deals | Strongest | The CRM, or the founder's memory, written down |
| Customer interviews on why they bought | Strong | Five conversations, thirty minutes each |
| Support tickets and objections | Strong | The inbox, the WhatsApp group, the sales call notes |
| Landing page tests across segments | Medium | Cheap and fast, small samples |
| Ad platform audience data | Weak | Tells you who clicked, not who buys |
| Founder intuition | Weak but not worthless | Write it down, then test it |

## The message hierarchy

For each segment, four things must exist and be consistent across ad, page,
proposal and sales call:

1. **The problem in their words.** Not "operational efficiency" - "I am reconciling till midnight on the 5th every month".
2. **The promise.** One sentence, with a number if possible.
3. **The proof.** A customer like them, a number, a demo, a guarantee.
4. **The next step.** One action, priced in effort: a two-minute form, not "book a discovery call".

## Message-market fit, measured

| Signal | What it means |
|---|---|
| Replies mention the problem in the ad's words | The message is landing |
| Sales calls start with "how did you hear about us" answered specifically | The channel and message connect |
| Objections are about price or timing, not comprehension | The promise is clear |
| Prospects ask "how does it work" before "what does it cost" | The category is still unfamiliar |
| Conversion rate is stable across two different channels | The message works, not just the placement |

## Testing the promise cheaply

1. **Write five variants** of the same promise, same length, different angle: time saved, money saved, risk removed, status gained, alternative replaced.
2. **Run them as ad copy** to the same audience for one week, same creative.
3. **Take the two that earn the most clicks** into a landing page test.
4. **Take the winner into five sales conversations** and listen for the echo.
5. **Only then** scale the spend.

Cost: a week and a small budget. The alternative - scaling a message nobody wanted - costs a quarter.

## India-specific craft

- **Language changes the promise.** A vernacular version is not a translation; "stock out nahi hoga" sells differently from "never run out of stock".
- **Trust signals differ by segment.** GST registration and a physical address matter to a B2B buyer; reviews and returns policy matter to a D2C buyer.
- **WhatsApp is a sales surface.** A promise that survives a WhatsApp forward survives the market.
- **Price anchoring** - quoting in lakhs per year versus thousands per month changes who replies, and both are honest if the billing matches.
- **Festive and wedding seasonality** shift both the message and the timing of the spend.

## Anti-patterns

- **Segmenting by demographics only.** Age and city do not predict a buying trigger.
- **A promise with no number.** "Save time" is not a promise; "close in three days" is.
- **Copy that the sales team does not use.** If the pitch in the call differs from the ad, one of them is wasted.
- **One message for every channel.** Search intent, social interruption and email permission need different first lines.
- **Testing message and offer together.** You will not know which one moved the number.
