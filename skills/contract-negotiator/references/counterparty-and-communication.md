# Counterparty and communication

A redline is a proposal, and it is read by a person who has to defend the deal internally. The words decide whether
they can.

## The covering note

Four short sections, in this order:

1. **What we accept.** Name the clauses agreed, without comment. It sets the tone and makes the document shorter.
2. **What we propose to change, and why** - one line per clause, business reason, no adjectives. "A 3-month cap
   does not match the cover we hold" is a reason; "this is unacceptable" is a position without an argument.
3. **What we need to understand.** The open questions, numbered, answerable in one line each.
4. **What happens next.** Who does what, by when.

## Language that works

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| "This is a non-starter" | "We cannot accept an uncapped indemnity; we propose a cap at 12 months of fees" |
| "Per our legal team" | "Our position is ... because ..." |
| "You must" | "We propose" |
| "As discussed" | "Following our call on <date>, where we agreed ..." |
| "Standard practice" | "What we have agreed with other customers on similar volumes is ..." |
| "Kindly revert" | "Could you confirm by <date>?" |

## The three moves that keep a deal alive

- **Give them a version they can defend.** "We can accept 45 days if the cap moves to 12 months" is a sentence a
  counterparty can take to their finance director.
- **Separate the person from the clause.** The other side's lawyer is doing a job. Never make a redline personal,
  and never let a call become a memory that costs the next round.
- **Never bluff a walk-away you do not mean.** It is discovered in one round, and then nothing you say has weight.

## When they escalate

- **Stay on the paper.** Reply with the clause, the number and the proposal. Emotion in a contract thread is
  evidence against you later.
- **Ask what problem the clause solves.** A customer who demands unlimited liability is usually protecting against
  a specific fear - a data breach, a failed launch. Solve the fear with a narrower obligation and the clause moves.
- **Record every agreement in writing the same day.** "Confirming what we agreed on the call: ..." prevents the
  version drift that costs a week.

## When to stop negotiating

Three rounds without movement on a must-have, or a counterparty who changes agreed language back, means the deal is
not ready. Say so plainly, keep the relationship, and put the decision to the founder with the value and the
walk-away in front of them. Ending a negotiation cleanly is a skill; the next deal with the same counterparty is
often two quarters away.
