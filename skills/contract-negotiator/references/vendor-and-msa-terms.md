# Vendor and MSA terms

Buying is the mirror of selling, with one asymmetry: the paper is usually theirs, the renewal is automatic, and
nobody reads it until the price rises.

## The inbound review

1. **What are we buying, and what happens if it stops?** A tool that runs the dispatch process is a critical
   dependency; a design tool is not. The clause standard is different.
2. **Term and renewal.** Start date, end date, the notice window, and whether the renewal price is fixed. Diarise
   the notice date at signature, not at renewal.
3. **Price escalation.** Fixed, CPI-linked, capped, or silent. A silent clause means the vendor sets next year's
   price.
4. **Data.** Where it is stored, who can access it, what happens on exit, and whether the vendor can use our data
   to train anything. This is the clause most often missing and the most expensive later.
5. **Exit.** Export format, transition assistance, deletion on termination, and whether the data comes back in a
   usable form or a PDF.
6. **Suspension and termination rights.** What the vendor can switch off, and how quickly, on a late payment.
7. **Liability and indemnity.** Usually one-sided in the vendor's favour; ask for a mutual cap, and for the data
   and confidentiality obligations to sit outside it.
8. **The audit and benchmarking right.** Rarely given, always worth asking for on a multi-year deal.

## The clauses that cost money in year two

| Clause | The trap | Ask instead |
|---|---|---|
| Auto-renewal, 90-day notice | The window passes and you are locked for another year | 30-day notice, and a calendar reminder at signature |
| "Price may be adjusted annually" | Uncapped escalation | CPI-linked with a 5% cap |
| Per-seat pricing with a floor | The floor becomes the price when the team shrinks | True-up at renewal only |
| Overage at list price | One busy month costs a quarter's budget | A pre-agreed overage rate, capped |
| Data deletion in 90 days after termination | No time to migrate | 30-day export window, then deletion with a certificate |
| Unilateral right to change the service | The product you bought is not the product you keep | Material changes with 60 days notice and a termination right |

## The renewal calendar

Every agreement gets a row: counterparty, what it is for, annual value, renewal date, notice window, notice
deadline, owner, and the decision made. The notice deadline is the renewal date minus the window, and that is the
date that goes into the calendar. A vendor agreement that renews without a decision is a decision to keep paying.

## Negotiating as the buyer

- **The price is not the only lever.** Term, payment terms, reference rights, support level, onboarding help and
  the data terms are all tradeable, and often cheaper for the vendor than a discount.
- **Ask for the standard terms in writing.** "What is your standard MSA?" in the first email saves a month.
- **Never sign the vendor's order form without the MSA attached.** The order form is where the price lives; the MSA
  is where the risk lives.
- **Escalate the data and liability clauses**, even when the price is small. A ₹5,000 a month tool holding customer
  data is a material risk, not a small purchase.
