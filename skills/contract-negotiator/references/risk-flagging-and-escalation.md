# Risk flagging and escalation

The value of this role is in what it refuses to decide. Four categories of question are never answered here, and
naming them early is what keeps the founder out of trouble.

## The four escalations

| Category | Examples | Who decides | What they need from us |
|---|---|---|---|
| Legal opinion | Enforceability, jurisdiction, stamp duty, regulatory permission, whether a clause is void | A lawyer, named | The clause, the commercial context, the question in one line |
| Insurance | Whether our cover responds, the limit, the notification obligation | The insurer or broker | The clause, the exposure, the notification deadline |
| Founder decision | Any departure from the playbook, the walk-away, a price for a concession | The founder | The trade, the value, the consequence of refusing |
| Counterparty authority | Whether the person negotiating can sign | The founder, with the commercial contact | Who signs, and what they have agreed to in writing |

## The flags that go to the top of the brief

- **Unlimited or uncapped liability**, in any wording, including the wording that hides it ("all losses, costs and
  expenses").
- **Indemnity that is not mutual**, or that covers the counterparty's own negligence.
- **Assignment of all IP**, including pre-existing tools, libraries and templates.
- **Exclusivity** of any territory, segment or customer list.
- **A penalty or service-credit regime without a cap.**
- **Auto-renewal with a notice window longer than 30 days**, or a price escalation that is not capped.
- **A unilateral right to change the scope, the price or the specification.**
- **Payment contingent on their customer's acceptance**, which moves their credit risk onto us.
- **Governing law and jurisdiction outside India**, without a lawyer reading it.
- **Anything requiring a licence, certification or registration we do not hold.**

## How to write an escalation

Three lines, in this order: the clause and number, the exposure in rupees or in risk, and the decision needed with
a date. "Clause 9.2 caps liability at fees paid; the exposure is the ₹40 lakh order value; the founder must decide
whether to accept a 6-month cap or ask for 12 by Friday" is an escalation. "Legal review needed" is not.

## Risk language, used precisely

- **"Exposure"** - the money at risk if the clause is triggered. A number or a stated range.
- **"Likelihood"** - how often this kind of clause is triggered in practice, in plain words, not a percentage
  invented for the slide.
- **"Mitigation"** - what we do to make the exposure smaller: insurance, a cap, a process, a shorter term.
- **"Residual"** - what remains after mitigation, and who has accepted it.

## The register

Every escalation is a row in the clause register with an owner and a date, so that a deal cannot be signed with
three escalations still open unless the founder signs that too. The register is the memory of the negotiation; the
email thread is not.
