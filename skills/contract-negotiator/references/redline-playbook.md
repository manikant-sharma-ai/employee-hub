# Redline playbook

A playbook is a written position per clause, so that the answer does not depend on who is reading the paper or how
much they want the deal. This is a commercial position, not a legal one: a lawyer still reads the final version.

## The clauses that decide the deal

| Clause | Our ask | Acceptable | Floor / hard bound | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Limitation of liability | 12 months of fees paid | 6 months | 3 months, and never unlimited | The cap is the maximum loss the business has agreed to insure itself against |
| Indemnity | Mutual, and only for third-party IP claims caused by us | One-way for our IP, capped | Uncapped indemnity is a hard escalation | An uncapped indemnity is a liability cap that has been quietly removed |
| IP ownership | We keep our background IP and license the deliverables | Customer owns bespoke deliverables, we keep the tools | Assignment of all IP including pre-existing is a hard escalation | The reusable parts are the business; the bespoke part is the job |
| Payment terms | 30 days | 45 days | 60 days, then a financing cost | Every extra 15 days is working capital, priced at the borrowing rate |
| Termination for convenience | 30 days notice either way | 45 days | 90 days, with fees for work in progress | A customer who can leave in 30 days will; a vendor who cannot be replaced in 90 is a lock-in |
| Auto-renewal | None, or with a 60-day exit notice | Annual with a notice window | 12 months with a price cap | Auto-renewal converts a decision into a default |
| Price escalation | Fixed for the term | CPI-linked, capped at 5% | 10% a year, never retrospective | An uncapped escalation on a multi-year deal is a price you have not agreed |
| Service levels | 99.5% uptime, service credits capped at the month's fee | 99%, credits only | Credits never exceed 25% of annual fees | An uncapped credit regime is a penalty clause in disguise |
| Late-delivery penalty | None | Capped at 2% of the order value | 5% and never a termination trigger | Penalties should price a risk, not transfer the customer's business risk |
| Exclusivity | None | Named accounts or one city, time-boxed | Territory-wide exclusivity is a hard escalation | Exclusivity is a permanent cost against a temporary order |
| Confidentiality | Mutual, 3 years, standard carve-outs | 5 years | Perpetual only for trade secrets | Standard carve-outs: already public, independently developed, legally required |
| Jurisdiction | Our city | Neutral city | The counterparty's country, only with a lawyer | Enforcing a judgment abroad costs more than most disputes |

## Reading a clause

1. **What does it oblige us to do, in one sentence?** If the sentence needs three clauses to complete, the clause
   is doing something hidden.
2. **What is the maximum exposure?** In rupees, or in a blocked opportunity. A clause with no number attached has
   not been read yet.
3. **Who bears the risk today, and who would after the change?** A "clarification" that shifts risk is a change.
4. **Is it mutual?** One-way clauses are the most common asymmetry in a first draft and the easiest to fix.

## The five traps in a first draft

- **"Reasonable" and "material" left undefined**, so the other side's lawyer defines them later.
- **The liability cap that excludes the things that actually go wrong** - data loss, IP, confidentiality - which
  makes the cap decorative.
- **Termination for breach with a 7-day cure period** on obligations that take a month to fix.
- **The audit right** that lets a customer inspect the books with two days' notice.
- **The reference and logo clause** that lets them advertise the relationship before the first invoice is paid.

## How to redline

Replace the sentence, keep the numbering, add a comment with one business reason. Never rewrite the whole clause
unless the structure is wrong - a rewritten clause reads as an opening position, a replaced sentence reads as a
proposal.
