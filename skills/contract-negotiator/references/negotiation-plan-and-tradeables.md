# Negotiation plan and tradeables

A negotiation is won before the call, in a document with three lists in it. On the call there is only execution.

## The three lists

**Must-haves** - two or three, no more. If everything is a must-have, nothing is. Typically: the liability cap, the
IP of the reusable parts, and the ability to leave. Each is written with the reason, so the founder can say why
rather than "our legal team says".

**Tradeables** - everything else, each with a price. A tradeable without a price becomes a gift:

| Tradeable | What it is worth to them | What we want in return |
|---|---|---|
| Payment terms 30 to 45 days | Working capital, roughly the fee x 15/365 x borrowing rate | A longer term, or a higher price |
| A named-account exclusivity | A competitor blocked in their biggest account | A volume commitment, or a 12-month minimum |
| A 60-day exit instead of 30 | The comfort of not being locked in | A setup fee that is not refundable |
| A reference and logo right | Marketing value | Payment terms of 30 days, or a case study |
| A 5% price escalation cap | Budget certainty | A 24-month term |

**The walk-away** - one sentence, written, agreed with the founder. Examples: unlimited liability; assignment of
all IP; exclusivity beyond a named list; a penalty that exceeds the margin on the order. If the walk-away is
crossed, the deal does not close today and the call ends politely.

## The fallback ladder

For each position: **ask** (what we open with), **acceptable** (what we will sign), **floor** (the last stop before
the walk-away), and **the trade** we request at each step down.

| Position | Ask | Acceptable | Floor | Trade requested at each step |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Liability cap | 12 months of fees | 6 months | 3 months | A higher price, or a shorter term |
| Payment terms | 30 days | 45 days | 60 days with interest | A 12-month commitment |
| Termination | 30 days either way | 45 days | 90 days with work-in-progress fees | A notice window on their side too |

A ladder without trades is a discount schedule. The other side learns in two rounds that pushing works.

## Sequencing

1. **Open on the two that matter**, not on the formatting. A counterparty who concedes the small items early is
   testing whether you are reading.
2. **Concede in packages, never one by one.** "We can accept 45 days and the named-account exclusivity if the cap
   is 12 months" is a trade. Three separate concessions are three losses.
3. **Let them win something visible.** A term they asked for that costs us little, given late, makes the signature
   easier and the relationship better.
4. **Never negotiate against yourself.** If they have not responded to a position, do not improve it.

## What the founder needs on the call

One page: the three lists, the ladder, the two sentences that explain the cap and the IP position in business
language, and the walk-away. Not the contract. A founder reading a contract during a call has already lost the
call.

## After the call

Write what moved, what was traded, and what was left open, the same day, in `notes/journal/`. The next round starts
from that note, not from memory - and the same concession is not made twice.
