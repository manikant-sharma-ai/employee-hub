# Negotiation prep: one page, decided before the call

A negotiation with a written ladder is a conversation. Without one it is a mood that ends in a signature.

## The one-page sheet

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| The deal in one line | What the business gets, for how much, for how long |
| Our three asks, in order | Ranked by rupee exposure, not by how annoying the clause is |
| Our one early concession | Something cheap that signals good faith |
| Our two holds | The clauses that do not move |
| Our walk-away | The clause or term that ends the deal, agreed with the founder |
| Their likely asks | The three things they will want from us, and our answer |
| Who speaks | One voice per issue; the other person takes notes |
| The next step | The date and the document that follows the call |

## The ladder, per clause

| Level | Wording | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Ask | The full standard position | "Mutual cap at 12 months of fees" |
| Trade | What is acceptable if something is given back | "Cap at 12 months, but we accept their governing law" |
| Walk | What ends the deal | "Unlimited liability for our own negligence" |

Three levels, written, for the two or three clauses that will actually be argued. The other forty are settled by
the redline.

## Sequencing

1. **Open with the easy one.** A clause they will certainly accept, to establish that the redline is reasonable.
2. **Move to the expensive one second**, while there is still goodwill and time.
3. **Hold the walk-away for last**, and only if the trade failed.
4. **Never trade two must-haves for one nice-to-have.** That is how a contract becomes worse than the one you
   started with.

## What to give away early

Cheap, visible, and worth something to them: the notice period, the invoice format, their governing law when both
are Indian, a case study permission, a reference call, a slightly earlier delivery on the first milestone. Give
one, name it as a concession, and get something named back.

## Who speaks

- **One person per issue.** Two voices negotiating the same clause is how a concession is given twice.
- **The founder speaks on commercial importance; the terms person speaks on clauses.** Never the reverse - a
  founder arguing clause numbers sounds like a lawyer without a licence, and a terms person arguing about the
  relationship sounds presumptuous.
- **Silence after an ask.** The first person to fill it usually concedes.

## When the other side says "that is our standard paper"

- **Ask which clauses have moved before.** Large counterparties move on liability, payment terms and sometimes
  data, for deals above a threshold.
- **Ask for the exception route**: "who approves a deviation, and what do they need to see?" Often a one-page
  business case unlocks a clause that a lawyer could not.
- **Price it instead.** If the clause will not move, the read states what the exposure costs and the founder
  decides whether the deal still works. That is a legitimate outcome, not a defeat.
- **Do not spend a week redlining non-negotiable paper.** Decide in the first ten minutes which kind of document
  it is.

## Anti-patterns

- Negotiating without the deal context, so a must-win client is treated like a commodity vendor.
- Conceding a clause because the meeting is running long.
- A walk-away decided in the meeting by whoever is most tired.
- Winning every clause and losing the deal, which is a loss with extra steps.
- Agreeing on the call and never sending the revised document, so the agreement evaporates.
