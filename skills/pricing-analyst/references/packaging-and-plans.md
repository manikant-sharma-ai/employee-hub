# Packaging and plans

Price is what you charge; packaging is what you charge it for. Packaging decides
more revenue than the number on the page, because it decides who chooses which.

## The three shapes

| Shape | Structure | Best for | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Good-better-best | Three tiers, feature-gated | Clear segment differences | The middle tier becomes the only one sold |
| Usage-based | Per unit, per seat, per transaction | Variable consumption | Bill shock, and unpredictable revenue |
| Hybrid | Platform fee plus usage | Predictable base plus growth | Complexity in the sales conversation |

## Designing the tiers

| Tier | Buyer | Job it does | Limit that fences it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Starter | Solo, first purchase | One job, done simply | 1 user, 100 transactions a month |
| Growth | Small team | Collaboration and reporting | 5 users, 1,000 transactions |
| Scale | Mid-market | Control, audit, integration | Unlimited users, API access, SLA |

Rules:

- **The difference must be a difference someone cares about.** Not "priority support"
  in all three tiers with different response times nobody measured.
- **The fence should grow with value.** Users, volume, locations, integrations - a
  limit tied to the customer's size is fair; a limit tied to their frustration is not.
- **Three tiers, not five.** Five tiers produce a decision the buyer postpones.
- **The middle tier is the one you design for.** It is chosen most often; make it the
  one that fits the buyer you want most.

## Anchoring, honestly

- A high-priced tier makes the middle look reasonable - that is legitimate anchoring.
- A deliberately useless decoy tier is a manipulation, and buyers notice.
- Showing the annual price first makes the monthly look small. Legal, common, and
  only acceptable if the annual price is the one you want sold.

## Price points that read as intended in India

| Ending | Reads as | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| ₹499 | Consumer, app, self-serve | Monthly subscription |
| ₹999 | Consumer plus, small business entry | Prosumer tool |
| ₹2,499 | Small business | Team plan |
| ₹4,999 | Professional | Serious small business |
| ₹9,999 | Mid-market entry | Departmental tool |
| ₹48,000 a year | B2B, invoice-based | Annual contract, purchase-order friendly |
| ₹1,00,000+ | Enterprise, negotiated | Quote-based, procurement involved |

- **Round numbers read B2B; 9-endings read consumer.** A ₹5,00,000 quote ending in
  999 looks like a consumer product wearing a suit.
- **Annual pricing in months-free terms** - "pay for 10, get 12" is understood faster
  than "16.7% discount".
- **GST**: quote ex-GST to businesses, inclusive to consumers, and say which.

## Free tiers and trials

| Model | When it works | The trap |
|---|---|---|
| Free tier forever | The product spreads by usage; support cost is near zero | Support cost grows faster than conversion |
| 14-day trial, no card | Low-friction categories | Tourists, and a low conversion you must staff for |
| 14-day trial with card | Higher-intent categories | Fewer starts; more serious ones |
| Freemium with a hard limit | Consumer, viral | The limit becomes the product for most users |
| Paid pilot | B2B, mid-market | Needs a defined success criterion or it never ends |

State the conversion you need for the model to work before choosing: a free tier
converting 2% at ₹500 needs 50 free users per paying one, and their support cost
comes out of that ₹500.

## When to raise prices

Four signals, and three of them are not about the market:

1. **Discounts are rarely used.** If nobody negotiates, the price is below the ceiling.
2. **Win rate is high and the sales cycle is short.** You are the obvious choice, and
   probably the cheap one.
3. **Costs moved.** Input costs, delivery, payment fees - a 10% cost rise on a 25%
   margin needs a 6% price rise to hold the rupee profit.
4. **The product is materially better than at the last price.** You shipped two years
   of work and never charged for it.

## Anti-patterns

- Feature-gating something the customer needs to get value at all.
- A "custom" tier for every large prospect, until there is no price list.
- Charging per seat when the value is per transaction, or the reverse.
- A free tier whose support cost exceeds its conversion value.
- Three tiers that differ only in a limit nobody reaches.
- Copying a competitor's packaging when your cost structure and buyer are different.
