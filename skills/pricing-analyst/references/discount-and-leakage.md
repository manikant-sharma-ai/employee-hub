# Discounts and leakage

Discounting is where a pricing strategy goes to die quietly. The list price is a
statement; the realised price is the business.

## The discount distribution

Before any policy, measure the current state from the CRM or the invoice register:

| Statistic | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| Median discount | The habit |
| 90th percentile | The worst deals, and who gives them |
| Share of deals with any discount | Whether the list is real |
| Discount by salesperson | Where the authority has leaked |
| Discount by segment | Which buyers know you will move |
| Win rate at 0-5% vs 20%+ | Whether the discount buys anything at all |

If the win rate at full price is close to the win rate at 20% off, the discount is
buying nothing but a smaller invoice. That single comparison usually ends the debate.

## The authority ladder

| Discount | Approves | Requires |
|---|---|---|
| 0-5% | Salesperson | Nothing, it is theirs to give |
| 5-10% | Sales head | A logged reason in the CRM |
| 10-20% | Founder or finance head | A give-get: term, volume, prepay or reference |
| Above 20% | Founder, written | A business case, and a review date |

Set the numbers for the business. The rule that matters: **the level is by deal size
and discount depth, not by who is friendly with whom.**

## The give-get table

| They want | You give | You get |
|---|---|---|
| A lower price | 5% | Annual prepay, or a 12-month term |
| A lower price | 10% | A named case study and two reference calls |
| A lower price | 15% | Double the volume, or a three-year term |
| A pilot price | 30% for 90 days | A success criterion and a conversion date |
| Free onboarding | Waived fee | A signed order before the month ends |

Nothing comes free. A discount with no counterpart teaches the buyer that the price
was never real.

## Where leakage hides

1. **Renewal discounts that were meant for year one.** The customer keeps them for five years.
2. **Bundled free months** at signing, forgotten in the renewal.
3. **Payment-term drift** - 30 days becomes 60, which is a 1.5% discount nobody logged.
4. **Free add-ons** given by support to close a complaint.
5. **Reseller discounts** applied on top of a promotional price.
6. **Grandfathered prices** carried into a new product generation.
7. **Side letters** and WhatsApp promises that contradict the contract.

The quarterly audit: compare realised price against the price list by segment, list
every gap over 5%, and ask one question per gap - approved, expired, or unexplained.

## Price increases and the discount

The cheapest way to raise the realised price is not to raise the list - it is to
withdraw the discount. "The list stays ₹1,000, but the 15% launch discount ends on
31 March" is easier to sell than "the price is now ₹1,000 from ₹850", and it is the
same rupee.

## Talking about price without apologising

- **Say the number and stop.** The silence after the number is the negotiation.
- **Never lead with the discount.** A price presented with an immediate discount is a
  price nobody believes.
- **Compare to the alternative, not to a competitor's list.** "It costs less than the
  two hours a week it replaces" is a price argument; "we are cheaper than X" is not.
- **Have a lower-scope option ready.** A smaller package is a better answer than a
  discount, because it protects the price of the full one.

## Anti-patterns

- A discount policy that exists but is not enforced in the quoting tool.
- Approving discounts verbally, so nobody can audit them.
- Discounting to win a deal that will churn at renewal because it was bought on price.
- A "special price" for a large prospect that becomes the reference price for everyone.
- Measuring salespeople on revenue and not on realised price.
- Raising list price while quietly increasing the average discount, which changes nothing.
