# Discount and negotiation policy

A discount policy is not a restriction on the sales team, it is what stops the tenth customer getting a better price than the first.

## The ladder

| Level | Discount | Who may grant | What it costs | When it is allowed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0% | Anyone | Nothing | Default. The price is the price |
| 1 | up to 5% | Sales, no approval | 1/6 of a 30% contribution | Annual prepay, or a first order above the stated threshold |
| 2 | 5-10% | Sales lead | A third of contribution | Competitive displacement with evidence, or a multi-year commitment |
| 3 | 10-15% | Founder | Half of contribution | Strategic account, with a written reason and a review date |
| 4 | above 15% | Founder, in writing | Contribution gone or negative | Only with something else traded, never on price alone |

Write the rupee cost next to each level. "5% off" is abstract; "₹4,500 of contribution" is not.

## Trade, do not give

Every discount above level 1 buys something:

| Give | Ask for |
|---|---|
| 5% | Annual prepay, or payment within 7 days |
| 10% | A case study, a reference call, or a logo |
| 10% | Volume commitment in writing, with a minimum |
| 15% | A multi-year term, or a paid pilot converting to annual |
| Anything | A shorter delivery window, a narrower scope, or self-service onboarding |

If nothing is available to trade, the answer is no, and saying it is the salesperson's job, not the founder's.

## What never gets discounted

- **The price below the floor**, ever, unless the loss is a stated marketing spend with an end date.
- **A discount to fix a service failure.** Fix the failure; a discount on top teaches that complaining works.
- **A discount granted retroactively** to a customer who already paid full price, unless it is offered to everyone in the cohort.
- **A discount on a renewal without a change** in term, volume or scope.

## Leakage audit

Run this quarterly. Most businesses lose 3-8% of revenue to discounts nobody decided.

| Check | What it finds |
|---|---|
| Discount frequency by salesperson | The one who always gives 10% |
| Average discount by segment | The segment that has learned to ask |
| Discounts granted without an approval record | Policy that exists only in the document |
| Coupons still live after their campaign ended | Silent, permanent price cuts |
| Orders below the floor | A pricing bug or an unauthorised deal |
| Renewals at a lower price than the original | Churn priced in advance |

## Scripts for the three objections

**"It's too expensive."** Ask what it is being compared to. Half the time it is compared to a cheaper product that does something different, and the answer is a clarification, not a discount.

**"Competitor X is 20% cheaper."** Name what the price includes - delivery, support, the return policy, the warranty. If the comparison is fair, say so and offer the trade: "We can meet that on a two-year term."

**"Give us a discount and we'll sign today."** "I can do 5% on annual prepay. On monthly, the price is the price." One alternative, one condition, no haggling.

## India-specific notes

- **Bargaining is expected in B2B and in wholesale.** A published ladder that the salesperson can apply without escalating preserves both the relationship and the margin.
- **The "final price" must be final.** A second round of discount after the first teaches the buyer to always ask twice.
- **Cash discounts** (1-2% for payment within 7 days) are cheaper than trade discounts and improve working capital. Prefer them.
- **GST is on the discounted value** for a trade discount shown on the invoice, and on the full value if it is a post-invoice credit note - a bookkeeping difference that also changes the customer's input credit. Route the treatment to `bookkeeper`.
- **A discount to a distributor is a channel margin, not a discount.** Model it as a separate price list.

## Anti-patterns

- A policy with no rupee cost attached to each level.
- Approval levels that exist but are granted retroactively by email.
- Discounting to hit a monthly target, then raising the list price to compensate.
- Coupons that never expire.
- Different prices for identical customers, discovered by the customer.
- Trading price for a promise ("we'll refer you") that has no value in writing.
