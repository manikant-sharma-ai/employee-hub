# Salary structure and CTC

A structure is not an arithmetic exercise. It decides what the employee takes home, what the business
pays, what is statutory, and what happens at the exit.

## The ladder from CTC to net

```
CTC
 - employer PF, ESI, EDLI, admin, gratuity provision, insurance premium
 = Gross salary
 - employee PF, ESI, professional tax, TDS
 - other deductions (advance, loan, canteen, notice recovery)
 = Net pay
```

Most offer conversations confuse the top and the bottom of this ladder. State both, every time.

## The components and what each one does

| Component | Typical share | Statutory treatment | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Basic | 40-50% of gross | PF applicable | The anchor for PF, gratuity and often bonus |
| DA | Where applicable | PF applicable | Common in manufacturing and public-sector-linked pay |
| HRA | 40-50% of basic | Not PF; tax exemption under the old regime with rent proof | A structure with no HRA and rent paid is a missed exemption |
| Conveyance / special allowance | Balance | PF treatment depends on whether it is really salary | A large "special allowance" that is salary in substance is a PF risk |
| Reimbursements (telephone, books, fuel) | Small, evidenced | Not PF if genuinely reimbursement | Needs bills; a fixed reimbursement without bills is salary |
| Variable pay / incentive | Per policy | PF generally not applicable until paid | State the payout date and the pro-rata rule for part-year |
| Statutory bonus | 8.33%-20% where the Act applies | Not PF | Eligibility wage ceiling and calculation ceiling both matter |

## Setting a structure that survives an inspection

1. Basic is a real share of gross, not the minimum that satisfies PF. A ₹12,000 basic on a ₹60,000
   gross is a structure built to avoid a contribution, and it is read that way.
2. Allowances have a purpose and a name that matches it.
3. Reimbursements have a bill process from day one, or they are salary.
4. Gratuity is provided monthly in the accounts, so the exit is not a cash shock.
5. The structure is documented in the appointment letter, not only in a spreadsheet.

## Minimum wage and the floor

Every structure must clear the state's minimum wage for the scheduled employment and skill category.
Minimum wages are revised by notification, differ by zone and category, and are the one payroll rule
that cannot be traded. Check the current notification for the state and the classification before
signing any structure at or near the floor.

## A revision, done properly

1. Effective date in writing, with the arrears computation stated.
2. New structure, not just a new gross - the split matters for PF and gratuity.
3. Arrears paid in a separate line so the variance story stays legible.
4. Revised letter issued and acknowledged.
5. Register updated from the effective date, with the old structure retained for the record.

## Anti-patterns

- **CTC quoted as take-home.** The single most common cause of a first-payslip dispute.
- **A structure designed around the PF ceiling.** It is a compliance finding, and the employee's
  retirement money is the thing being saved.
- **Gratuity ignored until the exit.** A five-year employee leaving with ₹1.2 lakh of unprovided
  gratuity is a cash problem created by a spreadsheet omission.
- **Reimbursements with no bill process.** They become salary with a paper trail that says otherwise.
- **Arrears merged into the month.** It distorts PF, TDS and every comparison afterwards.
- **No written structure.** "As discussed" is not a structure.
