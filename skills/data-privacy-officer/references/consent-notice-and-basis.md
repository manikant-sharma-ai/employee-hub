# Consent, notice and the basis for holding data

Consent is the default basis, and it is harder to get right than a checkbox. This file writes a notice that can be
honoured.

## What consent has to be

| Requirement | What it means in practice | What fails it |
|---|---|---|
| Free | No bundling with the service the person needs | "Accept marketing to place the order" |
| Specific | Per purpose, not one blanket | "I agree to the privacy policy" covering everything |
| Informed | The notice is read before consent | A link to a 9-page policy nobody opens |
| Unconditional | Not tied to unrelated terms | Consent as a condition of employment beyond what is needed |
| Unambiguous, with clear affirmative action | A positive act | A pre-ticked box, or silence |
| Limited to the purpose | No reuse for a new purpose | Numbers collected for delivery used for marketing |
| Withdrawable | As easy to withdraw as to give | A withdrawal route that requires an email to a person who left |

The withdrawal route is the test of the whole system. If withdrawing consent takes more steps than giving it, the
consent was not free.

## The notice, in the order a person needs it

| Item | Plain-language version | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Who is asking | The business name and a contact | "We are <business>, and we run <site>." |
| What is collected | Item by item, not categories | "Your name, phone, delivery address and what you ordered." |
| Why | The purpose, one per item | "To deliver your order and to handle returns." |
| Who it goes to | The kinds of recipients | "Our delivery partner, our payment gateway, our accountant." |
| How long | The period per purpose | "Order records for 8 years, as tax law requires." |
| Their rights | Access, correction, erasure, grievance, nomination | "You can ask to see, correct or delete your data." |
| How to exercise them | A route that works | "Write to <address> or use <link>. We reply within <period>." |
| Withdrawal | How consent is withdrawn, and what happens then | "Reply STOP, or use <link>. We stop marketing immediately." |
| Grievance | Who to complain to first, and then the Board | "Our grievance contact is <role>; you may also approach the Data Protection Board." |
| Children | Whether the service is for children and what changes | "We do not knowingly collect data from children under 18." |
| Transfers | Whether data leaves India | "Our CRM stores data in <region>." |

## Language

The notice must be understandable to the people it applies to. In India that usually means English plus the
language of the region the business sells in - Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, Bengali, Marathi, Kannada, Gujarati, Malayalam,
Odia or another. A notice in English only, given to a customer who reads Tamil, is a notice in a language they were
not informed in. Produce the version in the language the customers actually use, and keep the versions in step.

## Choosing the basis

| Situation | Basis to consider | What to write down |
|---|---|---|
| Marketing email to a new contact | Consent | When, how and where it was taken |
| Delivering an order | Consent at checkout, or a legitimate use for the performance of the request | The exact wording shown |
| Statutory records (tax, payroll) | A legal obligation | The statute and the period |
| Medical emergency, disaster response | The limited legitimate uses the law provides | The circumstances, dated |
| Employment records | Consent plus statutory obligations, kept to the minimum | Which records, which statute |
| CCTV in a shop or office | Notice at the site, and a stated purpose | Signage, retention, who views it |
| Fraud prevention | Often a legitimate use; check the current position | What is checked, and what is kept |
| Children's data | Verifiable parental consent, and no behavioural tracking or targeted advertising | How verification works |

Where the answer is "we never asked", that is a finding. The fix is a notice going forward, a re-consent where the
law requires one, and a stop to the collection that has no basis.

## The consent record

For each consent, keep: who, what purpose, which wording, when, how (channel and method), and when it was
withdrawn. Without the wording, a consent cannot be proved; the person's word against a database flag is not a
defence.

## Children

If the business serves anyone under 18 - schools, coaching, gaming, toys sold direct, a kids' app - the obligations
change materially: verifiable parental consent, no tracking or behavioural monitoring, no targeted advertising, and
care with any data that could identify the child. A business that says "we are not for children" but collects dates
of birth without checking is not protected by the statement.

## Anti-patterns

- One checkbox consenting to everything, including things added later.
- A cookie banner that loads the tracking tags before the choice is made.
- A privacy policy that describes a deletion right with no process behind it.
- A marketing list bought from a vendor and described as "consented".
- Consent collected in English from customers who read another language.
- Withdrawal handled by a person who has left the company.
- Treating a "legitimate use" as a general permission. It is a narrow list, and it has conditions.
