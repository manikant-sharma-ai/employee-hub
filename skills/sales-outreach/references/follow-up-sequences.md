# Follow-up sequences

Most replies come after the first email, and almost nobody follows up. That asymmetry is the cheapest advantage in outbound.

## A cadence that works

| Touch | Day | Channel | What it adds |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0 | Email | The trigger, the problem, the ask |
| 2 | 3 | Email | One new proof point or a relevant case in their industry |
| 3 | 7 | LinkedIn / WhatsApp | Short, different channel, no pitch - "sent you a note about X" |
| 4 | 14 | Email | A resource they can use without replying (checklist, benchmark, teardown) |
| 5 | 21 | Email | The reframe: a different angle on the same problem |
| 6 | 28 | Email | The close-out: "I'll stop here - is this a 2027 problem or not?" |

Rules:

- **Four to six touches, three to four weeks.** Fewer than four and you are leaving the majority of replies unclaimed; more than six and you are the reason they mark you as spam.
- **Each touch must add information.** A touch whose only content is "bumping this" trains the buyer to ignore the thread.
- **Never send two touches in one day across two channels.** It reads as pressure, and it is.
- **Space increases, it does not stay constant.** 3, 4, 7, 7, 7 days reads like a person; 3, 3, 3, 3 reads like a machine.

## The break-up email

The last touch earns more replies than any other, because it removes the obligation to answer.

> Subject: closing the loop
>
> Hi Prakash - I've written three times about multi-site stock counts and heard nothing, which is a fair signal that it is not a priority this year.
>
> I'll stop here. If it becomes one, the checklist I mentioned is here: [link]. Good luck with the Hosur ramp.
>
> Manikant

Why it works: it is honest, it gives something, and it invites a "not now, but ask me in March" which is the second most valuable reply in outbound.

## Reading the silence

| Signal | Most likely meaning | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| No open, no click, no reply | Wrong address or filtered | Verify the address, check the domain's reputation, try another contact |
| Opens, no reply | Message read, offer not relevant enough | Change the angle, not the words |
| Clicks, no reply | Interested, not convinced | Send the case study, then ask a diagnostic question |
| "Not now" with no date | Politely closing the door | Ask for one date; if none, close and set a 90-day reminder |
| "Not now, ask in March" | A real deferral | Log the date in the CRM, diarise it, and stop everything else |
| Reply to a colleague, copied in | Internal routing, a good sign | Answer the colleague directly, thank the router, keep both on the thread |

## Reply handling

- **Answer within four working hours.** Reply speed is the strongest predictor of a booked meeting.
- **Match their length.** A one-line reply gets a two-line answer, not a 200-word proposal.
- **Answer the question they asked**, then add one thing, then one ask. In that order.
- **Never send a calendar link as the first response to a positive reply.** Propose two specific times; a link feels like a queue.
- **Log the reply verbatim** in the CRM. Paraphrase loses the objection.

## Stop rules

The sequence stops, permanently, on any of these:

- An explicit no, at any touch, in any channel.
- An unsubscribe, a "remove me", or a request not to be contacted again - added to the suppression list across every mailbox.
- Two negative replies on the same thread.
- A bounce that reveals a role change - the contact is replaced, not re-emailed.
- A reply from the company's legal or compliance team.

## Anti-patterns

- **"Just following up"** as the entire content of a touch. It adds nothing and it is the reason people ignore threads.
- **Restarting a sequence at touch one after a long gap.** Say "we spoke in March and you asked me to come back in the new financial year" - that is the whole advantage of a CRM.
- **Following up on a deal that has moved to a colleague** without mentioning the colleague.
- **Sending touch five to a person who replied "no" at touch two.** This is the failure that turns a prospect into a complaint.
- **Long cadences with no exit.** Six touches and then nothing is a plan; six touches and then restarting is harassment.
- **Using a different sender identity to dodge a suppression.** It is the fastest way to lose the domain and the reputation.
