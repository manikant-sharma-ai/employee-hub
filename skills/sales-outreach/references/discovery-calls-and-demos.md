# Discovery calls and demos

The reply is only permission to talk. The deal is won or lost in the first call,
and most outbound teams have no craft for it at all.

## The first-call structure (20 minutes)

| Minute | What happens | What you must leave with |
|---|---|---|
| 0-2 | Frame: what this call is, how long, what happens after | Their agreement on the agenda |
| 2-8 | Their situation, in their words - five questions, no pitch | The problem, its cost, who feels it |
| 8-13 | Confirm and reflect: "so the real issue is X, costing Y" | A yes, or a correction |
| 13-17 | The narrow next step - not the full product | Agreement on a specific next action |
| 17-20 | Logistics, and what you will send | A date, a person, a decision criterion |

## The five questions

1. "Walk me through how you handle <the job> today."
2. "What made you take this call?" (their trigger, not your pitch)
3. "What have you already tried?" (the competition is usually a spreadsheet)
4. "What happens if nothing changes this year?" (cost of inaction)
5. "Who else is involved in a decision like this?" (the real map)

Never ask "what are your pain points" - it is a survey question and gets a survey answer.

## The demo, if there is one

- **Never demo before discovery.** A generic tour teaches the buyer that you did not listen.
- **Show three screens maximum**, each mapped to something they said. "You said approvals take a week - here is that step."
- **Show one thing broken or limited.** Credibility buys the rest.
- **End on the decision, not on the feature list.** "What would you need to see to move forward?"

## Qualifying, honestly

Disqualify fast and say why. The three disqualifiers from the ICP test apply on the
call too: wrong size, no budget in this financial year, no one who can decide in
the room. A polite no on day two saves the quarter.

## The follow-up that gets a reply

Within two hours: three lines - what you heard, the one thing you promised, the
date. Not a 12-page proposal. The proposal comes after the second conversation.

## Indian B2B realities

- **The decision-maker is often not on the first call.** Ask early who signs.
- **WhatsApp is the follow-up channel**, with a summary, not a pitch.
- **Financial year and festival calendars** decide urgency: January-March is budget season, October-November is slow for new projects.
- **A visit still closes deals** in many categories. Offer it when the deal size justifies it.

## Anti-patterns

- A 40-minute monologue demo.
- Pitching before the third question.
- No next step agreed at the end of the call.
- Sending a proposal before the buyer has described the problem in their own words.
- Logging "interested, follow up" with no date.
