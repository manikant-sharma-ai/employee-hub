# India outbound norms

Outbound that ignores Indian channel realities gets filtered, ignored or reported. These are the practical rules, not legal advice - for a specific campaign, the business's lawyer decides.

## Channel by segment

| Segment | First channel | Second | Rarely works |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mid/large enterprise (500+) | Email to a named role | LinkedIn, then a warm intro | WhatsApp to a personal number |
| SMB (20-200) | WhatsApp Business, then call | Email | Long-form email |
| Manufacturer / trader | Phone, then WhatsApp | IndiaMART enquiry reply | LinkedIn InMail |
| D2C brand | Email to founder | Instagram DM | Cold call |
| Government / PSU | GeM, or the department's tender process | Formal letter on letterhead | Any cold email |
| Channel partners / distributors | Phone, then a visit | WhatsApp | Email alone |

## Consent and contact rules

- **Email:** India has no general anti-spam statute equivalent to CAN-SPAM, but the DPDP Act governs personal data. Work from a documented basis - an existing business relationship, a publicly listed business contact relevant to their role, or explicit consent - and honour every opt-out immediately.
- **Calls and SMS:** TRAI's DND framework applies to commercial calls and SMS. Check numbers against DND where a scrubbing service is available; never call a number that has asked not to be called.
- **WhatsApp Business:** template messages need approval and an opt-in. Free-form replies are only allowed inside the 24-hour customer service window after the person messages you. Sending marketing templates to a list that never opted in gets the number banned.
- **Personal data:** collect the minimum needed for the job. Do not store phone numbers, addresses or personal email in the repo, in notes, or in files that leave the conversation.

## Timing

| Window | Guidance |
|---|---|
| 10:00-13:00 IST | Best for calls to owners and founders |
| 14:00-17:30 IST | Best for email; after lunch the inbox gets read |
| Before 09:00 or after 19:00 | Do not call; message only if they messaged first |
| Saturday | Half-working day for many SMBs - fine for WhatsApp, poor for email |
| Sunday | Do not send |
| Month-end and quarter-end | Owners are in closing mode; expect low replies |
| Financial year end (March) | Budget conversations, high intent, low attention |
| First two weeks of April | New budget year, the best outbound window of the year |
| Diwali week, Holi, Eid, Pongal, Onam | Do not run campaigns; schedule around them |
| Exam and school admission season | Irrelevant for B2B, decisive for education and family retail |

## Register and language

- **English is the default for B2B email**, but the reply often comes in Hinglish. Match the register of the reply, not the register of your template.
- **Hinglish is not broken English.** "Sir, stock count ka issue solve ho jayega" is a normal business sentence. Do not translate it into formal Hindi, and do not correct their English.
- **"Sir/Madam" is normal in Indian B2B** and not servile; using it is safer than first-name familiarity with an older owner.
- **Regional language beats English for SMB owners** in Tamil Nadu, Gujarat, West Bengal and the Hindi belt when the product is operational rather than strategic. Ask which language they prefer; do not guess from the city.
- **Do not use the recipient's first name in the first line** with an older owner or a PSU contact - use the title.

## Deliverability realities

- **A new domain sending cold email gets filtered.** Warm for two to three weeks, 20-30 a day, and only then ramp.
- **Free-mail senders (Gmail to Gmail)** have low tolerance for volume and high tolerance for a plain, short email. Plain text often beats designed HTML here.
- **Corporate Indian domains** (large groups, banks, PSUs) filter aggressively and often quarantine unknown senders entirely. Expect phone or referral to be the only route in.
- **Links to a newly registered domain** trigger filtering. Send the first touch without a link.

## Anti-patterns

- **Blasting WhatsApp templates to a purchased list.** The number gets banned, and the ban is per-number, not per-campaign.
- **Calling at 20:30 because "the owner is free then".** It reads as disrespect, and DND rules aside, the relationship is gone.
- **Running a Diwali-week campaign.** The creative looks tone-deaf and the inbox is dead.
- **Assuming a `.co.in` domain means the company is real.** Check MCA21 or GSTIN status.
- **Using a personal Gmail to look "small and friendly"** for enterprise outbound - it looks unverifiable instead.
- **Ignoring the gatekeeper.** In Indian B2B, the EA or the purchase assistant decides whether the email is ever read; write to them with respect and they will route you.
