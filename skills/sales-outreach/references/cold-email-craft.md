# Cold email craft

A cold email has one job: earn a reply from someone who has no reason to give you one. Everything below serves that.

## The anatomy

| Part | Job | Budget |
|---|---|---|
| Subject | Get it opened without promising anything | 3-6 words, lowercase often better |
| First line | Prove you know who they are | One specific, verifiable fact |
| Second line | Name the cost of the status quo | One sentence, in their language |
| Third line | The offer, as an outcome | One sentence |
| Proof | One number or one named customer | One sentence or a link |
| Ask | A small, specific next step | One question, answerable in four words |
| Signature | A human | Name, role, phone |

Total: 50-125 words. If it does not fit on a phone screen without scrolling, it is too long.

## Six opening patterns that work

| Pattern | Example | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger event | "You opened the Hosur plant in August - congrats. The first quarter is when stock counts usually break." | You have a dated, verifiable event |
| Peer comparison | "Three auto-parts makers in Coimbatore moved to barcode-based counting last year. All three cut stock-outs." | The segment is small and competitive |
| Cost of the status quo | "Most 200-person manufacturers lose 3-5% of stock value a year to untracked transfers." | The pain is quantifiable and well known |
| Specific observation | "Your IndiaMART listing shows 14 SKUs, your site shows 40. That gap usually means two systems." | You actually looked and found something |
| Credential-free proof | "We did this for a Pune tier-1 supplier: 11 days to 2 for monthly reconciliation." | You have one strong customer story |
| Question that is not rhetorical | "How many people touch a stock transfer before it reaches the ledger?" | The buyer likes diagnosing |

## Subject lines

- Best performers are boring and specific: "stock counts at Hosur", "question about tier-1 audits", "Tally to barcode".
- Never: "Quick question", "Following up", "Revolutionise your inventory", anything with the recipient's name and a colon, anything in all caps, emoji.
- No subject line at all sometimes outperforms - it reads like a human in a hurry. Test it, do not assume it.
- The subject must not promise something the body does not deliver.

## The ask ladder

From lowest to highest commitment. Pick the lowest one that still moves the deal.

1. "Worth a look?" - yes/no
2. "Should I send the one-pager?" - yes/no, gives them control
3. "Is this a problem for you?" - diagnostic, invites a real answer
4. "Who else should I talk to?" - routing, useful even on a no
5. "Can I have 15 minutes on Thursday?" - a real ask, use only at touch three or later

Asking for a meeting in touch one is the single most common reason for silence. The buyer has not yet decided you are worth 15 minutes.

## What actually kills replies

| Mistake | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| Opening with "I hope this finds you well" | Signals a template in the first four words |
| Paragraph about the company's founding year | Nobody buys from a company history |
| Three links and an attachment | Deliverability penalty and it looks like a brochure |
| Feature list | Features answer a question the buyer has not asked |
| "Let me know if you'd like to hop on a call" | Puts the work of scheduling on them |
| Fake personalisation | `{first_name}` visible, or a compliment that could be sent to anyone |
| Too clever a subject | Reads as marketing, gets deleted unopened |
| No ask at all | A newsletter, not outreach |

## Worked example

> Subject: stock counts at Hosur
>
> Hi Prakash,
>
> You opened the Hosur plant in August, which usually means stock counts across two sites for the first time.
>
> We do barcode-based counting for auto-parts makers - a Pune tier-1 supplier went from 11 days to 2 for monthly reconciliation, with the same three people.
>
> Is multi-site counting a problem you are solving this year, or already handled?
>
> Manikant, [company], +91 XXXXX XXXXX

Why it works: verifiable trigger, their language, one proof with a number, an ask answerable in five words, 74 words total.

## Anti-patterns

- **Writing to a persona instead of a person.** "Dear Purchase Manager" is a template; "Prakash" is outreach.
- **Explaining the product before naming the problem.** The buyer buys relief, not capability.
- **Sending the same email to five roles in one company.** They forward it to each other and all five feel handled.
- **Longer emails to senior people.** Seniority shortens attention, not lengthens it.
- **A/B testing subject lines while the offer is weak.** Fix the offer first; a good subject on a bad offer just gets read and rejected faster.
- **Claiming urgency you cannot support.** "Only this month" every month teaches the buyer to ignore you.
