# The scheme landscape

There are hundreds of schemes and about seven things they actually do. Knowing the seven
is faster than knowing the hundred.

**Read this first: no threshold, rate, cap or window appears in this file on purpose.**
Every parameter must be read from the current guideline at the official source on the day
it is used, and recorded with the date and the source. A parameter remembered is a
parameter wrong.

## The seven families

| Family | What it does | Who it usually fits | Where to start |
|---|---|---|---|
| Credit guarantee | covers part of a lender's risk so the loan is sanctioned, often with better terms | a business with a viable loan and no collateral | the lender's MSME desk, and the guarantee trust's own guideline |
| Capital subsidy | reimburses or offsets part of an eligible fixed investment | manufacturing, expansion, plant and machinery | the state industrial policy, and the state development body |
| Interest support | reduces the interest cost on a term loan | manufacturing, sometimes services | the state policy or the central ministry's scheme |
| Tax incentive | an exemption, a holiday or a deduction for eligible years | recognised startups, specified sectors, specified locations | the tax department's own notification, and the CA |
| Export incentive | a remission or a duty benefit on exported goods | exporters with an IEC and a shipping record | the customs and foreign trade policy notifications, and the export promotion council |
| Procurement preference | a share of public purchasing reserved or preferred for a class of supplier | MSMEs, startups, make-in-India suppliers | GeM and the public procurement policy |
| Grant | a non-repayable amount for a defined activity - market development, technology, training | specific sectors, specific activities, usually competitive | the ministry or the state body running the programme |

If a scheme does not fit one of these seven, it is either a loan (a bank product, not an
incentive) or a service (a facilitation centre). Both are useful and neither is an
incentive.

## The hierarchy that decides everything

1. **Central scheme** - the ministry's guideline governs. Uniform across states.
2. **State scheme** - the state's industrial policy governs, and states differ sharply on
   what they subsidise, for how long, and for which talukas.
3. **Both, sometimes** - a central credit guarantee on a loan that also earns a state
   interest subsidy is common; a central capital subsidy stacked on a state capital
   subsidy usually is not. The exclusion is written in one of the two guidelines, and it
   is the first thing to check.
4. **The implementing agency** - the ministry may write the scheme and a bank, a trust or
   a state corporation administer it. The agency's circular, not the scheme name, decides
   the process.

## How to read a guideline

Read in this order, and write down the clause number for each answer:

1. **The objective** - one paragraph. If the business is not the intended beneficiary, stop
   here; that is the cheapest rejection to avoid.
2. **Definitions** - of turnover, of investment, of a micro or small enterprise, of a
   startup, of a unit. Every rejection in this field is a definitions rejection.
3. **Eligibility** - entity type, age, registration, sector, location, and the exclusions.
4. **The benefit** - the formula, the cap, the basis on which it is computed, and whether
   it is reimbursement or up-front.
5. **Conditions** - what must be true after the money arrives, and for how long.
6. **The application process** - the window, the portal, the documents, the certifying
   authority.
7. **The claim process** - how the money is actually released, which is a different
   process from the approval and where most of it is lost.

## Where the official sources are

- **The ministry's own site** for the scheme guideline and its amendments.
- **The gazette notification** where a scheme is statutory; the notification number is the
  reference the office will ask for.
- **The state industrial policy** for the incentive package, with the government
  resolution that operationalises it.
- **The implementing agency's circulars** - the bank, the trust, the state corporation.
- **The portal's own help and FAQ**, which is often more current than the PDF.

A consultancy blog, a WhatsApp forward, a newspaper column and a competitor's claim are
not sources. They are leads, and they are worth exactly one click to the official page.

## What to tell a founder who arrives with a rumour

Three sentences: what the scheme family does, what the eligibility usually turns on, and
the one fact that decides it for this business. Then the check. Founders who arrive with a
rumour usually need a yes or no within a day, and the honest answer is "probably, if your
turnover is under the threshold in the current guideline - let me read it, one hour".

## Anti-patterns

- **The twelve-scheme listicle** with no eligibility test.
- **A scheme named without its authority and its notification number**, which cannot be
  checked by anyone.
- **Quoting a benefit in rupees** when the guideline states a formula with a cap.
- **Assuming a state scheme applies** because a neighbouring state has an equivalent.
- **Skipping the exclusion clause**, which is where stacking is decided.
