# Rejection, resubmission and the calendar after approval

A rejection is a document with a reason in it. Read the reason before reacting to it.

## The five real reasons

| Reason as written | What it usually means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| "Ineligible investment" | the asset does not meet the definition - land, vehicle, second-hand, or bought before the cut-off | re-read the benefit clause; recompute what is eligible; resubmit on the eligible portion if the window allows |
| "Turnover exceeds the limit" | a different turnover definition was applied, or a group company was clubbed | get the computation in writing; the CA's certificate against the definition |
| "Documents incomplete" | an annexure was missing or illegible | the cheapest rejection to fix; resubmit within the window |
| "Registration not valid on the relevant date" | Udyam, GST or the licence post-dates the investment or the application | a date problem, not an eligibility problem - check whether the scheme allows a cure |
| "Duplicate benefit" | the same asset took a benefit elsewhere, or the office believes it did | produce the exclusion analysis; if it is right, withdraw and pick the better scheme |

## Resubmission

- **Ask for the rejection reason in writing** if it was verbal. The written reason is the
  specification for the next attempt.
- **Fix the cause, not the symptom.** Resubmitting the same pack with an added covering
  letter is the most common wasted month in this field.
- **Check the window.** Some schemes allow resubmission inside the same window, some in
  the next cycle, some not at all for the same asset.
- **Record the reason in `notes/learnings.md`** as a rule, because the same cause will
  appear in the next application.
- **Escalate in writing** where the reason contradicts the guideline, quoting the clause.
  Rarely, and with the clause in hand.

## The calendar after approval

Most of the value in this area is lost after the sanction, at a date nobody wrote down.

| Event | What it is | When it bites |
|---|---|---|
| Claim window opens | the period in which the claim may be filed | usually short, and starts on the investment date |
| Instalment claim | each tranche of a staged benefit | quarterly or per milestone |
| Compliance condition | production, employment or location conditions to keep the benefit | checked at the next claim or at audit |
| Lock-in | the period the asset must stay in use or the unit must stay in the state | a sale or a shift triggers recovery |
| Renewal or annual return | some schemes require an annual filing to stay eligible | quietly missed |
| Audit and recall | the office's right to verify and to recover with interest | the reason to keep records for the full period |

Put every one of these in the calendar on the day the approval arrives, with the evidence
needed at each date.

## Records to keep

- The application, the acknowledgement and every version of the pack.
- The sanction letter and its conditions, read line by line.
- Invoices, payment proofs, installation and Chartered Engineer certificates.
- The claim filings and the disbursement advices.
- The correspondence, including the clarifications received.
- The asset register entry, so the lock-in is visible to whoever might sell the machine.

Keep them for the period the scheme specifies, and longer if the lock-in runs longer.
Recovery with interest is the standard remedy for a broken condition, and it is
recoverable from a business that has since sold the asset.

## Anti-patterns

- **Arguing with the rejection instead of reading it.**
- **Resubmitting unchanged**, which burns the window.
- **Celebrating the sanction** and forgetting the claim.
- **Selling an asset inside its lock-in**, which converts a subsidy into a liability.
- **No single owner for the claim calendar**, which is how a date gets missed.
