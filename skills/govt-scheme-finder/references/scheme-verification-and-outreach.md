# Verification and outreach

Everything in this space is verifiable, and almost nothing on the internet is verified. This file is
how a fact becomes safe to put in front of a founder.

## The verification ladder

| Rung | Source | Trust |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The scheme's own guidelines or notification, on the government domain | Cite it |
| 2 | The administering office, in writing (DIC, nodal agency, bank) | Cite it, with the date and the officer |
| 3 | The scheme portal's current status page | Cite it, with the date |
| 4 | A government press release or budget speech | Cite it as an announcement, not as an operative rate |
| 5 | A consultant's brochure, a blog, a YouTube video, a newspaper summary | A lead. Never evidence |
| 6 | Memory of how it worked two years ago | Nothing |

Rule: **anything from rung 4 or below is written into the brief as "announced, verify at the
portal"**, with the date it must be checked. A founder who is told to verify is informed; a founder
who is told a number that turns out to be wrong stops trusting the whole exercise.

## The three facts that change most often

| Fact | Check how | Check when |
|---|---|---|
| The rate or ceiling | The scheme guidelines and the latest notification | Before every brief |
| The application window | The portal's notice, and the DIC | Weekly while an application is open |
| District or sector eligibility | The state notification and the DIC | Before the application, in writing |

If those three are current, the rest of the brief can be built with confidence. If they are not, say
so in the first line.

## Who to ask, and how

| Office | What they decide | How to ask |
|---|---|---|
| **District Industries Centre (DIC)** | State incentives, PMEGP routing, local eligibility | In person, with the Udyam certificate; ask for the officer's name and a written reply |
| **State MSME / industries directorate** | The state policy, the window, the interpretation | Email, quoting the clause; keep the reply |
| **Bank MSME desk** | CGTMSE, Mudra, the guarantee, the sanction | A written proposal, and a named relationship manager |
| **Nodal agency for the sector scheme** | Sector-specific eligibility and the claim | The portal's grievance or query route, with the application number |
| **Export promotion council** | RCMC, MAI, market access | Membership, then the council's scheme desk |
| **DGFT regional office** | IEC, authorisations, RoDTEP queries | The portal, then the regional office |
| **CA** | Turnover, investment, certification, the UC | A specific question with the document attached |

Three rules for the ask: **ask one question per email**, **quote the clause you are asking about**,
and **ask for the answer in writing**. A verbal "it should be fine" from a counter is the most
expensive sentence in this domain.

## The consultant and the tout

| Signal | Reading |
|---|---|
| "Guaranteed sanction" | A lie, and the first sign of a fraud |
| A large success fee with no written scope | Paying for access, not work |
| Asking for the portal login and the originals | No visibility, and no control |
| A fee paid before any work | Unusual for a professional engagement |
| "I know someone in the office" | A tout, and a risk to the file |
| No GST invoice, cash only | Never |
| A rate quoted higher than the official one | Either a fraud or a misunderstanding of the cap |

What a good consultant is worth paying for: the project report, the financials, the technical
write-up, the follow-up, and the time the founder does not have. What they are not worth paying for:
access, a guarantee, or the filing itself, which the founder can do free on the portal.

## The brief's verification block

Every scheme brief ends with this, filled in:

```
Verified on <date>:
  Rate / ceiling ............ <source, URL, date read>
  Window .................... <source, and the closing date>
  District / sector ......... <DIC or notification reference>
  Definitions tested ........ <new unit / expansion / activity / band>
Not verified, and must be before filing:
  <the fact> ............ confirm at <office> by <date>
```

That block is what makes the brief usable by someone else, and it is the difference between advice
and a claim.

## Anti-patterns

| Anti-pattern | Cost |
|---|---|
| Quoting a rate from a video or a blog | A number the office contradicts, and trust lost |
| No date on a verification | Nobody knows if it is current |
| A verbal confirmation treated as approval | It is not, and it is disputed later |
| One email with eight questions | Four go unanswered, and the fifth is misread |
| Not quoting the clause | The reply answers a different question |
| Recommending a consultant without the fee structure stated | The founder pays for access and calls it expertise |
| Skipping the verification because the scheme is "well known" | Well-known schemes change their definitions most often |
