# Claim and disbursement discipline

The sanction is not the money, and the money is not the end. Between the two sit milestones,
certificates and an audit that can take it all back.

## The stages, and what each one can fail on

| Stage | What happens | What kills it |
|---|---|---|
| Application | Filed, with the pack | A figure that does not match the ITR |
| Query and response | The office asks for clarifications | Missing the deadline, silently |
| In-principle or sanction | Approved, with conditions | Conditions the founder cannot meet |
| Expenditure | The unit buys and installs | Buying outside the eligible list, or before the sanction |
| Claim submission | Invoices, payment proof, commissioning certificate | A payment made in cash, with no trail |
| Verification | An inspection or a document check | A machine that is not where the file says |
| Disbursement | Money reaches the account | A change in bank details, or an incomplete UC |
| Utilisation certificate | Proof that the money was used as sanctioned | No records, and a demand for a refund |
| Retention period | Conditions held for the stated years | A sale, a closure, or a change of activity |

## The sanction letter, read line by line

| Clause | What to extract |
|---|---|
| Amount and the basis | The rate, the eligible investment, the cap |
| Eligible items | The list, and the negative list |
| Conditions precedent | What must happen before the first disbursement |
| Milestones | What must be achieved, by when |
| Disbursement tranches | How much, on what proof |
| Utilisation certificate | The format, the signatory, the deadline |
| Retention and lock-in | Years, and what triggers a refund |
| Employment or production conditions | Numbers, and the period |
| Inspection rights | Who may inspect, and what they will look at |
| Recovery clause | Interest, penalty, and the process |
| Reporting | What must be filed, and how often |

Anything in that table the founder cannot meet is a reason not to accept the sanction. A sanction
with an employment condition the unit cannot sustain is a liability with a cheque attached.

## The claim file, built as the money is spent

| Evidence | Why it is asked for | How it is lost |
|---|---|---|
| Invoice in the entity's name and GSTIN | Proof of purchase | Billed to a proprietor personally |
| Payment proof from the entity's bank account | Proof the unit paid | Paid in cash, or from another entity |
| Delivery challan and installation report | Proof it arrived | A single invoice with no receipt |
| Commissioning certificate | Proof it works | No date, no signatory |
| Machine serial number and photographs | Proof of identity | No plate, no photo |
| Fixed asset register entry | Proof it is capitalised | Expensed in the books |
| Insurance, where required | A condition in some schemes | Taken after the claim |

The rule: **pay by bank, from the entity's account, and keep the serial number.** Cash payments are
the single most common reason a genuine claim is rejected.

## The utilisation certificate

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Sanction reference and date | The scheme's own reference |
| Amount received | Tranche-wise, with dates |
| Amount utilised | Against the sanctioned heads |
| Unspent balance | And where it is held |
| Assets created | Item, value, location |
| Signatory | The CA, and the promoter |
| Supporting | Invoices, payment proof, the asset register |

A UC filed late or with an unexplained balance is the most common trigger for a recovery notice.
File it on time even when the project is incomplete, with the reason and a revised date.

## Records to keep, and for how long

| Record | Period |
|---|---|
| Sanction letter and all correspondence | The retention period, plus 3 years |
| Invoices, payment proof, delivery and installation | The same |
| Utilisation certificates and filings | The same |
| Asset register and insurance | The life of the asset |
| Employment or production evidence | The condition period |
| Inspection reports and replies | The same |

Keep them in one folder per sanction, physical and digital, with the file number on the spine. A
recovery is decided on documents, and the unit that can produce them in an hour usually keeps the
money.

## Common recovery triggers

| Trigger | What happens |
|---|---|
| The asset is sold inside the lock-in | Pro-rata recovery, with interest |
| The unit closes or shifts outside the state | Recovery under most state policies |
| Employment or production condition missed | Recovery or a withheld tranche |
| The subsidy was claimed twice for the same asset | Full recovery, and a bar on future schemes |
| The asset was never installed at the stated site | Recovery and, in serious cases, an inquiry |
| A false declaration in the application | Recovery with penalty, and possible prosecution |
| The UC is never filed | The balance is demanded back |

## The cash-flow truth

| Belief | Reality |
|---|---|
| "The subsidy funds the purchase" | The purchase funds the subsidy; the money comes after commissioning |
| "Sanction means the money is mine" | It is a commitment, conditional on the milestones |
| "I can claim it next year too" | Windows close, and budgets are finite |
| "It is a grant, so it is free money" | It is a conditional transfer with an audit |
| "The consultant will handle the UC" | The signatory is the founder, and the liability is the entity's |

Plan the funding as if the subsidy will arrive six to eighteen months after the application, and
treat it as a recovery of cost, not as a source of finance. Any project that only works if the
subsidy arrives on time does not work.

## Anti-patterns

| Anti-pattern | Cost |
|---|---|
| Accepting a sanction with conditions nobody read | A breach, a recovery, and a bar |
| Cash payments to a vendor | A claim that cannot be proved |
| No serial numbers or photographs | A verification that fails on identity |
| An expense booked to a director's account | The unit did not pay, so the claim is invalid |
| A late utilisation certificate | A demand for the balance |
| No folder per sanction | An inspection the unit cannot answer |
| Selling the subsidised asset early | Pro-rata recovery with interest |
| Treating the subsidy as working capital | A cash-flow gap at the worst moment |
