# Performance and Appraisal Cycle

An appraisal cycle is a calendar plus a form plus a conversation. Most companies have the form and neither of the other two, which is why the outcome is always a surprise to somebody.

## The cycle, dated

| Stage | Who | Output | Typical window |
|---|---|---|---|
| Goal setting | Manager and employee | 3-5 objectives with a measure each | Start of the year |
| Mid-cycle check | Manager and employee | Written note, goals adjusted if the business changed | Month 5-6 |
| Self-assessment | Employee | Their own rating with evidence per objective | Month 11 |
| Manager assessment | Manager | Rating against the same objectives, with evidence | Week after |
| Calibration | Leadership | Ratings comparable across teams | Week after |
| Outcome conversation | Manager | Rating, reasons, and the decision on pay or role | Before the letter |
| Letter and appeal | HR | Written outcome, and the route to appeal | Within the cycle |

## The rating scale

Use four or five points, define each in one line, and give a distribution expectation rather than a forced curve. A five-point scale defined as "1 unacceptable, 2 below, 3 meets, 4 exceeds, 5 exceptional" is enough. What matters is that two managers reading the same definition would land on the same rating for the same evidence.

Objectives must be testable. "Improve customer satisfaction" is not; "raise the monthly CSAT from 82 to 88 by March, measured on the same survey" is.

## Calibration, and why it is the step that fails

Without calibration, a lenient manager's "meets expectations" and a strict manager's "meets expectations" are different ratings that look identical on the letter. Calibration is one session where ratings are read side by side across teams and adjusted to a common standard, with the reason recorded. It is uncomfortable and it is the entire value of the cycle.

## Performance improvement, done properly

A PIP is a fair process, not a paperwork step before termination. It needs:

- The specific gap, with evidence and dates.
- What good looks like, in measurable terms.
- The support the company will provide - training, tools, a changed scope.
- A defined period, usually 30 to 90 days.
- Named check-ins, with dates.
- What happens at the end, stated in advance, in both directions.

A PIP written after the decision to exit has been taken is not a process, and it will not survive a challenge.

## What to hand over

The dated cycle calendar, the goal-setting template, the assessment form with defined ratings, the calibration sheet, the outcome letter, the appeal route, and the PIP template.
