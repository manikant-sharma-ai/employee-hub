# Employee Relations and POSH

Three different routes, and the first job is to identify which one the matter belongs to. Running a harassment complaint through a general grievance route, or the reverse, is how organisations turn a manageable problem into a legal one.

## Route selection

| Situation | Route | Governing frame |
|---|---|---|
| A work complaint - process, workload, a decision, a colleague's behaviour that is not sexual | Grievance | Internal policy, natural justice |
| Any unwelcome sexual behaviour, whether a request, contact, remark, showing material or implied threat | POSH | The POSH Act and the Internal Committee |
| A rule broken, misconduct, theft, fraud, repeated absence | Disciplinary | Show cause, inquiry, penalty scale |
| A dispute over pay, leave or a settlement | Reconciliation, then grievance | The policy and the ledger |

## POSH: the structure the law requires

- **An Internal Committee** at every establishment of 10 or more, with a presiding officer who is a senior woman, at least two employee members, and one external member with expertise in harassment matters.
- **A written policy**, published and circulated, naming the committee members and how to reach them.
- **A complaint window** - the statute allows three months, extendable for good reason.
- **A time-bound process** - completion of inquiry within the statutory period, with the employer acting on the recommendation.
- **Confidentiality** - no publication of the identity of the complainant, the respondent or witnesses. Penalty attaches to breach.
- **Protection from retaliation** during and after the process.
- **An annual report** to the district officer, with the number of complaints and their disposal.
- **Awareness sessions**, at the stated frequency.

Where the threshold is not met, the district Local Committee handles it - and employees must be told that, in writing, with the route to reach it.

## What you do, and what you must never do

You **constitute** the route: policy, committee terms, intake format, timeline, record-keeping, awareness material. You **brief** the parties on process. You **draft** the neutral communication.

You never investigate, interview, weigh evidence, assess credibility or recommend a finding. You never advise the complainant on whether what happened "counts". You never share the complaint outside the committee, and never in a file that leaves the conversation.

## Grievance, in practice

- A named recipient by role, and an alternate in case the recipient is the subject of the complaint.
- A written acknowledgement within a stated number of days.
- A stated path: informal resolution where both sides agree, formal process otherwise.
- A written outcome, with reasons.
- An appeal to someone senior to the decision-maker.
- A log of grievances by category, without names, reviewed monthly - that log is how a company finds the manager everyone is quietly complaining about.

## Disciplinary, without procedural defects

Show cause with the specific allegation and the rule it breaches. Time to reply. An inquiry where the facts are disputed, by a person who is not the complainant and not the decision-maker. Findings on evidence. A penalty proportionate to the finding, from a published scale. An appeal. Everything in writing, everything dated. The most common reason a penalty is overturned is not the facts - it is a missing step.
