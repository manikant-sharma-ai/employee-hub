# Employee Handbook and Policy

The handbook is not a legal shield first. It is the document an employee opens on day two to find out how leave works. Write for that reader, and the legal purpose is served as a side effect.

## The shape that works

One page per subject. Each page carries the same six things, in the same order:

1. **Who it applies to** - all employees, or a named group. Contract staff and interns are usually excluded; say so explicitly.
2. **The rule** - one sentence, in the plainest words available.
3. **The number** - days, hours, amounts, notice periods. A policy without numbers is an opinion.
4. **The approver** - by role, not by name.
5. **The exception route** - who can approve a deviation, and how it is recorded.
6. **Effective date and version** - so a dispute about "which policy applied then" is answerable.

## The policy set a small company actually needs

Start with these nine and resist adding more until one of them fails:

| Policy | Why it exists | The number it must contain |
|---|---|---|
| Working hours and attendance | The base every leave and payroll argument sits on | Shift timings, grace period, weekly off |
| Leave | The most-asked question in any company | Entitlement per type, accrual, carry-forward cap |
| Notice period and separation | Sets what an exit costs both sides | Days by grade, recovery basis |
| Code of conduct | The behaviour that gets someone fired | Examples, and the consequence scale |
| POSH | Statutory, and the one with personal liability for the employer | Committee composition, complaint window |
| Grievance | The route before a complaint becomes a legal matter | Who receives it, response days |
| Disciplinary process | Fair procedure, so a penalty survives challenge | Steps, show-cause, appeal |
| Expenses and reimbursement | Where money leaks | Limits by category, approval, submission window |
| Work from home or hybrid | Now standard, rarely written | Eligible roles, days, equipment, availability |

Add IT and data security, travel, and mobile and internet allowance when the business has the volume to justify them.

## Writing rules

- **Second person, active voice.** "You may carry forward five days." Not "carry forward of five days is permitted".
- **No "as per company policy".** That phrase means the policy is not in the document. Put the rule in.
- **One term per concept, everywhere.** If it is "casual leave" in the handbook, it is not "CL" in one annexure and "personal leave" in another.
- **Numbers, not adjectives.** "Prompt intimation" becomes "within 24 hours, to your reporting manager, in writing".
- **State the consequence.** A rule with no consequence is a suggestion, and everyone learns that within a month.

## The version and consent problem

A handbook binds nobody until it is acknowledged. Two practical steps:

- Issue it with a one-page acknowledgement, signed at joining and re-signed on material revision.
- Keep a revision table at the front: version, date, what changed, who approved.

Where an employee was hired under an earlier version, the change applies from the stated date, and the appointment letter should not contradict it. If it does, that contradiction is the finding to raise before publishing.

## Language and reach

For a workforce that reads Hindi, Marathi, Tamil or Bengali more comfortably than English, publish a summary in that language alongside the English master. The English version governs; the summary is what people actually use. Do not machine-translate a legal clause and present it as the governing text.

## What to hand over

- The policy set, one page each, with the six fields filled.
- The revision table and the acknowledgement page.
- A gap list: policies the business needs that do not exist, ranked by the risk of not having them.
- Conflicts found between the handbook, the appointment letter and current practice - named, with the version to keep.
