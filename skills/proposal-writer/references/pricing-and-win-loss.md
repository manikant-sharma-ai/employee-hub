# Pricing and win-loss

A proposal price is a commitment attached to a scope. Both halves have to be
written down.

## The commercial section, in this order

1. **Price**, with the basis (per month, per unit, one-time, milestone).
2. **What is included** - scope, quantity, service levels, support window.
3. **What is not included** - the exclusions. This section prevents most disputes.
4. **Assumptions** - volume, data quality, access, timelines, third-party costs.
5. **Validity** - how long the price holds, and what moves it (FX, commodity, statutory change).
6. **Payment terms** - milestones, credit period, advance, retention.
7. **Taxes** - GST rate and whether prices are inclusive or exclusive.
8. **Change-request rate** - the hourly or per-item price for work outside scope.

A price without 2-8 is a number, not an offer.

## Setting the floor before the bid

Write the walk-away price before the negotiation, not during it:

```
cost of delivery  +  overhead allocation  +  minimum margin  =  floor
```

Record it in `notes/decisions.md` with the date. Then a discount inside a proposal
is an arithmetic question rather than a mood. Below the floor, the answer is no,
and the proposal says what scope would fit the budget instead - a smaller
compliant offer beats a discounted non-compliant one.

## Discount discipline

- **Never discount without removing scope.** A discount with the same scope teaches the buyer that the first price was not real.
- **Trade, do not give.** Longer term, faster payment, a reference, a case study, a smaller support window.
- **One discount authority.** The founder, in writing, before submission.
- **Do not lead with the discount** in the cover letter. It invites a second ask.

## Win-loss review

Every outcome, including no-decision, gets one entry:

| Date | Buyer | Value | Outcome | Stated reason | Our read | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

The "our read" column is the useful one. Losing on price is usually losing on
scope clarity, on a missed mandatory line, or on a reference that could not be
reached. Track the pattern monthly; if the same reason appears three times, it is
a process fix, not bad luck.

## The reuse library

Keep the content that is true and reusable: company overview, certifications,
security posture, delivery methodology, team bios, standard exclusions. Version
each block with a date and an owner. A proposal should be 70% reused, verified
content and 30% written for this buyer - anything else and the same paragraph gets
rewritten, with a different number, by a different person, every time.
