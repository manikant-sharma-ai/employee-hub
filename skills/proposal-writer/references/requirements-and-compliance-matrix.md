# Requirements and the compliance matrix

The matrix is the proposal. Everything else is prose arranged around it.

## Step 1 - shred the document

Read the whole requirements document once, without writing, then extract every
requirement into rows. Three sources:

| Source | What it looks like | Why it is missed |
|---|---|---|
| The requirement sections | Numbered clauses, "the bidder shall..." | Long, and people skim |
| The annexures | Forms, templates, declarations | Nobody reads the annexures until submission day |
| The instructions to bidders | Format, page limit, EMD, submission channel | Treated as boilerplate, and it disqualifies |

A 40-page RFP typically yields 120-250 rows. If your matrix has 20 rows, you have read
the summary, not the document.

## Step 2 - classify every row

| Type | Meaning | Consequence of missing it |
|---|---|---|
| **Mandatory** | "Shall", "must", eligibility, a required form | Disqualified, regardless of score |
| **Scored** | "Will be evaluated on", marks published or implied | Marks lost, ranking drops |
| **Administrative** | Format, copies, signatures, naming, EMD | Disqualified on a technicality |
| **Informational** | Background, context, no response needed | Nothing - and do not waste pages on it |

Tag by the verb, not by the section. "The bidder shall submit a bank guarantee" is
mandatory; "bidders may provide additional information" is not.

## Step 3 - the matrix

| # | Requirement (client's words, verbatim) | Type | Marks | Section | Page | Status | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Bidder shall have minimum 3 years' experience in X | Mandatory | - | 2.1 | 4 | Comply | Incorporation + 2 POs |
| 2 | Bidder shall have executed 3 similar projects in the last 2 years | Mandatory | - | 2.2 | 4-5 | Comply | 3 completion certificates |
| 3 | Approach and methodology | Scored | 20 | 3 | 6-9 | Comply | Method + Gantt |
| 4 | Local support within 50 km | Scored | 10 | 4 | 10 | Comply with deviation | Support centre at 38 km |
| 5 | 24×7 support with 2-hour response | Scored | 10 | 4 | 10-11 | Comply | SLA extract + escalation chart |
| 6 | Price schedule in Annexure C format | Administrative | - | 5 | 14 | Comply | Annexure C filled |
| 7 | EMD of ₹2 lakh by DD or BG | Administrative | - | Annexure | - | Comply | DD no., date |

Rules that keep the matrix honest:

- **Quote the requirement verbatim**, then paraphrase in the response. Paraphrasing in the
  matrix hides a misread.
- **One row per requirement.** Combining two requirements into one row is how the second
  one disappears.
- **Page numbers are filled at the end and checked by a second person.** A matrix with
  wrong page numbers is worse than none, because it sends the evaluator to the wrong place.
- **Status is one of three**: Comply, Comply with deviation, Not comply. "Partially" is a
  deviation and must be declared as one.

## Step 4 - the deviation register

Every "comply with deviation" and every "not comply" goes here, with a reason and an
alternative:

| # | Requirement | Deviation | Reason | Alternative offered | Score impact |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | Local support within 50 km | Support centre at 38 km; engineer on site within 4 hours, not 2 | Nearest staffed centre | Dedicated resident engineer for the first 6 months | Minor |

A declared deviation with a credible alternative is evaluated and often accepted. An
undeclared one is discovered in the contract negotiation, at the worst possible moment.

## Step 5 - clarification questions

Ask, in the client's window, for anything that changes the price or the compliance
position:

- A mandatory requirement that contradicts an annexure.
- A specification that names a brand or a model (and whether equivalents are allowed).
- An undefined volume, term or service level.
- A submission requirement that the portal cannot accept.
- A payment term that is outside the company's policy.

Ask each question once, in the client's format, numbered, and record the answer against
the matrix rows it changes. Never ask a question that reveals a weakness in the bid.

## The red-team read

Before submission, one person who did not write the proposal takes the matrix and the
document and, for each mandatory row, finds the answer in ten seconds. Every row they
cannot find is either a missing answer or a badly placed one. Both are cheap to fix now.

## Anti-patterns

| Anti-pattern | Cost |
|---|---|
| Matrix built from the executive summary | The mandatory clause on page 34 is missed |
| "Partially complies" | Treated as a deviation, undeclared |
| Requirement paraphrased in the matrix | A misread is invisible until the debrief |
| Page numbers filled from memory | The evaluator cannot find the answer |
| Deviations hidden to protect the score | The contract, or the termination, discovers them |
| No clarification questions | Risk priced as contingency instead of resolved |
| 20 rows for a 40-page RFP | The document was not read |
