# House style guide

A style guide is not a document about taste. It is a decision log that stops the
same argument happening in every document.

## The twenty decisions every guide needs

| # | Decision | Options | Default if the client has none |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Spelling variant | -ise / -ize / US | -ise (Indian English) |
| 2 | Serial comma | yes / no | No, except where it removes ambiguity |
| 3 | Numbers under ten | words / figures | Words under ten, figures from 10 |
| 4 | Numbers at sentence start | words / restructure | Restructure |
| 5 | Large numbers | lakh-crore / million | Lakh and crore for Indian readers |
| 6 | Currency | ₹ / Rs / INR | ₹ with no space, code on first use |
| 7 | Dates | dd/mm/yyyy / 6 Nov 2026 | 6 November 2026 in prose, dd/mm/yyyy in tables |
| 8 | Time | IST / GMT+5:30 | IST, 24-hour in schedules |
| 9 | Headings | sentence / title case | Sentence case |
| 10 | Job titles | capitalised / lower case | Lower case unless before a name |
| 11 | Product names | as the brand writes them | Never inflect: no "Zooming" |
| 12 | Company reference | we / the company | We in external prose, the company in policy |
| 13 | Abbreviations | spell out first use | Always, except GST, PAN, UPI |
| 14 | Contractions | allowed / not | Allowed in marketing, not in contracts |
| 15 | Percent | % / per cent | % with figures, per cent in prose |
| 16 | Units | metric / imperial | Metric, with the local unit in brackets once |
| 17 | Hyphenation | closed / open | Closed compounds, hyphen for modifiers |
| 18 | Emphasis | bold / italics | Bold for UI labels, italics for titles |
| 19 | Lists | full stop / none | No full stop on fragments, full stop on sentences |
| 20 | Second person | you / the user | You in anything a customer reads |

## What goes on the sheet beyond the decisions

- **The ten recurring errors**, with the correct form and an example.
- **The terminology ledger** - preferred term and banned variants.
- **The names that must never be changed**: legal entity, product, plan, certification.
- **The exception list** - documents governed by an external template, and which rules give way there.
- **The date and the owner.** A style guide without an owner drifts within a quarter.

## Building it in an hour, not a month

1. Collect five documents the company considers good.
2. Read them for the twenty decisions above and write down what they *actually* do, not what anyone says they do.
3. Where they disagree, ask the person who owns the brand, once, and record the answer.
4. Publish one page. Long guides are not read; one page with the ten recurring errors is used.

## The one-page version, as it actually gets used

Most teams never read past the first screen. Put these ten lines at the top and
the twenty decisions underneath as the reference:

```
1. -ise, not -ize. "organise", "prioritise".
2. No serial comma, unless it removes ambiguity.
3. Numbers: words under ten, figures from 10. Never start a sentence with a figure.
4. Money: ₹4,50,000 - Indian grouping, no space, no "Rs".
5. Dates: 6 November 2026 in prose, 06/11/2026 in tables.
6. Headings in sentence case. Job titles lower case unless before a name.
7. Our product is <name>, always spelled exactly that way, never pluralised.
8. "Customer" for anyone who pays us. Never "client", never "user".
9. We write "we" to the outside world and "the company" in policy.
10. No contractions in contracts. Contractions are fine everywhere else.
```

## Who owns what

| Role | Owns | Cannot change alone |
|---|---|---|
| Style owner (brand or marketing lead) | The twenty decisions, and exceptions | Nothing - but changes are dated and published |
| Copy editor | Applying the sheet, the ledger, the queries | The decisions themselves |
| Legal reviewer | Contracts, policies, statutory wording | Marketing copy |
| Designer | Typography, layout, alt text | Word choice |

## When the sheet conflicts with a client's template

The external template wins, and the sheet records it as an exception with the
client's name. What never gives way: a factual claim, a legal term, a number, or
a person's name spelled as they spell it.

## Keeping it alive

- **Date every change**, with the reason in one line.
- **Retire rules nobody follows.** A rule broken in every document is a rule that is wrong, not a team that is lazy.
- **Review once a quarter, for ten minutes**, against five recent documents - the sheet drifts silently otherwise.

## Anti-patterns

- **A 40-page guide nobody opens.** The one-page sheet plus a terminology ledger beats it every time.
- **Rules with no example.** "Use plain English" changes nothing; "replace 'in order to' with 'to'" does.
- **Silent exceptions.** An exception that is not written down becomes a new rule by accident.
- **Applying a marketing rule to a contract.** Contractions, second person and ₹ formats differ by document type; the sheet should say which rules apply where.
- **Style over substance in a proof.** Correcting commas in a paragraph whose number is wrong is a miss.
