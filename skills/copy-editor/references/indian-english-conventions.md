# Indian English conventions

Indian English is a variety with its own settled usage, not a failed attempt at
British English. Follow the audience, not a rulebook from elsewhere.

## Numbers and money

| Write | Not | Note |
|---|---|---|
| ₹4,50,000 | ₹450,000 / Rs 4.5 lakh | Indian digit grouping: 4,50,000 and 45,00,000 |
| ₹1.2 crore | 12 million | Use crore for Indian readers, million in a global deck |
| 12 lakh | 1.2 million | Keep the unit the reader thinks in |
| ₹4,999 | Rs. 4999 / INR 4999 | No space after ₹; ISO code on first use in formal docs |
| 2.5% | 2.5 per cent | % with figures |
| 18% GST | GST @ 18% | "at" only in tables |

Large numbers: 1 lakh = 100,000; 1 crore = 10,000,000; 1 crore = 100 lakh. Say it
once in the document where the audience may be mixed, then use one system.

## Dates and time

| Write | Not |
|---|---|
| 6 November 2026 (prose) | November 6, 2026 |
| 06/11/2026 (tables) | 11/06/2026 |
| 9:30 am IST | 9:30 AM GMT+5:30 |
| FY 2026-27 | 2026-2027 |
| Q3 FY27 | Q3 2026 |

Financial years run April to March. "This year" in a document dated in January is
ambiguous and should become the financial year.

## Addresses and identifiers

- PIN code, six digits, no space: 560001.
- State names as official: Odisha, not Orissa; Bengaluru, not Bangalore, in formal documents unless the entity's registered address uses the old form.
- GSTIN: 15 characters, state code first. PAN: 10 characters, quoted exactly.
- Phone: +91 98765 43210.

## Words that are correct here and wrong elsewhere

| Indian English | Note |
|---|---|
| prepone | Standard in Indian business writing; keep if the audience is Indian |
| do the needful | Avoid in external writing; it reads as a form letter |
| revert | Avoid; use "reply" or "respond" |
| kindly | Avoid in modern copy; one "please" is enough |
| the same | Avoid as a pronoun ("please find the same attached") |
| upgradation, shifted | Prefer "upgrade", "moved" |
| tie-up | Fine in business prose; "partnership" in formal writing |
| lakh, crore | Keep for Indian readers |

## Honorifics, titles and names

- **Shri / Smt / Dr** in formal and government correspondence; first name plus surname in business prose.
- **Designations**: lower case unless before a name - "the managing director", but "Managing Director Asha Rao".
- **South Indian names**: initials precede the given name (R. Karthik); do not invent a surname.
- **Sikh names**: Singh and Kaur are not surnames to be dropped.
- **Do not anglicise a name** to make it easier to read. Query the correct spelling instead.

## Register by document type

| Document | Person | Contractions | Serial comma |
|---|---|---|---|
| Website, marketing | Second person | Yes | Rarely |
| Proposal | We and you | Sometimes | Only for clarity |
| Policy, contract | The company | No | Yes, for legal clarity |
| Report | Third person | No | Rarely |
| Internal note | We | Yes | No |

## Anti-patterns

- **Converting lakh to million because a tool did.** The reader's frame is the point.
- **American spelling in an Indian regulator filing.** Use the spelling the form uses.
- **"Rs." with a full stop** in a modern document; it is a legacy of typewriter usage.
- **Correcting Indian English to British English** in a quotation. Quote as written.
- **Anglicising names.** It is the error readers remember.
