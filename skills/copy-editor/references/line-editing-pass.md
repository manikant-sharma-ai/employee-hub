# Line editing pass

Four passes, in this order, never merged. Each pass looks for one class of
problem, because the eye can only hold one question at a time.

## Pass 1 - Structure and cross-references

| Look for | Fix |
|---|---|
| A section that repeats another | Query the author; do not delete a section unilaterally |
| Headings that do not match the contents page | Align both to the text |
| A promise in the intro with no section | Query, with the heading that should cover it |
| Forward references to a section that moved | Renumber, and check every instance |
| Table numbering out of sequence | Renumber and update every reference in prose |

## Pass 2 - Line: sentence surgery

| Problem | Test | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Throat-clearing | Does the sentence start before the point? | Delete up to the first real subject |
| Nominalisation | Is a verb hiding in a noun? | "make a decision" becomes "decide" |
| Passive with a hidden actor | Who did it? | Name them, or keep the passive deliberately |
| Sentence over 35 words | Count the clauses | Split at the second conjunction |
| Stacked modifiers | Three nouns in a row | Turn one into a prepositional phrase |
| Redundancy | Two words doing one job | "end result", "past history", "future plans" |
| Empty intensifiers | Very, really, quite, actually | Delete; if the sentence dies, it was weak |
| Dangling modifier | Does the opening phrase attach to the subject? | Rewrite the subject |

## Before and after

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| "In order to be able to facilitate the onboarding process for new employees, it is necessary that..." | "To onboard a new employee, the manager..." |
| "A decision was made to discontinue the product line." | "The board discontinued the product line." |
| "We are currently in the process of reviewing the policy." | "We are reviewing the policy." |
| "The report provides an analysis of the performance of the sales team." | "The report analyses how the sales team performed." |
| "Due to the fact that the deadline was missed, the launch was delayed." | "Because the deadline slipped, the launch moved." |

## Pass 3 - Copy: consistency and correctness

- Terminology against the ledger; names against the approved list.
- Numbers: digits vs words, ranges (10-20, not 10 - 20), units, currency, thousands separators in the Indian system (₹4,50,000).
- Cross-references, captions, table and figure numbers, footnotes.
- Tense and person consistent within a section.
- Acronyms spelled out on first use in each document, not once in the set.

## Pass 4 - Proof: mechanics only

Spelling, punctuation, spacing (one space after a full stop), hyphenation,
quotation marks, apostrophes, ellipses, em and en dashes, capitalisation after a
colon, list punctuation, orphan headings, widows, page numbers, contents.

Proof on the near-final text, never on a draft. A proof of a draft is a copy edit
with extra steps.

## Reading technique that actually finds errors

- **Read backwards, paragraph by paragraph.** It breaks the brain's auto-correction.
- **Read aloud the section the client will read aloud** - the opening, the exec summary.
- **Search, do not read, for mechanical classes**: double spaces, "  ", " ,", "the the", straight quotes, "Rs".
- **Check every number twice**: once against its source, once against its other appearances.

## Anti-patterns

- **Four passes in one.** The fourth class of error is invisible while you are hunting the first.
- **Rewriting a sentence the author wrote deliberately** - a fragment, a repetition, a one-line paragraph. Voice lives there.
- **Fixing style in a document governed by an external template.** The template wins; the sheet says so.
- **Querying everything.** Twenty queries for one document is a refusal to decide; decide the ones the sheet covers.
- **Editing a PDF.** Every change becomes a query, and the count triples.
