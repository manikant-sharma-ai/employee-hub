# Vendor due diligence

The purpose is not to build a file. It is to answer one question before money
leaves: **will this vendor still be there, at this address, with this capability,
when we need them?**

## The five checks, in order of what catches most

| Check | How | What a fail looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Name agreement | GSTIN legal name = bank account name = quotation letterhead | Three slightly different names, or a personal account for a company invoice |
| 2. GSTIN validity | GST portal search, or the invoice's own QR/e-invoice | "Applied for", "in process", or a state code that does not match the address |
| 3. Existence | MCA21 for a company, Udyam for an MSME, the address on a map | A residential flat, a virtual office, or no registration at all |
| 4. Capability | Capacity stated against your volume, a site visit or a sample | "We can do any quantity" with no plant |
| 5. Behaviour | Two references called, of similar size and use | References that are the vendor's relatives, or that never answer |

## What each document gives you

| Document | Confirms | Does not confirm |
|---|---|---|
| GSTIN | Registration, state, filing regularity | Capability or honesty |
| PAN | Tax identity, name match | Anything about the business |
| MCA21 record | Incorporation, directors, charges, filing status | Solvency |
| Udyam certificate | MSME status, which may carry payment-timeline protection | Scale |
| Bank statement or cancelled cheque | Account name and number | That the account is used for this business |
| Two called references | Delivery behaviour at a similar size | Your specific requirement |
| Site visit or sample | Capacity, quality, working conditions | Financial stability |

## Red flags, in the order they predict loss

1. **Bank account name does not match the GST legal name.** The most common
   advance-payment fraud. Stop, and require a cancelled cheque plus a call.
2. **A changed bank account, notified by email.** Call a number from the original
   contract or the GST record, never a number in the email.
3. **A price 30% below every other quote**, with no explanation of why.
4. **Pressure for a large advance** on a first order, or an insistence on cash.
5. **A GSTIN that is suspended, cancelled or has not filed for several months.**
6. **No physical address you can visit**, or an address that is a co-working desk
   for a "manufacturer".
7. **Refusal to give a reference** of a customer of similar size.
8. **A quotation without a validity date or a GST rate.**
9. **A single proprietor handling sales, dispatch and accounts** on a ₹50 lakh order
   with no security - not disqualifying, but it changes the payment terms.
10. **A vendor who will not sign a PO** and insists on their own purchase terms.

## The MSME reality

- A registered MSME supplier has a statutory expectation of payment within 45 days
  (check the current rule with the CA before quoting it). Delayed payment carries
  consequences, so build the payment calendar around it rather than discovering it.
- Udyam registration is free and quick, and a genuine small vendor usually has it.
  Its absence is not a red flag by itself; its presence is a cheap confirmation.

## Site visit checklist

- Does the capacity exist, or is it promised? Count machines, people and stock.
- Is the quality system visible - measuring instruments, rejection bins, records?
- Who else do they supply, and can you see a dispatch?
- Is the working condition lawful? Child labour, safety and statutory registers are
  your reputational exposure as the buyer, not only theirs.
- Ask for one sample run at your specification, and pay for it.

## Recording the result

One table, five rows, and a verdict: **approved / approved with conditions /
not approved**, with the conditions named (e.g. "advance capped at 30%, balance
against GRN, bank details confirmed by call").

## Anti-patterns

- Checking the GSTIN after the advance is paid.
- Accepting a screenshot of a GST certificate instead of a live lookup.
- Calling the reference the vendor chose, and only that one.
- Treating a large office as proof of capacity.
- Skipping diligence because the amount is "small" - the frauds are calibrated to
  the threshold where nobody checks.
- Recording a verdict with no conditions, so the next buyer repeats the whole exercise.
