# Valuation and dilution

Valuation is an argument from comparables and growth. Dilution is arithmetic, and
the arithmetic is where founders are surprised.

## The three methods, used together

| Method | How | When it is the primary |
|---|---|---|
| Comparable transactions | Recent rounds for similar stage, sector, growth | Seed and Series A in India |
| Revenue multiple | ARR x multiple justified by growth and margin | SaaS, at Series A and beyond |
| VC ownership target | Cheque size / target ownership = post-money | Sanity check, always |

Worked sanity check: a fund writing ₹10 crore for 15% implies a ₹66.7 crore
post-money and a ₹56.7 crore pre-money. If the comparables say ₹40 crore, the
fund will either write less or ask for more ownership. That is the conversation,
and it is better to have it with yourself first.

## India multiple bands, as a starting point

| Business | Metric | Typical range |
|---|---|---|
| SaaS, early | ARR multiple | 4-10x, driven by growth and NRR |
| D2C | Revenue multiple | 1-3x, driven by contribution margin and repeat rate |
| Marketplace | GMV multiple | 0.5-2x, driven by take rate and repeat |
| Services with software | EBITDA multiple | 6-15x, driven by retention and margin |
| Fintech, lending | Book or AUM | 1-3x book, driven by cost of funds and NPAs |

These move with the market. Use them to frame a range, never as the answer.

## The dilution arithmetic, in full

```
Post-money      = pre-money + new money
Investor %      = new money / post-money
Founder after   = founder before x (1 - investor % - pool increase %)
```

Example: founder owns 70%, ESOP 10%, angels 20%. Raise ₹8 crore at ₹32 crore
pre-money gives a ₹40 crore post-money, so the new investor holds 20%. If the
pool is topped up from 10% to 15% post-money before the round, that 5% comes out
of the founders, not the new investor - the founder's 70% becomes roughly 52.5%
after the round.

| Scenario | Pre-money | New money | Investor % | Founder before | Founder after |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| No pool top-up | ₹32 cr | ₹8 cr | 20.0% | 70% | 56.0% |
| 5% pool top-up | ₹32 cr | ₹8 cr | 20.0% | 70% | 52.5% |
| Lower valuation | ₹24 cr | ₹8 cr | 25.0% | 70% | 52.5% |
| Higher money | ₹32 cr | ₹12 cr | 27.3% | 70% | 50.9% |

The pool top-up and the valuation move the founder's number by the same order of
magnitude. Negotiate both.

## The second-round surprise

Model the next round now. A founder at 52% after seed, raising a Series A that
dilutes 20% with a further pool top-up, lands near 38%. Two more rounds and the
founder is under 30% before the first exit conversation. That is normal, and it
is better to see it before signing than after.

| Round | Money | Dilution | Founder after |
|---|---|---|---|
| Seed | ₹8 cr | 20% | 52.5% |
| Series A | ₹40 cr | 20% | 40.0% |
| Series B | ₹120 cr | 15% | 32.5% |

## What a valuation is not

- **It is not the price of the company.** It is the price of this round, under these terms, with this preference stack.
- **It is not a score.** A higher number with a 2x participating preference can be worth less to the founder than a lower one with 1x non-participating.
- **It is not permanent.** The next round reprices it, and a flat round behaves like a down round for everyone with a preference.

## The instrument, in one table

| Instrument | Used for | Note |
|---|---|---|
| CCPS | The standard Indian equity instrument for investors | Compulsorily convertible, which is what FEMA requires for foreign investment |
| Equity shares | Founders, employees, some domestic angels | Simplest, no preference |
| CCD | Debt that converts | Interest, and FEMA conditions apply |
| SAFE / convertible note | Fast angel rounds | Not native to Indian law; a CCD or a note with conversion terms is the usual local version |
| Venture debt | Extending runway | Warrants, and it ranks ahead of equity |

Foreign investment brings FEMA pricing rules: shares cannot be issued below fair
value, and a valuation report from a registered valuer or merchant banker is
normally required. That is a CA and lawyer question - ask it before the term sheet,
not after.

## Anti-patterns

- **Optimising for the headline.** The preference stack decides the founder's outcome more than the pre-money does.
- **Forgetting the pool top-up** in the dilution maths. It is the most common surprise at signing.
- **A flat or down round disguised as a bridge.** It has the same effect on morale and options, with more paperwork later.
- **Raising more than the plan needs.** A larger round at a higher valuation sets a growth expectation the next 18 months must meet.
- **Quoting a multiple from a news article** as if it applied to this business.
