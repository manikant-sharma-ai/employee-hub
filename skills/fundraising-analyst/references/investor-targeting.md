# Investor targeting

A raise is a pipeline. The list is the work, and the list is where most raises
actually fail - not in the meeting.

## The stages and who writes the cheque

| Stage | Cheque (₹) | Typical investors in India | What they need to see |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-seed | 25 lakh-2 crore | Angels, operator networks, pre-seed funds, family offices | Founder, insight, early signal |
| Seed | 3-12 crore | Seed and micro-VC funds, angel syndicates | Early revenue or strong usage, a repeatable motion |
| Series A | 25-80 crore | Institutional VC funds | Proven unit economics, a channel that scales |
| Series B+ | 80 crore+ | Larger VC, growth funds, sovereign and strategic | Scale, margin path, a second engine |

Also worth knowing: **government and quasi-government capital** - Startup India
Seed Fund Scheme for idea-stage, state startup funds, BIRAC for biotech, SIDBI
Fund of Funds routed through VC funds. **Revenue-based financing and venture
debt** - for a business with revenue, often cheaper than equity for a bridge.

## Building the list of 25-40

For each name, fill six fields. If a field cannot be filled, the name is not on
the list yet.

| Field | Why |
|---|---|
| Fund and partner | The partner's name, not the fund's logo |
| Stage and cheque size | A ₹100 crore fund writing ₹8 crore needs 10-15% ownership |
| Thesis fit | One sentence: what they say they fund, in their words |
| Portfolio resemblance | A company they backed that looks like this one |
| Warm path | Who can introduce you, by name |
| Status and next date | Intro requested, meeting, partner meeting, passed, with a date |

## Thesis fit, tested honestly

- **Read the partner's last five investments**, not the fund's website. Partners invest in patterns.
- **Read what they have written or said** about the category in the last year.
- **Check the portfolio for a competitor.** A fund with a direct competitor will pass, or will use the meeting for information.
- **Check the stage discipline.** A fund that only leads Series A will not lead a seed, whatever the partner says.

## Warm paths, ranked

| Path | Conversion |
|---|---|
| A founder in their portfolio, introduced by you after a real conversation | Highest |
| A mutual contact who knows both sides | High |
| An angel who co-invests with the fund | Good |
| A conference or a demo day introduction | Medium |
| Cold email with a specific, relevant ask | Low but non-zero |
| A cold mass email to 200 funds | Near zero, and it burns the list |

## The intro request

Three sentences, forwardable, no attachment:

```
<Name> - <founder> runs <company>, which <one line with a number>. They are
raising <amount> at <stage> and I think it fits <fund>'s thesis on <topic>.
Would you be open to an intro? Happy to send the deck first.
```

Ask one person for one intro at a time. A forwardable request that the introducer
can paste without editing is worth ten paragraphs.

## The pipeline discipline

| Week | Target |
|---|---|
| 1-2 | 25-40 names, all six fields filled |
| 3-4 | 15 intro requests out |
| 5-8 | 10-15 first meetings |
| 9-12 | 4-6 partner meetings, 1-3 term sheets |

A raise with one live conversation is not a raise, it is a hope. Parallel
processes create price tension; a single process creates a discount.

## What to do when a fund passes

Ask one question: "what would have made this a yes?" The answer is either a fact
about the business or a fact about the fund's thesis. Write it in
`notes/learnings.md`. Three passes with the same answer is a signal, not bad luck.

## Anti-patterns

- **A list of famous funds.** They are famous because they are late-stage.
- **One process at a time.** No tension, no price, no urgency.
- **Sending the deck cold to a partner who has never heard of you.** It goes to the analyst's folder.
- **Not asking why they passed.** The cheapest market research available.
- **Targeting a fund with a competitor in the portfolio** and hoping they will not notice.
