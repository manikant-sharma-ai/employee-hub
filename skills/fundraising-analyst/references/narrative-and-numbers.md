# Narrative and numbers

Investors fund a story they can check. The story is five sentences; the numbers
are the proof of each one.

## The five-sentence story

1. **Who has the problem**, in one line a stranger would recognise.
2. **What it costs them today**, in rupees or hours.
3. **What we do**, in the same words a customer would use.
4. **The proof it works**, with one number and one cohort.
5. **Why now**, with a change in the world that makes this the moment.

If any of the five cannot be said without a slide, the story is not ready.

## The metric sheet, with definitions

| Metric | Definition to state | Why investors ask |
|---|---|---|
| ARR | Annualised value of active recurring contracts, excluding one-time fees | The headline number, and the one most often inflated |
| MRR growth | Month-on-month, trailing three-month average | Momentum, not a spike |
| Gross margin | Revenue minus delivery cost (hosting, support, COGS, shipping) | Whether the model can fund itself |
| NRR | Revenue from last year's cohort this year, as a % of last year | Whether customers expand or leak |
| CAC | Fully loaded sales and marketing / new customers | The number that is recomputed in the room |
| CAC payback | Months of contribution margin to repay CAC | Under 12 for SMB, under 18 for enterprise |
| Burn multiple | Net burn / net new ARR | Under 1.5 is good, above 2 is a warning |
| Contribution margin | Price minus variable cost, per order | Whether growth creates or destroys value |
| Logo churn | Customers lost / customers at start | Retention truth behind revenue |
| Runway | Cash / average net burn, in months | The clock |

State the definition beside the number, every time. Half of all diligence
arguments are definitional, and the founder who defines first controls the
conversation.

## The traction slide, in order of persuasiveness

1. **Retention by cohort** - a table of monthly cohorts with the revenue retained.
2. **Unit economics** - CAC, contribution margin, payback, by channel.
3. **Growth rate with the base** - 30% month-on-month on ₹2 lakh is not the same as on ₹40 lakh.
4. **A named customer with a number** - one logo, one outcome, one quote.
5. **Pipeline and repeatability** - the channel that produced the last ten customers.

A hockey stick with no cohort table is the slide investors discount first.

## Why now, with evidence

| Change | Example |
|---|---|
| Regulatory | GST e-invoicing thresholds, ONDC, DPDP compliance deadlines |
| Distribution | UPI, WhatsApp Business API at scale |
| Cost curve | Inference costs, cloud credits, logistics density |
| Behaviour | Tier-2 online buying, vernacular content consumption |

One credible change, with a date and a number, beats three vague ones.

## The three-case model

| Case | What changes | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Downside | Growth at 60% of plan, churn +20%, hiring frozen | Shows you have thought about it |
| Base | The plan | The ask |
| Upside | One channel works better than modelled | Shows the ceiling |

Every case starts from the same actuals and the same opening cash. A model whose
cases start from different numbers is three models, not three cases.

## The cash check

Before the model leaves the sheet:

```
Closing cash (month n) = opening cash + collections - costs - capex - debt service
Runway = months until closing cash < 0
```

If the closing cash in the model does not match the bank statement in the last
actual month, fix it before anyone else sees it.

## Anti-patterns

- **ARR that includes one-time setup fees or pipeline.** It is found in the second meeting.
- **A CAC that excludes salaries.** The most common "profitable" channel is one with the team's time priced at zero.
- **Gross margin that excludes support and hosting.** It flatters the model and fails diligence.
- **A hockey stick with no assumption behind the inflection.** Investors ask which month and why, and there is no answer.
- **Three cases with different starting points.** It reads as three wishes.
