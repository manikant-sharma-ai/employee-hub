# Term sheets and negotiation

A term sheet is a price list for risk. Read it in the order it costs money:
economics, then control, then the clauses that only matter in a bad year.

## Clause by clause, ranked by cost

| Clause | Market standard (India, seed-A) | What to watch | Cost if bad |
|---|---|---|---|
| Valuation (pre/post) | Negotiated | Whether the pool is pre or post-money | Direct dilution |
| Instrument | CCPS | Conversion ratio, dividend | Downside economics |
| Liquidation preference | 1x non-participating | 1x participating, 2x, or a preference stack from earlier rounds | Can take the whole exit in a modest outcome |
| Anti-dilution | Broad-based weighted average | Full ratchet | Punishing in a down round |
| ESOP pool | 10-15% post-money | Pool created pre-money comes out of founders | 3-6% of the company |
| Board composition | 2 founders + 1 investor, or 3 with an independent | Investor majority, or observer rights for every fund | Control |
| Protective provisions | Standard consent list | Anything that blocks hiring, budget or a new product | Operational paralysis |
| Pro-rata rights | Standard for the lead | Super pro-rata, or rights for every small angel | Future dilution control |
| ROFR / ROFO | Standard | Transfer restrictions on founder shares | Liquidity |
| Drag along | Standard with a threshold | Drag at a low price, or without a floor | Forced sale |
| Tag along | Standard | Missing entirely | Minority trap |
| Founder vesting | 4 years, 1-year cliff, often re-vested at the round | Re-vesting of already-earned shares | Losing equity on exit |
| Founder lock-in | 3-4 years, or until IPO | Full-time commitment, non-compete scope | Personal risk |
| Exclusivity / no-shop | 30-60 days | 90+ days, or no carve-out for existing conversations | Lost momentum |
| Information rights | Quarterly, annual audited | Monthly board-level packs for a small cheque | Admin burden |
| Fees | Company pays its own; investor fees capped | Uncapped legal fees | ₹10-30 lakh surprise |

## The three clauses that decide the founder's outcome

1. **Liquidation preference.** 1x non-participating means the investor takes the greater of their money back or their share. 1x participating means both - money back *and* their share of the rest. In a ₹60 crore exit on a ₹40 crore post-money, the difference is tens of percent of the founder's proceeds.
2. **The pool.** A 15% pool created before the round is a 15% gift from the founders. Ask for it post-money, or for it to be built into the valuation.
3. **Anti-dilution.** Broad-based weighted average is standard; full ratchet is a punishment for a down round that also hits every earlier investor.

## The negotiation, in order

1. **Agree the process first** - amount, valuation range, timeline, exclusivity length.
2. **Economics** - valuation, pool, preference. Nothing else matters if this is wrong.
3. **Control** - board, protective provisions, information rights.
4. **The tail** - drag, tag, ROFR, founder vesting, fees.
5. **Only then, the paperwork.** The SHA and SSA are `legal-drafter`'s work, with a lawyer.

## What to trade, not concede

| They want | Ask for |
|---|---|
| A 15% pool pre-money | A higher pre-money by roughly the pool's value |
| 1x participating | A higher valuation, or a cap on participation |
| Board majority | A shorter exclusivity, or a lower information-rights burden |
| Full ratchet | Broad-based, or a valuation adjustment |
| A 90-day no-shop | A 30-day no-shop with a carve-out for existing conversations |

## Questions to ask before signing

- What is the preference stack after this round, in rupees, at a ₹X exit?
- What does the founder own after the next round at this dilution?
- Who controls the board, and what needs consent?
- What happens if the next round is flat or down?
- What are the total fees, and who pays them?

## The legal boundary

You rank clauses, price the risk and recommend the fight. You do not draft, amend
or opine on enforceability. FEMA compliance, CCPS structuring, ESOP taxation and
SHA enforceability go to `legal-drafter` and a lawyer, and the note says so.

## Anti-patterns

- **Signing an exclusivity before the economics are agreed.** The clock runs while the leverage disappears.
- **Reading only the valuation.** It is the number that matters least to the founder's final outcome.
- **Negotiating clause by clause for weeks.** Momentum is an asset; three rounds of redlines is where deals die.
- **Accepting "standard" without reading.** Standard is what the other side's lawyer calls their template.
- **Letting the pool be an afterthought.** It is usually the largest single transfer of founder equity in the round.
