# Data room and diligence

The data room is a test of whether the company is run properly. Investors read it
that way before they read a single number in it.

## The index

| Folder | Contents | Red flag if |
|---|---|---|
| 01 Corporate | Certificate of incorporation, MOA, AOA, PAN, GST registration, board and shareholder resolutions | Name changes, objects that do not cover the business |
| 02 Cap table | Full cap table, ESOP plan and grants, all convertible instruments with terms | Not updated since the last round, verbal promises |
| 03 Financials | Audited statements (3 years), management accounts (monthly, current), bank statements | Revenue recognised on bookings, unreconciled accounts |
| 04 Tax and statutory | ITR, GST returns, TDS returns, PF and ESI challans | Unpaid dues, late filings |
| 05 Contracts | Customer agreements, vendor agreements, leases, loans | No termination or assignment clause, one client above 30% |
| 06 People | Employment agreements, IP assignments, consultant agreements, founder vesting | No IP assignment, a departed co-founder with unvested equity |
| 07 IP and tech | Trademarks, patents, licences, architecture note, security summary | Open-source licence conflicts, unregistered brand |
| 08 Compliance | Privacy policy, DPDP readiness, sector licences, insurance | Personal data in spreadsheets, no privacy notice |
| 09 Litigation | Notices, cases, disputes | Anything undisclosed |
| 10 Fundraise | Model, deck, this index, previous round documents, SHA | Model that does not foot |

## The twenty questions that always come

1. What is ARR, defined?
2. What is net revenue retention, by cohort?
3. What is gross margin, including support and hosting?
4. What is CAC, fully loaded, by channel?
5. What is the payback period?
6. What is logo churn, and why do customers leave?
7. Who are the top five customers by revenue, and what share?
8. What is the contract length and the termination right?
9. What is the burn multiple?
10. What is the runway, and what does the next round need?
11. Who owns what on the cap table, and what is unallocated in the pool?
12. Are all founder and employee agreements signed with IP assignment?
13. What statutory dues are outstanding?
14. What related-party transactions exist?
15. What is the data privacy position, and what personal data is held?
16. What open-source or third-party licences are in the product?
17. What litigation or notices exist?
18. What happens to the business if the founder leaves?
19. What is the sales cycle, and how does the pipeline convert?
20. What would you do with twice the money, and what would you cut with half?

Prepare a one-line answer for each. The answer is what you say; the document is
what they check.

## Red flags, named

Write these down, with the answer, before diligence. An investor who finds a
hidden one re-prices the whole deal; one who hears it from you, with a plan,
discounts it by half.

| Red flag | The answer that works |
|---|---|
| Customer concentration | "40% from one client, contract to 2027, and we are hiring two enterprise reps to bring it under 25% by FY28" |
| A departed co-founder | "Left in 2024, kept 4% with reverse vesting to 2026, agreement attached" |
| Statutory dues | "₹3.2 lakh TDS pending, being paid this month, challan attached" |
| Down round risk | "Last round was ₹30 crore; we will raise at ₹28-35 crore, flat is acceptable" |
| Key-person dependency | "Founder-led sales; the playbook is written and two reps are ramping" |

## Access control

- **One folder, per-investor links**, revoked when a process ends.
- **No personal data, no customer names** beyond what diligence needs; anonymise where possible.
- **No bank details or credentials** in the data room, ever.
- **A log of who accessed what**, so the follow-up is informed.

## Anti-patterns

- **A data room assembled in the last week.** It shows, and it shows in the gaps.
- **Everything at once to everyone.** Stage the release: overview first, full room after the partner meeting.
- **Hiding a red flag and hoping.** Diligence is designed to find it.
- **PDFs of spreadsheets.** They cannot be checked, and the investor assumes the worst.
- **A room with no index.** The diligence cost rises and so does the suspicion.
