# Audio in India

The listener here is not on studio monitors. Design for the actual device, the
actual language mix and the actual commute.

## Where the audience is

| Platform | Share of listening | What it rewards |
|---|---|---|
| YouTube (audio and video podcasts) | Largest for Indian podcasts | Searchable titles, chapters, a face on screen |
| Spotify | Growing, younger, English-heavy | -14 LUFS, strong first 30 seconds |
| JioSaavn / Gaana / Amazon Music | Music-first, podcast secondary | Short episodes, Hindi and regional |
| Apple Podcasts | Small but influential | Chapters, clean metadata, -16 LUFS |
| WhatsApp forwarded clips | The real distribution | 30-60 second clips that work without context |

Plan the episode for YouTube and the clip for WhatsApp. Those two decide whether
the show grows.

## Language and accent

- **Hinglish is the default for urban business content**, not an exception. Script the switch points rather than leaving them to the reader.
- **Indian English is not a defect to correct.** Retroflex t and d, syllable-timed rhythm, and stress on a different syllable are the audience's own voice. Do not send a note that says "neutralise the accent".
- **Regional languages change the pace.** Tamil and Bengali measured reads run 10-15% slower than English at the same word count. Re-time the script, do not speed the reader.
- **Names are the whole game.** A mispronounced founder name is the comment section. Build the pronunciation guide before the record.

## Devices and the real listening test

| Device | What breaks |
|---|---|
| ₹1,200 earbuds | Sibilance, and any bed above -30 LUFS |
| Phone speaker at 60% | Everything below 300 Hz; check intelligibility here |
| Car stereo in traffic | Dynamics; heavy compression is correct for this listener |
| Laptop speakers | Stereo width collapses; mono compatibility matters |

The gate before delivery: one listen on a phone speaker, one on cheap earbuds,
one in mono. If a sentence is unclear in any of the three, the mix is not done.

## Practical constraints

- **Data cost is real.** A 60-minute 192 kbps episode is roughly 85 MB. Chapter markers and show notes let a listener decide before downloading.
- **Power and connectivity.** Remote guests drop. Record locally on both sides, always.
- **Publishing times.** Commute windows - 8-10 am and 6-9 pm IST - beat a midnight drop for a working audience.
- **Festival calendar.** Diwali, Holi and the exam season change listening; a launch episode in Diwali week competes with everything.

## Metadata that earns search

| Field | Rule |
|---|---|
| Episode title | The listener's payoff, under 60 characters, no episode number first |
| Description | First 150 characters carry the hook; chapters after |
| Tags | Guest name, company, the one problem solved |
| Transcript | Publish it; it is the cheapest SEO a podcast has |

## Anti-patterns

- **Mastering for headphones only.** Most of the audience is on a phone speaker.
- **An English-only pronunciation guide for a Hinglish read.** Write both scripts.
- **A 90-minute episode for a 25-minute commute.** The format has to fit the journey.
- **Treating YouTube as an afterthought.** In India, it is usually the front door.
- **Ignoring the festival calendar** when scheduling a launch.
