# Interview and guest craft

Half the repair time in an episode is created before the record button. This file
is the setup that removes it.

## Guest prep, in one email

Send this 72 hours before the recording:

1. The three questions the episode is built around, in plain language.
2. The length and the shape: "45 minutes, four segments, we will cut hard."
3. One thing to bring: a number, a screenshot, a failure story.
4. The recording setup: link, headphones required, wired internet if possible, phone on airplane mode.
5. The one line they should be ready to say at the end - where listeners find them.

Ask for a 20-second voice note. It tells you the mic, the room and the accent
before the session, and it is the cheapest technical check there is.

## The question ladder

| Rung | Type | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Situation | "You run 14 stores in Gujarat. What does a Tuesday look like?" |
| 2 | Decision | "You closed the Surat store in 2023. What did the numbers say?" |
| 3 | Cost | "What did that cost you - money, or something else?" |
| 4 | Contrarian | "Most people in your position would have expanded. Why not you?" |
| 5 | Transferable | "What should someone with three stores copy from you tomorrow?" |

Start at rung 1 for the first ten minutes, even with a famous guest. Rung 4 in
the first five minutes gets a rehearsed answer.

## Remote recording

| Setup | Quality | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| Double-ender (Riverside, Zencastr, Cleanfeed) | Local tracks, best | Any guest who matters; the default |
| Each side records locally and sends files | Best, manual | Guests on slow connections |
| Platform recording (Zoom, Meet) | Compressed, brittle | Only when nothing else works |
| Phone call | Broadcast-quality no | Never for a published episode |

Non-negotiables for the guest: wired headphones, a room with soft furnishings,
phone on airplane mode, and the mic 15-20 cm from the mouth, off to the side of
the nose so plosives miss it.

## Room guide for a guest at home

| Room | Verdict | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Bedroom with a wardrobe | Best cheap option | Face the clothes, mic 20 cm |
| Carpeted living room | Good | Curtains closed, no ceiling fan |
| Kitchen or bathroom | Never | Tiles ring; move |
| Open-plan office | Bad | Book a meeting room, or record at 8 pm |
| Outdoors | Only with a windscreen and a reason | Accept the noise or move |

## Keeping the interview alive

- **Silence after an answer is a question.** Two seconds of nothing gets the second, truer sentence.
- **Ask for the number twice.** Once in their words, once as "so roughly how much".
- **Interrupt the tangent once, politely, with a redirect.** "Hold that - go back to the price you charged."
- **Never stack two questions.** The guest answers the easier one.
- **Name the discomfort.** "This next one is uncomfortable" gets a real answer where a soft question gets a press release.

## Anti-patterns

- **No pre-call.** The first 20 minutes become a technical and biographical warm-up you then cut.
- **A list of 22 questions.** It produces a survey, not a conversation.
- **Letting the guest read their own bio.** It is the least interesting 90 seconds of any episode.
- **Recording without a backup track.** One crashed laptop ends the episode.
- **Not telling the guest it will be edited.** It changes what they say next time, badly.
