# Activation and scorecard

A signed partner who sells nothing is the normal outcome, not the exception. This
file is the ninety days that decide which it is.

## The first 30 days - a checklist, not a hope

| Day | Action | Owner | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-3 | Welcome mail with the enablement folder and a named contact | Us | Folder opened (ask) |
| 5 | 60-minute enablement call: who buys, the pitch, the price, the objections | Us + their sales head | Recording or notes |
| 7 | Their sales team trained, not just the owner | Us | Attendance names |
| 10 | First target agreed in writing: n deals or ₹x by day 30 | Both | Mail |
| 14 | Co-sell on the first live opportunity | Both | Deal in the CRM |
| 21 | First pipeline review, 20 minutes | Both | Pipeline list |
| 30 | Target check: met, half, or zero | Us | Scorecard row |

Zero at day 30 is information, not failure. Zero at day 60 with no pipeline is a
decision: fix or exit.

## The enablement kit

Six things, all in one folder that opens without a login:

1. The one-pager, in their language if needed.
2. A demo they can run themselves - a sandbox, a video, or a script.
3. The price list with their tier, and the discount rules.
4. Five objection answers, in their words.
5. Two reference customers who agreed to be called.
6. A named person at our end, with a response-time commitment.

Anything else is optional. A kit with forty files is a kit nobody opens.

## The scorecard

Review monthly. Four columns, no more.

| Partner | Pipeline created (₹) | Deals won | Revenue (₹) | Support hours used |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Then the two derived numbers that decide the relationship:

- **Revenue per support hour.** Below roughly ₹5,000, the partner is consuming more
  than they produce, and a direct customer is cheaper.
- **Days since last deal.** Over 90 with no pipeline, the partner is dormant.

## The tiering that follows from the numbers

| Tier | Test | What they get |
|---|---|---|
| Gold | Above target for two quarters | First leads, best margin, joint planning |
| Silver | At or near target | Standard margin, quarterly review |
| Watch | Below target, pipeline exists | A 60-day plan with one named action |
| Exit | No pipeline in 90 days, or support cost above revenue | 30 days' notice, accounts handed back |

Never leave a partner in limbo. A partner who does not know they are failing will
be surprised and will say so publicly.

## The quarterly review, in 25 minutes

1. The four numbers, read out, agreed.
2. Two wins - what worked, in their words.
3. One blocker - pricing, supply, support, a competitor.
4. The next quarter's target, written down before the call ends.
5. One thing we will change at our end, dated.

## Exit, done properly

- Written notice per the term sheet; a call before the mail, never after.
- Pipeline handed back or transferred, with customer consent.
- Stock bought back at the agreed rate, or run-down permitted to a date.
- Brand assets withdrawn; a note to the customers they touched, if the relationship
  was sold as ours.
- The reason recorded in `notes/decisions.md` and, if it is a pattern, in
  `references/partner-types-and-fit.md`.

## Channel conflict

Publish the rule before the first partner signs:

- **Deal registration**: a partner registers an account, gets 30-60 days of
  protection, and must show activity.
- **House accounts**: named accounts that are always direct, listed openly.
- **Price floor**: a minimum resale price, or the channel undercuts your own site
  and the direct team stops selling.
- **Dispute resolution**: the first registration wins, unless the direct team was
  already in an active deal - recorded in the CRM, not remembered.

## Anti-patterns

- Onboarding with a deck and no target.
- Training the owner and never the sales team.
- A quarterly review that is a friendly call with no numbers.
- Keeping a dormant partner because the logo is on the website.
- Exiting a partner by stopping replies.
- Discovering channel conflict after two partners have quoted the same customer.
