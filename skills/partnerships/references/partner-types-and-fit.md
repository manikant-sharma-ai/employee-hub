# Partner types and fit

Six models get called "partnership" and they behave nothing alike. Choosing the
wrong one is the most expensive mistake in this role, because a channel that does
not work still costs support, discount and management time.

## The six models

| Model | What they do | They earn | Fits when | Fails when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reseller | Buys at a discount, sells at list, invoices the customer | Margin between buy and sell | The product needs a local relationship and light configuration | The deal needs a demo the reseller cannot give |
| Distributor | Buys stock, sells to resellers, carries credit | Distributor margin, plus terms | Reach into many small dealers, or physical goods | Volumes are small; stock sits and the relationship sours |
| Referral / agent | Introduces, does not sell or invoice | 5-15% of first-year value | High-trust categories - CA, legal, clinics, real estate | Attribution is unclear and both sides claim the same deal |
| System integrator | Bundles into a larger solution or tender | Project margin on the whole solution | Government, enterprise, infrastructure | Your product is a line item they can drop without losing the bid |
| Technology / integration | Connects their product to yours | Indirect - retention, upsell, reach | Their users need you and yours need them | The integration is a checkbox nobody maintains |
| Co-marketing | Shared campaign, shared audience | Leads, not margin | Both audiences overlap and neither sells to the other | It is a press release with a logo and no budget |

## The fit test - six questions

Answer all six in writing. Any "no" in the first three ends the conversation.

1. **Does the partner already sell to the buyer we want, in the same conversation?**
   Not "similar customers" - the same purchase, in the same meeting, from the same
   budget line.
2. **Do they make more money by selling us than by doing nothing?** A named number,
   per deal, after their cost to sell.
3. **Can they deliver the promise?** Demo, install, support, service. If they cannot,
   the customer blames us and we pay for it twice.
4. **Is the margin enough for both of us?** Their cut plus our floor must fit inside
   the price the market already accepts. See `deal-terms-and-economics.md`.
5. **Is there a first customer?** A named prospect, a waiting tender, an existing
   client who asked. Partnerships without a first deal do not start.
6. **Can we end it cleanly?** 30 days' notice, no stock stranded, no customer
   hostage, no exclusivity we cannot revoke.

## When the honest answer is "sell direct"

- Average deal size is high and the cycle is long, so a partner's 20% is more than
  a salesperson's cost per deal.
- The product needs a live demo, a custom integration or a proof of concept every time.
- The buyer wants to talk to the maker - regulated, safety-critical, or high-trust
  advisory work.
- The market is concentrated in twenty accounts you can name.
- The margin is under 30%: there is nothing to split.

Say this early and in writing. It saves a quarter.

## Partner-type selection by objective

| Objective | Model | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Enter a new state or district fast | Distributor + resellers | Local credit, local relationships, local language |
| Reach a segment we cannot call | Referral from trusted advisors | The advisor already has the trust we lack |
| Win government or large enterprise | System integrator | They hold the tender relationship and the EMD |
| Fill a product gap | Technology integration | Their users get the capability without switching |
| Build credibility in a new category | Co-marketing with a known name | Borrowed authority, cheap, measurable |
| Add revenue with no headcount | Reseller with a light enablement kit | Scales without a sales hire |

## India-specific realities

- **Distributors carry credit.** Payment terms of 30-60 days are normal, and the
  distributor's creditworthiness is a bigger risk than their enthusiasm.
- **State boundaries matter commercially.** GST state codes decide where the invoice
  is raised; a "pan-India distributor" usually means five relationships, not one.
- **System integrators are paid on the tender cycle.** Your invoice to them is not
  the payment date; plan working capital accordingly.
- **Referral fees to CAs and consultants are sensitive.** Check the professional
  body's rules on fee-sharing before designing the programme; when in doubt, use a
  fixed professional fee, not a percentage of the client's purchase.
- **Channel conflict is normal.** Publish a deal-registration rule before the first
  partner signs, or two partners will chase the same account and one will undercut.

## Anti-patterns

- Calling a customer's agency a "partner" to make the pipeline look bigger.
- Choosing a distributor because they are the largest, when they carry forty lines
  and will not push the forty-first.
- A referral programme with no attribution rule, so every deal is claimed twice.
- An integration partnership where neither side assigns an engineer.
- Signing a co-marketing deal with no budget and no date.
- Adding a channel to fix a sales problem that is actually a pricing or product problem.
