# Deal terms and economics

The commercial terms are yours. The contract language is not. This file is the
arithmetic and the term sheet; `legal-drafter` and a lawyer turn it into a contract.

## The two numbers that decide everything

1. **Our contribution margin per unit** after variable cost - not gross margin on
   the price list, which ignores freight, install, support and payment fees.
2. **The partner's net per deal** after their cost to sell - not their headline
   percentage, which they will compare against the other lines on their shelf.

If (1) minus the partner's cut falls below your floor, the deal is a loss leader.
Label it, or decline it.

## The margin ladder

| Element | Typical range | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Reseller discount off list | 20-35% | Enough for them to discount 5-10% and still earn |
| Distributor margin | 8-15% on top of reseller price | Plus credit terms, which cost you money |
| Referral fee | 5-15% of first-year value | Paid on collection, not on signature |
| System integrator discount | 25-40% | They carry the bid cost and the payment risk |
| MDF (market development fund) | 2-5% of partner purchases | Paid against receipts, never as cash upfront |

## Worked example - is the deal viable?

| Line | Value |
|---|---|
| List price (ex-GST) | ₹12,500 |
| Variable cost (hardware, freight, install, support, payment fee) | ₹7,000 |
| Contribution before partner | ₹5,500 (44%) |
| Reseller buys at 30% off list | ₹8,750 |
| Our contribution per unit | ₹1,750 (20%) |
| Partner's sell price | ₹12,500 |
| Partner's gross margin | ₹3,750 |
| Partner's cost to sell (4 hours) | ₹2,800 |
| Partner's net | ₹950 |
| Verdict | Too thin for them - they will not push it |

The fix is not a bigger discount. It is a smaller cost to sell: pre-built demo,
inside sales support, or a bundle that raises the deal value.

## Term sheet - the eight lines

| Term | What to write | The trap |
|---|---|---|
| Discount / split | Percentage off list, per tier | A percentage of a price that already includes a launch discount |
| Tiers | Volume bands with a review date | A tier reached once, kept forever |
| Territory | Named districts or states, and the customer type | "India" or "global" |
| Exclusivity | None by default; if granted, bought with committed volume and a review date | Verbal exclusivity on a call |
| Term | 12 months, auto-renew with a review | Auto-renew with no performance test |
| Notice | 30 days either side; 60 for stock | A lock-in with no exit |
| Payment terms | Advance or 15 days for the first three orders, then 30 | Credit before trust |
| Performance test | A named number by a named date, or exclusivity lapses | A target with no consequence |

## Exclusivity, honestly

Exclusivity is the most requested and least deserved term. Grant it only when all
four are true:

1. They commit to a volume, in writing, with a date.
2. The volume is at least what you would sell directly in that territory.
3. The term is short - six months - and renewal is earned.
4. It lapses automatically on miss, without a conversation.

## Payment and credit

- **First orders on advance.** A new distributor's creditworthiness is unknown and
  a bad debt costs more than the margin on ten good deals.
- **Credit is a price.** 45-day terms at 12% cost of capital is roughly 1.5% of the
  invoice - say it out loud when they ask for 60 days.
- **Security** - a post-dated cheque or a bank guarantee for large credit lines, per
  the business's own policy, not your judgement.
- **GST** - the invoice must carry the correct state code and place of supply; an
  inter-state supply billed as intra-state is a filing problem for them and a
  credit problem for you.

## What must go to the lawyer

Every term sheet ends with a handoff. `legal-drafter` drafts, a lawyer reviews:

- liability caps and indemnity,
- termination for cause and the consequence for stock in the field,
- IP and brand usage,
- data protection and who is the fiduciary under the DPDP Act,
- governing law, jurisdiction and arbitration seat,
- non-solicitation of each other's staff,
- any regulatory restriction on resale in the category.

## Anti-patterns

- Negotiating the discount before modelling the partner's net.
- A margin that only works if the partner sells at full list, which they will not.
- MDF paid upfront, spent on a banner, and never accounted for.
- Exclusivity granted to end an awkward conversation.
- A term sheet with no performance test, renewed automatically for three years.
- Handing a partner a contract template and letting them redline it alone.
