# Partner outreach

Partner outbound is not customer outbound. You are not selling a product; you are
offering a line that makes them money, to someone whose job is to add lines that
make money.

## Who to write to

| Partner type | Signer | The person who makes it sell |
|---|---|---|
| Distributor | Proprietor or director | The counter sales team and the field rep |
| Reseller | Owner | Their senior salesperson |
| System integrator | Partner / alliance manager, or the bid head | The pre-sales engineer who writes the proposal |
| Referral (CA, consultant) | The partner who owns client relationships | The article or manager who prepares the client's file |
| Technology | Head of product or alliances | The engineer who will build the integration |
| Co-marketing | Marketing head | The campaign manager |

Write to the second column's person in the first mail, and copy the first only
after interest. A mail to a managing director that never reaches the sales floor
produces a polite no.

## The first mail

Six lines, under 110 words.

```
Subject: <their customer> + <our product> - <the margin or the win>

Hi <first name>,

<Line 1: what we noticed about their business, specifically. 20 words.>
<Line 2: the offer, with the margin or the bid advantage in rupees. 25 words.>
<Line 3: the proof - one customer, one number. 20 words.>
<Line 4: the ask - 30 minutes, two time options. 15 words.>

<Name>, <role>, <company>, <phone>
```

## Warm introductions

A referral from a mutual contact converts several times better than a cold mail.
Ask for it in a way that is easy to say yes to:

> "Would you introduce me to <name> at <company>? One line from you is worth ten
> from me. I will send you a three-line forwardable note so it costs you nothing."

Then actually write the three lines they can forward, including the margin and the
ask. Never ask a contact to "connect us on LinkedIn" and leave them to invent the
context.

## The follow-up

One, at three working days, adding something:

- a worked deal with their likely volumes,
- a customer in their city who will take a reference call,
- a co-sell date for a specific tender,
- a competitor's partner terms, if you know them honestly.

Never: "bumping", "any thoughts?", "just checking in", "circling back".

## The polite no

Most inbound "let's partner" mails deserve a clear answer, and giving it fast
protects the relationship:

> "Thanks for the note. We are not adding <type> partners in <region> this quarter
> because <reason>. If <condition> changes - a named customer, a tender, a
> territory - write again and we will look properly."

That is more useful than a call that goes nowhere, and it is remembered.

## India channel realities

- **The first meeting is often a phone call, not a video call.** Offer both; do not
  insist on a calendar link that requires an email client they do not check.
- **WhatsApp is a legitimate channel** for scheduling and for sharing the one-pager,
  but the commercial terms go in email so there is a record.
- **Language matters.** A distributor in Rajkot or a CA in Nashik may prefer Gujarati
  or Marathi in the meeting even if the mail is in English. Ask which they prefer.
- **Festival and financial-year timing** - avoid pitching in the last week of March
  and the week of Diwali; both are consumed by closing.
- **The reference customer is the strongest asset.** A distributor will call the
  customer you name, so only name one who has agreed to be called.

## Measuring outreach

| Metric | Healthy range | What a low number means |
|---|---|---|
| Reply rate | 15-30% for warm, 5-10% cold | The list is wrong, or the mail is about us |
| Meeting rate from replies | 50%+ | The offer is unclear, or the wrong person replied |
| Term sheet within 45 days | 30-50% of meetings | The economics do not work for them |
| Signed to first deal | 60 days | The enablement is missing, not the will |

## Anti-patterns

- A mass mail to 200 "partners" with no margin and no product detail.
- Writing to a generic `info@` address and following up three times.
- Promising exclusivity in a first mail to create urgency.
- Sending a 30-slide deck before a conversation.
- Treating a distributor's polite interest as a commitment in the CRM.
- Not recording the "not now, ask again in Q3" answers, which are the best leads you have.
