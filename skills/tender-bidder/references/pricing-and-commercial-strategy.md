# Pricing and commercial strategy

In public procurement, price is usually the only differentiator that survives evaluation - which is
exactly why the floor has to be decided before the auction, not during it.

## The cost build-up that sets the floor

| Line | Include | Common omission |
|---|---|---|
| Landed cost | Purchase or production cost, including inbound freight and duty | Inbound freight |
| Outbound freight and insurance | To the delivery point, per the Incoterms in the notice | Last-mile and unloading |
| Installation and commissioning | Where the item needs it | Travel and stay |
| Taxes | GST at the correct rate; the buyer's GSTIN for the invoice | Rate for the HSN |
| EMD cost | Interest on the blocked amount for the bid validity period | The blocking period, not the bid date |
| PBG cost | Bank guarantee commission, margin money, and the period | The full contract period plus the claim period |
| Payment delay | The buyer's real payment cycle, funded at the borrowing rate | Treasury's actual cost of money |
| LD exposure | Probability x expected penalty, priced at the notice's rate | The cap and the weekly rate |
| Warranty and service | The warranty period's service cost | Travel for service calls |
| Overheads and margin | The business's own numbers | A "market price" instead |
| **Floor** | The price below which the order loses money | |

## L1, and what it really means

Lowest price among technically qualified bidders, evaluated on the total landed price to the buyer,
not on the unit rate. Two consequences: a line-item mistake in the BoQ can make a losing bid look
winning, and the buyer's total includes taxes, freight and installation where the notice says so.

Where a reverse auction follows, the price falls live. Decide the walk-away price in writing, with the
founder, before it starts, and hold it. Auctions reward the bidder who prepared a floor and punish the
one who prepared an ambition.

## The levers that are not the price

| Lever | How it helps | Where it is allowed |
|---|---|---|
| MSE price preference | A price-preference clause can let a higher MSE quote win | Where the tender carries it |
| Make in India local content | Class-I local supplier preference | Where the tender carries it |
| Delivery and warranty | A better service commitment can win a QCBS evaluation | Where evaluation is not pure L1 |
| Compliance quality | A clean, complete bid survives evaluation the cheap one does not | Everywhere |
| Payment terms | A shorter payment cycle reduces the funding cost and can fund a lower price | Negotiated after award, rarely before |

## Reading the LD clause

Liquidated damages are usually a percentage of the order value per week of delay, capped at a
percentage of the contract. Price the exposure: 0.5% a week with a 10% cap on a 45-day delivery means
the entire margin can disappear in ten weeks of delay. If the plant cannot hold the schedule, the
correct answer is not to bid, or to bid with a delivery period the operations team has signed.

## Anti-patterns

- **Pricing against a competitor's last bid.** You do not know their cost, their desperation or their
  mistake.
- **Forgetting the cost of the EMD and the PBG.** They are real money blocked for months.
- **Ignoring the buyer's payment cycle.** A 90-day cycle at 12% costs about 3% of the order.
- **Deciding the floor during the auction.** It becomes the last bid plus one step.
- **Winning on a specification deviation.** The order is then supplied at a loss or rejected at
  inspection.
- **Treating an L1 win as a relationship.** The next tender is a new tender.

## The pricing meeting, in fifteen minutes

Before any bid goes out, four numbers on one page, agreed with the founder:

1. **The floor** - the price below which the order loses money after LD risk and payment delay.
2. **The target** - the price that makes the bid worth the effort.
3. **The walk-away** - the lowest bid permitted in a reverse auction, written before it starts.
4. **The trigger to withdraw** - the delivery period or payment term that, if changed, makes the bid
   unacceptable.

Anything decided after the auction begins is decided by the auction. The fifteen minutes exist so that
it is not.
