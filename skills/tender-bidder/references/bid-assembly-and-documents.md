# Bid assembly and documents

A bid is lost more often to a missing page than to a bad price. This file is the assembly line and the
checklist that stops it.

## The standing document pack

Keep these current, in one folder, so a bid starts at 70%:

| Document | Validity | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Certificate of incorporation / Udyam / GST / PAN | Permanent, but re-download the latest | Founder's office |
| Audited financials, three years | Annual | CA |
| CA certificate for turnover and net worth | Usually 3-6 months | CA |
| Completion certificates for past orders | Permanent | Sales |
| OEM authorisation letters | Per bid, per OEM | Sales and the OEM |
| ISO / BIS / product certifications | Per certificate | Quality |
| DSC - class 3, signing and encryption | 2-3 years, renew early | IT |
| Cancelled cheque, bank details, MSME declaration | Permanent | Finance |
| Undertakings - blacklisting, local content, make in India | Per bid format | Legal |
| Catalogue or product literature, datasheets | Per product | Marketing |

## The technical bid, section by section

1. **Compliance sheet first.** Every requirement from the notice, ATC and specification, with the page
   or annexure where the response is. Evaluators read this before anything else.
2. **Specification response, point by point**, in the buyer's order and the buyer's words. "Complied"
   with no evidence is a rejection in a technical evaluation.
3. **Deviations, declared.** An undeclared deviation found later voids the bid; a declared minor
   deviation is often accepted. This is the single most misunderstood page in Indian tendering.
4. **Past performance**, with completion certificates attached and the value and year stated.
5. **OEM or authorisation**, specific to this tender.
6. **Delivery plan**, with the lead time, the schedule and the service arrangement.
7. **Documents annexure**, numbered, matching the checklist.

## The submission, hour by hour

| When | What | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 48 hours before | All documents final, DSC tested, files in the required format and size | Upload failures are common on the last day |
| 24 hours before | Upload complete, all files opened and checked | A corrupt PDF is invisible until it is too late |
| 12 hours before | Portal receipt and acknowledgement saved | Proof of submission, not proof of intent |
| 2 hours before | Portal status re-checked | Corrigenda and portal issues |
| Never | Uploading in the last hour | The portal is slowest exactly then |

## The rejection list

| Cause | Share of technical rejections | Prevention |
|---|---|---|
| Missing or unsigned document | High | The compliance sheet, checked by a second person |
| EMD not as specified | High | Instrument, amount, validity, and the MSE exemption clause |
| Technical specification not met | Medium | A point-by-point response, no assumptions |
| Past performance not evidenced | Medium | Completion certificates, not invoices |
| DSC or portal error | Medium | Test the DSC a week before |
| Price not in the required format or BoQ | Medium | Use the portal's BoQ, never a reformatted sheet |
| Late submission | Low, fatal | The 24-hour rule |

## Two-person rule

Every bid is checked by someone who did not write it, against the notice, line by line. Ten minutes of
a second pair of eyes is worth more than any amount of care by the first.

## Anti-patterns

- **A reformatted BoQ.** The portal's BoQ is machine-read; a spreadsheet with the same numbers fails.
- **"Complied" without evidence.** The evaluator cannot award what is not shown.
- **Undeclared deviations.** Found later, they void the bid and can invite action against the bidder.
- **One person assembling and submitting.** No second check, no audit trail.
- **No acknowledgement saved.** A submission dispute with no receipt is a lost bid.
- **Chasing the deadline with the DSC unregistered.** Registration on a portal takes days.
