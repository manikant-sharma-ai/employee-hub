# Eligibility and qualification

Eligibility is the cheapest part of a bid to check and the most expensive part to get wrong. This file
is the grid and the exemptions.

## The grid, criterion by criterion

| Criterion | What the notice usually asks | Where the proof lives | Common failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Legal entity | Registration, constitution, PAN, GSTIN | Certificate of incorporation, PAN, GST certificate | Name mismatch across documents |
| OEM status | Manufacturer of the item, or authorised dealer with an OEM letter | Factory licence, trademark, OEM authorisation on letterhead | A dealer bidding as OEM, or an OEM letter that has expired |
| Category and HSN | The exact item and code | Catalogue listing, product literature | A near-match that the evaluator rejects |
| Turnover | Average or minimum annual turnover for 2-3 years | Audited financials, CA certificate | Turnover stated in a format the evaluator cannot verify |
| Past performance | Value and number of similar orders executed | Completion certificates, work orders, invoices | Orders executed but no completion certificate |
| Experience | Years in the business or similar supply | Registration date, first invoice, client letters | Claiming experience the documents do not show |
| Certifications | BIS, ISO, FSSAI, drug licence, EMC | The certificate, valid on the bid date | Expired on the bid date, valid on the submission date |
| Registrations | GeM seller, state vendor registration, PF/ESI, labour licence | Portal profile, certificates | Registered but the category is not enabled |
| Financial standing | Net worth, no blacklisting, no default | CA certificate, undertaking | An undertaking signed without checking the facts |
| Delivery capability | Lead time, capacity, service network | Production records, service partners | Promising a lead time the plant cannot meet |

## The exemptions that change the answer

| Exemption | Who gets it | What it waives | What to check |
|---|---|---|---|
| MSE (Udyam) | Micro and small enterprises | EMD and tender fee in many central procurements; price-preference and reservation in some | Whether the tender is MSE-restricted, and whether the Udyam certificate matches the item's NIC code |
| Startup recognition | DPIIT-recognised startups | Prior turnover and experience in many tenders | Whether the tender carries the relaxation clause; not automatic |
| Local content / Make in India | Domestic manufacturers with declared local content | Class-I local supplier preference in many central tenders | The local content declaration and the CA certificate it needs |
| Women-owned MSE | MSEs with the required ownership | A share of procurement in some policies | The ownership documents, not just the Udyam entry |
| SC/ST MSE | Same | Same | Same |

Exemptions are tender-specific. The presence of an exemption in policy does not mean the notice
carries it; read the clause, and if it is absent, ask in the clarification window before the deadline.

## The dealer's problem, in one paragraph

A dealer bidding on an OEM-restricted tender needs an OEM authorisation letter that names the tender,
the item, the quantity and the validity, on the OEM's letterhead, signed by an authorised signatory.
A generic "we authorise X as our dealer" letter is rejected. Get it issued for the specific bid, with
time to spare, and keep a template with the OEM so the next bid takes an hour, not a week.

## Go / no-go, the four tests

1. **Can we be eligible?** Every mandatory criterion met or provably obtainable before the deadline.
2. **Can we deliver?** Capacity, lead time, serviceability at the delivery point, and the months the
   plant is full.
3. **Can we make money at a winning price?** The floor test in the pricing file, with the LD exposure.
4. **Is it worth the effort?** Person-days against the probability-weighted margin, not against the
   tender value.

Three yeses and a yes on effort is a bid. Anything else is a no, written down with the reason.

## Anti-patterns

- **Reading eligibility after writing the bid.** The most expensive sequence in this job.
- **A generic OEM letter.** Rejected, and the reissue takes longer than the deadline allows.
- **An expired certificate on the bid date.** Validity is tested on the date the notice specifies.
- **Assuming the MSE exemption applies.** It is clause-specific, and an EMD not paid is an automatic
  rejection.
- **Claiming past performance without completion certificates.** An invoice proves a sale, not a
  completed order.
- **Bidding outside the category to "get a foot in".** A rejected technical bid is not a relationship.
