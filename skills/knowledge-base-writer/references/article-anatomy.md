# Article anatomy

A help article has one job: get a person from stuck to unstuck in under two
minutes, on a phone, without reading carefully.

## The shape

| Part | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Title | The customer's search, under 60 characters, no product nouns | "Change the GST number on an invoice" |
| Answer line | The answer in one sentence, first line, no preamble | "You can change it yourself until the invoice is filed; after that, support has to do it." |
| When this applies | One line on who this is for and what it does not cover | "For invoices raised after 1 April 2024." |
| Steps | Numbered, one action each, exact labels | 1. Open **Billing** > **Invoices**. |
| What you will see | The confirmation, so they know it worked | "The status changes to **Pending reissue**." |
| If it does not work | Three failure modes with the fix | "If the field is greyed out, the invoice is filed." |
| Escalation | Exactly what to send support, and where | "Mail billing@ with the invoice number and a screenshot." |
| Related | Two links maximum | "Cancel an invoice", "Download a GST report" |

Nothing before the answer. No "Welcome to our help centre". No history of the
feature. No introduction paragraph explaining that invoices are important.

## Titles, written as searches

| Written as a feature | Written as a search |
|---|---|
| "Refund initiation workflow" | "When will I get my refund" |
| "Managing user roles and permissions" | "Add a team member who can see orders but not payments" |
| "Subscription lifecycle management" | "Cancel my subscription and stop the next payment" |
| "Invoice correction" | "My invoice has the wrong address" |
| "Troubleshooting authentication" | "I cannot log in, it says the OTP is wrong" |

Test the title by typing it into the site search and a search engine. If the
words are not what a person types, rewrite it.

## Steps that work under pressure

- **One action per step.** "Open Settings, then click Team, then invite the user" is three steps.
- **Bold the exact label**, in the interface's own capitalisation.
- **Say where the thing is**, in words a stranger recognises: "in the top-right menu, under your name".
- **No forward references.** Never "as described below" - the reader is scrolling.
- **Number the steps and keep them under nine.** Past nine, split the article.
- **Include the failure at the point it happens.** "If the Save button is greyed out, your plan has one seat left."

## Screenshots

| Do | Do not |
|---|---|
| Crop to the relevant area | Show the whole browser with twelve tabs |
| Annotate the one control with a box and an arrow | Draw on five things |
| Recreate with sample data if the real screen has customer data | Show a real name, order number, phone or amount |
| Keep the language consistent with the reader's | Mix languages within one screenshot |
| Update when the interface changes | Leave a screenshot of the old design |

A screenshot that is six months old is worse than none: it teaches the wrong screen.

## Length

| Type | Words | Steps |
|---|---|---|
| How-to | 150-350 | 3-9 |
| Troubleshooting | 200-400 | A decision tree, not a list |
| Concept or policy | 200-500 | No steps, but a clear answer line |
| Reference (fees, limits, timings) | A table, under 150 words of prose | None |

If it is longer than 400 words for a how-to, it is two articles or it is a manual.

## The tone

- Second person, present tense: "You can", "Open", "Select".
- No "simply", "just", "obviously", "easy". They are insults to someone who is stuck.
- No exclamation marks, no jokes, no emoji.
- Say "we" only for what the company does: "we will reissue it within 2 working days".
- Apologise once if the product caused the problem, then explain.

## Anti-patterns

- **The article that starts with the company.** "Founded in 2016, we are a leading..." The reader has already bought.
- **The article written for the person who wrote it.** It explains the architecture instead of the button.
- **Three questions in one article.** It ranks for none and answers none.
- **Steps that only work for admins.** The customer sees a different menu, and the article is wrong for everyone who reads it.
- **A policy stated as a guess.** "Refunds usually take 5-7 days" when the policy says 3 working days is a promise you will be held to.
- **The workaround with no warning.** If it risks data, say so before the steps, not after.
