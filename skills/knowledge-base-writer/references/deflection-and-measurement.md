# Deflection and measurement

A help centre that is not measured is a writing project. This is what to count,
and the honest limits of the numbers.

## The four numbers

| Metric | Definition | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| Search-to-article rate | Sessions where a search led to an article view, over all search sessions | Whether the titles and results match the words |
| Article-to-resolved rate | Readers who did not contact support within 24 hours of reading, over readers | The closest thing to deflection |
| Repeat contact rate | Tickets on a topic after its article published, against before | Whether the topic actually moved |
| Self-service share | Sessions in the help centre over sessions in help centre plus tickets | Whether the channel is being used at all |

Page views are not a metric. High views on a troubleshooting article can mean the
product is broken.

## Measuring a topic before and after

1. Pick the topic and its article.
2. Record the ticket count for the 8 weeks before publication, and the handle time.
3. Publish, and link it from the exact screen and from the top of the queue's reply templates.
4. Record the count for the 8 weeks after.
5. Report the change with the confounder named: a product fix, a season, a price change or a marketing push can all move the number.

A 20-30% reduction on a how-to topic is a good result. A policy or timing topic
often drops 50% because the answer is short and unambiguous.

## Topics that cannot be deflected by writing

| Topic | Why | What actually deflects it |
|---|---|---|
| "Where is my order" | Needs the specific order | A tracking page in the account |
| "Why was I charged this amount" | Needs the invoice | A billing page with a breakdown |
| "My payment failed" | Needs the gateway's reason | An in-product error message that says why |
| "Cancel my subscription" | Often a retention conversation | A self-serve cancel flow, with the article beside it |
| "Is this covered under warranty" | Needs the serial number | A lookup tool |

Write the article for all of them - it reduces the follow-up questions - but do not
promise a deflection rate a document cannot deliver. The fix is product.

## Search terms with no results

The most valuable report in any help centre. Every zero-result search is a
customer telling you what to write next.

- Pull the report monthly, group by intent, and add the top five to the gap log.
- Add the exact phrase as a synonym if the article exists under another name.
- If the phrase is a product name the customer invented, use their name in the title and the product's name in the body.

## What good looks like, as planning ranges

| Metric | Weak | Normal | Good |
|---|---|---|---|
| Search-to-article click rate | <40% | 55-70% | 75%+ |
| Article-to-resolved | <50% | 60-75% | 80%+ |
| Self-service share of contacts | <10% | 20-35% | 45%+ |
| Top-10 articles share of views | - | 60-80% | Normal; fix the tail instead |
| Articles past their review date | >40% | 20-30% | <10% |

Label these as planning ranges. Your own baseline, measured for eight weeks, is
the only number worth quoting to your team.

## Reporting to a founder, in five lines

1. The topic, and the tickets it cost before.
2. The article published, and where it is linked from.
3. The ticket count after eight weeks.
4. What else changed that could explain it.
5. The next topic, with its count.

No page views, no word counts, no "engagement". A founder acts on tickets avoided.

## Anti-patterns

- **Claiming deflection from page views.** A view is a hope; a missing ticket is a result.
- **Measuring one week after publication.** Search indexing and habit both take longer than that.
- **Ignoring the confounders.** A product fix in the same month will take credit for your article, and you should say so.
- **Optimising for search traffic from outside.** A help article that ranks for a marketing keyword attracts readers who will never buy or use the product.
- **Never measuring at all, because the helpdesk and the analytics are not connected.** Say so, write the article, and set the review date - a good article with no measurement still removes a question.
