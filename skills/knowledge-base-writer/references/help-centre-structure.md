# Help-centre structure

A help centre is navigated by someone who is already frustrated. The structure
decides whether they find the answer or mail you.

## Organise by task, not by product

| Structure that fails | Structure that works |
|---|---|
| Modules: Orders, Inventory, Billing, Settings | Tasks: Get paid, Ship an order, Manage my team, Fix a problem |
| The org chart's names | The customer's verbs |
| One long FAQ page | One page per question, linked from a task hub |

The test: a new customer reads your top-level categories and knows which one holds
their problem without clicking.

## The three layers

| Layer | Job | How many |
|---|---|---|
| Hub (task) | Route the reader in two clicks | 5-7 |
| Collection (sub-task) | Group 5-15 related articles | 2-4 per hub |
| Article | Answer one question | As many as needed |

More than seven hubs means the reader is choosing between categories instead of
finding their answer.

## A structure for most Indian SMB products

1. **Getting started** - sign up, first setup, the five things to do in week one.
2. **Orders and fulfilment** - create, edit, cancel, ship, track, returns.
3. **Payments and invoices** - pay, refunds, invoices, GST details, failed payments.
4. **My account and team** - profile, roles, adding people, notifications.
5. **Fix a problem** - login, OTP, app errors, integrations not syncing.
6. **Plans, pricing and policies** - limits, upgrade, cancellation, terms.

Adapt the names to the customer's words, not to yours.

## Navigation furniture that earns its place

| Element | Why | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Search, at the top, on every page | Most readers search, they do not browse | It must handle the customer's words, including misspellings |
| Popular articles on the home page | The five that resolve most tickets | Refresh monthly from the gap log |
| Contextual link inside the product | The highest-converting placement there is | Link from the exact screen where the question arises |
| Related articles, two per article | Keeps the reader in the help centre | Two, not eight |
| Contact support, always visible | The escape hatch | Put it after the answer, not before |
| Language switcher | India-specific and often forgotten | Only if the articles actually exist in that language |

## Duplicates, and what to do with them

- **Same question, two articles**: keep the one that ranks and reads better, redirect the other, and merge the unique steps.
- **Same article, two titles**: one canonical title, the other becomes a redirect. Never leave two competing pages.
- **Feature article plus task article**: the task article is canonical; the feature article links to it.
- **Old version plus new version**: archive with a dated note, never delete - search engines and old emails still point at it.

## The URL and title discipline

- Slug from the title: `change-gst-number-on-invoice`, not `kb-2041`.
- Never change a slug without a redirect. Links live in old emails and chat threads.
- One H1 per article, matching the title, and the title must be the search.
- Breadcrumb showing hub > collection > article, so the reader knows where they are.

## What goes on the home page

1. Search, large, with a placeholder in the customer's words: "Try 'refund', 'GST', 'cancel order'".
2. Six task hubs.
3. Five popular articles, refreshed monthly.
4. The status link - a real one, updated during incidents.
5. Contact support, with the response time stated honestly.

Not: a carousel, a welcome video, a mission statement, or a "submit a ticket" form
as the first thing.

## India-specific structure notes

- **WhatsApp as a channel** deserves its own entry point if support runs there, with the same articles linked.
- **GST, e-invoicing, HSN codes** belong under payments, not under "compliance" - that is the customer's word for the problem.
- **COD, prepaid, RTO** are search terms of their own for any D2C business; give each a page.
- **Language**: if the business serves Hindi, Tamil or Bengali speakers, the top ten articles matter more than a full translation of everything.
- **Regional delivery promises** vary; state them per zone rather than one national line.

## The migration, when the structure changes

1. Export every URL and its views.
2. Map each to its new home. Keep the top 50 URLs working with redirects.
3. Publish the new hubs with the five most-viewed articles, not all of them.
4. Redirect the rest, and keep the redirects for at least a year.
5. Tell support, so agents send the new links.

## Anti-patterns

- **A flat list of 300 articles.** Nobody browses it; everybody searches, badly.
- **Categories named after internal teams.** The customer does not know which team owns their problem.
- **A help centre behind the login.** Half the questions are pre-purchase.
- **The same content in the blog and the help centre.** Two pages compete, and neither wins.
- **A "Contact us" form as the only path.** It converts a findable answer into a ticket.
