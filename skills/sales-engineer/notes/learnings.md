# Learnings - sales-engineer

1. **Find the constraint, then write a verdict.** Environment, integration surface, data, compliance,
   owner and the thing that would kill it - a deal without a written verdict stays in evaluation for
   three months.
2. **The demo is an argument, not a tour.** Three workflows in the buyer's own order on the buyer's
   own data, with a second "tell" after each "show" so the champion can repeat it upward.
3. **"Not supported" said in week one wins more deals than a stretch.** A gap found in UAT costs the
   contract, the reference and the renewal.
4. **No POC without a decision date and a named client owner.** An open-ended POC is unpaid
   consulting, and it converts worse than a short one with a date in the diary.
5. **Scope needs volume and frequency.** "Integration with ERP" is an argument at delivery; "two-way
   sync of item master and stock every 15 minutes for 4,200 SKUs" is a commitment.
6. **Effort is a range with the assumption written.** A single number becomes a fixed price and a
   fixed date in the buyer's notes.
7. **Concede one row in the comparison.** A table where you win everything is read as marketing, and
   the buyer's trust moves to the competitor who was honest.
8. **Security review runs from day one, in parallel.** Started in week three, it ends the deal in
   week six - after the technical win, which is the most expensive way to lose.
