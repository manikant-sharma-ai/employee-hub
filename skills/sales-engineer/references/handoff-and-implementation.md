# Handoff and implementation

A deal is not won when it is signed. It is won when delivery starts without discovering
something the seller knew and did not write down.

## The handoff note

One document, written by this role, read by delivery. Six sections:

1. **The customer's world** - what they do, the process being replaced, the volume, the
   peak, the season.
2. **The stack map** - every system, how it connects, what is tested and what is not.
3. **What was promised** - each commitment, who made it, and whether it is in the
   contract. Anything promised and not contracted is flagged loudly.
4. **The assumptions** - what we believe about their data, their process and their
   timeline, and what happens if one is wrong.
5. **The risks** - the integration most likely to slip, the approver most likely to
   delay, the data most likely to be dirty.
6. **The people** - who signs, who champions, who blocks, who does the work, and what
   each is measured on.

## What must never appear only in a call recording

- A promised date.
- A promised integration.
- A discount or a term.
- A customisation agreed "because it is small".
- A data-residency commitment.

If it was said, it is written, and if it was written it is either in the contract or in
the risk list.

## The scope note

A short document that turns the sale into a plan:

| Item | In scope | Out of scope | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Data migration | 24 months of transactions | historical beyond that | customer |
| Integration | nightly ERP export | real-time | us, phase 2 |
| Training | two 60-minute sessions | per-user onboarding | customer |
| Custom report | the month-end pack | ad-hoc reporting | us |

The out-of-scope column is the one that protects the relationship. Every project that
went wrong had a customer expecting something that was never written down.

## Handing over

- **A 45-minute session, not a document drop.** Delivery asks questions; the answers are
  the valuable part.
- **Record it**, and attach the note to the deal and to the project.
- **Introduce the people directly** - the buyer's engineer and our delivery lead in the
  same thread before the kickoff.
- **Stay available for the first two weeks**, then step out. A seller who stays in the
  delivery channel for three months is a seller who has not handed over.

## The promises register

Keep one, per deal: promise, date made, who made it, where it is recorded, and its
status. Review it at handoff and at every QBR. This is the single document that prevents
the "but your salesperson said" conversation, and it also protects the customer, who is
entitled to what they were told.

## Anti-patterns

- **The handoff that is a forwarded email thread.**
- **"Delivery will figure it out"** - they will, expensively, in front of the customer.
- **A scope note written by delivery without the seller**, which loses the context of
  what was actually promised.
- **The seller who disappears at signature**, which is the fastest way to lose the
  reference and the renewal.
- **A customisation agreed in the room and never priced**, which arrives as a delivery
  surprise and a margin problem.
