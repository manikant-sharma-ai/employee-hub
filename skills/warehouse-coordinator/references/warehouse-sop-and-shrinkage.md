# Warehouse SOP and shrinkage control

An SOP is not a document for an auditor. It is what a new picker reads on day one and does correctly on day two.

## Writing one

- **One page per process.** Receiving, putaway, picking, packing, dispatch, returns, cycle count. Seven pages, not
  seventy.
- **Written in the language of the floor**, with the local word for the thing beside the English one if that is
  what people say.
- **Numbered steps, one action per step**, with a photograph for anything that can be done two ways.
- **The exception is part of the SOP**: what to do when the item is not in the bin, when the count is short, when
  the box is damaged. Procedures that only cover the happy path get abandoned at the first exception.
- **A named owner and a review date** at the top. An SOP with no date is a rumour.

## Training that holds

1. **Show it once, do it together once, watch them do it once.** Then sign the training sheet with the date.
2. **The first week is supervised**, and the errors of the first week are recorded as SOP defects, not as person
   defects.
3. **Refresher after any variance above tolerance** in the area concerned - the SOP is probably the cause.
4. **Cross-train one person per critical process.** A warehouse where only one person can receive has a single
   point of failure that also happens to be a control risk.

## Shrinkage, controlled honestly

Shrinkage is the residue after paperwork, misplacement and damage are ruled out. Controls that work:

- **Segregation of duties**: the person who counts is not the person who adjusts, and the person who receives is
  not the person who pays.
- **A locked quarantine and a scrap bin with a count.** Damage that is thrown away without a record is
  indistinguishable from theft.
- **Cycle counts on A-items monthly.** Frequency is a better control than severity of punishment.
- **Random spot checks on high-value lines at dispatch**, with the result recorded either way.
- **CCTV at the pack bench and the gate**, disclosed, and used to review exceptions rather than to watch people.

Controls that do not work: a poster, a threat, a single annual count, and a rule nobody wrote down.

## The weekly routine

| Day | Routine | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Cycle count of 20 A-items | Variance list, recount requests |
| Wednesday | Location audit - 10 random bins | Misplacement count, label fixes |
| Friday | Wrong-item and GRN exception review | One process change, owner, date |
| Monthly | Full variance read and scorecard | The count pack |

## The one change rule

Each read proposes exactly one process change. Five changes proposed means none implemented. The change has an
owner, a date and the metric it will move, and it is checked at the next read - that is the whole improvement
system.
