# Cadence and distribution

The day is the product. A page that arrives when it is ready is a page people check for
and stop checking.

## The rhythm

| Artefact | Day | Time | Contents |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weekly page | Monday | 07:30 IST | Previous week, MTD and run rate |
| Flash note | Any day | Within 2 hours | A single material event: a lost account, a stock-out, a payment failure |
| Monthly pack | By the 5th working day | 10:00 IST | Full month, variance review, three drivers, decisions |
| Outside pack (lender, board, investor) | Fixed date agreed with them | Same day each month | Their format, reconciled to the internal page |
| Quarterly review | 10 days after quarter close | - | Trend, plan vs actual for the quarter, re-forecast |

Weekly page on Monday morning is not arbitrary: it is the only morning the week can
still be changed.

## Distribution matrix

| Reader | Weekly | Monthly | Extra |
|---|---|---|---|
| Founder / CEO | Full page | Full pack | The number not to quote outside |
| Department heads | Their function | Full pack | Their drivers in detail |
| Board | - | Board variant | Only what the board pack needs |
| Lender | - | Their format | Receivables ageing and stock statement |
| Investor | - | Agreed five to seven metrics | Same definitions every month |
| Accountant / CA | - | Reconciliation summary | The gap note, in writing |

## The send

- **One email, one attachment or one link, one time.** No "updated version" twenty
  minutes later.
- **Subject line carries the period and the headline**: "Week 45 - revenue ₹9.8 lakh vs
  plan ₹10.5 lakh, 2 reds".
- **The page in the body where it fits**, so it is read on a phone without opening a
  file.
- **The link is to a read-only folder per period.** Never to a live workbook that will
  change under the reader.

## Version control

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| File named `MIS-YYYY-MM-DD` | Sortable, unambiguous |
| Read-only once sent | The number a reader saw does not change |
| A correction is a new version, marked `v2`, with a one-line note | Silent corrections destroy trust faster than errors |
| Definitions sheet travels with the file | The reader can check the formula |
| Archive keeps every version for 24 months | Auditors and lenders ask for history |

## The mid-period rule

No new metric, no new layout, no new definition inside a period. Collect requests in a
list and apply them from the first period of the next month. A page that changes shape
every week is a page that has to be re-learned every week.

Exception: a metric that is wrong is fixed immediately, with a note.

## When the page is late

Say so before the deadline, not after: one line, the reason, the new time. Then send it,
even if one number is missing and marked as missing. A page at 90% on time beats a
perfect page on Wednesday.

## The archive as an asset

Twelve months of consistent pages is the most valuable document a small business owns:
it is the lender's evidence, the investor's diligence answer, the board's trend line and
the founder's memory. It exists only if the format held for twelve months.

## Anti-patterns

| Anti-pattern | Cost |
|---|---|
| Sending when ready | Readers stop checking; the page loses its authority |
| Two emails with two versions | Nobody knows which number is real |
| A live link that changes | A reader quotes a figure that no longer exists |
| A different format for the lender | Two sets of numbers, and a question at sanction |
| Silence when late | The one failure that reads as concealment |
| A monthly pack with no fixed date | It becomes a quarterly pack within two months |
