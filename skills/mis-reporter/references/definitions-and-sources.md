# Definitions and sources

Most disagreements about a number are disagreements about a definition. Write the
definition once, get the owner to agree, and the argument stops recurring.

## The definition card

One card per metric. Keep them in the dictionary sheet, one row each.

```
Metric: Revenue (net)
Formula: gross sales - returns - discounts - GST collected
Grain: calendar month, by invoice date
Source: Zoho Books > Reports > P&L, "Total Income" line
Pull: 1st working day, by finance-ops
Known weakness: credit notes raised after month end land in the next period
Owner: founder
Agreed: 2026-11-08
```

Six fields and a date. If a metric has no card, it is not ready to publish.

## The five definition traps

1. **Gross versus net.** Revenue before or after returns, discounts and GST. Always state which. For an Indian business, GST is collected on behalf of the government and is never revenue.
2. **Order date versus dispatch date versus invoice date.** Three different periods, three different numbers, all defensible. Pick one, write it down, keep it.
3. **Count of orders versus count of customers.** One dealer placing eight orders is one customer and eight orders. Both are useful; they are not the same metric.
4. **Calendar month versus 4-5-4 versus a 5-week month.** A 5-week month against a 4-week month produces a false alarm every time. State the period length on the page.
5. **Including or excluding inter-company, samples, and internal consumption.** Samples are a marketing cost, not revenue, and internal transfers are not sales.

## One authoritative source

When two systems disagree, decide which is authoritative **for what** and write it
on the page:

- **Money:** the accounting system. The store's revenue figure is an operational number, not the number.
- **Orders and fulfilment:** the store or the marketplace panel.
- **Traffic and behaviour:** the analytics tool.
- **Pipeline:** the CRM, provided the stages are defined.

Then reconcile once, monthly, and note the gap and its cause. A permanent
reconciliation gap of 3% that is explained is fine. An unexplained one is not.

## Restatements

If a prior period changes, publish the change:

```
Restated: October revenue ₹38.2 lakh -> ₹37.6 lakh. Cause: two credit notes
dated 28 October were posted on 3 November. Source unchanged.
```

Silent restatement is the fastest way to make a page untrustworthy, because the
reader eventually notices that last week's numbers were different and concludes
that the page is guesswork.
