# Variance and drivers

The variance is the information. The actual is only the news.

## Materiality, set in advance

| Line | Investigate above | Escalate to the page above |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 5% or ₹2 lakh | 10% or ₹5 lakh |
| Gross margin | 100 bps | 250 bps |
| Any cost head | 10% or ₹1 lakh | 20% |
| Cash | Any negative surprise | Always on the page |

Thresholds are agreed once and written in the definitions sheet. Without them, every
number is either fine or a crisis, depending on who is reading.

## The revenue bridge

The standard decomposition, in this order:

```
Opening plan
  + Volume effect      (units moved × plan price)
  + Price effect       (actual price - plan price) × actual units
  + Mix effect         (share shift between lines at their plan margins)
  - Discounts and returns
  - One-offs           (a large order, a channel launch, a customer loss)
= Actual
```

Worked example, a ₹4 crore trading business:

| Line | Plan | Actual | Effect | Explanation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Units | 12,000 | 10,800 | -₹1.9 lakh | Two distributor orders slipped to next month |
| Realisation | ₹340 | ₹356 | +₹1.7 lakh | Price rise from 1st, held on 80% of volume |
| Mix | - | - | -₹0.6 lakh | Higher share of the low-margin economy line |
| Returns | 1.2% | 2.4% | -₹0.9 lakh | One batch of 300 units returned for a packing defect |
| **Total** | **₹40.8 lakh** | **₹39.1 lakh** | **-₹1.7 lakh** | |

Three effects and a one-off. That is a page, not a report.

## Cost variance

| Type | Question |
|---|---|
| Fixed cost overrun | Was it price, or was it a new commitment? |
| Variable cost overrun | Did the rate rise, or did consumption rise per unit? |
| One-off | Name it, date it, and say whether it recurs |

## The five why, stopped early

Ask why until you reach something a person can change, then stop. Three levels is
usually enough:

1. Contribution fell ₹2.4 lakh. Why? → Freight per order rose 34%.
2. Why? → Average order value fell from ₹2,100 to ₹1,540.
3. Why? → The ₹999 bundle replaced the ₹2,499 pack as the best seller in October.

Now it is a decision: restore the pack, or re-cost freight for small orders. A fourth
"why" here produces a theory about the market and no decision.

## The so-what line

Every driver gets one sentence with a consequence and a horizon:

- Weak: "Freight costs increased."
- Strong: "Freight is now 11% of revenue, up from 8%; at this mix, the quarter's
  contribution target needs either a ₹250 minimum order or a courier renegotiation by
  15 November."

## One-offs, declared

A one-off is only a one-off if it is named, dated and does not recur. The test: did the
same "one-off" appear last period? If yes, it is a run-rate cost and it belongs in the
base.

| Claim | Test |
|---|---|
| "Diwali stocking" | Does it appear in the same months each year? Then it is seasonal, and it belongs in the plan |
| "One large order" | Name the customer and the value; if it is over 10% of revenue, the concentration is the story |
| "A customer left" | Revenue churn, not a one-off, unless it was a single non-recurring contract |
| "A machine broke" | Maintenance cost; a one-off only if the repair is capitalised |

## The variance review

Monthly, 30 minutes, four questions per gap:

1. Is the gap real, or a timing difference?
2. What is the driver, in one sentence?
3. Is it recoverable this quarter, and by what action?
4. Who owns the recovery, and by when?

Anything not answered in the meeting goes on the action list with an owner. A variance
review without owners is a reading group.

## Anti-patterns

| Anti-pattern | Fix |
|---|---|
| Variance shown only in percent | Rupees first; percent hides the size |
| "Lower than expected" as an explanation | Name the driver or leave the cell empty |
| Every gap explained | Explain the three that matter; the rest are noise |
| One-offs that recur every quarter | Move to the base and restate the plan |
| Variance against a re-forecast | Variance against the approved plan, or say which |
| Drivers with no owner | Every adverse driver above threshold has a name |
