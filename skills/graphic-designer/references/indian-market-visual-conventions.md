# Indian market visual conventions

Design that travels well in India is not "Indian-styled". It is design that respects how the audience actually reads: the languages, the festivals, the price expectations, the device, and the channels.

## Scripts and type

| Script | Where it matters | Practical notes |
|---|---|---|
| Devanagari | Hindi, Marathi, Nepali | Needs a Devanagari companion to the Latin face, not a mechanical match. Height and stroke weight differ; align the x-height, not the cap height |
| Tamil | Tamil Nadu, Sri Lanka | Rounder forms, longer ascenders; needs 10-15% more line height |
| Telugu, Kannada | AP, Telangana, Karnataka | Dense at small sizes - raise the minimum body size one step |
| Bengali | West Bengal, Tripura | Distinct headline forms; avoid all-caps equivalents (there are none) |
| Gujarati | Gujarat | Strong in trade and wholesale signage |
| Latin + one Indian script | Everywhere | Stack, do not squeeze side by side. Both are primary |

Rules:

- **Never machine-translate the headline** and set it. Length changes 20-40%, and a headline that fits in English will not fit in Tamil.
- **Never use a Latin display face for an Indian script** by substituting glyphs from another font. It is instantly visible and reads as careless.
- **Hinglish is a legitimate design language** for urban D2C and quick commerce - "Sale shuru" sets fine in Latin. Decide with the client, do not assume.
- **Test numerals.** Prices in Indian format use lakh/crore grouping: ₹1,49,999, not ₹149,999.

## Festival and occasion calendar

The commercial year in India runs on festivals. Plan creatives a month ahead of the print deadline, not the festival.

| Period | What it drives | Design note |
|---|---|---|
| Makar Sankranti / Pongal (Jan) | Regional, strong in Gujarat, TN, AP | Kite, harvest, sugarcane motifs; regional, not national |
| Republic Day (Jan) | Retail sales, patriotic | Tricolour is a national symbol - handle respectfully, no commercial mockery |
| Holi (Mar) | Colour, apparel, FMCG | Colour-splashed visuals compress badly on WhatsApp - use flat colour blocks |
| Gudi Padwa / Ugadi (Mar-Apr) | Maharashtra, Karnataka, AP - auspicious purchases | Gold, marigold, new beginnings |
| Eid | Apparel, gifting, food | Crescent and lantern motifs; avoid alcohol and non-halal imagery in food creatives |
| Raksha Bandhan, Janmashtami (Aug) | Gifting, sweets, jewellery | Rakhi, thread, sibling pairs |
| Onam (Aug-Sep) | Kerala, national reach for FMCG | Pookalam, boat race, banana leaf |
| Ganesh Chaturthi (Aug-Sep) | Maharashtra, strong retail | Modak, red-gold, avoid showing the idol disrespectfully or on footwear/leather |
| Navratri / Durga Puja (Sep-Oct) | Gujarat, Bengal, national apparel | Nine colours by day in Gujarat; Bengal pandal aesthetic differs completely |
| Dussehra / Diwali (Oct-Nov) | The biggest retail window of the year | Diyas, rangoli, gold. Start creative work 6-8 weeks out |
| Christmas / New Year (Dec) | Urban retail, gifting | Green-red-white; strong in Goa, Kerala, metro retail |

Anti-pattern: one pan-India festival creative. Regional festivals get regional creatives, in the regional language, or they read as a brand that does not know its customer.

## Colour meaning

- **Saffron, marigold, gold** - auspicious, festive, premium in traditional categories. Overused to the point of invisibility in October-November.
- **Red** - celebration and urgency both. In food it raises appetite; in finance it reads as loss on a dashboard.
- **Green** - auspicious for beginnings; also "veg" mark, and in finance it reads as gain. Never use the green dot casually near food.
- **White** - purity in some contexts, mourning in others. All-white festival creatives read as inauspicious to a large part of the audience.
- **Black** - premium in fashion and tech; inauspicious for weddings and gifting in many communities.
- **Blue** - safest, most corporate, least emotional. Good default for B2B and finance.

## Price and offer conventions

- **Show the price.** Indian retail audiences scan for it. Hiding it to "build desire" loses the click.
- **Strike-through MRP plus the offer price** is the standard grammar, and it must be the real MRP, not an inflated one.
- **"Starting at ₹X"** needs the qualifier visible, not in 6 pt.
- **Discount claims need a basis.** "Up to 60% off" needs at least one product at 60%.
- **₹ symbol** - use it, and set it in the same face as the numerals or a designed mark. A mismatched rupee symbol is the fastest way to look amateur.

## Channels and their visual grammar

| Channel | What works | What fails |
|---|---|---|
| WhatsApp status / broadcast | Flat colour, huge type, one offer, 9:16 | Gradients, fine detail, small text, PDF catalogues |
| Instagram | 4:5 photo-led, strong type | Text-only posts with no visual hook |
| LinkedIn | 1200x1500, clean type, real data | Festival greetings with stock imagery |
| IndiaMART, TradeIndia | Product on white, spec text, contact block | Lifestyle-only shots with no specification |
| Amazon, Flipkart | White background main image, compliance text | Text-heavy main images - rejected |
| Shop signage | 2-4 words, 10 m legibility | Full address and phone number in the main line |
| Print flyer in a newspaper insert | Price-led, dense but ordered | Minimalism - it disappears next to competitors |

## Device reality

Most of the audience is on a mid-range Android phone, on mobile data, at 50-70% brightness, often outdoors.

- Test at 50% brightness. If it only works at 100%, it does not work.
- Keep file sizes down - a 4 MB story creative loads slowly and gets skipped.
- Avoid thin hairlines and 1 px borders; they vanish on a compressed render.
- Vertical before horizontal. Design 9:16 first and adapt down, not the reverse.

## Anti-patterns

- A Diwali creative that is 90% gold gradient and 10% product. It joins a wall of identical creatives.
- English-only headline for a Tier-2/Tier-3 audience, with the regional language as a 12 pt footnote.
- Stock photos of Western families for an Indian middle-class audience.
- Religious imagery on merchandise, footwear, or anything the product might touch.
- Using a national flag or emblem as a decorative background.
- Assuming "India" means one language, one festival calendar or one colour code.
