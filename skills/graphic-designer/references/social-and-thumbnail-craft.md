# Social and thumbnail craft

Every surface has one number that decides whether the work is any good: how long the viewer actually looks. Design for that, not for the canvas.

## The surfaces and their real constraints

| Surface | Size | Real constraint |
|---|---|---|
| Instagram feed (4:5) | 1080x1350 | The tallest feed real estate; use it before 1:1 |
| Instagram / Facebook square | 1080x1080 | Safe default, weakest reach per pixel |
| Instagram story, reel cover | 1080x1920 | Top 250 px and bottom 340 px are covered by UI |
| LinkedIn post | 1200x1500 | Text-heavy is tolerated; the first 3 lines of caption do the work |
| X / Twitter | 1600x900 | Small in feed; bold shapes only |
| YouTube thumbnail | 1280x720 | Seen at ~168-210 px wide on a phone |
| WhatsApp status | 1080x1920 | Compressed hard; avoid fine detail and gradients |
| Google / Meta ad creative | 1200x628, 1080x1080, 1080x1920 | Text-to-image ratio no longer penalised, but small text is still invisible |
| Marketplace main image (Amazon, Flipkart) | 2000x2000, white background | Product fills 85% of frame; no text on main image |

Two numbers to memorise: **168 px** - the width a YouTube thumbnail is seen at in a phone feed; **250/340 px** - the UI that eats the top and bottom of a story.

## Thumbnails: the highest-leverage asset in the business

A thumbnail is not a small poster. It is a 168-pixel billboard in a column of competitors.

The five things that decide clicks:

1. **One subject, large.** A face with a clear expression, or the object at 40-60% of frame. Never a group, never a wide shot.
2. **Contrast against the feed.** Feeds are white and grey. A dark or saturated block with a bright subject separates. Look at what the niche does, then do the opposite on one axis only.
3. **Three to four words, maximum**, at 90-140 px, in a heavy weight. The title does the explaining; the thumbnail does the pulling.
4. **A visible emotional or informational gap.** "₹0 to ₹40L" beats "My business journey". Numbers and before/after are the two cheapest wins.
5. **Legibility at 168 px.** Shrink it to 168 px wide and look. If you cannot read it, it does not exist.

Anti-patterns: the full title repeated on the image; four faces; a screenshot of a spreadsheet; red arrows and circles on everything; a logo where the subject should be.

## Carousels

- **Slide 1 is a thumbnail.** Same rules: one promise, big type, high contrast. Slide 1 decides whether slides 2-8 are seen.
- **One idea per slide**, 12-25 words. If it needs more, it is two slides.
- **A visible progress cue** - slide numbers or a partial next-card edge.
- **Last slide is a single CTA**, not a summary. One action.
- **Keep the template identical across the set** so the whole carousel reads as one object when it scrolls.

## Stories and reels covers

- **Design inside the safe area**: content between y=250 and y=1580 on 1080x1920.
- **Assume sound off** and 1.5 seconds per frame. One message per frame.
- **Reel covers** appear in the grid as a 1:1 or 4:5 crop of a 9:16 canvas - put the subject and the type inside the centre square, or the grid crop will cut the headline.
- **A tappable-looking element** - an arrow, a "swipe", a circle - lifts completion. Do not fake a UI that is not there.

## Feed coherence

The single most common request, and it is solved by system, not by taste.

| Approach | When to use |
|---|---|
| Alternating type-led and photo-led tiles | Cheapest coherence; makes a grid look intentional |
| A fixed frame - same border, same logo position, same footer bar | Best for product catalogues |
| A colour rotation of three brand colours | Best for a content calendar with mixed authors |
| No system, "curated chaos" | Only when someone with taste posts daily. Rarely worth the risk |

Pick one and apply it for a quarter. Coherence is repetition plus one variable, never many variables.

## Ads

- **First frame must work muted and static**, because that is how it is first seen.
- **One claim, one proof.** The proof is a number, a before/after or a named mechanism - not an adjective.
- **Leave room for platform UI** - never put the CTA where the "Sponsored" label or the swipe-up arrow lands.
- **Make three variants that differ on one axis** - headline, subject or colour - so the test tells you something. Three variants that differ on everything teach nothing.

## Marketplace images

Amazon and Flipkart have rules, and a rejected image costs days.

- Main image: pure white background (RGB 255,255,255), product at 85% of frame, no text, no watermark, no props.
- Secondary images: lifestyle, scale reference, ingredient/material detail, packaging, what is in the box.
- Minimum 1000 px on the long edge for zoom; 2000x2000 preferred.
- No borders, no collages on the main image, no "best seller" badges you were not given.

## Anti-patterns

- Exporting one 1:1 and letting the platform crop it for stories. The headline will be cut.
- A gradient-heavy creative for WhatsApp status. Compression turns it into banding.
- White text on a white-ish product photo because it "looked fine" at 100% zoom.
- Ten text elements on an ad. The viewer reads zero.
- Using the same creative across YouTube, Instagram and a print flyer. Three different viewing distances, three different designs.
