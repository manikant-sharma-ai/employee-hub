# Packaging and label compliance

A label is the one design that can be illegal. The creative is the easy half; the
mandatory declarations are what send a print run back.

## The mandatory block, on a packaged product in India

| Declaration | Rule of thumb | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Product name and nature | Common name, not a brand fantasy | Brand |
| Net quantity | g/ml/pieces, in the prescribed format | Brand |
| MRP | "MRP ₹___ incl. of all taxes" | Brand |
| Manufacturer / packer name and address | Full address with pin code | Business |
| Consumer care details | Email and phone | Business |
| Batch number and date of manufacture | Printed or inkjet | Production |
| Best before / expiry | Where the category requires it | Regulatory |
| Veg / non-veg mark | The prescribed symbol, correct size | Regulatory |
| FSSAI licence number | Food only, with the logo where required | Regulatory |
| Country of origin | Where imported or where required | Business |
| Statutory warnings | Category-specific | Regulatory |

**This table is a checklist, not legal advice.** The category decides which rows
apply, and the exact wording changes. Get the final label signed off by whoever
handles compliance, in writing, before the plate is made.

## Print mechanics that come back to bite

- **Bleed 3mm on every edge**, trim marks, and no critical text inside the safe area.
- **Colour mode CMYK**, and never assume a bright RGB orange survives - check the proof.
- **Minimum type size 6pt** for the mandatory block, 8pt for anything a customer must read.
- **Barcode**: EAN-13 with the correct quiet zone (about 3.6mm each side) and a real, registered number. A drawn barcode fails at the scanner.
- **Fonts outlined or embedded**, images at 300dpi at final size.
- **Metallic or white ink** needs its own plate and a conversation with the printer, not a layer effect.
- **Dielines**: get the printer's actual dieline file; a redrawn one is a rejected run.

## Compliance claims that carry risk

- "100% natural", "no preservatives", "immunity boosting", "clinically proven", "ayurvedic" - each has a definition and a substantiation requirement. Do not set a claim you cannot evidence.
- **Comparatives** ("India's best") need proof or they invite a complaint.
- **Green and sustainability claims** are being enforced more strictly; "eco-friendly" with no basis is a risk.
- **Health and nutrition claims** on food need the regulatory format, not a marketing line.

## The pre-print gate

- [ ] Every mandatory declaration present and legible at final size
- [ ] Compliance sign-off, in writing, with the date
- [ ] Barcode scans in a shop's scanner
- [ ] Proof approved on the actual substrate, not on screen
- [ ] Bleed, trim, safe area, colour mode, resolution, outlined fonts
- [ ] File named, versioned and archived with the approval

## Anti-patterns

- A beautiful label with the net quantity in 4pt grey.
- Copying a competitor's mandatory block - their category is not yours.
- Approving a PDF proof for a print that will be laminated (the colour shifts).
- A barcode drawn as decoration.
- Shipping the design before the compliance sign-off "to save time".
