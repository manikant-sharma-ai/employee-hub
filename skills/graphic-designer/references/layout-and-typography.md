# Layout and typography

Layout is the order in which the eye is allowed to move. Everything here exists to make that order obvious at a glance, at the size the asset will actually be seen.

## Hierarchy: decide three levels, then stop

Before any styling, write the three levels down.

| Level | Purpose | Typical treatment |
|---|---|---|
| 1 - Focal | The one thing remembered in two seconds | Largest, highest contrast, most space around it |
| 2 - Support | Qualifies the focal point | Half the size, same weight or one step down |
| 3 - Detail | Price, date, terms, CTA, logo | Small, consistent, pushed to an edge or corner |

Rules:

- **One level-1 element.** Two is a tie, and a tie means the viewer reads neither.
- **Aim for a 1.6x-2x size jump between levels.** A 10% difference reads as a mistake, not a distinction.
- **Position the levels.** Focal in the upper-left or centre-left for left-to-right scripts; the eye enters there and exits bottom-right.
- **Whitespace is the cheapest hierarchy tool.** Doubling the space around the headline does more than another colour.

## Grid

- **Pick one grid per canvas** and hold it. Four columns for a square post, twelve for a deck slide, six for an A4.
- **Margins at 5-8% of the short edge.** Anything closer to the edge looks like a printing error.
- **Align to something.** Every element shares an edge with at least one other element. Deliberate misalignment needs a reason.
- **Break the grid once, for the focal point only.** A second break reads as sloppiness.

## Type scale

Set a scale, do not nudge sizes one asset at a time.

| Canvas | Headline | Subhead | Body | Caption |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1080x1080 post | 72-110 px | 40-56 px | 28-34 px | 20-24 px |
| 1080x1350 post | 78-120 px | 44-60 px | 30-36 px | 22-26 px |
| 1080x1920 story | 84-130 px | 48-64 px | 32-40 px | 24-28 px |
| 1280x720 thumbnail | 90-140 px | 44-56 px | - | - |
| A4/A5 print | 28-40 pt | 16-22 pt | 9-11 pt | 7-8 pt |

Minimums that are not negotiable: **body text never below 28 px on a phone-sized asset**, and **never below 9 pt in print**. Below those, the text is decoration pretending to be information.

## Legibility on a photograph

Text on an image fails for one reason: local contrast. Fix it in this order.

1. **Find a quiet area** of the photo and place the text there.
2. **Add a scrim** - a solid or gradient overlay at 40-70% opacity in the brand's dark colour.
3. **Add a solid block** behind the text, with padding equal to the x-height.
4. **Drop shadow** - last resort, tight and dark, never a glow.
5. **Reconsider the photo.** If the headline needs a scrim across 80% of it, the photo is not the background you want.

## Contrast and colour

- **Body text on background: aim for 4.5:1.** Headlines can go to 3:1 because of their size. Measure it; do not eyeball it.
- **Never white text on a light mid-tone**, never yellow on white, never red on green. These are the three most common failures in Indian festival creatives.
- **Reserve the strongest colour for the focal point.** If everything is bright, nothing is.
- **Check on a cheap phone screen at 50% brightness.** That is the real viewing condition for most of your audience.

## Alignment and rhythm

- **Left-align multi-line text** in Latin scripts. Centred text is fine for a headline of two lines or fewer; beyond that, the ragged edges fight the grid.
- **Line length 40-60 characters** for any reading text. Wider is a wall.
- **Line height 1.1-1.25 for headlines, 1.4-1.6 for body.**
- **Letter-spacing:** slightly tight (-1% to -3%) on large headlines, slightly open on small caps and all-caps labels.
- **Repeat the rhythm.** If the gap between headline and subhead is 24 px, the gap between subhead and body is 24 px or a clear multiple of it.

## Anti-patterns

- **Faux bold and faux italic.** If the weight does not exist in the font file, use another weight or another face.
- **Six sizes on one post** because each line was adjusted until it "fit". Fit is not the goal; order is.
- **Stretching type to fill a box.** Set the box from the type, not the type from the box.
- **Outlined or stroked text as a legibility fix** on a busy photo. It reads as a 2009 meme.
- **Centring everything** because centring feels safe. It flattens hierarchy.
- **Decorative script faces for anything longer than three words.** Illegible, and the licence is usually personal-use only.
- **Justified text with no hyphenation.** Rivers of white space down the paragraph.
- **Text inside the outer 8% of a story or reel.** The platform UI eats it.
