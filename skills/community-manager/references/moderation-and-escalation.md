# Moderation and escalation

Moderation is where a community manager's judgement gets tested in public. The rule is simple and unpopular: decide by policy before the incident, so the decision during the incident is not about how you feel.

## What comes down, and what stays

| Content | Action | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Spam, scam links, "earn from home" | Remove, block, log | It targets your audience, not you |
| Slurs, harassment, threats of violence | Remove, screenshot, escalate, block | Safety, and a record |
| Doxxing - phone, address, workplace of a private person | Remove immediately, escalate | Harm that compounds by the hour |
| Impersonation of the brand or staff | Report to the platform, log | Fraud risk |
| Health or medical claims about the product | Do not remove; escalate before replying | Legal exposure either way |
| A legal notice or a lawyer's letter in public | Do not reply, escalate with a screenshot | Anything you write becomes evidence |
| Criticism, anger, a bad review with detail | **Stays** | Deleting it converts a complaint into a scandal |
| A competitor's claims | Stays; one factual correction at most | Arguing amplifies |
| Political or communal provocation | Usually stays, no reply; escalate if it names staff | Removing it invites a pile-on |

**Screenshot before removal, always.** Timestamped, with the URL visible. The moment you remove something, the claim becomes "they silenced me", and the screenshot is the only answer.

## The pile-on

When one post is spreading and the comments are filling faster than they can be read:

1. **Stop replying individually.** Twenty individual replies read as panic.
2. **Write one holding statement** - what happened, what you are doing, when you will say more. Specific, dated, no adjectives. Pin it.
3. **Escalate to the human who can authorise a real answer** within the hour, with the numbers: reach, comment velocity, the top three accusations.
4. **Take the specific cases to DM** in parallel, so the individuals are not left in the crowd.
5. **Do not delete the original post.** Deleting the post under a pile-on is the single most damaging move available.
6. **Log the timeline as it happens.** Reach at 10:00, 12:00, 18:00. The post-mortem needs the curve, not the memory.

## Escalation that reaches a person

An escalation without context is a forward, not an escalation. Five fields, always:

```
What: one line - the issue and why it needs a human
Where: link, channel, timestamp
Who: handle, whether they are a customer (and the CRM record if you can see it)
Said so far: the exact text of anything the brand has already published
Decision needed: what you want the human to authorise, by when
```

## Who to escalate to

| Issue | Goes to | Speed |
|---|---|---|
| Refund, replacement, money | Whoever owns customer support, or the founder | Same day |
| Legal threat, notice, defamation | Founder, then a lawyer - never a drafted reply | Immediately, do not reply |
| Health, safety, injury | Founder plus the product owner | Immediately, do not reply |
| Data or privacy complaint | Whoever owns data protection - this has a statutory clock | Immediately |
| Staff complaint, ex-employee allegation | Founder or HR - never the community inbox | Same day |
| Media or journalist | `pr-writer` | Same day |
| Coordinated abuse or bot attack | Platform report plus the founder | Immediately |

## India-specific notes

- **The IT Rules, 2021** set timelines for acting on complaints about unlawful content on intermediaries, and require a grievance officer with published contact details. You do not need to quote the rules in a reply; you do need to know that "we will look into it" is not a compliance answer, and that the business should have a named grievance channel.
- **Screenshots of a deleted post are evidence in a defamation complaint** - for either side. Keep them, with timestamps.
- **A public accusation about a product causing harm** should not be answered with a denial written by a community manager. It goes up the chain first.
- **Regional-language abuse** is still abuse, and it is often missed by keyword filters written in English. Read it, do not rely on the filter.

## Anti-patterns

- Deleting a negative comment to "protect the brand" - the screenshot always exists.
- Replying to a legal threat with a polite denial.
- Blocking a customer mid-complaint; it guarantees the next post is about being blocked.
- Escalating with "see above" and no link.
- Moderating in a mood: yesterday's joke deleted, today's abuse tolerated.
- Answering a health claim with "our product is 100% safe" - a claim the business may not be able to defend.
- Treating a bot attack as customer anger, and replying to 200 bots individually.
