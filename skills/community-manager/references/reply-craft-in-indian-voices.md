# Reply craft in Indian voices

A community reply is 15 to 40 words. Almost all of its quality is decided by register, specificity and whether it ends with a way forward.

## The shape of a reply that works

1. **Answer, first word.** "Yes", "₹1,499", "We ship to Guwahati in 4 days". Not "Thanks for reaching out".
2. **One specific detail** that proves a human read it - the order, the city, the variant, the thing they mentioned.
3. **One way forward**, in one step. A link, a DM invitation, a name.

Three sentences is the ceiling. If it needs more, it belongs in a DM or a support ticket.

## Register ladder

Pick one rung per brand and stay on it. Switching rung mid-thread reads as a different person.

| Rung | Sounds like | Use for |
|---|---|---|
| Formal Hindi (आप) | "आपका ऑर्डर कल तक पहुँच जाएगा।" | B2B, older audiences, finance, health |
| Neutral Hinglish (aap) | "Aapka order kal tak pahunch jayega, tracking DM mein bhej raha hoon." | Most D2C and services in India - the default |
| Casual Hinglish (tum/bhai) | "Bhai order kal tak pahunch jayega, DM check karo." | Youth brands, gaming, food, fashion streetwear |
| English-only | "Your order lands tomorrow. Tracking is in your DM." | B2B SaaS, export buyers, LinkedIn |

Rules that hold on every rung:

- **Never mix script and romanisation inside one reply.** "आपका order" is not a register, it is a typo with extra steps.
- **Match the commenter's language, not their script.** A comment in romanised Hindi gets romanised Hindi back. Devanagari in, Devanagari out.
- **English loanwords are normal, not lazy.** "order", "tracking", "delivery", "refund", "offer" are how people actually speak. Translating them into pure Hindi makes the reply harder to read, not more Indian.
- **Names and honorifics.** "ji" is a courtesy, not a joke. Use it when the commenter did.

## Per-language notes

| Language | Watch for |
|---|---|
| Hindi | Formal/informal split is the whole game; आप vs तुम changes the brand. Numerals: use 1,2,3 - Devanagari digits read as bureaucratic. |
| Marathi | More formal than Hindi by default; "तुम्ही" is the safe address. Avoid Hindi slang - it is heard as not-from-here. |
| Tamil | Purist Tamil for product terms reads as a textbook. Code-mixing with English nouns is the norm in Chennai commerce. |
| Telugu | Register varies sharply by region; keep replies short and let the English nouns carry the specifics. |
| Bengali | Warmth is expected in the opening; a flat reply reads as rude rather than efficient. |
| Gujarati | Trade register - price and terms up front, pleasantries after. |
| Kannada | Formal address for services; avoid Hindi loanwords that Marathi would accept. |

## The specific replies

**Price question.** "₹1,499 for the 500ml, ₹2,499 for the 1L. Both in stock, ships in 2 days - link in bio." Not "DM for price" unless the price genuinely varies.

**Where is my order.** "Order #4471 left our warehouse Tuesday, Delhivery says out for delivery today. If it does not land by 7pm, DM me and I will chase it." Then actually chase it.

**Complaint, valid.** "That is not what should have happened. Send me the order number in DM and I will get a replacement out today." No "we apologise for the inconvenience".

**Complaint, unclear.** "Which order is this for? Send the number in DM and I will look." One question, not five.

**Complaint, unreasonable.** "I understand you are frustrated. We replaced the item on 12 October and again on 28 October - DM me and we will find a third option." Facts, no sarcasm.

**Public abuse.** No reply. See `moderation-and-escalation.md`.

**"Is this genuine?"** "Yes - GSTIN 27ABCDE1234F1Z5, registered address in Pune, and cash on delivery if you would rather not prepay." A verifiable fact beats reassurance every time.

## Anti-patterns

- "Thanks for reaching out! We appreciate your feedback." - says nothing, answers nothing.
- "Kindly DM" as the entire reply. It reads as a deflection and most people do not.
- Emoji confetti on a complaint.
- Correcting someone's spelling or grammar.
- "As per our policy" in public - it is a wall, not an answer.
- Replying in English to a Hindi comment because the brand's website is in English.
- Copying the same "we're sorry for the inconvenience" to six people in a row on the same thread.
