# Comment and DM triage

The whole job is the sort. A reply written before the sort is a guess about priority, and priority is what the audience is watching.

## The six buckets

Every inbound item goes in exactly one. If it fits two, the higher-risk bucket wins.

| Bucket | What it looks like | Channel | First response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Question | "price?", "does it come in blue?", "do you ship to Guwahati?" | Public reply | Same day, ideally within 2 hours |
| Complaint | "third time asking", "still not delivered", a photo of a broken item | Public reply, then DM | Within 2 hours |
| Buying signal | "interested", "DM me", "how do I order", "do you do bulk" | Public reply, then DM | Within 1 hour - this one has money in it |
| Praise | "love this", a tag, an emoji | Optional public reply | Within 48 hours, or a like is enough |
| Abuse or spam | Slurs, threats, scam links, "DM me to earn from home" | No reply - hide or remove | Immediately |
| Off-topic | Politics, unrelated arguments, competitor bait | No reply | Ignore, log if it repeats |

## Urgency is not risk

Two scores, not one. They decide different things: urgency decides *when*, risk decides *who*.

| | Low risk | High risk |
|---|---|---|
| **High urgency** | Price question on a launch post | Broken product, public, with photos |
| **Low urgency** | "Nice post" | A health claim, a legal threat, a safety issue |

High urgency, low risk: answer it yourself, now.
High risk, any urgency: it does not get a reply until a human has seen it. The public clock is the price of being careful.

## The SLA that is worth writing down

| Bucket | First touch | Closed |
|---|---|---|
| Buying signal | 1 hour (business hours) | Same day, handed to sales |
| Complaint | 2 hours | Within 24 hours, or a named owner and a date |
| Question | Same business day | Same day |
| Abuse or spam | Immediately | Immediately |
| Praise | 48 hours | Not tracked |

Publish these internally. An SLA nobody wrote down is a feeling, and feelings do not survive a launch week.

## The triage pass, in order

1. **Sort by bucket first, not by recency.** Recency ordering is how the loudest item gets answered and the refund gets missed.
2. **Pull the high-risk items out entirely.** They do not sit in the queue, they go to the escalation list in the same pass.
3. **Deduplicate.** Ten people asking "price?" is one answer and ten replies, or one answer pinned and a link. Count it as one decision.
4. **Check the person before the reply.** Customer, prospect, competitor, troll - the same sentence lands differently. If the CRM is not connected, say you cannot tell.
5. **Answer the batch, not the item.** A batch of 40 comments usually has 6 real questions in it. Write six answers and reuse them, without sending identical text to the same person twice.
6. **Log the counts.** Bucket totals per day are the only way to see a product problem forming before it is a crisis.

## Reading a comment for what it is

- **"How much?"** is a buying signal in India more often than a research question. It is also the most common thing left unanswered, because it feels like a price list will do.
- **"Worst company ever"** with no detail is usually a delivery problem. Ask one specific question in public, move to DM.
- **A long, detailed complaint** is a gift. It is the only free product research in the batch. Tag it for the product owner.
- **"Is this a scam?"** is a trust question, and it is answered with a verifiable fact, not reassurance: a GST number, a registered address, a delivery policy link.
- **Emoji-only and "🔥"** need nothing. Answering them inflates the reply count and tells the algorithm nothing useful.
- **A question in Hindi or Hinglish** deserves an answer in the same register. See `reply-craft-in-indian-voices.md`.
- **"DM sent" from an account with no history** is usually a scam harvesting your audience. Do not reply, do not click.

## Anti-patterns

- Answering the newest comment first because it is on screen.
- A queue sorted by likes - the popular complaint is not the urgent one.
- Replying to praise while a refund sits unanswered, because praise is pleasant.
- Treating a DM and a public comment as the same channel. One is private and can be specific; the other is a performance.
- Leaving a buying signal for "the sales team" with no name attached to the handoff.
- Answering a health, safety or legal claim because it looked like a normal question.
