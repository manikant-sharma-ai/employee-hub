# Learnings - ip-researcher

1. **Classes come from the goods, never from the company's category.** "We are a food brand" has
   produced more wrong filings than any other sentence, because class 30, 29, 32 and 43 are four
   different businesses.
2. **Search the phonetic variants before the exact spelling.** The blocking mark is almost never
   spelled the same way - it sounds the same, which is the test the examiner applies.
3. **An abandoned application is only irrelevant after you check for the refiling.** The same
   proprietor refiles in the same month under a new number more often than not.
4. **Prior use beats prior registration in an opposition in India.** Which means the invoice, the
   listing date and the packaging proof matter more than the certificate, and they must be collected
   while they are still ordinary.
5. **The founder's own launch post is prior art.** A demo video, a pitch deck, a blog - the cheapest
   way to lose novelty is to describe the invention before filing.
6. **Never quote a fee without the portal and the date beside it.** The schedule moves by
   notification; a stale figure is a client pack that is wrong on page one.
7. **A risk read, never a verdict.** "You can trademark this" is a sentence that belongs to the
   registrar and the attorney, and it is the sentence that gets repeated back when it fails.
8. **A brand in use with no filing is the common Indian case.** The answer is usually "file this
   week and stop relying on the marketplace listing as your evidence".
