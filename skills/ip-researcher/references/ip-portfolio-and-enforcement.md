# Portfolio, renewal and enforcement

An IP right is a diary entry with a legal consequence. This file is the diary and the response plan.

## The portfolio register, one row per asset

| Field | Why it is there |
|---|---|
| Asset | Mark, design, patent, copyright work, domain |
| Type and number | Application or registration number |
| Class or classification | The scope, in one glance |
| Applicant and owner | The entity that can enforce it |
| Filed and registered dates | The clock |
| Status | Pending, registered, opposed, lapsed, abandoned |
| Next action and date | Renewal, response, affidavit of use |
| Proof of use | Invoices, ads, packaging, dated |
| Document of ownership | Assignment, employment contract, invoice with IP clause |

If a founder cannot produce this table, the portfolio is not a portfolio; it is a set of emails.

## The annual clock

| Event | Frequency | Consequence of missing it |
|---|---|---|
| Trademark renewal | Every 10 years, 6-month grace | Removal from the register; someone else files the name |
| Design renewal | 10 years, then 5 | The look becomes free to copy |
| Patent renewal | Annually from year 3 | The patent lapses; the invention is public |
| Working statement (Form 27) | Annually for granted patents | Penalty, and a bad look in diligence |
| Madrid renewal | Every 10 years, via WIPO | Same as national |
| Watch report | Monthly | A conflicting mark registers inside the opposition window |
| Affidavit of use | On demand, or before removal proceedings | The mark is removed for non-use |

Put every one of these in the calendar with a 90-day advance event. A renewal is never urgent until it is
impossible.

## The watch routine, thirty minutes a month

1. Search your own marks' classes for applications published in the last month.
2. Search the phonetic and structural variants of your marks.
3. Search your own trade channels - marketplaces, app stores, Google - for actual use.
4. Search the company registry for same-name entities in your state.
5. Log every hit with the date, the number and the decision: oppose, monitor, ignore.
6. If a hit is in the opposition window, the decision is made now, with a date, not next month.

## When someone copies you

| Situation | The evidence to assemble first | The action |
|---|---|---|
| A marketplace seller uses your photos and copy | Listing URLs with timestamps, your original files, your invoices | Platform takedown, then a notice |
| A seller uses your mark on similar goods | Your registration, their listing, a test purchase | Notice through the advocate, platform complaint |
| A competitor copies the product's look | Your design registration, dated photos of both | Design infringement notice |
| A competitor copies the mechanism | Your patent, a claim chart | Advocate's opinion first, always |
| An ex-employee or agency uses your material | The contract, the assignment, the delivery record | Assignment demand, then a notice |
| A blog or an ad uses your name | Screenshots, dates, your registration | Notice, then a platform complaint |

The sequence is always evidence, then the advocate, then the notice. A notice sent before the evidence is
collected is a notice that gets answered with a counter-claim.

## The takedown routes that work in India

| Route | Speed | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Marketplace IP complaint (Amazon, Flipkart, Meesho) | Days | Registration certificate or a pending application, in most programmes |
| Google/ Meta IP forms for ads and listings | Days | Registration, screenshots |
| Customs recordal with Indian Customs | Proactive | Registration; stops imports at the border |
| Intermediary notice under the IT Rules | Weeks | A court order or a government notice, in most cases |
| Civil suit for infringement or passing off | Months | An advocate, evidence, and money |
| Criminal complaint for falsification | Rare in practice | Strong evidence, a willing police station |

## Assignment, licensing and the paperwork nobody did

| Document | What it does | The common gap |
|---|---|---|
| IP assignment | Moves ownership from person to company | Missing for anything a freelancer or a co-founder made |
| Employment IP clause | Makes work-for-hire the default | Absent in the offer letter, or silent on IP |
| NDA | Protects the disclosure | Signed, then never enforced |
| Licence | Grants use without ownership | Oral, unpriced, unlimited |
| Founder IP transfer | Moves a personally-owned mark into the company | Done late, at diligence, at a price |

The order that matters: an assignment is signed **before** the application, because the applicant is the
owner of record and changing it later needs a fresh form and a reason.

## Anti-patterns

- **Enforcing before registering.** A passing-off claim on an unregistered mark is possible and expensive;
  the registration is the cheap part.
- **A notice sent in anger.** It is a document that will be read in court. Every sentence is a commitment.
- **Letting a renewal lapse to save a fee.** Re-registration costs more than the renewal and the name may be
  gone by then.
- **A watch that only runs when someone complains.** By then the mark has registered and the remedy is
  cancellation, which takes years.
- **Treating a marketplace takedown as the end.** The same seller relists in a week under a new ID; the
  registration is what makes the second complaint fast.
