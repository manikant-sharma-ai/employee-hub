# Trademark search and classes

A knockout search answers one question: what will the examiner find, and what will the market
find. Two searches, two log columns, one brief.

## The three passes

| Pass | Query | What it catches | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Identical | the exact word, all classes first, then the chosen classes | the obvious block | IP India wordmark search |
| 2. Similar | phonetic and structural variants, prefix and root searches, the logo's verbal description | the real block - marks that sound the same | IP India + WIPO Global Brand Database |
| 3. Proprietor | every mark held by the two or three nearest competitors | the portfolio, and the pattern in it | IP India proprietor search |

Run pass 1 wide (all 45 classes) before pass 2 narrow. A mark identical in class 29 when you sell
class 30 is not a block but it is a signal, and the signal is free.

## Building the variant list

Write the variants before you search, or you will search the variant you already thought of.

- **Spelling** - the mark as printed, the mark as typed by customers, and the mark as typed by a
  phone autocorrect.
- **Phonetic** - how it is said aloud. "Veda", "Vaida", "Wayda" are one mark to an examiner.
- **Transliteration** - Devanagari, Tamil or Bengali script renderings of a Latin mark, and back.
- **Root and prefix** - search the stem alone. "Nutri" returns the whole shelf.
- **Logo** - the verbal description ("a leaf inside a circle"), since image search is weak.

## Reading a hit

| Field | Where it comes from | Why it decides |
|---|---|---|
| Application number | register | The row identity; quote it, never paraphrase |
| Class(es) | register | Same class is a conflict; adjacent class is a risk |
| Status | register - Proposed, Objected, Opposed, Registered, Abandoned, Removed | Abandoned and removed marks stop mattering; proposed marks matter |
| Proprietor | register | A competitor's mark is a strategic signal, a dead shell company's is not |
| Goods and services | register | The actual overlap test, and the field most often skipped |
| Filing date | register | Earlier filing wins; earlier *use* can beat it |
| Date read | your log | A register changes; a brief without this date is unfalsifiable |

Score each hit **high / medium / low** with one line of reason. High means same or near-identical
mark on same or overlapping goods. Medium means similar mark or adjacent goods. Low means the same
word in a class nobody will confuse. Never publish a hit list without the scores.

## Classes come from the goods

Write what is sold in plain words first, then map. The mapping is judgement, not lookup.

| Plain words | Likely class | Trap |
|---|---|---|
| Packaged snacks, namkeen, biscuits | 30 | Spice blends and seasonings are also 30; fresh fruit is 31 |
| Ready-to-drink juice, mineral water | 32 | Fruit drinks with milk are 29 |
| Spice mixes sold as a service of cooking | 43 | The service and the product are different filings |
| Software as a download | 9 | The same software sold as a service is 42 |
| Software as a subscription service | 42 | Most SaaS needs 9 and 42 together |
| Retail store, e-commerce marketplace | 35 | The strongest class in India for D2C - do not skip it |
| Clothing | 25 | Retail of clothing is 35, not 25 |
| Restaurant | 43 | Packaged food sold by the same brand is 29 or 30 |
| Consulting, advertising, business services | 35 | Different from 41, which is education and entertainment |
| Training, workshops, content | 41 | Online training is 41; the platform is 42 |
| Financial services, lending, insurance | 36 | Fintech apps are 9, 36 and 42 |
| Beauty salon | 44 | Cosmetics are 3 - a salon brand usually needs both |
| Logistics, delivery, warehousing | 39 | The software that runs it is 42 |

The related-class pairs that decide most Indian filings: 9 and 42, 35 and 42, 25 and 35, 29 and 30,
29 and 43, 5 and 44, 3 and 44, 16 and 41, 36 and 45, 41 and 42, 12 and 37. If two of a pair are
filed, expect a cross-citation in an opposition even when the examiner does not raise it.

## The section 11 test, in the examiner's order

1. Is the mark identical or similar? (sound, look, meaning)
2. Are the goods or services identical or similar?
3. Is there a likelihood of confusion, including association?
4. Is the earlier mark earlier - by filing, or by use?

Section 9 sits beside it: is the mark distinctive, or descriptive of the goods, or generic, or
customary in the trade? A descriptive mark can be filed and will be objected to; say so up front
rather than discovering it at examination.

## Anti-patterns

- **Searching only the class you believe you are in.** The blocking mark is usually one class over.
- **Searching only the exact spelling.** The examiner searches phonetically; so must you.
- **Reporting "no identical marks found" as clearance.** Absence of an identical hit is the start of
  the read, not the end.
- **Treating "Abandoned" as irrelevant without checking the refiling.** Many abandoned applications
  are refiled the same month under a new number.
- **Quoting a class heading from memory.** Headings are long and the words in them are the words an
  examiner uses against you.
- **Ignoring the marketplace.** Amazon Brand Registry, Flipkart and IndiaMART brand names are prior
  use evidence whether or not anyone filed.
