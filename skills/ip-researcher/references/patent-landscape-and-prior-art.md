# Patent landscape and prior art

A patent search is not a name search. You are looking for the *mechanism*, published anywhere, by
anyone, before a date. The product's name is irrelevant to the search and relevant only to the
report.

## What the founder actually needs to know

| Question | The answer that helps | The answer that does not |
|---|---|---|
| Is this already patented? | "Four live Indian applications on this mechanism, two granted, one expiring 2029" | "There are some patents in this area" |
| Can I sell it? | Freedom-to-operate read: which live claims cover what you will ship | "You should consult a lawyer" |
| Can I protect it? | Novelty read: what is closest, what your claim would have to say to be new | "It seems novel" |
| Is it worth the money? | The commercial window - years of exclusivity against the cost and the design-around risk | "Patents are valuable" |

## The four searches, in this order

1. **Problem search.** The problem in the customer's words, in patent language: "reduces foam in
   washing", "locks a hinge without a screw". Classify with CPC/IPC after the first pass, not before.
2. **Mechanism search.** How it works: the geometry, the material, the sequence. This is where the
   closest art lives, and where founders never look.
3. **Applicant search.** The three competitors and the two research institutes in the space, whole
   portfolios, including their abandoned filings - abandoned art still destroys novelty.
4. **Citation crawl.** Take the two closest hits and read what they cite and what cites them. Two
   hops is usually the whole landscape.

## Where to search

| Database | Best for | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| InPASS (Indian Patent Advanced Search System) | Indian filings, status, examination | Clunky query syntax; export is manual |
| Espacenet | Worldwide, classification search, family view | Publication lag of 18 months |
| WIPO Patentscope | PCT applications, international families | Same lag |
| Google Patents | Fast full-text, good for a first read, citation graph | Ranking is opaque; never cite it as a search record |
| The applicant's own website and brochures | Unfiled public disclosure - the killer of novelty | Nobody logs this, and it decides cases |

## Novelty, in one table

| Situation | Effect on an Indian filing |
|---|---|
| Described in a published patent anywhere, before the priority date | Novelty destroyed |
| Sold or offered for sale in India before filing | Novelty destroyed |
| Described in the founder's own blog, pitch deck or launch post | Novelty destroyed by the founder's own act |
| Shown at a trade fair | Grace period exists but is narrow - do not rely on it |
| Published after the priority date | Not prior art |

The 18-month publication lag means the closest art may be invisible. Say so in every landscape:
"applications filed in the last 18 months are not yet published, so this read is a floor, not a
ceiling."

## Freedom to operate versus patentability

Two different questions and founders conflate them constantly.

- **Patentability** - is there anything new here that I could claim? A crowded field can still have a
  narrow claimable improvement.
- **Freedom to operate** - can I make and sell this without infringing someone's live claim? A thing
  can be entirely novel and still infringe, because the claim covers the way it is built.

An FTO read maps each live claim element against the product's actual elements. If every element of
a claim is present, there is a risk; if one element is missing, the claim does not read on the
product. That element-by-element table is the deliverable - never a paragraph saying "there may be
some risk".

## Reading a claim without a law degree

- The claim is one sentence. The preamble ("A device for...") is context; the elements after it are
  the fence.
- "Comprising" is open - extra features do not escape it. "Consisting of" is closed.
- Dependent claims narrow the parent. Infringing the parent is the risk; infringing only a dependent
  claim is a smaller risk.
- Check the legal status and the fee/annuity history. A lapsed patent in the target market is not a
  risk, and half the "blocked" landscapes dissolve at this step.

## Cost and timeline reality

| Route | Typical official cost | Typical professional cost | Time to grant |
|---|---|---|---|
| Indian provisional then complete | Low official fee, two filings | The larger cost; drafting is the work | 3-5 years, expedited faster |
| PCT then national phase | Higher, multiplied by countries | Scales with countries | 4-6 years total |
| Design registration (shape only) | Low | Low | Months, not years |

Quotes are ranges on purpose. Get the actual fee from the patent office page on the day and write
the date beside it.

## Anti-patterns

- **Searching by product name.** Nobody patents a brand name; they patent a mechanism.
- **One database and done.** InPASS alone misses the PCT family that will national-phase into India.
- **Ignoring the founder's own disclosure.** The launch post, the YouTube demo, the investor deck -
  the most common self-inflicted novelty loss in small business.
- **Presenting a landscape as an FTO opinion.** A search finds published documents; only a claim
  chart answers "can I sell it".
- **Recommending "patent it" without the commercial window.** A twenty-year right on a product with
  an eighteen-month market life is a filing fee, not an asset.
