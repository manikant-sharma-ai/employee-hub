# Patent and design landscape

Most small businesses do not need a patent. Some need a freedom-to-operate answer, and all of them need to
know which of the two they are asking for. This file is the difference.

## Which right covers which thing

| What you built | The right | What it protects | Term (India) |
|---|---|---|---|
| A mechanism, a process, a formulation, a technical effect | Patent | The way it works | 20 years from filing |
| The look of a product - shape, configuration, surface, ornament | Design registration | What it looks like, not how it works | 10 years, renewable 5 |
| A brand name, logo, tagline | Trademark | Source identity | 10 years, renewable forever |
| Code, content, drawings, photographs | Copyright | The expression | Life + 60 years |
| A recipe, a customer list, a method kept off the register | Trade secret | Nothing, but nobody knows it | As long as it stays secret |

A product can carry three of these at once and usually needs two: a design registration for the look, a
trademark for the name. The patent is the expensive one and the one that is most often unnecessary.

## Patent search, in the order that works

| Step | Do this | Where |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Write the invention as a problem and a solution in three sentences, with the technical effect | On paper, first |
| 2 | Break it into features; the feature that is new is the only one that matters | On paper |
| 3 | Keyword search in title, abstract and claims - the problem words and the effect words | Google Patents, Espacenet, IP India |
| 4 | Classification search - IPC/CPC codes near the technology | Espacenet classification browser |
| 5 | Applicant search - the three companies who would own this space | Espacenet, Google Patents |
| 6 | Citation chase - forward and backward citations on the two closest hits | Google Patents |
| 7 | Read the claims of the five closest, not the abstracts | Full text |

The claim is the boundary. An abstract tells you what a patent is about; the claim tells you what it stops
you doing. A landscape written from abstracts is a landscape of the wrong question.

## The claim read, in plain language

For each close hit, write four lines:

1. **The independent claim, paraphrased.** What must be present for the claim to bite.
2. **The element you do not have.** A claim is defeated by one missing element - find it or admit you do not.
3. **The prosecution history.** Was the claim narrowed during examination? A narrowed claim is a weaker claim.
4. **Status and geography.** Granted, pending, lapsed, or never filed in India. A US patent with no Indian
   family does not stop an Indian manufacturer.

## Provisional or complete

| | Provisional | Complete |
|---|---|---|
| What it needs | A description good enough to support the claims later | Full description, claims, abstract, drawings |
| What it buys | A priority date, twelve months to file the complete | The actual application, examined on its merits |
| Cost shape | Small; often a tenth of the complete | Larger, plus examination and response costs |
| The risk | If the provisional does not support the later claim, the priority date is lost | None beyond cost |

The rule: file the provisional when a launch or a conversation is about to disclose the invention publicly
and the claims are not settled. Never file a provisional that is a paragraph long - it will not support the
claim you need twelve months later, and the date it bought is worthless.

## What is not patentable in India, and trips founders up

- A business method, an algorithm or software *per se* - unless it produces a technical effect or a hardware
  coupling. The claim must be drafted around the effect, not the code.
- A mere discovery of a new form of a known substance without enhanced efficacy.
- Anything contrary to public order or morality, and plants and animals other than micro-organisms.
- A traditional knowledge claim. India rejects these, and an examiner will cite the text that anticipates it.

## Design registration, the cheap one

| Requirement | The detail |
|---|---|
| Novelty | Not published in India or elsewhere before the filing date |
| Applied to an article | A design in the abstract is not registrable; it must be applied |
| Judged by the eye | Shape, configuration, pattern, ornament - not function |
| Not a trademark | A mark on a bottle is not the bottle's design |
| Locarno class | The classification for the article |
| Views required | Front, back, top, bottom, sides, perspective - consistent, plain background |

A design registration is the fastest and cheapest way to stop a copy of a physical product in India, and it
is the one most often skipped because the founder is thinking about patents.

## Freedom to operate, in one page

An FTO is not a patentability search. Patentability asks "is this new"; FTO asks "will selling this infringe
someone else's live claim in the markets I sell in".

1. List the countries where the product is made, sold or shipped.
2. Search granted, in-force patents in those countries, in the product's classification.
3. Read the claims of the closest, and map each element of the claim against the product.
4. A claim with one element the product lacks is a claim you do not infringe - write that element down.
5. Where every element is present, the answer is design around, license, or do not launch. Say which.

## Anti-patterns

- **"We should patent it" with no search.** A paid filing on something already published is the most
  expensive way to learn about the prior art.
- **Reading abstracts only.** The claim is the invention; the abstract is marketing.
- **Publishing first, filing second.** A blog post, a trade show demo or a marketplace listing is
  disclosure, and most of the world's patent offices treat it as fatal to novelty.
- **Confusing the design with the trademark.** The bottle's shape and the label's name are two filings.
- **Ignoring the foreign family.** A patent that was never filed in India does not constrain an Indian
  seller; check the family before you write "blocked".
- **A landscape with forty hits and no read.** Five claims read properly beats forty abstracts summarised.
