# Filing route and costs, India and abroad

Every number here is a *shape*, not a quote. The fee moves by notification, by applicant category
and by the number of classes. Read it on the portal the day you write it down, and put the date in
the same sentence.

## The Indian route, step by step

| Step | What happens | Typical time | What can go wrong |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Search | Knockout on the mark and the classes | 1-3 days | Done on the wrong class |
| 2. File TM-A | Application per class, with the mark and the goods | Same day, e-filing | Wrong applicant name; wrong class wording |
| 3. Receipt and number | Application number issued | Immediate to a week | — |
| 4. Examination report | Registrar raises objections under section 9 or 11 | 3-18 months | Objection needs a reply within 30 days |
| 5. Reply and hearing | Written reply, sometimes a hearing | 1-12 months | Missed deadline = deemed abandoned |
| 6. Advertisement | Published in the Trademark Journal | After acceptance | Third parties get 4 months to oppose |
| 7. Opposition, if any | Evidence, counter-statement, hearing | 1-3 years | The expensive branch |
| 8. Registration | Certificate issued | 6-24 months total in practice | — |
| 9. Renewal | Every 10 years | — | A lapsed mark is a mark someone else can take |

Use of the ™ symbol is available from filing. The ® symbol is lawful only after registration, and
using it before is an offence under section 107 - a small thing that appears in every compliance
review.

## Applicant categories and the fee

| Applicant | Official fee shape | Proof needed |
|---|---|---|
| Individual / sole proprietor | Lowest | None |
| Startup (DPIIT recognised) | Same as individual | DPIIT certificate number |
| Small enterprise (Udyam) | Same as individual | Udyam registration number |
| Others - company, LLP, partnership | Roughly double the individual rate | None |

The fee is **per class**, not per application. Three classes is three fees, and that is the number
founders get wrong when they budget. Government fees are also payable per form: the application, the
renewal, and any request for an expedited examination are separate.

Never bake a rupee figure into a document without the date. Instead write:

> Official fee: ₹<amount> per class for a <category> applicant, read on <portal> on <dd/mm/yyyy>.
> Confirm before payment - the schedule changes by notification.

## Madrid: when it is worth it

| Situation | Route | Why |
|---|---|---|
| India only, for now | Indian application | Cheapest, fastest, no coordination cost |
| 2-3 export markets, same mark | Madrid Protocol, India as office of origin | One application, one currency, one renewal date |
| A single market with a local blocker | National filing there, with local counsel | Madrid gives you the same objection, later |
| Gulf, ASEAN, Africa | National or regional, case by case | Madrid coverage is patchy in some of these |

Madrid costs are in Swiss francs, per designated country, plus a handling fee, plus the Indian
office's transmission fee. The mark must first be filed or registered in India, and the Madrid
registration stays dependent on the Indian mark for five years - if the Indian mark falls in that
window, the international one falls with it.

## The cost structure of a filing

| Cost line | Nature | Rough shape |
|---|---|---|
| Official fee | Per class, per form | Fixed, published, changes |
| Search and advice | Per mark, per territory | The line that saves the most money |
| Drafting the goods and services | Per class | Cheap done well, expensive done badly |
| Examination reply | Per objection | Ranges widely; a section 11 reply is real work |
| Opposition defence | Per proceeding | The unpredictable line - budget for one in ten |
| Renewal at 10 years | Per class | Same as the filing fee, ten years later |
| Watching and enforcement | Annual | Optional, and the one most SMBs skip |

Budget the filing plus one examination reply. Budget nothing for an opposition and know what one
would cost before it arrives.

## Design and copyright, in one paragraph each

- **Design registration** protects the *shape, configuration, pattern or ornament* of an article, not
  how it works, and only if it is new and original. It is cheap, fast, and the right tool for a
  product's look when a patent is out of reach.
- **Copyright** exists on the logo artwork, the label copy, the photographs and the code from the
  moment they are created. Registration is optional in India but is the cheapest evidence you will
  ever buy. Get the assignment in writing from the freelancer who drew the logo - the default
  position, without an assignment, is that the designer owns it.

## Anti-patterns

- **Quoting a fee from a blog post.** Every blog is a year out of date somewhere that matters.
- **Filing in one class and launching in four.** The cheapest filing is the one that covers what you
  will actually sell.
- **Filing a descriptive mark because the name is loved.** Section 9 will object and the reply will
  cost more than the naming exercise would have.
- **Letting the attorney choose the classes without the goods list.** They will choose the classes
  you named, and you named one.
- **Skipping the assignment from the designer.** The logo belongs to whoever drew it until a signed
  assignment says otherwise.
