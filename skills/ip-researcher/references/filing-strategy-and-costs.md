# Filing strategy and costs

A filing is a project with a date, a fee, an owner and a clock. This file is the project plan.

## The Indian trademark route, step by step

| Step | What happens | Who does it | Clock |
|---|---|---|---|
| Search and clearance | This role | You | 1-3 days |
| TM-A filing, one class | Form, mark, goods, applicant, use date, PoA | Agent or applicant | Same day |
| Application number issued | You can use ™ from here | Portal | 1-3 days |
| Examination report | Registrar objects or accepts | Registrar | 1-3 months, longer in practice |
| Response to objection | Written reply, evidence of use, hearing | Agent + advocate | 30 days from the report |
| Journal publication | Opens the opposition window | Registrar | After acceptance |
| Opposition window | Any third party may oppose | Third party | 4 months from publication |
| Registration certificate | ® may be used | Registrar | On completion |
| Renewal | Every 10 years, indefinitely | Owner | 6-month grace |

The two facts founders get wrong: ™ needs no registration and may be used from the day of filing, while ®
may be used only after registration and using it early is an offence. And the opposition window is four
months from journal publication - miss the watch, and the mark registers.

## Official fees, and why the applicant type matters

Fees for trademarks in India are set by schedule and change by notification. The structure to know:

| Applicant | Relative fee | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Individual, sole proprietor | Lowest | Natural person |
| Small enterprise / startup (Udyam / DPIIT recognised) | Concessional | Certificate required, attached at filing |
| Others - company, LLP, partnership | Highest | Full fee |

Two consequences. First, a startup that files before its Udyam registration pays several times the fee for
the same mark. Second, the concessional rate applies per application, so a five-class filing is five fees -
which is why the class choice is a cost decision, not a completeness decision. Quote the current schedule
with the date it was read and link the fee page; never quote a remembered number.

## Patent route in India

| Route | What it is | When to use it |
|---|---|---|
| Provisional then complete | Priority date now, claims within 12 months | Launch or disclosure is imminent, claims unsettled |
| Complete direct | Full application in one go | Claims are settled, no disclosure pressure |
| PCT international | One application, many countries, 30 months to enter national phase | Export or licensing is realistic |
| Paris Convention | 12 months to file abroad claiming Indian priority | One or two foreign markets only |
| Convention route via a foreign filing licence | Required for Indian residents filing abroad first | Always check before filing abroad |

Statutory shape: request for examination must be filed within the prescribed window or the application is
treated as withdrawn; publication happens at 18 months unless early publication is requested and paid for;
renewal is due from the third year after filing and annually thereafter. Every one of these is a dated event
that goes into the calendar the day the application is filed.

## The cost table the founder actually wants

Build it as a range, not a number, and separate official from professional:

| Line | What to quote | What to say about it |
|---|---|---|
| Official fee | By applicant type, dated | Exact, from the schedule |
| Agent or attorney fee | A range per class or per application | Depends on the firm and the objections |
| Search fee | Often bundled | Say if it is |
| Objection and hearing cost | A range, contingent | The number founders forget |
| Translation, notarisation, apostille | Per document, if filing abroad | Only for Madrid or PCT |
| Renewal | Per 10 years, per class | The lifetime cost, not the first cost |
| Watch service | Monthly or annual | Cheaper than one opposition |

The honest framing: the first fee is the smallest number in the life of the mark. A ten-year renewal on
five classes, with one objection fought, costs several times the filing.

## Filing order when there is more than one asset

1. **The name**, in the classes actually traded, before the packaging or the listing goes live.
2. **The logo as a separate device mark**, if it will be used alone. A combined mark protects the combination
   and weakly protects each part.
3. **The design registration**, before the first public sale or catalogue.
4. **The provisional patent**, before any demo, blog post or trade show.
5. **The foreign filings**, inside the priority window, in the markets where the product will actually sell.

## Anti-patterns

- **Filing after the print run.** The packaging is the disclosure, and the packaging is the first thing a
  competitor photographs.
- **Filing a word mark and using a stylised version.** File what you use, or file both.
- **One class "to save money", in the class the website describes** rather than the class the invoice is
  raised in.
- **Missing the examination report.** It arrives by email, it has a 30-day reply window, and a missed reply
  is an abandoned application with a live fee.
- **Filing abroad without a foreign filing licence.** For an Indian resident, the first filing abroad has a
  statutory step; skipping it is a defect that cannot be fixed later.
- **No renewal calendar.** A lapsed mark that the business has used for nine years is the worst of both
  worlds - reputation without the right.
