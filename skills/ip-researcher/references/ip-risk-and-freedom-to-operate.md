# IP risk and freedom to operate

Two situations arrive here: a notice has landed, or the business is about to do something that could
produce one. Both are handled the same way - facts first, tone second, decision third.

## When a notice arrives

The first hour decides the cost. In order:

1. **Read the whole document, including the annexures.** Who is the sender, what right do they claim,
   what exactly do they demand, and by when.
2. **Establish the deadline.** A cease-and-desist usually sets 7 or 15 days. A court summons has a
   date that is not negotiable. Diarise it before anything else.
3. **Do not reply yet.** Not to acknowledge, not to deny, not on WhatsApp. A casual reply is an
   admission or a waiver in the wrong hands.
4. **Check the claimed right is real.** Search the registration number. Is it registered, in which
   classes, is it live, does it cover the goods you sell, and is it the sender's own mark or an
   assigned one.
5. **Assemble the facts** - when the business started using the mark, in what territory, on what
   turnover, with what evidence (invoices, listings, ads, packaging dates). Prior use is the defence
   that matters most in India.
6. **Call the attorney with the file, not with the story.** A brief with the numbers is a
   15-minute call; a brief without them is a week of billed discovery.
7. **Stop only what you must.** Pausing a live campaign may be prudent; dismantling packaging on the
   strength of a letter is a decision for the founder with advice.

## The risk read, in one table

| Finding | Risk | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Identical mark, identical class, live registration, earlier date | High | Rename or license; continuing is a deliberate bet |
| Similar mark, same class, earlier date, active proprietor | Medium-high | The overlap test decides; get the attorney's read |
| Similar mark, adjacent class, no confusion | Low-medium | Defensible, and worth documenting the reasoning |
| Registered but abandoned or removed | Low | Check for refiling before concluding |
| Unregistered but visibly in use earlier | Medium | Prior use is a real defence and a real threat |
| No filing anywhere, but the business has used it for years | Low from the register, medium from the market | File now; you are building the evidence late |

## Freedom to operate, element by element

For a product, the FTO table is the deliverable:

| Claim element | Your product | Present? | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| A housing with an inlet and an outlet | Same housing, same arrangement | Yes | |
| A filter cartridge removable without tools | Twist-lock, no tools | Yes | |
| A pressure sensor upstream of the cartridge | Sensor is downstream | No | This element breaks the claim |
| A controller that shuts the inlet on a pressure drop | Same behaviour | Yes | |

If one element is absent, the claim does not read on the product - that is the finding, and it is a
finding only a claim chart produces. Give the chart to the attorney and let them confirm it.

## Marketplace and platform takedowns

Amazon, Flipkart and IndiaMART all run brand-registry complaint processes. They are fast, they are
administrative, and they are wrong often enough to matter.

| Stage | What to do |
|---|---|
| Listing suspended | Screenshot the notice, note the complaint number and the claimed right |
| Evidence pack | Registration certificate, first-use invoices, packaging photographs, brand authorisation |
| Response | Filed by the brand owner through the registry, factual and dated, no argument |
| If you are the complainant | Register the brand first; an unregistered mark makes the complaint weak |

A takedown is not a legal finding. It is a platform applying a policy on the papers in front of it.

## Domain, handle and marketplace squatting

Cheap to check, expensive to fix. Before a name is chosen, check in this order: the .in and .com
domains, the marketplace brand names, the Instagram and LinkedIn handles, and the company name at
MCA. If one is taken by an unrelated business, note it; if it is taken by a competitor in the same
class, that is a risk finding, not a nuisance.

## Anti-patterns

- **Replying to a notice to be polite.** Politeness is what the first paragraph of a settlement is
  made of.
- **Assuming the sender's registration covers you.** Check the classes; half of these letters claim
  a right that does not reach the goods.
- **Destroying the evidence of your own first use.** Invoices, courier receipts and ad invoices are
  the defence; keep them in the Drive folder from day one.
- **Presenting a search as an FTO opinion.** A search finds documents; a claim chart answers the
  question.
- **Advising the founder to "just change the logo a bit".** A confusingly similar mark with a new
  font is the same problem with a new invoice.
