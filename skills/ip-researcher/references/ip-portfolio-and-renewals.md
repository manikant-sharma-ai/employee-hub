# Portfolio and renewals

A registered right is a subscription, not a purchase. This file is the calendar that keeps it alive
and the review that decides what to stop paying for.

## The register, as a sheet

One row per right. Columns that earn their place:

| Column | Why |
|---|---|
| Right type | Trade mark, patent, design, copyright |
| Mark or title | Exactly as filed |
| Number | Application or registration |
| Classes / claims | Trade mark classes, patent claim count |
| Jurisdiction | India, US, EU, UAE |
| Filed / registered date | Filing date drives priority; registration date drives renewal |
| Status | Proposed, objected, advertised, registered, opposed, lapsed |
| Next action | The single next thing, with a date |
| Next deadline | The date that costs money if missed |
| Agent | Who files and who gets the reminder |
| Annual cost | Fee, annuity, watch service |
| Commercial value | High, medium, none - reviewed yearly |

## The deadline calendar

| Right | Deadline | Consequence of missing |
|---|---|---|
| Trade mark application | Examination reply, 30 days from the report | Application deemed abandoned |
| Trade mark | Opposition notice, 2 months from advertisement | Registration proceeds without you |
| Trade mark renewal | Every 10 years, with a 6-month grace period on payment of a surcharge | Mark removed; someone else may file it |
| Patent | Request for examination within the prescribed period | Application lapses without examination |
| Patent | Annuities, escalating from year 3 | Patent ceases; restoration is expensive and not guaranteed |
| PCT | 30/31 months from priority for national phase | The international application dies |
| Design | Renewal at 10 years, extendable to 15 | Protection ends |
| Copyright assignment | Not a deadline - a document | Ownership sits with the freelancer |

Diary these 60 days ahead, in the calendar, with the agent cc'd. A renewal that depends on a
memory is a renewal that will be missed in the year the founder is busiest.

## The yearly portfolio review

Four questions per right, in order. Answer all four or the review is decoration.

1. **Is it still used?** An unused registered mark is vulnerable to non-use removal after five
   years, and it costs money every decade to keep.
2. **Does it still cover what the business sells?** A mark registered in class 35 while the business
   now sells its own product is a mark that does not cover the business.
3. **Is the territory still right?** Export markets entered, markets exited.
4. **Is it worth the renewal fee?** Compare renewal cost against the cost of refiling if it lapses -
   and against the risk that a competitor files in the gap.

| Verdict | Action |
|---|---|
| Core to the brand | Renew, and widen classes to cover what is sold now |
| Used, defensive | Renew if cheap; let it lapse if the class is dead |
| Unused, no plan | Let it lapse deliberately, and write the decision down |
| Blocked abroad | Decide: local filing, licence, or a different mark in that market |

## Watching

Optional and underrated. A watch service or a weekly manual check of the Trademark Journal catches:

- a competitor filing your mark or a near variant,
- a former distributor filing your brand in their own name,
- a foreign brand entering India under a name you already use.

Catching it at advertisement gives you an opposition window; catching it after registration gives
you a rectification petition, which costs more and takes longer.

## The file that should exist

Per right, in the Drive folder:

- the application receipt and the registration certificate,
- the examination report and the reply,
- the assignment or licence deed, if any,
- the first-use evidence: an invoice, a dated listing, a packaging proof,
- the agent's engagement letter and fee schedule.

If the business changes attorneys, this folder is what makes the handover an afternoon rather than a
month.

## Anti-patterns

- **A renewal reminder that lives in one person's inbox.** It leaves with the person.
- **Renewing everything out of anxiety.** A portfolio of unused marks is a subscription to nothing.
- **Letting a mark lapse and refiling later.** The gap is an invitation, and the new filing date is
  the one that gets cited against you.
- **No first-use evidence.** The certificate proves registration; the invoice proves the brand is a
  business.
- **Filing abroad "for later" with no market plan.** Foreign filings are the most expensive way to
  feel prepared.
