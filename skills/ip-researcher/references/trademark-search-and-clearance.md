# Trademark search and clearance

A clearance is a search plus a read. Most founders do the search badly and skip the read.

## The registers, in the order you search them

| Register | What it covers | Why you use it | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| IP India public search (`tmrsearch.ipindia.gov.in`) | Indian applications and registrations, all classes | The only register that decides an Indian objection | Status lags by days; some records incomplete |
| WIPO Global Brand Database | International registrations, Madrid designating India | Catches a Madrid application naming India before the national record updates | Not every national filing |
| TMview | Aggregated national registers, 70+ offices | Export markets and cross-checking | Aggregation lag, differing status vocabularies |
| Company and MCA name search | Registered company and LLP names | A company name is not a trademark, but an identical one signals a claimant | Says nothing about goods |
| Domain and marketplace search | Use in trade | Common-law use rights appear on no register | Weak evidence, strong signal |
| Google, app stores, IndiaMART, Amazon | Actual use | Finds unregistered use in the same trade | Unstructured, sample it |

Search four ways, every time: **identical**, **phonetic** (the way an Indian buyer says it), **structural**
(the two words joined, split, with a prefix or suffix), and **transliterated** (Devanagari, Tamil, Telugu
or the Roman spelling the business will print). An examiner searches all four; so do you.

## What the class actually does

The class decides whether a hit matters. A mark identical to yours in a class you do not trade in is
usually irrelevant; a similar mark in your class is the whole problem.

| Class | Typical Indian business | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 9 | Software, apps, electronics | Almost every SaaS business needs 9 and 42 |
| 16 | Printed matter, packaging, stationery | Packaging-only claims are weak |
| 25 | Clothing, footwear | Crowded; expect citations |
| 29, 30 | Food, spices, snacks, beverages | 30 covers most packaged foods; 32 beverages |
| 35 | Retail and marketplace services, advertising | The class for a store or an agency, not for the goods |
| 41 | Education, training, entertainment | Coaching, courses, content |
| 42 | SaaS, technology services, design | Pairs with 9 |
| 43 | Restaurant, cafe, food service | Distinct from 30 - the goods are not the service |
| 44 | Medical, beauty, veterinary, agriculture | Clinics, salons, agri services |
| 5 | Pharma, nutraceuticals, supplements | Heavily cited |

File the goods you sell today and will sell in five years. A class filed "for completeness" costs money
every ten years; a class missed costs a rebrand.

## Reading a hit

For each relevant record, extract seven fields. Anything less and you are guessing.

| Field | Why it decides |
|---|---|
| Mark as filed | Word, stylised, device, or a combination - a device mark blocks differently |
| Class and the goods listed | The goods decide, not the class heading |
| Applicant and address | A related entity, a distributor or a competitor changes the read |
| Filing date and user date | Prior use can defeat a later filing; earlier use strengthens yours |
| Status | Registered / opposed / objected / lapsed / removed / abandoned |
| Journal publication date | Starts the four-month opposition window |
| Agent or attorney | Tells you who will answer, and how hard they will fight |

## The similarity tests an examiner applies

| Test | What it asks | Example shape |
|---|---|---|
| Identical | Same mark, same goods | Straight refusal under s.11(1) |
| Deceptive similarity | Would an ordinary buyer, with an imperfect recollection, confuse them | The main battlefield |
| Phonetic | Do they sound the same said aloud | "Veda" vs "Vedaa" vs "Wayda" |
| Visual and structural | Do they look the same at a shelf distance | Same first four letters, same silhouette |
| Conceptual | Same idea in a different word | "Sunrise" vs "Suryodaya" |
| Class of goods | Are the goods the same or allied | Software and SaaS consultancy are allied |

Two defences to know before you write the verdict: **honest concurrent use** (long, documented, peaceful
use of both marks) and **dissimilar goods** (a real argument only when the trade channels genuinely differ).
Neither is a defence a founder should bet a launch on without an advocate's view.

## Clearance verdicts, and the words for each

| Verdict | When | What the brief says |
|---|---|---|
| Clear to file and use | No identical or deceptively similar live mark in the trading classes | File now; the register is not a warranty, and the search date is stated |
| File with risk | A similar mark exists but the goods differ, or the mark is weak but registrable | File, and prepare the argument and the evidence of use now, not after the objection |
| Blocked | A live identical or deceptively similar mark in the same class | Do not launch under it. A rename brief, with two alternatives searched |
| Cannot answer | The goods are unclear, or the mark is a common word in the trade | Ask the one question, or say the mark is descriptive and weak |

## Anti-patterns

- **Searching only the exact spelling.** The objection comes from the phonetic variant, never the exact one.
- **Searching only one class** because the website says what the business is. The register does not read
  websites.
- **Treating a lapsed mark as a clearance.** Status first, similarity second.
- **Reporting a count of hits.** "Eleven results" is not a clearance. Seven fields per relevant hit, or nothing.
- **Forgetting the company name and the domain.** A same-name private limited somewhere in the same trade
  is how a small business ends up in a passing-off suit it did not see coming.
- **Clearing a name after the packaging is printed.** The search is cheapest before the first print order;
  put it in the launch checklist, not after it.
