# Cash and scenario planning

Profit is an opinion. The bank balance is a fact. This file is the one that keeps a profitable
business from running out of money.

## The cash budget, line by line

| Line | How to build it | Trap |
|---|---|---|
| Opening balance | The bank statement, not the books | Books carry cheques not cleared |
| Collections | Revenue × collection pattern (see below) | Assuming everything collects in the month of sale |
| GST outflow | Output tax on collections - input credit | Paying GST on invoices before the customer pays |
| Payroll | CTC × 1.18, paid on the 1st or 7th | Bonus and gratuity provisions |
| Rent | Contract value, paid quarterly in some leases | A quarterly rent is a 3x month |
| Vendor payments | Purchase × payable days | Credit terms shorten when you are new |
| Loan EMI | From the sanction letter | Moratorium end dates are often missed |
| Advance tax | Quarterly instalments, 15/45/75/100% | A big year means a big June payment |
| Capex | The date the money leaves, not the date it is ordered | 50% advance is common |
| One-offs | Insurance, audit fee, licence renewals | They cluster in Q4 |

## Collection patterns beat collection days

"Receivables are 45 days" is an average that hides the shape. Use a pattern:

| Customer type | Month 0 | Month 1 | Month 2 | Month 3+ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D2C / prepaid | 95% | 3% | 1% | 1% |
| B2B small | 30% | 45% | 15% | 10% |
| B2B large / PSU | 5% | 25% | 40% | 30% |
| Government | 0% | 10% | 30% | 60% |

Then the cash low point is visible instead of being discovered. If 40% of revenue comes from
customers who pay in month three, the business funds three months of that revenue.

## The three numbers that matter

1. **The lowest closing balance and the month it lands in.** One number, and it decides
   whether the year needs a working-capital line.
2. **The runway in months of fixed cost.** Closing cash ÷ monthly fixed cost. Under three is a
   conversation; under one is a crisis.
3. **The funding gap.** The most negative cumulative cash position, and whether an overdraft,
   a limit enhancement or a slower growth rate closes it.

## Working capital, in days

```
Cash conversion cycle = inventory days + receivable days - payable days
Working capital need  = (daily revenue at cost) x cycle
```

| Lever | Typical move | Cash effect |
|---|---|---|
| Inventory days | Cut a slow SKU's cover from 90 to 45 | Immediate release |
| Receivable days | 2% early-payment discount, or an advance for new customers | 5-15 days |
| Payable days | Renegotiate from 15 to 30 with the top three vendors | 15 days, costs goodwill |
| Advance collection | Deposit or booking amount on made-to-order | Removes the cycle entirely |
| Invoice discounting / TReDS | Sell the receivable | Costs 9-14% a year; buys time |

## Scenarios: three, not seven

| Scenario | What changes | What to report |
|---|---|---|
| **Base** | The plan as approved | Revenue, EBITDA, cash low point |
| **Downside** | Revenue -20%, collections 15 days slower, one hire delayed | Cash low point, the month it arrives, the first action |
| **The named risk** | The one thing that would actually hurt: top customer leaves, a rate reset, a stock-out, a key person exits | Cash effect and the decision date |

Each scenario is one column, not a new workbook. A founder reading three columns makes a
decision; a founder reading seven does nothing.

## Sensitivity: what actually moves the answer

| Variable | ±10% effect on cash | Rank |
|---|---|---|
| Collection days | High - usually the biggest | 1 |
| Gross margin | High | 2 |
| Revenue volume | Medium, because cost flexes too | 3 |
| Payroll | Low per 10%, but it is 30-40% of the base | 4 |
| Rent | Low, and it is fixed | 5 |

Rank them, and put the top two in the summary. That is the whole of sensitivity analysis worth
doing in a small business.

## Contingency, sized not guessed

| Situation | Contingency |
|---|---|
| Established business, stable | 5% of total cost |
| Growing 30%+, new channel or outlet | 8-10% of total cost |
| New product line or new geography | 12-15% of that line's cost |
| Regulatory change pending | The full expected one-off cost, plus the compliance run rate |

Contingency is a line with an owner and a rule for release, not a slush fund. If it is spent in
month two, the plan was understated.

## Anti-patterns

| Anti-pattern | Cost |
|---|---|
| P&L budget with no cash view | The classic profitable insolvency |
| GST paid on invoices, not collections | A permanent 1-2 month hole in working capital |
| Quarterly rent or annual insurance averaged monthly | A 3x or 12x month looks like a crisis |
| Collections assumed at sale | The plan needs funding it never asked for |
| Runway measured in profit | Months of cash, not months of margin |
| Contingency used as a plug | The real cost was never planned |
| One scenario | The first bad month becomes an emergency board call |
