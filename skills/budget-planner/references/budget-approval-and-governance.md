# Approval and governance

A budget that is not approved in writing is a spreadsheet. This file is how it becomes a
decision the business is held to.

## Envelopes

An envelope is a cost block, a number, a monthly shape and one owner. It is the unit of
control - not the total, and not the chart of accounts.

- **One owner per envelope.** Two owners is zero owners.
- **The owner confirms the number in writing** - a reply in the mail thread is enough.
- **The envelope carries its own monthly shape**, because a marketing envelope that is
  flat across a seasonal year is wrong in every month.
- **Transfers between envelopes need both owners and a note**, and they do not change
  the total. Transfers are how budgets stay honest without a re-approval cycle.
- **Overspend is a decision, not an accident.** The rule is written before the year
  starts: who is told, when, and what happens if it repeats.

## Capital versus operating

- **Opex** - consumed inside the year; approved as an envelope.
- **Capex** - creates an asset; approved individually above a threshold, with a payback
  or a justification, and its depreciation appears in the P&L later. Mixing the two
  makes a bad year look good and a good year look bad.
- **Threshold** - one number, stated in the approval note, above which every capex item
  needs its own approval regardless of envelope.

## Contingency

Hold a contingency line - typically 3-8% of controllable cost, more in a volatile year -
owned by the founder, not by a department. It is drawn only with a written reason, and
the remaining balance is reported every month. A contingency that is quietly distributed
to departments in month one is not a contingency.

## The approval note

One page. If it does not fit, the plan is not understood well enough to be approved.

1. **What is being approved** - scope, financial year, total revenue, total cost, EBITDA,
   lowest cash month.
2. **The three assumptions that matter**, each with the downside if wrong.
3. **What changes versus last year**, in three lines: what we are buying, what we are
   stopping, what we are holding.
4. **The risk** - the one thing that would make this plan wrong, and the trigger that
   moves us to the downside case.
5. **The decision asked for** - approve, approve with a condition, or send back with a
   question. Never "for information".

## Governance rhythm

| Cadence | What happens | Who |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly, by the 7th | variance pack against envelopes | envelope owners, founder |
| Quarterly | reforecast and re-approval of the landing point | founder or board |
| Half-yearly | envelope review - is the split still right | founder |
| Annually | new cycle: drivers, build, envelopes, approval | all owners |

## What the board or lender sees

- The plan in one page: revenue, EBITDA, cash, the three assumptions, the downside.
- The variance to the plan for the year to date, with causes.
- The landing point and its confidence.
- Not the workbook. A board that receives a 40-tab file reads none of it and remembers
  only the number it disagrees with.

## Anti-patterns

- **Approval by silence** - circulated, nobody objected, therefore agreed. Ask for a
  yes.
- **A budget approved after the year started**, which is a record of what already
  happened.
- **Envelopes that do not match the ledger structure**, so nobody can reconcile them.
- **Contingency owned by nobody**, which is spent by everybody.
- **A version that keeps changing after approval** with no date and no label.
