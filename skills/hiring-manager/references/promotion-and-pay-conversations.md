# Promotion and pay conversations

A raise request is not a negotiation to be survived. It is a signal that someone
has been thinking about their future for months, and the answer they get decides
whether they are still there in a year.

## First, separate the two questions

| Question | Evidence | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Is the scope bigger than the title? | The next level's work, already being done, for at least a quarter | Manager, with founder approval |
| Is the pay below the band for the current level? | The band, and where the person sits in it | Founder, with finance-ops |
| Is this a retention problem in disguise? | A competing offer, or a market shift | Founder |

Three different problems, three different answers. Answering all three with a
number is how businesses end up with a team of overpaid, mis-titled people.

## Building the promotion case

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| Current level | Title, scope, who they own |
| Proposed level | Title, scope, who they would own |
| Evidence of the next level | Three results that a person at the current level would not have delivered |
| Duration | How long they have been doing it - a quarter minimum |
| Gap | What is still missing, and whether it can be closed in the role |
| Risk | What breaks if they leave, and who could cover |
| Decision | Promote, promote with a plan, or not yet, with the date |

The strongest sentence in a promotion case is "they have been doing the next
level's job since July, here are the three things that prove it".

## The bands

- **Set the band before the conversation.** A band invented during a negotiation is a number with no anchor.
- **A band is a range, not a point.** Position within it reflects scope and evidence, not tenure.
- **Write the band down** in `notes/decisions.md` with the date, and revisit it annually.
- **Benchmarking data is `research-analyst`'s job**, not a number invented in the meeting. If there is no benchmark, say so and use internal relativities.

## Saying no, well

A no that keeps the person has four parts:

1. **The decision, plainly.** "Not this cycle." No hedging, no "let's see how the next quarter goes".
2. **The reason, tied to evidence.** "The scope is still the current level - the two client accounts are still run by Priya."
3. **What would change it.** "Own both accounts end to end and hit the retention number, and it is a yes at the next cycle in April."
4. **Something real now.** A stretch project, a training budget, a title review date, flexible hours - something with a date on it.

A no with no date is a resignation with a delay.

## Saying yes, well

- **Confirm in writing the same week**, with the effective date and the new scope.
- **Hand it to `finance-ops`** for the payroll change and the revised CTC structure.
- **Hand contract and title changes to `legal-drafter`** if the employment terms change.
- **Announce the scope, not the money.** The team needs to know who owns what; nobody needs to know what someone else earns.

## Retention risk, honestly assessed

| Signal | Read |
|---|---|
| Asked for a raise with a competing offer in hand | Decision needed this week, not this quarter |
| Asked twice in 12 months with no change | The answer is being tested; the second no is final |
| Went quiet after a no | The resignation is being written elsewhere |
| Asked for scope, not money | Wants a future, not a number - the cheaper and better problem |

## Anti-patterns

- **Matching a counter-offer without fixing the cause.** The person who accepted a counter-offer usually leaves within a year anyway.
- **Paying the loudest, not the best.** Everyone works out the rule within one cycle.
- **Promoting to avoid a conversation.** A title that comes with no scope change is a debt the business pays later.
- **Promising a review date you will not hold.** The date is the commitment; the money is the outcome.
- **Discussing one person's pay with the team.** It always gets out, and it is always the wrong version.
