# The review cycle

A review is a scheduled conversation with evidence in it. If it feels like a
verdict, the cycle has failed somewhere earlier.

## The cycle, in order

| Step | When | Owner | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cycle dates published | 6 weeks before | hiring-manager | Everyone knows the dates and the process |
| Self-review written | 4 weeks before | The person | One page: what I delivered, what I did not, what I need |
| Evidence collected | 3 weeks before | Manager | Delivery record, numbers, peer input, missed commitments |
| Draft review written | 2 weeks before | Manager | One page per person, rating, two examples each way |
| Calibration | 1 week before | Founder and managers | Ratings consistent across teams |
| The conversation | Cycle week | Manager | Agreed outcome, two changes, one ask |
| Written confirmation | Within 2 days | Manager | The same document, signed off, sent |
| Pay and promotion decisions | 2-4 weeks after | Founder | Communicated with dates and reasons |

## The one-page review

| Section | Content | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| The outcome | The role's one sentence, restated | 1 line |
| What was delivered | Three results with numbers | 3 bullets |
| What was not | One or two honest misses, with the reason | 2 bullets |
| How the work was done | One strength, one behaviour to change, both with an example | 2 bullets |
| The rating | The level and what it means here | 1 line |
| Next cycle | Two objectives, with numbers | 2 bullets |
| The ask | What the person needs from the manager or the business | 1 line |

One page. Longer reviews are written to avoid the conversation, not to have it.

## Ratings that mean something

| Rating | Means | Distribution guide |
|---|---|---|
| Exceptional | The next level already being done | 5-10% |
| Strong | Delivered the outcome, some of it beyond scope | 20-30% |
| Solid | Delivered the outcome as agreed | 50-60% |
| Below | Delivered part of it, with a named reason | 5-15% |
| Not meeting | The outcome was not delivered and the gap is not new | 0-5%, and a PIP follows |

If 80% of the team is "exceptional", the scale is measuring the manager's
generosity, and the one person who is genuinely exceptional has no signal.

## Calibration, for a small team

- Every manager brings their draft ratings before any conversation happens.
- Ask one question per person: what did they deliver that someone at the next level down would not have?
- Ratings move to be consistent across teams, not to fit a curve.
- Write down the two or three calibration decisions and their reason - the same argument returns next cycle otherwise.

## The conversation, in four moves

1. **Open with the outcome, not the rating.** "Your job was X. Here is what happened against it."
2. **Evidence, both ways.** Two specifics for the strength, one for the change. No adjectives without an example.
3. **The rating and what it means.** Say it plainly, early, and do not soften it into ambiguity.
4. **The ask.** What they need, what you will do about it, and the date you will come back to them.

Twenty to forty minutes. Silence is allowed - people need a moment after a rating
they did not expect.

## Anti-patterns

- **The sandwich.** Praise, criticism, praise. The person hears only the criticism and stops trusting the praise.
- **"You are doing great" with no evidence.** It reads as "I have not looked".
- **Recency.** One bad week in March does not outweigh ten good months.
- **The surprise.** If it was not said before the review, it is a management failure, not a finding.
- **Ratings with no consequence.** A "not meeting" rating with no PIP and no change teaches the whole team that the process is theatre.
- **Pay decided in the review meeting.** Money is a separate conversation with a separate approver, and it is always later.
