# Retention and attrition signals

People rarely leave for the reason given in the exit interview. They leave because
of a signal nobody read in month four.

## The attrition curve

| Window | What it usually means | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Days 1-30 | The offer, the onboarding or the reality did not match | Did the role match the JD; was there a plan and a buddy |
| Days 31-90 | The work is not what they were sold, or the manager is absent | The 30-60-90 check-ins; the manager's calendar |
| Months 4-12 | Pay, growth, or a bad team dynamic | Stay interview at month 9; the last two reviews |
| Year 2-3 | Ceiling, title, or a competitor's offer | Scope, ownership, comp benchmark |
| Year 5+ | Gratuity vested, inertia, or a life change | Career conversation, not a retention bonus |

Track the rate by window, not one number. A 25% annual attrition made entirely of
month-two exits is an onboarding problem, and a bonus will not fix it.

## Signals worth acting on

- **Silence in meetings that used to have opinions in them.**
- **Leave taken in odd single days** rather than planned breaks - often interviews.
- **A drop in the quality of written work** from someone whose work was careful.
- **Refusing new scope** they would have taken three months ago.
- **A LinkedIn profile update**, seen by a colleague. Not evidence, but a prompt.
- **Asking about the notice period, the relieving letter or the PF transfer** in a
  general way.

None of these is proof. Two together is a reason for a conversation this week.

## The stay interview, month nine

Thirty minutes, six questions, no performance discussion:

1. What do you want to be doing in two years, and does this role move you toward it?
2. What part of your week would you remove?
3. What have you learned in the last six months that you could not have learned elsewhere?
4. What would make you consider leaving?
5. What do you need from me that you are not getting?
6. If you got a 20% raise elsewhere, what would keep you here besides money?

Write the answers down. Three stay interviews with the same answer is a company
problem, not a person problem.

## When the resignation lands

| Hour | Action |
|---|---|
| 0-4 | Acknowledge, do not counter-offer in the room, ask for the reason in their words |
| 4-24 | Decide: counter, accept, or negotiate the date. One counter, maximum, and only if the reason is fixable |
| 24-48 | Confirm in writing: last working day, handover owner, notice buyout if any, relieving letter date |
| Week 1 | Handover plan with named files, accounts and a successor or an owner per item |
| Last day | Exit interview with someone other than the direct manager, assets returned, full-and-final timeline stated |

A counter-offer that fixes only the money buys about six months and teaches the
team that resigning is how raises are obtained.

## Exit interviews that tell the truth

- Run them by someone who is not the manager, and offer a written alternative.
- Ask three questions: what made you start looking, what would have kept you, and
  what should we fix for the person who replaces you.
- Report themes quarterly, never attributed to a person.
- An exit interview with no follow-up action is a form, and everyone knows it.

## India reality

- **Gratuity** accrues after five years of continuous service; the date matters
  and is worth tracking from day one.
- **PF and ESI transfers** need the relieving letter and the UAN; a delayed letter
  is a real cost to the person and a real reason for a complaint.
- **Notice period buyout** is contractual, and a negotiated early release should
  be written down, not agreed on a call.
- **Counter-offers in a small team are visible.** If one person gets one, the
  salary story spreads within a week.

## Anti-patterns

- **The retention bonus as a strategy.** It buys a date, not a reason to stay.
- **The exit interview nobody reads.** Themes collected and never acted on are
  worse than not asking.
- **The guilt conversation.** "After everything we did for you" ends the reference
  and the alumni network.
- **The counter-offer reflex.** Matching a number within an hour tells the team
  the salary was arbitrary.
- **Treating the resignation as betrayal.** The person leaving is the best source
  of truth about the job you have, for the next hire.
