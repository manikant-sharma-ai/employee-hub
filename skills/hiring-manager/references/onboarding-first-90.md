# Onboarding: the first ninety days

The offer is the promise. This is the delivery. Most early attrition is decided in
the first three weeks and blamed on the person in the third month.

## The gap between offer and day one

The most common loss is not a bad first day - it is silence between the offer and
the joining date, during which a counter-offer, a family objection or a better
brand wins.

| When | What goes out | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Same day as acceptance | Welcome note with start date, time, address or link, reporting manager's name, and one line on what to bring | Removes the "did they forget me" week |
| 7 days before | First-day logistics: who meets them, where to sit, what the first week holds | The single highest-value message of the whole process |
| 3 days before | Access checklist confirmed in writing: email, laptop, tools, payroll documents | Surfaces the blocked account before it blocks a person |
| Day 1 morning | A named buddy, introduced by name in a message | A buddy is the difference between asking and guessing |
| Day 3 | Manager's 20-minute "how is it actually going" | Catches the wrong first impression while it is cheap |

## The pre-boarding checklist

Documents and access, all confirmed before day one:

- Appointment letter signed and returned; background verification initiated where the role requires it.
- Statutory documents collected into the HR system: PAN, Aadhaar (masked where possible), bank details for salary, PF and ESI details where applicable, previous employer's relieving letter and Form 16 for TDS continuity.
- Email account, laptop, phone or SIM, and every tool the role needs - CRM, helpdesk, repo, Drive folder.
- Payroll and attendance enrolment, and the leave policy in writing, not in a hallway conversation.
- A seat, a monitor and a login that works on day one.

If any of that is missing, the honest sentence is: "Access will be ready by day
three; here is what we do on day one without it." That is fine. Silence is not.

## Day one, hour by hour

| Time | What happens | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 09:30 | Someone is physically or virtually there to receive them | Buddy |
| 09:45 | Desk, laptop, logins verified working | IT or ops |
| 10:00 | 30 minutes: what the company sells, to whom, how money is made | Founder or manager |
| 10:45 | 30 minutes: how this role fits, the three outcomes, the 30-60-90 handed over | Manager |
| 11:30 | Team intro, buddy named, channels joined | Buddy |
| 12:00 | One small real task, finished today | Manager |
| 16:30 | 15 minutes: questions, and what tomorrow looks like | Manager |

The small real task matters more than the tour. A person who finished something on
day one believes they can work here; a person who watched slides does not.

## Week one

- Two hours a day of structured learning, not eight hours of shadowing.
- One meeting with each person they will depend on, with a stated purpose.
- Written answers to the five questions every new joiner has: what do we sell, who buys it, how is success measured for me, who decides, what do I do when I am stuck.
- Friday: 20 minutes with the manager - what was confusing, what was missing, one fix.

## The 30-60-90 shape

| Phase | Posture | Typical outcomes |
|---|---|---|
| Days 1-30 - Learn | Consume, ask, shadow, do small real work | Can explain the product and the customer; has completed 5-10 real tasks with review; knows who to ask for what |
| Days 31-60 - Contribute | Own a slice with a safety net | Handles a defined workload unaided; one improvement suggested from observation; first independent customer or stakeholder interaction |
| Days 61-90 - Own | Accountable for a number or a process | Delivers the role's first outcome without a reminder; the manager can be absent for a week without it breaking |

Each phase needs two or three outcomes that someone else can observe, and one
"support offered" line, because a plan with no support is a test, not onboarding.

## The five check-ins

Day 7, 30, 60, 85, and one informal at day 45. Same three questions every time:

1. What is harder than you expected?
2. What have you been told that turned out to be wrong?
3. What do you need that you have not asked for?

Write one line after each. The day-85 conversation is where the probation
recommendation is formed, not the day it is due.

## Anti-patterns

- **The induction dump.** Six hours of policy on day one teaches a new joiner that the company confuses documentation with care.
- **The invisible manager.** A manager in back-to-back meetings for two weeks reads as "you are not a priority", whatever the intent.
- **The buddy who was never asked.** Assign one person, tell them what the job is, and give them 30 minutes a day for the first week.
- **Learning by osmosis.** "Sit with Ramesh and pick it up" produces a copy of Ramesh, including his workarounds.
- **No written plan.** If it is not on a page with dates, it is a hope.
- **Probation as a formality.** If the criteria were never written, the confirmation is a signature on nothing.
