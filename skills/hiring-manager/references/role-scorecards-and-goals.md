# Role scorecards and goals

A scorecard is the answer to "what would make us say this hire worked". Written
before the person starts, used at every check-in, and the basis of the probation
decision.

## The three layers

| Layer | Question it answers | Example (account manager) |
|---|---|---|
| Outcomes | What must be true at day 90? | 15 accounts active, 90% renewal rate on the book, one upsell closed |
| Competencies | How must the work be done? | Discovery calls that end in a written next step; CRM updated same day |
| Leading indicators | What predicts the outcome? | 8 calls a week, 3 stakeholder meetings a month, 100% of escalations acknowledged in 4 hours |

Outcomes alone hide a person who hits a number by burning the team. Competencies
alone hide a person who is lovely and produces nothing. Use both, and keep the
list to three outcomes and three competencies.

## Writing an outcome that can be checked

Bad: "improve customer satisfaction".
Good: "CSAT on the 40 accounts in the book at or above 4.2 by day 90, measured
from the survey that already runs on the first of each month".

Four tests, applied to every line:

1. **Observable** - someone other than the person can see it happened.
2. **Dated** - a day it must be true by.
3. **Bounded** - a scope: which accounts, which product, which shift.
4. **Sourced** - where the number comes from, so it cannot be re-argued.

If a line fails any of the four, rewrite it or delete it.

## 30-60-90 targets, by phase

| Phase | Weight on | Not yet |
|---|---|---|
| 1-30 | Learning outcomes, process compliance, quality of questions | Revenue, independent judgement |
| 31-60 | Volume with review, first independent deliverable | Full quota, sole ownership of a key account |
| 61-90 | The role's real outcome, at a reduced bar | Everything the role will eventually do |

A common and expensive error is putting full quota in month one. The other is
putting nothing measurable in month three, which is how a probation confirmation
becomes a formality.

## Probation criteria, written in advance

Three columns, agreed with the manager before day one:

| Criterion | Evidence | Threshold |
|---|---|---|
| Role outcomes | The tracker or dashboard | 2 of 3 met, none below 70% |
| Competencies | Dated observations in the log | No repeated breach of a core behaviour |
| Conduct and attendance | Attendance record, policy compliance | No unresolved conduct issue |

Decision rule: **all three met - confirm. One shortfall - extend once, in writing,
with the specific gap and a date. Two or more, or any conduct issue - exit
properly.** Write the rule down before you need it; deciding it in the room is how
managers confirm someone they wanted to exit.

## Goals for a team of five to fifty

- **Three to five goals per person, no more.** Beyond five, everything is a
  priority and nothing is.
- **One goal must be the person's own improvement**, or the cycle becomes a
  scoreboard with no development in it.
- **Company goals cascade as context, not as copies.** A junior's version of
  "grow revenue" is "call 40 dormant accounts a month", not the same sentence.
- **Review the goals quarterly, not annually.** Annual goals set in April and
  reviewed in March are a museum piece.
- **Numbers have a source and a definition.** Two people reporting "leads" from
  two systems is a goal that cannot be scored.

## Anti-patterns

- **The activity plan.** "Attend 10 meetings" is not an outcome; it is a diary.
- **The copy-paste scorecard.** Every role in the company with the same three
  criteria means nobody wrote a scorecard.
- **The moving target.** Criteria changed after the person struggled, without a
  written revision and a new date, is the fastest route to a dispute.
- **The unreachable bar.** A day-90 target nobody has ever hit at month six
  teaches the team that the scorecard is theatre.
- **No manager commitment column.** Every scorecard needs what the manager owes:
  time, reviews, introductions, decisions. Otherwise it is a one-sided contract.
