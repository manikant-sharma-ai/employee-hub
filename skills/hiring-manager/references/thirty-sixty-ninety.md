# The 30-60-90

A 30-60-90 is a contract about what "on track" means. It exists so that day 90 is
a review of agreed milestones rather than an argument about expectations.

## The three phases

| Phase | The question | The evidence | What is fair to expect |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30 days - Learn | Do they understand the business, the customer and the job? | Notes, questions asked, a summary of what they found | Context, not output |
| 60 days - Contribute | Can they do the work with review? | Work shipped with the buddy or manager checking | Output on scoped tasks |
| 90 days - Own | Do they run it without being managed? | The outcome delivered, the number moved, others relying on them | Independent ownership of the agreed outcome |

The most common mistake is writing 30-day output targets. A month in, the person
is still learning the customers' names. Output targets at day 30 produce either
theatre or an early exit.

## Writing a milestone

Four fields, all required. A milestone missing any of them is an intention.

| Field | Bad | Good |
|---|---|---|
| What | "Get up to speed on sales" | "Run the weekly pipeline review unsupervised" |
| Number | "Improve conversion" | "Lift demo-to-proposal conversion from 38% to 45%" |
| Date | "In the first quarter" | "By 15 January" |
| Witness | "Everyone will know" | "Verified by the CRM report the founder reads on Mondays" |

Three to five milestones per phase is right. Nine means none of them matter.

## A worked 30-60-90 - operations manager, 40-person D2C brand

| Phase | Milestone | Number | Date | Witness |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30 | Map the order-to-delivery process as it actually runs | One page, all six handoffs, three bottlenecks named | Day 28 | Founder reads it |
| 30 | Meet every vendor above Rs 1 lakh a year | 11 vendors, notes per vendor | Day 30 | Vendor list signed off |
| 60 | Cut dispatch delays on the Delhi NCR route | From 22% late to under 10% | Day 58 | Courier dashboard |
| 60 | Take over the weekly ops meeting | Runs it, agenda and actions out same day | Day 55 | Meeting notes circulated |
| 90 | Own inventory accuracy | Stock count variance under 2% | Day 88 | Monthly stock report |
| 90 | Deliver one cost saving | Rs 40,000 a month, identified and realised | Day 90 | Ledger shows the reduction |

## Running the check-ins

- **Day 7, 30, 60, 90** - twenty minutes each, on the calendar from week one.
- **Same three questions every time:** what went well, what is in the way, what changes before the next check-in.
- **Write it down and send it.** A verbal check-in with no note becomes a disputed review.
- **The milestone can change, the process cannot.** If a milestone is wrong, change it in writing on the day, with the reason.

## When someone is behind at day 60

| Signal | Read | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Behind on milestones, asks good questions | Learning curve steeper than expected | Reset milestones with a date, add help |
| Behind, no questions, no notes | Disengagement or wrong role | Direct conversation this week |
| On milestones, but the work needs rework | Capability gap in craft | Pair with the strongest person, set a craft milestone |
| Ahead on everything | Under-stretched | Give the next-level scope now, not at review time |

## Anti-patterns

- **Milestones that are activities.** "Attend 20 meetings" measures attendance.
- **No witness.** If nobody can check it, it will be argued about.
- **The 90-day plan written on day 45.** It is a plan for the first ninety days, written in the first week.
- **A plan nobody opens again.** Put the check-in dates on the calendar and the plan in the doc the person uses daily.
