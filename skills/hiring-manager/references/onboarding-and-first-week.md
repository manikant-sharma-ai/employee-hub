# Onboarding and the first week

The first week decides the first ninety days. A person who spends week one waiting
for a laptop and guessing at priorities has already learned that the job is
unstructured - and that lesson outlasts the laptop.

## The pre-start checklist

Everything here is done before day one, by someone with a name against it.

| Item | Done by | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Offer letter signed, joining date confirmed in writing | hiring-manager | A verbal start date is a no-show |
| Contract, NDA, IP assignment signed | legal-drafter / HR | Signed before access, not after |
| Laptop, phone, SIM, email, seat | IT owner | Day-one idling is the most expensive hour in the year |
| Tool accounts: mail, Slack, drive, project tool, CRM | IT owner | Access requests take 2-4 days in most small firms |
| Payroll and statutory enrolment started (PF, ESI where applicable) | finance-ops | Retrospective enrolment is a compliance problem |
| Named buddy, briefed and willing | hiring-manager | The buddy answers the questions the manager never hears |
| Week-one calendar populated with real meetings | hiring-manager | An empty calendar reads as "nobody planned for you" |
| First deliverable chosen - small, real, finishable | hiring-manager | A shipped thing in week one builds more confidence than a month of reading |
| Team told who is arriving, what they own, and what to bring them | hiring-manager | The team's first message sets the pecking order |

## Day one, hour by hour

| Time | What | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 09:30 | Welcome, tea, seat, laptop on the desk | Manager |
| 10:00 | Paperwork, documents, bank and PF details collected | HR / finance-ops |
| 10:45 | The one-page context: what we sell, to whom, what is broken right now | Manager |
| 11:30 | The outcome of the role, in one sentence, written down | Manager |
| 12:00 | Team introductions, one line each on what they own | Buddy |
| 13:00 | Lunch with the buddy, not alone | Buddy |
| 14:30 | Tool walkthrough: where work lives, where decisions are recorded | Buddy |
| 16:00 | The first deliverable, scoped and started | Manager |
| 17:30 | Fifteen minutes: what was confusing today | Manager |

The 17:30 slot is the highest-value meeting of the week. It catches the confusion
that otherwise becomes a month of silent guessing.

## Week one, day by day

- **Day 1** - context, outcome, access, first deliverable started.
- **Day 2** - shadow the two people they will work with most. One page of notes on what surprised them.
- **Day 3** - read the last three months of the work that matters: last quarter's numbers, the last five customer complaints, the current backlog.
- **Day 4** - do a piece of real work with the buddy reviewing it.
- **Day 5** - present the first deliverable to the team in ten minutes, and agree the 30-day milestones.

## The context one-pager

Every new hire gets the same page. It is written once and updated quarterly.

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| What we sell | The offer in one sentence, and the price |
| Who buys | The two or three customer types that pay the bills |
| How money arrives | Channel, cycle, who signs |
| What is broken | The three problems the business is actually working on |
| Who decides what | The real decision map, not the org chart |
| Your outcome | One sentence, this role, this quarter |
| How we measure you | The two or three numbers, and where they live |

## Anti-patterns

- **The reading list.** A folder of 40 documents is not onboarding; it is homework with no deadline.
- **Sink or swim.** "They will figure it out" works for people who already know the job, which is not why you hired them.
- **The buddy who is too busy.** If the buddy is at 100% utilisation, name a second one.
- **Week one with no deliverable.** Confidence comes from shipping something, however small.
- **No written outcome.** Without it, the 30-60-90 becomes a personality assessment.
