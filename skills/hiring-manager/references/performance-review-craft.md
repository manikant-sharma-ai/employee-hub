# Performance review craft

A review is a record of what happened, agreed with the person, and a decision
about what changes next. It is not a summary of personality.

## Before the review: the evidence log

Keep a running log per person, one line per event:

```
2026-10-14 | missed the pricing approval deadline | impact: invoice to client late by 2 days | told same day
2026-10-21 | caught the duplicate vendor invoice | impact: Rs 84,000 not paid twice | told same day
```

Two rules make the log usable: **every line is dated and has an impact**, and
**anything in the log was said to the person within 48 hours**. A review that
surfaces a three-month-old complaint is a failure of management, not of the
employee.

## The order of the review

1. **Criteria first.** Send the scorecard and the log extract a week ahead.
2. **Self-review before manager review.** Three questions: what went well, what
   did not, what do you need from me. Their words change the conversation.
3. **Evidence, then rating.** Never rating, then justification.
4. **Two things to change, one thing to keep.** More than three changes is a
   list nobody acts on.
5. **The manager's own commitments**, with dates - the thing that makes the
   review two-way.
6. **Written, signed, filed.** Both people keep a copy.

## Ratings that mean something

Use one scale company-wide, defined in writing:

| Rating | Definition | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| 5 - Exceptional | Outcomes exceeded and raised the bar for others | Consider for scope, not automatically pay |
| 4 - Strong | All outcomes met, some exceeded, competencies consistent | Normal progression path |
| 3 - Solid | Outcomes met at the expected level | The healthy majority; not a warning |
| 2 - Below | One or more outcomes missed with a named cause | Written plan and a review date |
| 1 - Unsatisfactory | Sustained miss or a conduct issue | Formal process, not a rating alone |

Calibrate across the team before delivery: list everyone at 4 and 5 and ask what
each did that the 3s did not. If the answer is "worked longer hours", the rating
is measuring presence.

## The review conversation, 45 minutes

- **0-3 min** - purpose, and that this is a conversation, not a verdict.
- **3-10 min** - their self-review, uninterrupted.
- **10-25 min** - the evidence, criterion by criterion, with the log in front of both.
- **25-35 min** - the two changes and the one thing to keep; agree the support.
- **35-42 min** - their questions, including pay and title, answered honestly or deferred with a date.
- **42-45 min** - what gets written and when they will see it.

If the review contains a surprise, the process failed earlier. Say so and fix the
process, not just the rating.

## Common biases, named

| Bias | What it looks like | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| Recency | The last three weeks decide the year | The log, read in order |
| Halo | One great project lifts every criterion | Score each criterion separately |
| Similarity | "Cultural fit" for someone like the manager | Behavioural evidence, not rapport |
| Presence | Hours seen, not output produced | Outcomes with a source |
| Leniency drift | Everyone a 4 because the conversation is awkward | Calibration, before delivery |
| Contrast | Compared to the last person in the role | Compared to the criteria |

## India reality

- **Reviews and salary are separate conversations.** Promising a number in a
  review and delivering a different one in April destroys the next review.
- **Increment letters and revised CTC structure** are payroll documents, not
  review documents; `bookkeeper` and the payroll system produce them.
- **Documentation is the defence.** In a dispute, a dated log with the employee's
  acknowledgement outweighs a manager's recollection.
- **Language matters.** If the person is more comfortable in Hindi or Tamil, run
  the conversation in that language and write the summary in English - never the
  reverse without a translation.

## Anti-patterns

- **The sandwich.** Praise, criticism, praise. People remember the praise and miss
  the criticism; say the hard thing plainly and early.
- **The annual-only review.** Twelve months of silence followed by a document is
  an ambush with a template.
- **Ratings with no consequence.** A 2 with no plan and no date is a 3 with a
  stern face.
- **The peer-review ambush.** Anonymous feedback used as evidence, unattributed
  and unverified, is not evidence.
- **The manager who never writes.** If the log is empty in month nine, the review
  is a memory test and the employee will win it.
