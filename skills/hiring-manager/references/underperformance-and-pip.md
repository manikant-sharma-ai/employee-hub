# Underperformance and the PIP

Most underperformance is a management problem wearing a performance costume:
an unclear outcome, no feedback for months, or a person in the wrong role.
Fix those first, because a PIP cannot fix them.

## The early signal, and the four-week rule

| Signal | What it usually means |
|---|---|
| Deadlines slip without warning | Priorities are unclear, or the person cannot say no |
| Work needs rework every time | Craft gap, or an unclear standard |
| Quiet in meetings, no questions | Disengaged, or afraid to be wrong |
| Busy but nothing finishes | Task switching, or no authority to close things |
| The team routes around them | Trust has already gone |

Rule: **four weeks between noticing a pattern and naming it.** Beyond that, the
person has a fair complaint that nobody told them.

## The first conversation

Twenty minutes, private, and it is a conversation, not a verdict.

1. **State the observation with evidence.** "Three of your last four reports went out after the date you committed to."
2. **Ask, then stop talking.** "What is happening?" The answer is often the actual problem: a dependency, a bad handover, a role that does not fit.
3. **Agree what good looks like**, with a number and a date.
4. **Offer the specific help** - pairing, a template, a training slot, a reallocation of work.
5. **Write it down and send it the same day.** Five lines: observation, agreement, help, date, next meeting.

That email is the difference between a fair process and a disputed one.

## When the PIP is right

A PIP is right when the gap is specific, the person has the ability, and the help
has been given and not taken up. It is wrong when the real issue is a role misfit,
a bad manager, or a decision already made.

## The PIP document

| Element | Requirement |
|---|---|
| The gap | Specific, evidenced, with dates - not a trait |
| The standard | What "met" looks like, with numbers |
| The support | What the business will provide, named, with a date |
| The duration | 30, 60 or 90 days - short enough to be real |
| The check-ins | Weekly, on the calendar, with written notes |
| The consequence | Stated plainly and once: what happens if the standard is not met |
| The signatures | Person, manager, HR or founder witness |

## A worked PIP line

Bad: "Improve communication and ownership."

Good: "By 15 February, all four weekly client reports are sent by Friday 17:00
without a reminder; two of the four include the variance analysis the client asked
for. Support: the reporting template, a 30-minute review with the analyst every
Thursday, and the client list frozen for the period. Check-ins: Thursdays 16:00.
If the standard is not met by 15 February, the next step is a separation
conversation."

## Running it fairly

- **Weekly check-ins happen even when the news is bad.** A PIP with missed check-ins is unenforceable.
- **The help is real.** If the promised support did not appear, the PIP clock restarts.
- **No new scope during a PIP.** Adding work to someone under review guarantees failure.
- **The person may bring a colleague** to the review meetings, and should be told so.
- **The decision is made on the criteria, on the date.** Extending indefinitely is worse for everyone than deciding.

## When it ends

| Outcome | What happens |
|---|---|
| Standard met | Written confirmation, close the PIP, and no lingering penalty in the next review |
| Standard partly met | Only if the gap is now small and the trajectory is clear - otherwise it is a no |
| Standard not met | Separation conversation, notice as per contract, settlement handed to finance-ops |
| Role misfit identified | A different role inside the business, on a fresh 30-60-90, if one exists |

## Anti-patterns

- **The surprise PIP.** Nine months of silence then a document. This is the pattern employment tribunals look for.
- **Vague criteria.** "Show more initiative" cannot be met or failed, so it can only be argued about.
- **A PIP as cover for a decision already taken.** People can tell, and the rest of the team can too.
- **No documentation.** The email after every conversation is the whole defence.
- **Public criticism.** Correct in private, always, however small the team.
