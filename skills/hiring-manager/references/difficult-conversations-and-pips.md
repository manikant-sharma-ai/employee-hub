# Difficult conversations and performance plans

The conversation you avoid in month two costs a legal process in month eight.

## The five-minute rule

Say the small thing within 48 hours, in private, in one or two sentences:

> "On the 4 o'clock call yesterday you told the client the report would be ready
> today, and it was not. When we promise a date we hold it or we call before it
> passes. What happened?"

Specific, dated, impact stated, question at the end. No preamble, no "we need to
talk about your attitude".

## The structure that works

| Step | Line to use | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | "I want to talk about the vendor report that went out on Tuesday." | Names the event, not the person |
| Fact | "It went out with last month's prices." | One fact, checkable |
| Impact | "The client has been quoting 8% under our cost for two days." | Cost, customer, or risk |
| Ask | "What happened?" | Listen before concluding; you are often half wrong |
| Agreement | "So from now, the report is checked by you against the price list before it leaves." | A specific behaviour, not an attitude |
| Close | "I will note this and we will look at it again on the 30th." | The record and the follow-up |

## When it is a pattern, not an incident

Escalate in steps, each in writing:

1. **Conversation** - dated note in the log, no letter.
2. **Written summary** - a short email: what was discussed, what was agreed, what
   happens next, by when. Not a warning; a record.
3. **Performance improvement plan** - the formal step, with the structure below.
4. **Formal process** - `legal-drafter` and a labour lawyer, before any letter
   that changes employment terms.

## A PIP that is defensible

| Element | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Gaps | Two or three, each with a dated example and the standard it missed |
| Measures | Observable, numeric where possible, with the source named |
| Support | What the company provides: training, shadowing, reduced scope, a weekly review |
| Duration | 30, 60 or 90 days, stated with dates |
| Reviews | Weekly, with a named reviewer and a written note each time |
| Outcome | What happens at the end: confirmed, extended once, or exited |
| Acknowledgement | Signed by the employee, with their comment if they disagree |

A PIP with no support is a termination with extra steps, and it reads that way to
anyone reviewing it - including a tribunal.

## The conversation itself

- **Private, seated, no audience, 30 minutes booked.** Never in a corridor, never
  on a Friday evening.
- **One message.** If there are three problems, pick the two that matter; a
  five-item list reads as a case being built.
- **Expect emotion, and do not fill the silence.** "I understand this is not what
  you wanted to hear" and then wait.
- **No surprises about consequence.** If the outcome could be exit, say so plainly
  and kindly, once.
- **Write it down the same day**, while the facts are exact, and send the summary
  within 24 hours.

## India reality

- **There is no at-will employment.** The appointment letter, the standing orders
  where they apply, and state Shops and Establishments rules govern notice,
  probation and termination. Do not paraphrase them from memory.
- **Documentation decides disputes.** Dated notes, acknowledged emails and a PIP
  with reviews are what a labour consultant will ask for first.
- **Full-and-final settlement and the relieving letter** are the two things a
  departing employee needs; delaying them converts a clean exit into a claim.
- **Notice period and leave adjustment** are contractual; check the letter before
  quoting a number.
- **POSH and harassment allegations are not performance issues.** Route them to
  the internal committee immediately and do not investigate here.

## Anti-patterns

- **The ambush PIP.** A plan produced the day after a bad quarter, with no prior
  conversation, is the weakest version of this document.
- **The vague gap.** "Needs to be more proactive" cannot be measured, supported or
  defended.
- **The moving goalposts.** Changing measures mid-PIP voids the process.
- **The public correction.** Feedback in a team channel costs more than the error.
- **The manager who delegates the hard conversation** to HR, a peer, or a message.
- **The silent exit.** Letting probation lapse and hoping the person leaves avoids
  nothing and postpones everything.
