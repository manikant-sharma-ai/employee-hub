# Titles and search words

A title has one job: contain the words the buyer types, in the order they matter,
inside the character limit.

## The structure that works on Indian marketplaces

```
[Brand] [Product type] [Key differentiator] [Material / size / quantity] [Use case]
```

- **Brand** first where the brand is searched; later where it is not. A buyer searching "cotton kurta for women" does not care about an unknown brand; a buyer searching the brand name does.
- **Product type** in the buyer's words, not the catalogue's. "Kurta", not "ethnic upper garment".
- **Differentiator** - the one thing that makes this the right result: block print, organic, 500 ml, pack of 6, 5-star rated.
- **Material, size, quantity** - because these are the filters buyers use.
- **Use case** last, if characters remain.

## Where the words come from

1. **The channel's own search suggestions** - type the product into the search box and record what autocompletes. That is the buyer's phrasing, ranked.
2. **The category's filter labels** - the filter names are the search terms the channel itself indexes.
3. **The buyer's own words in enquiries and returns** - "kurta that does not shrink", "bottle that fits a car holder".
4. **A competitor's top three listings** - to see which words rank, never to copy the sentence.

Do not invent synonyms the market does not use. "Kurta" is searched; "tunic" is
not, in India, at scale.

## What to avoid

- **Keyword stuffing** - "kurta kurti women girls ethnic party wear casual office" reads as spam and is ranked down on Flipkart and Amazon.
- **ALL CAPS and symbols** - `!!!`, `***`, `₹₹₹`, emoji.
- **Promotional language in the title** - "sale", "free shipping", "best price", "limited offer".
- **Unverifiable claims** - best, no. 1, guaranteed, original (unless you are the brand owner).
- **Size or colour in the title where variations exist** - the variation carries it; putting it in the title creates duplicates.
- **The channel's own name** in the title.

## Character limits in practice

Write to the limit, not over it. Amazon truncates in mobile search results at
roughly 80 characters, so the first 80 must contain brand, product type and the
differentiator. Flipkart's 120 is the hard limit and mobile truncates earlier.
IndiaMART titles work best at 40-70 characters with the trade term included.

## The test

Show the title to someone who sells the category and ask one question: "what did
you search to find this?" If their answer is not in the title, rewrite it.
