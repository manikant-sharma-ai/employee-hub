# Employment and consultant agreements

Two different documents with two different risks. The most common SMB mistake is
using one for the other.

## Employee vs consultant - the test that matters

| Question | Employee | Consultant |
|---|---|---|
| Who controls when and how the work is done? | The business | The person |
| Exclusive? | Usually | Usually not |
| Paid a fixed monthly amount? | Yes | Per deliverable or per day |
| Tools and workplace provided? | Yes | Their own |
| Statutory deductions (PF, ESI, TDS on salary)? | Yes | TDS under 194C/194J |
| Notice and severance | Governed by the appointment letter and law | Governed by the contract term |

A "consultant" who works 9-to-6, exclusively, with a company laptop is an employee
in substance. Getting this wrong is a PF and gratuity liability, not a paperwork error.

## The employment agreement - the clauses that matter

- **Role, reporting line and location**, with a clause allowing reasonable change.
- **Compensation structure**: fixed, variable, the formula for the variable, and the payout date.
- **Working hours, leave and holidays**, referring to the company policy as a separate, dated document.
- **Probation and confirmation**, with the notice period during and after.
- **Confidentiality**, with a defined term and what survives termination.
- **IP assignment**: work created in the course of employment belongs to the business, stated plainly.
- **Non-compete**: post-employment restraints are generally not enforceable in India; do not rely on them. Protect with confidentiality, garden leave or a notice period instead.
- **Non-solicitation** of clients and colleagues, with a reasonable, time-limited scope.
- **Termination**: notice, immediate termination for cause, and final settlement timing.
- **Dispute resolution**: seat of arbitration or jurisdiction, named.

## The consultant agreement - different clauses

- **Scope with deliverables and acceptance criteria**, not "services as required".
- **Term and renewal**, with a stated end date.
- **Fees, invoicing, payment terms, and who bears GST.**
- **TDS applicability**, stated so the vendor does not claim short payment.
- **IP assignment on payment**, not on signature.
- **No employment relationship** clause - helpful, but never decisive on its own.
- **Confidentiality and data handling**, especially if they touch customer data.
- **Non-solicit of your clients**, time-limited.
- **Termination for convenience**, with notice and payment for work done.

## India-specific practicalities

- **Stamping**: agreements may need stamping per the state's Stamp Act; an unstamped agreement is a problem in enforcement, not a nullity. Route the amount to the CA.
- **E-signature** is valid under the IT Act for most commercial agreements; keep the audit trail.
- **Offer letters vs appointment letters**: the offer is the promise, the appointment letter is the contract. Both should agree.
- **Statutory registers and the Shops and Establishments Act** apply by state; the contract does not override them.

## Guardrails

This file is a drafting checklist, not legal advice. Every employment agreement
that involves equity, restraint, or a senior hire needs a lawyer's review, and the
final document is signed by the business, not by the drafter.

## Anti-patterns

- A consultant agreement used to avoid PF, with a full-time schedule.
- A non-compete with a two-year, pan-India restraint that will not survive.
- IP assignment missing, so the contractor owns the code.
- Notice period different in the offer letter and the appointment letter.
- No acceptance criteria, so "delivered" is an argument.
