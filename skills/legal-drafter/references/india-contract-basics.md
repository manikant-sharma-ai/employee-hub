# India contract basics

The mechanics that decide whether a well-drafted agreement is also a usable one. Every item here is framed as a question to confirm, because rates, forms and thresholds change and vary by state.

## Formation and formality

- **A contract can be oral.** Most commercial contracts in India are enforceable without writing. Writing exists to prove terms, not to create the obligation.
- **Writing is effectively required** for anything with a term over a year, an arbitration clause, an IP assignment, or a transfer of immovable property.
- **Consideration** must be real; a contract with no consideration is not a contract, whatever it says.
- **Authority to sign** matters more than the signature: check the signatory's designation, and for a company, whether the board or a power of attorney authorises it.

## Stamp duty

Stamp duty is a state subject and depends on the instrument and the state of execution. Practical approach:

| Question | What to do |
|---|---|
| Which state's stamp law applies? | The state where the instrument is executed, or where the property is, depending on the instrument |
| What is the rate? | Ask the CA or the sub-registrar - never quote a rate as fact in a draft |
| Can it be paid online? | In most states, yes, through the state's e-stamp portal |
| What if it is unstamped? | An instrument that is not duly stamped may be inadmissible as evidence until duty and penalty are paid - a real problem when you need to enforce it |
| Is an agreement to agree stampable? | An MSA often is; an SOW under it usually is not separately stamped |

Put a line in the draft: "Stamp duty to be borne by <party>, as advised." Do not compute it.

## E-signature

- **Electronic signatures are recognised** for most commercial documents under Indian law, through the IT Act framework, but the reliable route for enforceability is a certified electronic signature service provider or an Aadhaar-based e-sign with a proper audit trail.
- **A scanned signature page pasted into a PDF** is common, weak, and contested when a deal sours.
- **Not permitted** for negotiable instruments, wills, trusts and certain registrable documents.
- **Keep the audit trail**: signer identity, timestamp, IP, and the document hash. A signature without a trail is an assertion.

## Dispute resolution

| Route | Use when | The clause must say |
|---|---|---|
| Courts | Small value, or a relationship that will not survive arbitration | Which court, by name: "the courts at Bengaluru shall have exclusive jurisdiction" |
| Arbitration | Cross-border, confidential, or a technical dispute | Seat, number of arbitrators, appointment method, language, governing rules |
| Mediation first | Relationship worth preserving | A defined negotiation period before either route |

Common defects that make an arbitration clause useless: no seat named; two different institutions named; a clause that says "arbitration or courts" at the option of one party; a fee split that makes arbitration uneconomic for the amount in dispute. Arbitration is expensive - for a ₹5 lakh dispute it is usually the wrong route, and saying so is part of the job.

## Tax clauses to include

- **GST**: state whether the consideration is inclusive or exclusive, the rate where known, and that the recipient will provide a GSTIN for a tax invoice.
- **TDS**: state that the payer may deduct tax at source as required, and that the deduction is not a breach of the payment obligation. Without this, a vendor can claim short payment.
- **Place of supply**: for services across states, this determines CGST+SGST versus IGST; get it wrong and the invoice is defective.
- **Reimbursements**: state whether they are part of the value of supply - the treatment differs and it changes the tax.

The rates and thresholds are the CA's call. The clause is yours.

## Restrictive covenants

- **Non-compete during employment** is generally enforceable; **post-employment non-compete** is generally not, in Indian courts, absent specific protectable interests.
- **Non-solicit** of clients and employees is more defensible than a non-compete and is what most businesses actually need.
- **Garden leave** and notice periods are the practical alternative to a non-compete.
- Never draft a restraint that a court will strike down and present it as protection. Say plainly that the enforceable protection is confidentiality plus notice.

## Employment versus contractor

Three tests decide it, in substance not in the label: control over how the work is done, who provides the tools and the economic risk, and whether the person is integrated into the business. Misclassification exposes the business to PF, ESI, professional tax and TDS consequences. Flag it, do not decide it.

## Anti-patterns

- **A governing law of England with an Indian seat of arbitration** and no explanation.
- **"Subject to Mumbai jurisdiction"** in a contract with a Delhi counterparty who never noticed - the clause is enforceable and both sides will regret it.
- **An unstamped agreement** relied on years later in a recovery suit.
- **An arbitration clause for a ₹2 lakh dispute** where the arbitration costs exceed the claim.
- **A contract signed by an employee with no authority**, which the company later disowns.
- **Stating a statutory threshold as fact.** Say "confirm with the CA" and move on.
