# IP and brand protection

A brand is an asset only if the business owns it and can prove it. Most SMBs discover
the gap when someone else uses their name, or when an investor asks for the paperwork.

## The four kinds of protection

| Asset | Mechanism | Where | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brand name, logo | Trade mark | Class-wise, per jurisdiction | 10 years, renewable |
| Inventions, processes | Patent | Per jurisdiction | 20 years |
| Creative work, code, copy | Copyright | Automatic on creation, registration helps evidence | Life + 60 years in India |
| Confidential information | Contract and practice | - | While secret |

## Trade marks, the practical sequence

1. **Search before you print** - the registry, plus a web and marketplace search for the same name in the same category.
2. **Pick the classes that matter** - a brand selling clothes and also running a cafe needs both classes.
3. **File, then use the ™ symbol.** ® only after registration is granted; using it early is an offence.
4. **Watch the journal** for conflicting applications during the opposition window.
5. **Renew at 10 years** - a diary entry, not a memory.

## What the business actually owns

- **Freelancer and agency work**: copyright vests in the creator unless assigned in writing. Every design, website and video needs an assignment clause.
- **Employee work**: belongs to the employer for work done in the course of employment, but a written IP clause removes the argument.
- **Stock assets**: the licence decides usage - commercial use, print runs, resale, modification. Keep the licence with the file.
- **AI-generated assets**: the ownership position is unsettled; assume you own the output only as far as the tool's terms allow, and never rely on it for a trade mark.

## The everyday protections

- **NDA before a detailed conversation** with a vendor, an agency or a prospective partner.
- **Confidentiality clause in every contractor agreement**, surviving termination.
- **Access hygiene**: shared drives with permissions, no client data in personal email, no brand assets in a personal Canva account.
- **Domain and handle registration** in the business's own name, not the developer's. This is the single most common hostage situation.

## When someone copies you

1. Gather evidence: screenshots with dates, listings, the infringing use.
2. Send a cease-and-desist through a lawyer, not from a personal inbox.
3. If a marketplace, use its IP complaint process - usually faster than court.
4. Register first if you have not. Enforcement is much harder without a registration.

## Guardrails

Filing strategy, class selection and enforcement are a lawyer's work. This file is
what to gather and what to ask, not a substitute for the filing.

## Anti-patterns

- A logo designed by a freelancer with no assignment clause.
- Domain and social handles registered under the agency's account.
- ® used before registration.
- No search before printing 5,000 boxes with a name someone else owns.
- Trade mark filed in one class, used across five.
