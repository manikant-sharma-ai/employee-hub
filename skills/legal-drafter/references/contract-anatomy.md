# Contract anatomy

A contract is a machine for allocating risk. Each section exists because something went wrong once, for someone, and they wrote a clause about it. Knowing what each section is for is what lets you draft quickly without missing the part that matters.

## The sections, in order

| # | Section | Its job | The mistake |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Parties | Exact legal names, entity type, address | A brand name instead of the registered entity - unenforceable against nobody |
| 2 | Recitals | One paragraph of context | Five "whereas" paragraphs that contradict the operative clauses |
| 3 | Definitions | Fix the meaning of every capitalised term | Terms used before being defined, or defined twice differently |
| 4 | Scope | What is being done, and what is not | A scope so broad it is unpriceable, or so vague it is unarguable |
| 5 | Obligations | Who does what, by when, to what standard | "Reasonable efforts" with no milestone anywhere |
| 6 | Payment | Amount, currency, GST, invoice timing, due date, late consequence | "Payment as agreed" - there is no such thing |
| 7 | Term and termination | Start, duration, notice, for-cause and for-convenience exit | No exit at all, or exit only for the other side |
| 8 | IP | Who owns what was created, and what was licensed | Silence, which defaults to the author in most systems |
| 9 | Confidentiality | What is protected, for how long, with what exceptions | An NDA that forbids the recipient from telling its own lawyer |
| 10 | Liability | Cap, exclusions, carve-outs | Unlimited liability accepted in a template nobody read |
| 11 | Indemnity | Who covers third-party claims | An indemnity wider than the insurance behind it |
| 12 | Force majeure | What excuses performance | A list that excludes the obvious local risks |
| 13 | Dispute resolution | Notice, negotiation, arbitration or courts, seat, language | An arbitration clause with no seat - unworkable when needed |
| 14 | Governing law and jurisdiction | Which law, which courts | Two different laws named in two clauses |
| 15 | Notices | Where a formal notice goes | An email address nobody monitors |
| 16 | Entire agreement, amendment, severability, assignment | The boilerplate that decides what counts | Left out because it "looks standard" |
| 17 | Signatures and schedules | Authority, date, the details in annexures | Signed by someone with no authority to bind |

## Length by document type

| Document | Pages | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Mutual NDA | 2-3 | Longer NDAs are not stronger, they are less read |
| One-way NDA | 2 | Vendor or contractor seeing your data |
| Service agreement, single engagement | 4-6 | Scope in a schedule, not in the body |
| Master services agreement + SOW | 8-10 + 2 per SOW | The MSA is stable, the SOW changes |
| Retainer | 3-4 | Monthly scope, hours, and what happens when they run out |
| Website terms and privacy policy | 3-6 | Written for a reader, not for a court |
| Internal policy | 1-3 | One page that is followed beats six that are not |

## The five-line deal test

Before drafting, write these five lines. If any line is missing, ask.

1. **Party A** will provide **<deliverable>** to **Party B**.
2. By **<date or milestone>**.
3. For **₹<amount>**, paid **<when, how>**.
4. The deliverable is accepted when **<objective test>**.
5. If it is late or rejected, **<consequence>**.

Line 4 is the one people skip, and it is the one that produces the argument.

## Drafting order that avoids rework

Definitions last but placed first. Draft the operative clauses, then read them for every capitalised term, then write the definitions from that list. Definitions written first tend to describe a deal that the clauses then contradict.

## Anti-patterns

- **Copying a foreign template.** It will reference a jurisdiction, a statute and a signing formality that do not apply here.
- **A 20-page contract for a ₹40,000 job.** The cost of reading it exceeds the risk it allocates.
- **Schedules left as placeholders.** The schedule is where the deal actually lives.
- **Two documents that both claim to be the agreement** - a proposal and a contract - with different payment terms.
- **"Standard terms" referenced but not attached.**
- **A recital that promises something the operative clauses then exclude.**
