# Policy drafting

A policy is not a legal document that happens to be read; it is an instruction that happens to be legal. If the person it applies to cannot follow it, it is decoration with a compliance claim attached.

## The three kinds

| Kind | Who it binds | Test it must pass | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customer-facing terms | Users of a product or site | Can a normal person understand what they agreed to? | 3-6 pages |
| Privacy policy | The business, towards users and a regulator | Does it describe what is actually done, not what is aspirational? | 2-4 pages |
| Internal policy | Employees and contractors | Can a manager apply it without asking? | 1-3 pages |

## Website terms of service

Required sections, in this order: who the business is and how to contact it; what the service is; account and eligibility; acceptable use and prohibited conduct; payment, pricing and refunds if applicable; intellectual property; user content and licence; disclaimers; limitation of liability; termination; changes to terms; governing law and jurisdiction; grievance officer where required.

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| Link terms at the point of payment, not only in the footer | Incorporation by reference is contested if the user never saw them |
| State the refund policy explicitly | Silence becomes a consumer-protection problem |
| Name the grievance officer with contact details, if the business is an intermediary | Required in substance for intermediaries under Indian rules |
| Date the version and keep old versions | "Terms as of" is evidence; an undated page is not |
| No clause that removes a consumer's statutory right | Void, and it undermines the rest |

## Privacy policy

Describe what is actually collected, not what a template collects:

1. What data is collected - account, payment, device, cookies, location, communications.
2. Why - each purpose, tied to a real feature.
3. Who it is shared with - payment gateway, courier, analytics, CRM, cloud host. Name categories, not just "partners".
4. Where it is stored - country matters for cross-border transfer.
5. How long it is kept, and what happens at the end.
6. How a person exercises their rights - access, correction, deletion, withdrawal of consent.
7. How to contact the person responsible for data.
8. Security measures, described honestly and without promising perfection.

The legal specifics of India's data-protection regime - consent notices, breach notification, the role of a consent manager - are the territory of `data-privacy-officer` and a lawyer. Draft the policy, and flag the parts that depend on a compliance decision.

## Internal policies

The five most useful to a small business, in order:

| Policy | Core content | The line that makes it work |
|---|---|---|
| Leave | Types, accrual, notice, carry-forward, encashment | "Apply 7 days ahead except for sick leave" |
| Expenses and reimbursement | What is allowed, limits, receipts, approval, timeline | "No receipt, no reimbursement, except local travel under ₹500" |
| Working hours and remote | Core hours, expectations, attendance | "Available 10am-6pm; the rest is your judgement" |
| POSH / prevention of harassment | Definition, committee, how to complain, no retaliation | Named committee members and a phone number |
| IT and data use | Devices, passwords, what may be installed, what is monitored | "Company data stays in company systems" |

Each one: purpose in one line, who it applies to, the rule, the exception process, the consequence, the owner, the review date.

## Drafting rules for any policy

- **Second person.** "You must" and "we will". Not "the employee is required to".
- **One rule per paragraph**, numbered so it can be cited in an email.
- **Every rule has an exception route.** A policy with no exception process is ignored at the first real case.
- **State the consequence.** "Repeated non-compliance may lead to disciplinary action" - not a threat, a boundary.
- **Name the owner and the review date** at the bottom. A policy with no owner is dead in a year.
- **Match the practice.** A policy that says something the business does not do is evidence against the business in a dispute.

## The one-hour policy test

Give it to someone who has to follow it and ask: what do I do on Monday? If they cannot answer, rewrite. If they ask a question the policy should have answered, add the answer.

## Anti-patterns

- **Copying another company's privacy policy**, complete with their vendors and their country.
- **A 40-page employee handbook for a team of 6.** Nobody reads it, and the one clause that mattered is unfindable.
- **A policy that promises a service level the business does not meet.**
- **A "zero tolerance" clause with no process behind it.**
- **Terms with no date, no version and no governing law.**
- **A policy written to protect the company from its employees rather than to tell them what to do.**
