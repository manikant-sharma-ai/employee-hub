# NDA and confidentiality

An NDA does one job: it lets two parties talk honestly without either of them losing the information they shared. Everything else in an NDA is decoration, and decoration is what makes people refuse to sign.

## The two directions

| Type | Use when | Who is bound |
|---|---|---|
| Mutual (bilateral) | Two businesses exploring a deal, sharing pricing, plans, data | Both, symmetrically |
| One-way | A vendor, contractor, intern or advisor sees your information and shares nothing of their own | Only the recipient |

Sending a one-way NDA to a prospective partner signals distrust and slows the deal. Use mutual unless there is a genuine asymmetry.

## The six clauses that matter

1. **Definition of confidential information.** Specific categories, plus a catch-all "any information marked or confirmed in writing as confidential within 7 days". A definition that covers everything is unenforceable; one that covers nothing is useless.
2. **Exclusions.** Independently developed, publicly available, already known, received from a third party without breach, or required to be disclosed by law. Without these, the NDA is not signable by a company with a compliance function.
3. **Obligations.** Use only for the stated purpose, protect with at least reasonable care, share only with those who need it and are bound, no reverse engineering.
4. **Term.** Typically 2-3 years for the confidentiality obligation, with trade secrets continuing while they remain secret. Perpetual NDAs get redlined and delay the deal.
5. **Return or destruction.** On request or termination, with a carve-out for copies required by law or by a backup policy.
6. **Remedies.** Injunctive relief acknowledged as appropriate, and no requirement to prove actual loss - this is the clause that gives the NDA teeth.

## What an NDA must never contain

- **A non-compete in disguise.** "The recipient shall not engage in any similar business for two years" is a restraint of trade, and Indian courts read such clauses narrowly. It belongs in an employment or share-purchase agreement, drafted by a lawyer, not smuggled into an NDA.
- **A non-solicit of all employees**, in a mutual NDA with a prospective partner. It reads as bad faith.
- **A ban on disclosing unlawful conduct.** An NDA cannot stop a person reporting an offence, and drafting one that tries is a serious problem.
- **An assignment of IP.** Confidentiality is not ownership; if the deal needs IP assignment, that is a separate clause with its own consideration.
- **A penalty clause** for breach - a fixed sum payable on breach is generally treated as a penalty and is unenforceable; liquidated damages must be a genuine pre-estimate.
- **A restriction on the recipient's own lawyer, auditor or regulator**, without a carve-out.

## Practical clauses people forget

| Clause | Why |
|---|---|
| Residuals | Knowledge retained in unaided memory may be used; without it, engineers refuse to sign |
| Compelled disclosure | Notice to the discloser where lawful, so they can seek protection |
| No licence | Nothing in the NDA grants rights to the information |
| No obligation to proceed | Neither party is committed to the deal |
| Governing law and seat | An NDA with no forum is a letter of intent |
| Counterparts and e-signature | So it can be signed in two cities on the same day |

## Stamping and formality

An NDA is an instrument that may attract stamp duty in some states, and the amount depends on the state and the instrument type. Treat this as a question for the CA or a lawyer, and in practice most commercial NDAs are executed on plain paper with a nominal stamp. Never state a rate as fact. If the counterparty is a large company, follow their process - they will have one.

## When an NDA is the wrong instrument

- **A one-off small purchase.** The invoice terms cover it.
- **An employee.** The confidentiality clause belongs in the employment contract, along with IP assignment and exclusivity.
- **A regulated disclosure.** A data-processing agreement is the right instrument when personal data is being handled; an NDA does not satisfy DPDP obligations.
- **A deal where you are the one who needs to see their data.** Sign theirs rather than sending yours.

## Anti-patterns

- **A 12-page NDA for a 30-minute intro call.** Nobody signs it and the call does not happen.
- **"Confidential information" defined as "all information of any kind".** Unenforceable and reads as amateur.
- **A perpetual term with no exclusions.** Redlined every time.
- **Sending an NDA before saying hello.** The relationship is the enforcement mechanism; the NDA is the reminder.
- **Assuming an NDA covers personal data.** It does not; separate obligations apply.
