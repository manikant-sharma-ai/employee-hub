# Chart of accounts and coding

The chart of accounts is the business's vocabulary. If it is 400 ledgers deep, nobody uses it consistently and every report needs an explanation.

## The shape that works for a small business

Six groups, roughly 40-60 ledgers total:

| Group | Ledgers |
|---|---|
| Income | Product sales, service income, other income, discounts given |
| Direct costs | Purchases, freight inward, direct labour, subcontract |
| People | Salaries, contractor fees, staff welfare, recruitment |
| Premises | Rent, electricity, water, repairs, security |
| Selling | Advertising, commissions, gateway fees, travel, samples |
| Admin | Software, professional fees, bank charges, telephone, printing, insurance |
| Tax | Input CGST/SGST/IGST, output CGST/SGST/IGST, TDS payable, TDS receivable |
| Balance sheet | Bank, cash, debtors, creditors, stock, fixed assets, loans, capital |

If a business needs a hundred expense ledgers, it usually needs cost centres instead - one "advertising" ledger split by channel, not fourteen advertising ledgers.

## The coding rules

1. **Code by what it was for, not by who was paid.** A payment to Amazon is not "Amazon"; it is office supplies or software.
2. **Code to the narrowest existing ledger that is true.** Do not stretch "admin expenses" to cover a purchase of stock.
3. **Recurring vendor, stable ledger.** The same vendor's monthly bill goes to the same place every month, or the trend is meaningless.
4. **Capital versus revenue.** Anything used for more than a year and above the business's capitalisation threshold is an asset, not an expense. Agree the threshold once and record it.
5. **One ledger for one thing.** Two ledgers for the same expense is how the P&L ends up with two small numbers instead of one real one.

## The judgement calls, and how to make them

| Question | Test | If still unsure |
|---|---|---|
| Stock purchase or expense? | Will it be resold, or consumed in operations? | Resale = purchase; consumed = expense |
| Repairs or improvement? | Does it extend life or capacity? | Extends = capitalise; restores = expense |
| Freight inward or outward? | Getting it in, or getting it to the customer? | Inward adds to stock cost; outward is selling |
| Staff meal or staff welfare? | Part of employment or an event? | Either way it is not "office expenses" |
| Software: asset or expense? | Annual subscription = expense; perpetual licence = asset | Confirm with CA for large amounts |
| Professional fees: legal or admin? | Was it a specific matter? | Code to the matter, not the catch-all |

## When no ledger fits

Do not invent one. Write the question instead: "₹18,400 to a printing vendor for the trade-show stand - is this marketing or a fixed asset? Suggested: marketing, since it is used once."

One clear question per item, with a recommendation, gets answered in a minute. A new ledger invented at midnight gets renamed by the accountant and the history is lost.

## Cleaning a chart that has grown wild

1. Export the trial balance for the last twelve months.
2. List ledgers with fewer than three entries or under ₹5,000 annual value - candidates to merge.
3. Group them by the six groups above and propose a merge map: old ledger → new ledger.
4. Get it approved before any reclassification, then post one reclassification entry per merged ledger with a note.
5. Rename, never delete. Deleted ledgers orphan the history behind them.

## Anti-patterns

- **A ledger per vendor.** The vendor belongs in the narration or a party ledger, not in the expense head.
- **"Miscellaneous expenses" with 200 entries.** It is a refusal to categorise.
- **Recoding last year's books to make this year's report look better.** That is not bookkeeping.
- **Cost centres for a five-person business.** They add work and produce no decision.
- **A chart copied from another company's software template** with 300 heads nobody recognises.
