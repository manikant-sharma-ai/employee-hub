# Common entry errors

Ten mistakes account for most of what an accountant sends back. Run this list before handing over a period - it takes fifteen minutes and saves a week.

## The ten, in order of frequency

| # | Error | How to spot it | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Duplicate bill | Same vendor, similar amount, within 30 days, different number | Check the invoice number against the books before entering; never after |
| 2 | Wrong period | Entry date outside the open period, or a March bill entered in April | Post to the document date within the cut-off rule; note it if outside |
| 3 | Missing tax split | One line for a GST-inclusive amount | Reconstruct from the bill; never estimate the split |
| 4 | Payment booked as an expense | Expense ledger debited instead of the creditor | Reverse and re-post: creditor debit, bank credit |
| 5 | Gateway net booked as revenue | Revenue equals the bank credit exactly | Gross revenue, fee as an expense |
| 6 | Round-number guess | ₹50,000 with no bill | Exception list, not an entry |
| 7 | Capital booked as expense | Equipment under "office expenses" | Reclassify to the asset ledger and start depreciation |
| 8 | Owner's personal spend as business expense | Swiggy, a school fee, a holiday | Drawings, not expenses |
| 9 | TDS not deducted, or deducted on the GST-inclusive value | Payment equals the invoice exactly when a 194C bill is in hand | Correct at entry; a late deduction is a penalty |
| 10 | Inter-state GST booked as CGST+SGST | Place of supply differs from the vendor's state | IGST at the full rate; correct before filing |

## The fifteen-minute self-check

1. **Duplicates**: sort the entry schedule by vendor and amount; look for pairs within 30 days.
2. **Period**: filter for dates outside the period; every hit needs a reason.
3. **Zero and round numbers**: filter for amounts ending in 0000 - each one needs a document.
4. **Negative amounts**: a negative debit is usually a reversal that should have been a credit.
5. **Ledgers used once**: a ledger with a single entry is often a typo of an existing one.
6. **Missing narrations**: any entry without a description gets one before handover.
7. **Tax totals**: input GST in the books against the 2B position for the period - the difference is the question for the CA.
8. **Bank balance**: does the closing book balance look plausible against the statement, even before reconciliation? A large gap means a missing entry.

## What to do when you find an error after posting

- **Never edit the posted voucher** to change its amount or date. Post a reversal and a correct entry, both dated today, both with a narration referencing the original voucher number.
- **Tell the accountant in the handover note**, not in a separate message nobody reads.
- **If it affects a filed return**, say so immediately. A GST or TDS error found late has a cost that grows with the delay.

## Errors that are not errors

- **A bank charge you did not know about** - it belongs in the books when the statement shows it.
- **A small difference of a few rupees between the invoice and the payment** - record the difference to a rounding ledger and note it; do not chase a vendor for ₹3.
- **A vendor bill dated before the GST registration date** - no input credit; note it.
- **A payment in a different currency** - record at the transaction rate and note the rate used; the exchange difference is its own entry.

## Anti-patterns

- **Fixing an error by changing the opening balance.** It hides the error in a place nobody looks.
- **A "corrections" journal with twenty lines and no narration.** Six months later it is a mystery.
- **Waiting for the accountant to find the duplicates.** They will, and the whole set becomes suspect.
- **Reversing an entry and forgetting to re-post the correct one.**
- **Treating a failed self-check as a reason to delay the handover.** Hand over with the exception list; silence is worse than an open item.
