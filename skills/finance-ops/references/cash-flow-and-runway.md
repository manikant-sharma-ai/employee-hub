# Cash flow and runway

Profit is an opinion; cash is a fact. A business can be profitable on paper and die
in a month, and this is the file that stops that.

## The weekly cash bridge

```
opening cash
+ collections this week          (money actually received)
- vendor payables due
- payroll and statutory dues
- rent, utilities, subscriptions
- loan EMI / interest
- tax set-aside (GST, TDS, advance tax)
= closing cash
```

Show it in weeks, not months. A month hides the week where payroll and GST land
together, and that is the week that breaks a business.

## The five numbers to watch

| Number | Definition | Warning sign |
|---|---|---|
| Runway | Closing cash ÷ average weekly net burn | Under 13 weeks is urgent |
| Collection period | Average days from invoice to cash | Rising while sales are flat |
| Payables stretch | Days you take to pay vendors | Rising because you cannot pay |
| Cash conversion | Cash collected ÷ revenue billed | Under 85% needs a conversation |
| Committed outflow | Signed obligations for the next 90 days | More than 60% of expected inflow |

## Collections, the highest-leverage work

- **Call the top five overdue accounts first.** They are usually 60-70% of the total.
- **Invoice on time, with the PO number and the right GST details**, or the payment waits a cycle.
- **Offer a small early-payment discount** only when the cash is worth more than the margin.
- **Escalate in writing** at 30, 45 and 60 days, with the original terms quoted.
- **Never let a dispute sit silent** - an argued invoice still gets paid; an ignored one does not.

## Tax and statutory timing

- **GST**: monthly or quarterly by turnover, due the 20th (GSTR-3B). Set aside the day the invoice is raised, not the day it is due.
- **TDS**: deducted at payment, deposited by the 7th of the next month, returns quarterly.
- **Advance tax**: four instalments - June, September, December, March. The one everyone forgets is December.
- **PF/ESI** monthly where applicable. Missing these is a compliance issue, not a cash-flow choice.

## When cash is tight, in order

1. Stop discretionary spend, today.
2. Chase receivables with a named owner and a call schedule.
3. Negotiate payables - ask for terms, do not simply delay.
4. Defer non-critical capex.
5. Reduce or delay owner drawings.
6. Only then, and last, consider debt - and price the cost of it honestly.

## What the readout says

Three lines: runway in weeks, the largest single outflow in the next 30 days, and
the one action this week that improves cash. Everything else is context.

## Anti-patterns

- A cash forecast built on invoiced revenue rather than collections.
- Treating GST collected as available cash.
- A runway in months that ignores a quarterly tax instalment.
- Paying a vendor early while a payroll loan sits unpaid.
- No named owner for collections.
