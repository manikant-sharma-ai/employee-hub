# Short form and hooks

A short post is not a trimmed long post. It is a single move, executed cleanly. The first line does most of the work, and it is the only line that is guaranteed to be read.

## What a short post is actually for

| Goal | What the post must do | Length |
|---|---|---|
| Comments | Take a position someone will disagree with | 80-150 words |
| Saves | Be useful enough to keep | 120-250 words, list or steps |
| Reposts | Say something the reader wishes they had said | 60-120 words |
| Profile visits | Show a specific competence | 150-300 words |
| Replies / DMs | Ask a real question the reader can answer | 40-90 words |
| Traffic | Give one reason to click, not a summary of the article | 50-100 words |

Decide the goal first. A post written for saves and measured on comments will look like a failure.

## The hook

Line one, before the "see more" fold, decides everything. About 100-140 characters is all the reader sees.

| Hook type | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Number + contradiction** | A figure that does not fit expectation | "We cut our support load by 60% by writing one page." |
| **Specific scene** | A time, place and detail | "Tuesday, 11pm. My accountant called about a ₹400 mismatch." |
| **Admission** | A cost the writer paid | "I lost a ₹12 lakh client because I answered in four days." |
| **Direct address** | Names the reader's situation | "If your best salesperson left tomorrow, what would you actually lose?" |
| **Reframe** | Turns a common belief over | "Hiring a marketing person is the most expensive way to avoid writing." |
| **Before/after** | The delta, not the journey | "Six months ago: 3 clients. Today: 19. Same ad budget." |
| **List promise** | Specific, countable, bounded | "Five things I check before signing any vendor contract." |
| **Question with teeth** | Cannot be answered with "yes" | "What did your last price increase actually cost you in churn?" |

Hooks that fail: "I'm excited to announce", "Here's what I learned about...", a question the reader can answer "no" to and scroll on, a vague noun phrase ("On consistency."), anything that starts with context.

## Post architecture

**The workhorse: 6 moves**

1. **Hook** - one line, stands alone.
2. **Tension** - why this is not obvious, in one or two lines.
3. **The substance** - the specific thing: the number, the steps, the mechanism.
4. **The turn** - the reframe, the cost, or the part that surprised you.
5. **The proof** - one concrete detail that shows it is real.
6. **The close** - a question or one action. Not a summary.

**Line breaks are structure.** One idea per line in the first five lines. White space is what makes a post scannable; a 900-character paragraph gets skipped.

## Platform differences that matter

| Platform | What changes |
|---|---|
| LinkedIn | First two lines are the whole game; comments are the metric; links suppress reach, so put the link in a comment or after the fold |
| Instagram caption | The first 125 characters show; visual carries the hook, caption carries the detail |
| X / Twitter | One idea per post; threads only when the sequence is genuinely sequential |
| WhatsApp broadcast | Under 300 characters, one offer, one link; people forward, so make it forwardable |
| Email subject | Under 45 characters for mobile; the preview text is a second subject line, use it |
| Newsletter intro | One paragraph, the promise of the issue, no throat-clearing |

## Captions

- **Lead with the thing the image cannot say.** If the image already shows the product, the caption should not describe it.
- **First line must survive truncation.** Test the first 125 characters alone.
- **One CTA**, and make it the smallest possible action.
- **Hashtags: 0-5, relevant.** Twenty hashtags reads as spam on every platform now.
- **Credits and disclosures** at the end, small, but present where required.

## Editing short form

- Cut the first line if it is context. Then the post has a new first line - check it again.
- Remove every sentence that only sets up another sentence.
- One idea. If you find two, that is two posts.
- Read it as if you were scrolling. Would you stop? If not, the hook is the problem, not the body.
- End on the strongest sentence. Move it up if needed.

## Anti-patterns

- The LinkedIn "broetry" ladder - one word per line for twelve lines. It worked in 2021 and now reads as a template.
- Announcing a lesson without the story that earned it.
- Engagement bait with no substance ("Agree?").
- A hook that promises a list and a body that delivers an anecdote.
- Emoji as punctuation on every line.
- Tagging ten people who had nothing to do with the post.
- A post that is a screenshot of a longer post.
- Writing the hook last. Write five hooks, pick one, then write the body to it.
