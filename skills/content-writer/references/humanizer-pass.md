# The humanizer pass

A draft that reads like a machine gets scrolled past, and increasingly it gets filtered. This is the pass that removes the tells. It is not about beating a detector - no edit reliably does that - it is about the reader who has learned to spot the pattern.

Run this after the draft is structurally finished. It is a cutting pass, not a rewriting pass.

## The tells, ranked by how often they appear

| # | Tell | What it looks like | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The "not just X, it's Y" bridge** | "This isn't a tool problem. It's a thinking problem." | Delete the negation, keep the claim |
| 2 | **Stacked triads** | Three adjectives, three examples, three clauses, everywhere | Keep the best one, or the best two |
| 3 | **Performed sincerity** | "And honestly? That changed everything." | Cut, or replace with the specific fact |
| 4 | **The summary paragraph** | A closing paragraph that restates the piece | Delete entirely |
| 5 | **Staccato fragments for drama** | "No fluff. No noise. Just results." | Merge into one sentence |
| 6 | **Uniform paragraph length** | Every paragraph 3-4 sentences | Break the rhythm deliberately |
| 7 | **Vocabulary tells** | delve, leverage, robust, seamless, tapestry, testament, landscape, journey, unlock, elevate, foster, myriad, crucial, pivotal | Replace with the plain word |
| 8 | **Empty intensifiers** | "truly", "genuinely", "incredibly", "remarkably" | Cut |
| 9 | **The hedge-then-assert** | "While there are many factors, the key is..." | Assert |
| 10 | **Symmetrical headings** | Every heading the same length and grammar | Vary them, or make them claims |
| 11 | **"In today's..." openers** | "In today's fast-paced digital landscape" | Start with the scene or the number |
| 12 | **Rhetorical question openers** | "Ever wondered why...?" | Answer it instead |
| 13 | **Em-dash as a personality** | — every — few — words — | Use a comma or a full stop |
| 14 | **The listicle reflex** | Every paragraph becomes bullets | Use prose where it is an argument |
| 15 | **Meta-commentary** | "Let's dive in", "Here's the thing", "But here's where it gets interesting" | Cut |

## Density rule

One tell is invisible. The problem is density.

- **More than one of the top five in 300 words:** the piece reads as machine-written.
- **Three or more vocabulary tells in a paragraph:** rewrite the paragraph, do not swap words.
- **Two consecutive paragraphs with the same shape** (claim, then dash, then reframe): change one.

Count them. A pass that is not counted is a pass that did not happen.

## The mechanical pass

Do these in order. Each is fast and each changes the text.

1. **Delete the first paragraph** and read from the second. If it is better, keep the cut.
2. **Delete the last paragraph.** If the piece still lands, it was a summary.
3. **Cut every adverb of degree.** truly, very, really, quite, extremely, incredibly.
4. **Cut every "very X"** and replace X with the right word, or delete.
5. **Convert one passive construction per paragraph** to active.
6. **Break one long sentence in half and merge two short ones**, so the rhythm is uneven.
7. **Replace one abstract noun with a specific.** "Efficiency" becomes "eleven minutes per invoice".
8. **Add one detail only a person who was there would know.** A time, a name of a role, a number, a hesitation.
9. **Read aloud.** Every place you stumble, rewrite. Every place you would not say it, cut.
10. **Check the opening line is still the strongest sentence.** If not, move the strongest up.

## What to add, not just remove

Removing tells produces clean text. Clean text can still be empty. Add:

- **A number with a date and a unit.**
- **A named constraint** - "we had ₹40,000 and eleven days".
- **An admission of cost** - what went wrong, what was given up.
- **A specific objection and a straight answer.**
- **One sentence shorter than six words.**

Specificity is the only reliable human signal. A machine can be fluent; it is rarely specific about a Tuesday.

## Anti-patterns in the humanizing itself

- Swapping "leverage" for "use" and calling it human. The structure is still a template.
- Adding typos or slang to seem real. It reads as a costume.
- Inserting a personal anecdote you invented. That is fabrication, and it is worse than slop.
- Making every sentence punchy. Uniform short sentences are as robotic as uniform long ones.
- Humanizing the piece but leaving the headings in template form.
- Passing a draft that has no point, on the grounds that it now reads well.
