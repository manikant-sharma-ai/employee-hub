# Distribution and repurposing

Publishing once is the most common way good content is wasted. One strong piece should produce five to eight assets, and that should be planned at the brief, not improvised a week later.

## The one-piece rule

Write the piece for the deepest placement, then cut down. Never the reverse - you cannot grow a caption into an argument.

```
Long piece (blog, 1200-1600 words)
  ├── LinkedIn post      - the strongest single claim, 150 words
  ├── 5-slide carousel   - the argument in five steps, one per slide
  ├── Newsletter issue   - the long piece plus a short intro and one ask
  ├── 3 short posts      - one claim each, over three weeks
  ├── WhatsApp broadcast - the offer or the single takeaway, 300 characters
  ├── Email to list      - the problem and the link, 120 words
  └── Sales one-pager    - the proof and the mechanism, for the pipeline
```

## Sequencing a launch

| Day | Asset | Why this order |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Long piece published | The thing everything points at |
| 0 | Email to the list | Highest-intent audience first |
| 1 | LinkedIn post, strongest claim | Drives the first wave of traffic |
| 2 | Carousel | For the audience that does not click links |
| 4 | Short post, second claim | Second wave, different entry point |
| 7 | WhatsApp broadcast | The warm network, once, not twice |
| 10 | Newsletter roundup | Catches the people who missed it |
| 14 | Short post, third claim | Long tail |
| 21 | Sales one-pager | Feeds the pipeline, not the feed |

Two weeks of distribution from one writing session. The writing is the expensive part; the distribution is the part people skip.

## Cutting rules per channel

| Channel | Take | Leave behind |
|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn post | The single most contestable claim plus the proof | The methodology, the caveats, the full data |
| Carousel | Five steps, one idea per slide, 12-25 words each | Nuance that needs a paragraph |
| Short post | One number or one scene | Everything else |
| Newsletter | The piece, a two-line why-now, one ask | Nothing - this is the full version for subscribers |
| WhatsApp | The offer and one reason | All argument |
| Sales one-pager | Mechanism and proof | Story, history, philosophy |

## Reformatting, not repeating

The failure mode is the same paragraph pasted four times. Each channel gets a version written for it.

- **Different hook.** The LinkedIn hook is a claim; the email subject is a benefit or a curiosity gap; the WhatsApp line is the offer.
- **Different first sentence.** Never the same opening twice - the audience overlaps more than the analytics suggests.
- **Different CTA per channel.** Follow, read, reply, book, forward. One each.
- **Different length.** A 300-character broadcast is not a truncated post.

## Evergreen vs timely

- **Evergreen pieces** (how-to, framework, comparison) should be revisited every six months: update the numbers, refresh the examples, re-share. This is the cheapest content in the business.
- **Timely pieces** (festival, launch, news reaction) have a 48-hour window and no afterlife. Do not invest a week.
- **Keep a running list** in `notes/decisions.md` of the three evergreen pieces that still bring traffic - those get the next repurposing round.

## Measuring what you cannot see

- Where analytics is connected (GA4, Search Console, LinkedIn, the ESP), record the real numbers: impressions, click-through, replies, conversions.
- Where it is not, record what you do have: replies, DMs, people quoting the piece, sales mentioning it.
- Write "not measured" rather than an estimate. A guessed number in a content report is worse than a gap, because someone will act on it.

## Anti-patterns

- Writing the piece and then asking "where should we post this?" at the end.
- Posting the identical text to LinkedIn, X and Instagram within an hour. The overlap audience sees a bot.
- Repurposing a weak piece five times. Five bad assets is not distribution.
- Building a content calendar of thirty ideas and finishing three.
- Publishing and never revisiting. The best-performing post from six months ago is still your best asset.
- Measuring impressions because they are available, when the goal was replies.
- Letting the carousel designer invent claims the article did not make. The long piece is the source of truth.
