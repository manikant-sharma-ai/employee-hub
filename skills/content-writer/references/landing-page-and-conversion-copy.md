# Landing pages and conversion copy

A landing page is not an article with a button. Every line has one job: keep the
right reader moving toward one action. Everything else is deleted.

## The page, in order

| Block | Job | Length |
|---|---|---|
| Headline | Say what it is and who it is for | Under 12 words |
| Subhead | The mechanism or the proof | 1-2 sentences |
| Hero proof | A number, a logo, a screenshot | One element |
| Problem | The cost of the current situation, in their words | 2-4 lines |
| What it is | The offer, concretely, no adjectives | 3-5 bullets |
| How it works | Three steps maximum | Numbered |
| Proof | Testimonial, case, data, guarantee | One strong, two supporting |
| Objections | The three real reasons they hesitate | FAQ or inline |
| Price | The number, what is included, what is not | Transparent |
| CTA | The same action, worded the same way | Repeated 3-4 times |
| Risk reversal | Guarantee, trial, cancellation | One line |

## Rules that decide conversion

- **One page, one action.** A navigation bar with nine links is nine exits.
- **The headline is the offer**, not a slogan. "GST filing in 2 days for ₹999" beats "Accounting, simplified."
- **Specifics beat superlatives.** "Ships in 48 hours to 12,000 pin codes" beats "fast delivery".
- **Proof must be verifiable.** A testimonial with a name, a role and a photo; a number with its source; a case with the before and after.
- **Objections go on the page, in order of frequency.** The FAQ is where the sale is lost or saved.
- **Price on the page**, unless the product is genuinely bespoke. Hidden pricing reads as expensive.
- **The CTA says what happens next.** "See pricing" and "Start free" are different promises.
- **Mobile first**: 70%+ of Indian traffic. The CTA must be reachable with one thumb, above the fold, and again at the end.

## Message match

The page must continue the ad or the email that brought the reader, in the same
words. A mismatch between the ad's promise and the headline is the single largest
silent leak in paid traffic.

## Copy length, honestly

Long pages work when the purchase is considered, expensive, or unfamiliar. Short
pages work when the reader already knows the product. Decide by the question the
reader is asking, not by taste.

## What to measure before rewriting

Conversion rate by traffic source, scroll depth, which CTA gets clicked, and the
exit points. Rewriting a page without those numbers is a guess with better typography.

## Anti-patterns

- A hero video that says nothing in the first three seconds.
- Six CTAs with five different labels.
- A testimonial with no name and no photo.
- "Revolutionary", "seamless", "world-class" - words that carry no information.
- A form with nine fields above the fold.
- A guarantee buried in the footer.
