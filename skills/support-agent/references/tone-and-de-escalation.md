# Tone and de-escalation

Anger is almost never about the product. It is about being ignored, being made to repeat themselves, or being made to feel stupid. Address those three and most angry customers calm down within one reply.

## What the customer is actually asking for

| What they said | What they need |
|---|---|
| "This is unacceptable" | To be believed, and a named person owning it |
| "I've emailed three times" | An acknowledgement that the process failed, not an explanation of it |
| "I want to speak to your manager" | To feel the decision is not being made by someone powerless |
| "I'm going to post this everywhere" | A reason not to. Usually a fast, concrete commitment |
| "Do you even care?" | Evidence that someone read the specific message |
| "Just give me my money back" | An exit that is not humiliating |

## The five-part reply for an angry customer

1. **Acknowledge the specific thing**, in their words, in the first line. "Your order was supposed to arrive on the 27th and it is now the 31st with no update." Not "we apologise for the inconvenience".
2. **Say what happened**, plainly, if you know it. If you do not know, say that, and say when you will.
3. **Say what you are doing about it**, in one sentence, with a time.
4. **Say what happens if that does not work** - the escalation, or the fallback. This is what removes the fear.
5. **One apology, one place.** Then stop apologising and start solving.

## Language that escalates

| Phrase | Why it inflames |
|---|---|
| "As per our policy..." | Reads as "we have decided not to help you" |
| "Unfortunately..." | Signals a refusal before the sentence arrives |
| "You should have..." | Blames the customer |
| "Calm down" | Guarantees the opposite |
| "I understand your frustration" | The most recognisable scripted line in support |
| "Kindly..." | Formal distance; in Indian English it often reads as cold |
| "We are unable to..." | Passive, and hides who decided |
| "It is out of our hands" | True and useless |

## Language that de-escalates

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| "As per policy" | "Here is what I can do today: ..." |
| "Unfortunately we cannot refund" | "A refund is not available after 30 days, but I can do X or Y - which suits you?" |
| "I understand your frustration" | "Three days with no update is not good enough, and I am sorry." |
| "You should have contacted us earlier" | "The 30-day window closed on the 12th; let me see what I can still do." |
| "Calm down" | Nothing. Do not say it. |
| "We are unable to process" | "I cannot process this myself - Priya in accounts can, and I have asked her to call you today." |

## When they are wrong

Correct the record without a fight. The pattern:

1. **Do not contradict in the first line.** Answer the part that is true first.
2. **State the fact, not the fault.** "The delivery address on the order is the Whitefield flat, and the courier delivered there on the 28th with a photo." Not "you gave us the wrong address".
3. **Offer the path forward without requiring them to admit anything.** "If that is not where you are, tell me and I will have it redirected."
4. **If they insist and you are certain**, hold the line once, plainly, and offer the escalation. Do not argue a second round.

## When the business is wrong

- **Say so in one clean sentence.** "We shipped the wrong size. That is our mistake."
- **Do not explain the internal reason** unless it helps them. "Our warehouse was short-staffed" is not their problem.
- **Offer the fix and, where policy allows, one thing beyond it.** A free return pickup, a small credit. The gesture matters less than the speed.
- **Never ask them to prove it** when the record already shows it.

## Threats to go public

- Take it seriously and do not lecture them about defamation.
- **Reply with speed, not with legal language.** Most public escalations are prevented by a reply within the hour.
- **Move to a private channel**: "May I call you on the number on the order?" Almost everyone says yes, and a five-minute call ends most of these.
- **If it is already public**, respond once, publicly, briefly, with the fix and an invitation to continue privately. Never argue in the comments.
- **Never offer money in exchange for deleting a review.** It breaches platform rules and it reads as a bribe.

## Threats of self-harm or harm to others

Stop the support workflow. Respond with care, directly, without policy language, and provide local crisis support. In India, Tele-MANAS (14416) and KIRAN (1800-599-0019) are the national mental-health helplines; 112 is the emergency number. Escalate to a human immediately. Do not attempt to resolve the commercial issue in the same message.

## Anti-patterns

- A long apology with no information in it. The customer reads it as stalling.
- Explaining the internal process in detail. They did not ask how the sausage is made.
- Copy-pasting a template without changing the first line. They can tell.
- Matching their anger. Never.
- Over-promising to end the conversation: a refund you cannot authorise, a date you cannot meet.
- Going silent after a promise. Silence after "I will update you by 6pm" is worse than the original failure.
- Apologising for something that is not the business's fault in a way that admits liability - "we clearly damaged your product" when the cause is unknown.
