# Indian channel norms

Where Indian customers actually write, how they write, and what a reply has to respect. Channel decides length, tone, timing and how fast you must answer.

## The channels and their rules

| Channel | Typical customer | Expectation | Reply shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| **WhatsApp Business** | Most retail, D2C, services | Reply within minutes during business hours; voice notes are normal | Short, 2-4 lines, no formal salutation, one link |
| **Instagram DM** | D2C, younger buyers | Very fast, casual | 1-3 lines, emoji acceptable if the brand uses them |
| **Marketplace chat** (Amazon, Flipkart) | Marketplace buyers | Fast; platform monitors response time | Formal, factual, no off-platform links or phone numbers - it breaches platform rules |
| **Email** | B2B, invoices, escalations | Within a working day | Structured, with reference numbers |
| **Phone** | Anything urgent or emotional | Answered, or called back same day | Short, and followed by a written summary |
| **Google / Zomato / JustDial review** | Public audience | Within 48 hours | 3-4 lines, public, then move to private |
| **IndiaMART enquiry** | B2B wholesale buyers | Within a few hours - buyers send to many suppliers | Price, MOQ, delivery, one call to action |

## WhatsApp specifically

- **Use the official Business API for anything at scale.** Personal-number broadcasting gets the number banned.
- **Templates need approval** for business-initiated messages outside the 24-hour customer service window. Plan the wording in advance; an unapproved template fails silently.
- **The 24-hour window is the rule that catches people out:** after a customer's last message, you can reply freely for 24 hours; after that, only with an approved template.
- **Voice notes**: reply in text unless the customer clearly prefers voice, then reply in voice - and keep it under 60 seconds.
- **No long paragraphs.** Four lines maximum before people stop reading.
- **One link per message.** Two links looks like spam.
- **Never send a PDF catalogue as the first message.** Send one product image and a question.

## Language and script

- **Reply in the language and script the customer used.** Devanagari in, Devanagari out. Romanised Hindi (Hinglish) in, Hinglish out. Do not "upgrade" them to English.
- **Hinglish is normal and professional** in retail and services. "Aapka order kal dispatch ho jayega, tracking link bhej dunga" is a better reply than a stiff English paragraph for that customer.
- **Match the register, not just the language.** A customer writing "bhai" is not asking for a formal letter.
- **Mixed-language replies are fine** where the product vocabulary is English - "invoice", "tracking", "refund" often stay in English inside a Hindi sentence.
- **Never machine-translate a policy statement** into a language nobody on the team can check. Write it once, properly, and reuse it.
- **Regional scripts matter**: Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Bengali, Marathi, Gujarati. If the business serves those markets, the reply templates should exist in those languages.

## Timing

| Fact | What it means for support |
|---|---|
| Business hours differ by trade - retail often 10am-9pm, B2B 10am-7pm | Publish your hours; answer inside them |
| Sunday is quiet for B2B, busy for D2C and food | Staff accordingly |
| Festival weeks (Diwali, Onam, Pongal) are peak volume and reduced courier capacity | Send proactive delay messages before the complaints arrive |
| Courier networks slow during festivals and monsoon | Do not promise dates the network cannot hold |
| Customers expect a reply within the hour on WhatsApp | If you cannot, set an auto-reply that says when |

## Money and logistics realities

- **COD is a large share of Indian e-commerce.** "Refund" for a COD order means a bank transfer or UPI, not a card reversal - ask for the details, and confirm the account name matches.
- **UPI reversals** are usually fast but depend on the bank; a failed UPI debit is a hold, not a charge.
- **Failed deliveries are frequently address or phone issues**, not courier failures. Confirm the number before blaming the network.
- **RTO (return to origin)** is normal and expensive. A proactive address confirmation message prevents a large share of it.
- **Cash-on-delivery refusals** are a real cost - do not treat them as fraud by default.
- **GST invoice requests** are routine and often urgent for B2B buyers; have the process ready, and never promise an invoice date the accounts team has not agreed.
- **Pincode serviceability** is the single most common pre-sale question. Answer it with a check, not a guess.

## Public reviews

- **Reply to every 1-2 star review within 48 hours**, briefly, without arguing, and move it to a private channel.
- **Reply to some 4-5 star reviews** - short and specific, not a template.
- **Never offer money for deletion.** It breaches platform rules on Google, Zomato and Amazon, and it can be screenshotted.
- **Never identify the customer's order details publicly.**

## Anti-patterns

- A formal English reply to a customer who wrote in Hinglish. It reads as a different company.
- Sending a marketplace buyer a WhatsApp number - it breaches the platform's rules and can get the listing penalised.
- Copy-pasting a WhatsApp broadcast to 500 people from a personal number and losing the number.
- Promising "delivery in 2 days" during Diwali week.
- Auto-replying in English to a Tamil-language message.
- Treating a COD refund like a card reversal and leaving the customer waiting for a credit that was never going to arrive.
- Arguing in a public review thread.
