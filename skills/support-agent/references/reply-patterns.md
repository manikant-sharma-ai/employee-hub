# Reply patterns

The catalogue of situations that make up most of the inbox. Each pattern is a skeleton - fill it with the specific facts, or it reads as a template and the customer will say so.

## The universal skeleton

```
1. The specific fact, in their words        (one line)
2. The answer or the status                 (one or two lines)
3. What happens next, with an owner and a time
4. The one thing you need from them, if any
5. Sign-off with a name
```

Under 150 words. If it needs more, offer a call.

## Order is late

```
Your order #<id> was due on <date> and has not arrived. I have checked: it left our
<city> warehouse on <date> and the courier's last scan was <status> on <date>.

I have raised a trace with <courier> today; they respond within 48 hours. If there is
no movement by <date+2>, I will ship a replacement at no cost rather than keep you waiting.

Nothing needed from you for now - I will message you on <date> either way.
```

Do not: promise a new date you have not been given, blame the courier without having contacted them, or say "it is in transit" when the last scan is five days old.

## Wrong or damaged item

```
You received <wrong item> instead of <ordered item>. That is our error.

I have arranged a <replacement / pickup> for <date>. The courier will collect the
original - it is already packed, so you only need to hand it over.

If you would rather have a refund than a replacement, tell me and I will switch it.
```

Ask for a photo **only** when the damage or the mismatch is not already visible in the record, and ask for one, not five.

## Refund requested

```
You have asked for a refund of ₹<amount> for order #<id>.

<Within policy:> I have initiated it today. It reaches your original payment method in
<5-7 working days> for cards and UPI, and it is the bank's timeline after we release it.
<Outside policy:> The returns window closed on <date>, so a standard refund is not
available. What I can do is <alternative>. If you would like me to ask for an exception,
say so and I will put the case to <owner> today.
```

Never say "the refund has been processed" unless a refund was actually created in a system you can see. "Initiated" and "processed" are different words and customers know it after the first delay.

## Refund not received

```
Your refund of ₹<amount> was released on <date> to <method>, reference <id>.

UPI and wallets usually credit in 24-48 hours; cards take 5-7 working days; netbanking
can take up to 10. If it has not appeared by <date>, send me the bank's reference number
and I will chase it with <gateway> directly.
```

## Payment failed but money deducted

Treat as S2. The money is gone and there is no product.

```
₹<amount> left your account on <date> but the order did not confirm. That is a failed
payment, not a charge - the amount is held by your bank or the gateway, not with us.

I have raised a reversal with <gateway> under reference <id>. It normally returns in
5-7 working days. If it has not by <date>, I will escalate it to their nodal officer.
```

Never ask the customer to pay again before the first amount is resolved.

## Warranty or repair

State the warranty period, what it covers, the nearest service point, and the turnaround. If the product is out of warranty, say the paid-repair option and its approximate cost before they ask.

## Pre-sale question

Answer the question fully, including the honest limitation. A pre-sale question answered with "yes, absolutely" and a wrong product afterwards is an S2 and a return.

## Feature request

```
That is not something we do today. I have logged it for <owner> with your use case.

I will not promise a date - what I can say is that we review these monthly, and I will
tell you if it moves. In the meantime, <workaround> gets you most of the way.
```

Log it. Do not imply it is coming.

## Complaint about a person, praise, and abuse

- **Complaint about staff:** acknowledge, do not defend the colleague, do not name them, route to the operations head, and ask what outcome they want.
- **Praise:** reply briefly and specifically, and ask once whether they would leave a review. Log the quote; do not publish it without written permission.
- **Abuse:** follow `references/tone-and-de-escalation.md`. One reply that solves; if it continues, "I want to help and I will keep helping, but I cannot continue if I am spoken to this way - I can call you instead." Then route to a manager.

## The reply that must never be sent

| Situation | Why | Do instead |
|---|---|---|
| "We have received your query and will revert shortly" | It answers nothing and delays the real reply by a day | Answer the part you can, ask the one thing you need |
| "As per company policy..." as the whole reply | Reads as a refusal | State what you can do |
| "This is a known issue" | Then it should have been communicated before they asked | Say what you are doing and when |
| Any reply with no name at the end | Feels automated | A first name and a role |

## Anti-patterns

- Sending a pattern without the specific facts. The customer can tell, and it doubles the thread.
- Answering three questions in one message and missing the fourth, which was the real one.
- Offering a discount to close a complaint about a broken product. It reads as hush money.
- Ending without a next step or a date.
