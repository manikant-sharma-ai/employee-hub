# Subject lines and previews

The subject and the preview are one sentence split across two fields. Written separately, they repeat each other and waste the most valuable line in the email.

## Length, as the inbox actually renders it

| Field | Safe length | Hard limit | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Subject | 38-50 characters | 60 | Mobile shows about 35-40; Gmail clips around 70 |
| Preview | 40-90 characters | 100 | Gmail appends the first body text if you leave it empty |
| Sender name | 20 characters | 30 | A person's name beats a company name for replies |

In India, a large share of B2C opens happen on Android Gmail, where the subject is truncated earlier than on desktop. Write the first 35 characters to carry the meaning.

## Formulas that work, and why

| Formula | Example | Works because |
|---|---|---|
| Specific number | "4 days to Kochi, ₹99 shipping" | A number is a promise someone can check |
| The reader's words | "Is this OK for a 2-year-old?" | It is the question they already typed |
| Consequence first | "Your GST invoice is missing one thing" | Loss aversion beats gain, mildly |
| Contrarian but true | "Our cheapest plan is not the one you want" | Honesty about a limitation earns the click |
| Deadline, real | "Ends Friday, 6pm IST" | Only works if it is true |
| Question with a stake | "Paying ₹4,000 a month for two tools?" | Names a cost they recognise |

## What fails

- **Vagueness dressed as intrigue** - "You won't believe this". It reads as a newsletter, not a message.
- **All caps and multiple exclamation marks.** Filters and humans both discount them.
- **"Re:" and "Fwd:" on a first contact.** Deceit that costs the domain.
- **Emoji as decoration.** One is fine if the brand uses them; three is a discount flyer.
- **The company name as the subject.** The sender field already says who you are.
- **Fake personalisation** - "Thought you'd like this, [Name]".
- **Claims the body cannot support** - "Save 50%" when it is 50% on one SKU.

## Testing without fooling yourself

1. **One variable per test.** Subject only, or send time only. Not both.
2. **Minimum 1,000 recipients per arm**, or the winner is noise. Below that, test with a holdout over four sends instead.
3. **Judge on clicks or revenue, not opens.** Machine opens (Apple Mail Privacy Protection, corporate scanners) inflate opens by 15-40% and are not evenly distributed.
4. **Decide the winner before the test runs** - which metric, what difference counts.
5. **Stop testing subject lines when the body is the problem.** A great subject on a weak email produces one open and one unsubscribe.

## Subject patterns worth keeping

| Pattern | Template |
|---|---|
| Specific outcome | "<Result> in <timeframe>: <how>" |
| The objection | "<Objection> - here is the answer" |
| The named thing | "<Product>, now in <variant>" |
| The date | "<Event> on <date>: <what to do>" |
| The honest limit | "What <product> cannot do" |
| The reply ask | "One question about your <thing>" |

## Anti-patterns

- Subject and preview saying the same words.
- Preview text left to the template, so it reads "View this email in your browser".
- A subject written after the body and never checked against it.
- Testing four subject lines on 200 people each and declaring a winner.
- Using "free" three times in one subject.
- A subject that only makes sense if you saw the last email.
