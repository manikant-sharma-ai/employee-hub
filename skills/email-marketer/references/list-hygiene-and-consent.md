# List hygiene and consent

The list is the asset. A 4,000-person list that opens is worth more than a
40,000-person list that does not, and it costs less to send to.

## Consent, in plain terms

- **Every address needs a source and a date.** Signup form, checkout opt-in, event, download. If you cannot say where it came from, it is not usable.
- **Consent must be specific and recorded.** "Send me offers" is not the same as "send me the newsletter". Keep the wording that was shown at signup.
- **Purchased, rented or scraped lists are out.** In India the DPDP Act requires a lawful basis and honours withdrawal; a bought list has neither a basis nor a record.
- **B2B is not exempt.** A work email is still personal data.
- **Withdrawal is a right, not a favour.** One click, immediate, and honoured across every sequence.

## The hygiene routine, monthly

1. **Hard bounces** - remove immediately, always. They are dead addresses and a spam signal.
2. **Soft bounces** - remove after three consecutive failures.
3. **Never-opened in 6-12 months** - run a re-permission mail ("still want this?"), then suppress the non-responders. Suppressing them improves deliverability for everyone else.
4. **Complaints** - any address that complains is suppressed forever. Never re-add.
5. **Duplicates and role addresses** - merge duplicates, drop `info@`, `sales@`, `noreply@` from consumer sends.
6. **Field hygiene** - no empty first names, no `test@test.com`, no placeholder rows.

## Segmentation that pays

| Segment | Definition | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Engaged | Opened or clicked in 90 days | Default for every campaign |
| New | Signed up in 30 days, no purchase | Welcome and first offer |
| Buyers | At least one order | Cross-sell, not acquisition offers |
| Lapsed | Bought, then nothing in 180 days | Win-back with a real reason |
| Never engaged | No opens since signup | Re-permission, then suppress |

Send to the smallest segment that can hit the goal. It protects the domain and
makes every number you report mean something.

## Re-permission, done properly

Subject: "Still want these emails?" - one paragraph, one button, one clear
consequence: "If you do nothing, we will stop mailing you. No hard feelings."
That is honest, it is legal, and it recovers 10-20% of a tired list.

## Anti-patterns

- Buying a list "just to test the market".
- Adding event attendees who never opted in to marketing.
- Mailing the whole list because segmentation is effort.
- Making unsubscribe a login-required preference centre.
- Keeping unengaged addresses because the list size looks good in a deck.
