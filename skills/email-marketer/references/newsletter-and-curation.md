# The newsletter and curation

A newsletter is a habit, not a campaign. Its job is to be worth opening when there
is nothing to sell, so that the one time there is something to sell, it gets read.

## The shape of an issue

| Block | Length | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Subject + preview | 40 + 90 chars | Earn the open, honestly |
| Opening line | 2-3 sentences, personal | Continue the subject, establish voice |
| Lead item | 150-250 words | The one thing worth the reader's time |
| Two to four short items | 60-100 words each | Range, without dilution |
| One practical takeaway | 3-5 lines | Something they can use today |
| Sign-off | 1-2 lines, human | A person, not a brand |

## Cadence and consistency

- **Weekly or fortnightly, on the same day.** Predictability is the product.
- **Same length, roughly.** A 300-word issue followed by a 2,000-word essay reads as two newsletters.
- **Miss a week, say so.** Silence with no explanation trains people to forget you exist.

## Curation, which is most of the work

- **One lead, chosen for this list.** Not the most important news in the world; the most useful to these readers.
- **The link is the least valuable part.** Your sentence about why it matters is the value.
- **Kill the filler.** Three strong items beat eight thin ones.
- **Never curate your own content only.** A newsletter that is a catalogue of your blog is an RSS feed with a subject line.
- **Attribute, always**, with the source and the date.

## Editing your own issue

- Read it aloud; cut the first paragraph if it is throat-clearing.
- Every item gets a "so what" line, or it goes.
- One CTA maximum, and usually not in every issue.
- Check every link, on mobile.
- The plain-text version must be readable - some of the list reads it that way.

## Growth that is honest

- **A forward is the best acquisition channel.** Ask once, in the footer, in one line.
- **A landing page with one field**, and a stated cadence.
- **Never buy a list, never import a conference attendee list, never add a customer because they bought once.**
- **Referral asks work when the newsletter is genuinely useful** - so build that first.

## Measuring it

Open rate (trend, not absolute), click rate on the lead item, replies (the strongest
signal), forwards, and unsubscribes per issue. A reply is worth twenty clicks.

## Anti-patterns

- A newsletter that is a sales email in disguise.
- Subject lines that promise a story the issue does not tell.
- Ten items with no opinion about any of them.
- Inconsistent cadence with an apology every third issue.
- No archive, so a new subscriber sees one issue and no reason to stay.
