# Sequence design

A sequence is a series of jobs, not a series of mails. Each mail exists because
the previous one made the next one possible.

## The five sequence types

| Sequence | Length | Job of each mail |
|---|---|---|
| Welcome | 3 | Deliver what was promised, say who you are, give the one next step |
| Lead nurture | 4-6 | Answer the real objections in order, prove with evidence, then offer |
| Abandoned cart | 3 | Remind without nagging, remove the friction, then the deadline |
| Launch | 5-7 | Announce, explain, prove, handle objections, last call, close |
| Re-engagement | 2-3 | Ask if they still want mail, offer a reason to stay, then suppress |

## Writing one mail

1. **The job in one line.** "Prove we actually ship in 3 days." If you cannot write that line, the mail does not belong.
2. **The first line** - the only line most people read after the subject. It continues the subject, it does not restart.
3. **One idea, one proof, one link.** Three paragraphs maximum for a nurture mail.
4. **The CTA as a sentence, not a button label.** "See the three plans" beats "Submit".
5. **The exit.** Every mail should be fine to ignore. Pressure in every mail trains people to unsubscribe.

## Timing

- **Welcome: immediately, then day 2, day 5.** Waiting a week after signup wastes the moment of highest intent.
- **Abandoned cart: 1 hour, 24 hours, 72 hours.** The first one should be helpful, not a discount.
- **Launch: announce, mid, last call.** Do not send seven mails in seven days to a small list.
- **Nurture: 4-7 days apart**, and stop if there is no engagement after the sequence - suppress, do not keep pushing.
- **Respect the timezone of the list**, and one send per day maximum per person across sequences.

## What every sequence needs before it goes live

- [ ] A goal per mail, written
- [ ] The segment defined, with the suppression rules (bought recently, unsubscribed, inactive)
- [ ] Exit conditions - what removes someone mid-sequence (they bought, they replied, they unsubscribed)
- [ ] The measurement - which number decides whether step 3 stays
- [ ] A plain-text version
- [ ] The approval on the offer and the claims

## Anti-patterns

- A sequence that never ends.
- The same CTA in every mail.
- A discount in mail one, which teaches the list to wait for mail three.
- Sending the whole sequence to the whole list regardless of what they did.
- No exit condition, so buyers keep getting "still thinking about it?".
