# Subject lines and open rate

The subject line is the whole product decision compressed into six words. It is
read in a list next to a bank statement and a colleague's reply.

## What the inbox shows

- **Mobile shows roughly 30-40 characters** of subject, then the preview text.
- **Desktop shows 60-70.** Write for the mobile truncation first.
- **The preview text is free real estate** and is usually wasted on "View this email in your browser".

## Patterns that work, and their honest limits

| Pattern | Example | Works when | Fails when |
|---|---|---|---|
| The specific number | "3 things that cut our packaging cost 18%" | The number is real and interesting | The number is small or invented |
| The named thing | "Your Diwali stock plan, in one page" | The segment is expecting exactly this | It is generic to the list |
| The question the reader has | "Is ₹499 too cheap to trust?" | You answer it in the mail | It is clickbait with no answer |
| The deadline | "Closes Friday, 6pm" | There is a real deadline | It is a fake countdown |
| The plain statement | "We changed our delivery partner" | It is genuinely new | It is a sales mail in disguise |
| The curiosity gap | "The one line we deleted from our invoice" | The mail delivers the payoff | The gap is a trick |

## Rules

- **Never a lie.** A subject that promises an order, a refund or a personal message when there is none costs complaints, and complaints cost the domain.
- **No ALL CAPS, no more than one exclamation mark, no "FREE!!!".** Filters and humans both discount them.
- **Personalisation only when the data is reliable.** "Hi {first_name}" with an empty field is worse than no personalisation.
- **Test one variable at a time** - subject only, not subject plus send time plus segment. Otherwise the result means nothing.
- **A split needs volume.** Under about 1,000 recipients per variant, the difference is noise. Say so rather than declaring a winner.
- **Judge on clicks, not opens.** Opens are inflated by image-blocking and Apple's privacy features; a subject that gets opens but no clicks has failed.

## Diagnosing a falling open rate, in order

1. **Deliverability** - is the mail landing in spam or promotions? Check the platform's placement and complaint rate first.
2. **List decay** - are you mailing people who have not opened in a year? That is a suppression problem, not a copy problem.
3. **Frequency** - did you increase sends? Fatigue shows up as opens falling and unsubscribes rising together.
4. **Subject craft** - only then. If 1-3 are clean, the line itself is the problem.
5. **Sender name** - a change from a person's name to a brand name can move opens either way, measurably.

## Anti-patterns

- "Newsletter #47" as a subject.
- Emoji spam.
- The same pattern every week, so the list learns to skip it.
- Testing subjects with no hypothesis. Write down what you expect and why.
