# Long form and YouTube

A 12-minute video is a different craft from a 20-second reel: it earns attention
with structure, not with a hook, and it is judged on retention at the 30-second
mark and the average view duration.

## The structure that holds

| Beat | Time (12-min video) | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Promise | 0:00-0:20 | What the viewer gets, and the proof it is coming |
| Stakes | 0:20-0:50 | Why it matters now, for this person |
| First payoff | 0:50-2:30 | Deliver something real early - this is where retention is won |
| Body | 2:30-9:00 | Three to five chapters, each with its own mini-arc |
| Turn | 9:00-10:30 | The objection, the failure case, the honest limitation |
| Close | 10:30-12:00 | The one action, plus the next video |

## Retention mechanics

- **Chapters every 90-150 seconds.** A new visual, a new location, or an on-screen chapter marker.
- **Cut the throat-clearing.** "So, um, today I wanted to talk about..." - delete the first 15 seconds of every take and see what is left.
- **Open loops**: name the thing you will show later, then show it.
- **Pattern interrupts** at the drop-off points - usually 0:30, 2:00 and 60% through. Check the retention graph, do not guess.
- **End on the next video**, not on a subscribe animation.

## Thumbnail and title, before the edit

Decide the click before editing. The thumbnail is one face or one object, three
words maximum, readable at 168px. The title makes one claim the video actually
delivers. If the pair is weak, no edit saves it - and `graphic-designer` owns the
thumbnail file, you own the promise.

## Production reality for a small business

- **Audio before camera.** A ₹3,000 lavalier beats a ₹80,000 camera with on-board sound.
- **Two camera angles maximum.** A talking head plus a detail shot is enough.
- **Shoot the b-roll in one session**, ten shots, five seconds each.
- **Do not chase 4K.** 1080p at a good bitrate looks better after upload than 4K compressed.

## Delivery spec

1080p or 1440p, 24 or 30fps, H.264, high bitrate, audio normalised to -14 LUFS,
captions burned or uploaded as a subtitle file, chapters in the description with
timestamps, and a 16:9 still for the thumbnail.

## Repurposing the long form

A 12-minute video is 8 shorts if it is any good: three from the payoffs, two from
the turn, two from the objections, one from the close. See `short-form-structure.md`
for the cut, and hand the vertical crops to whoever owns the social calendar.

## Anti-patterns

- A 3-minute intro logo animation.
- Chapters that are just cuts, with no change of visual.
- Reading a script to camera for 12 minutes.
- A title that promises what the video does not deliver.
- No captions on a video watched silently on a bus.
