# Edit and cut craft

Pace is not speed. Pace is the rate at which something new arrives - a new idea, a new angle, a new sound. A fast cut of the same shot is not pace.

## When to cut

| Cut when | Because |
|---|---|
| The sentence's meaning is complete | Cutting mid-thought reads as a mistake, not as energy |
| The speaker repeats themselves | The second version is almost never better |
| The speaker looks away, resets, or says "um" twice | Dead air is the cheapest thing to remove |
| A new idea starts | The viewer needs a visual signal that the topic changed |
| The energy drops in the audio waveform | Visible in the waveform before it is audible |

## Shot lengths, as a starting point

| Content | Typical shot length |
|---|---|
| Talking head, fast reel | 2-4 seconds between changes (cut, punch-in, b-roll) |
| Talking head, explainer | 5-8 seconds |
| B-roll over voice | 1.5-3 seconds per shot |
| Screen demo | As long as the action takes, sped up 2-4x where safe |
| Testimonial | 6-12 seconds, cut only on sentence ends |

If nothing changes on screen for four seconds in a short, change something: a punch-in of 10-15%, a b-roll insert, a text card, or a cut.

## The jump cut, used well

- **Remove the breath, keep the beat.** Cutting the inhale makes the speaker sound certain.
- **Change the frame at the same time** - a 5-10% punch-in or a slight angle shift - and the jump reads as deliberate.
- **Never jump-cut across a topic change.** Use a b-roll insert or a full-frame card instead; a jump there reads as a glitch.

## B-roll rules

| Rule | Reason |
|---|---|
| B-roll illustrates the noun, not the verb | "We fixed the counting" over a clipboard is noise; over the actual scanner it is proof |
| Never cover the hook with b-roll | The face is the hook; the b-roll is the support |
| Match the light and the lens | A phone clip inside a lit interview looks like stock |
| Keep the original audio under it | Silence under b-roll makes the edit feel dubbed |

## Text, lower thirds and graphics

- **One font family, two weights.** A third font is a third voice.
- **Position by safe zone**, not by eye: check on the platform's own preview at phone size.
- **Numbers get the biggest type in the frame** - a proof point that cannot be read is not a proof point.
- **No animation longer than 200ms** for anything the viewer must read.
- **Never put text over a busy region** - a face, a patterned shirt, a screen with small print.

## Reframing to vertical

| Approach | When |
|---|---|
| Native vertical shoot | Always best; plan it at the shoot |
| Reframe on the subject | Talking head, one subject, 16:9 source with room above and below |
| Split screen (subject top, visual bottom) | Demos, slides, product shots |
| Blurred background with centred frame | Last resort; reads as reposted |
| Auto-reframe tools | Acceptable for b-roll, never for a speaking face without a check |

Never crop a wide shot so the subject's head is cut off at the top. Check the first and last frame of every reframed clip.

## The review pass

1. **Watch with the sound off.** Does the story survive? If not, the captions or the visuals are carrying too little.
2. **Listen with the picture off.** Is the argument coherent?
3. **Watch at 1.5x.** Anywhere it feels slow is where a cut belongs.
4. **Check the first three seconds against a stranger.** Show it to someone who does not know the business and ask what the video is about.
5. **Check the last frame.** It is the one that gets screenshotted and shared.

## Anti-patterns

- **Cutting to a beat that fights the speech.** Music rhythm under a talking head should support, not chop.
- **A transition for every cut.** Wipes and zooms draw attention to the edit instead of the content.
- **Removing every pause.** Silence is punctuation; a cut of pure words sounds manic.
- **Keeping a good line because it was hard to get.** Sunk cost is not a reason to keep a shot.
- **Text that summarises the video before it plays.** It removes the reason to watch.
- **A 4-minute cut where a 40-second one would do.** Length is a cost the viewer pays.
