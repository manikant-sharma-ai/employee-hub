# Audio and music

Viewers forgive a soft picture. They do not forgive bad sound - it reads as amateur faster than anything visible, and they leave without being able to say why.

## Loudness targets

| Delivery | Target |
|---|---|
| Social video (Reels, Shorts, LinkedIn, TikTok) | -14 LUFS integrated, true peak -1 dBTP |
| YouTube | -14 LUFS integrated |
| Broadcast / TV | -23 LUFS (EBU R128) |
| Podcast / audio-only | -16 LUFS stereo |
| Website hero | -16 LUFS, since it is often a background element |

Set the target, measure it, and state the number in the delivery note. "Sounds fine" is not a measurement.

## The audio pass, in order

1. **Clean** - high-pass at 80-100 Hz to remove rumble and handling noise; de-hum at 50 Hz for Indian mains hum; light de-ess.
2. **Denoise** - only as much as needed. Over-processing produces a watery artefact that is worse than the fan noise.
3. **Level** - bring every clip to the same perceived loudness before mixing; a phone clip and a camera clip are 10-15 dB apart.
4. **Duck** - music under speech, typically 15-20 dB below the voice, with a 150-300ms ramp.
5. **Limit** - catch peaks, never as a substitute for levelling.
6. **Measure** - integrated loudness and true peak on the final file.

## Music

| Question | Rule |
|---|---|
| Is it licensed? | Name the licence in the delivery note: platform library, stock subscription, or a written sync licence |
| Does it fit the brand? | One music direction per brand, recorded in the brand kit |
| Where does it start? | At the first frame, or at the first cut - never a 2-second fade-in on a 15-second clip |
| Where does it drop? | On the hook or on the payoff, not randomly |
| Does it compete with the voice? | If you can hear the lyrics while someone talks, it is too loud |
| Is it trending audio? | Platform-native trending audio helps reach on Reels; it cannot be used in a paid ad |

## Fixing the four common problems

| Problem | Fix | Do not |
|---|---|---|
| Fan or AC hum | De-hum at the mains frequency, then a light noise gate | Apply heavy denoise to the whole track |
| Room echo | Shorten the reverb with a de-reverb pass, or re-record | Add more compression, which makes the echo louder |
| Clipping (distortion on peaks) | Accept it is unrecoverable; re-record or use another take | Try to "fix" it with a limiter |
| Two speakers at different levels | Level each clip separately before the mix | Ride a single fader through the whole clip |
| Wind on a phone clip | High-pass, then accept; a windscreen at shoot is the real fix | Denoise until the voice sounds synthetic |

## Recording notes that prevent the edit

- **Record 10 seconds of room tone** at every location. It is the material a denoiser needs and cannot invent.
- **Two audio sources** for anything that cannot be reshot - a lav and the camera mic.
- **Say the take number out loud** before each take; it makes the selects pass mechanical.
- **Clap or snap** at the start of a take; it gives a waveform spike to sync against.
- **Phone clips:** record in a quiet room, hold the phone 15-20cm away, and never cover the mic with a hand.

## Anti-patterns

- **Music louder than the voice.** The commonest amateur tell, and the easiest to fix.
- **A hard cut in the music under a sentence.** Cut music on a beat, and not during a word.
- **Normalising to peak instead of loudness.** Peak normalisation makes quiet passages quiet and loud ones loud.
- **Unlicensed music in a paid ad.** It gets muted, taken down, or billed - and the ad spend is lost with it.
- **A voice-over recorded on a laptop mic** for a video shot on a good camera. The mismatch is audible in three seconds.
- **Silence under b-roll.** It makes the edit feel unfinished even when the picture is good.
