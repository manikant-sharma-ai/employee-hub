# Short-form structure

Short form is not a shortened long form. It is a different object: a promise, a proof and a payoff, in that order, with nothing else.

## The four beats

| Beat | Duration (30s cut) | Job | Fails when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0-3s | Say who this is for and what they get | It is a logo, a greeting, or context |
| Context | 3-8s | The problem, in the viewer's words | It explains the company instead |
| Proof | 8-22s | The mechanism, the number, the demo, the before/after | It asserts without showing |
| Payoff + ask | 22-30s | What they now know, and one action | There are three asks, or none |

## Hook patterns that hold

| Pattern | Example | Works because |
|---|---|---|
| Direct address + stake | "If you run a godown, you are losing stock every month and you do not know it." | Names the viewer in the first second |
| Number + contradiction | "We cut reconciliation from 11 days to 2. The software was not the reason." | A claim plus a puzzle |
| In-media-res | Start mid-action, mid-sentence, mid-failure | No runway to scroll away from |
| Before/after flash | Two seconds of the broken state, then the fixed state | Shows the payoff immediately |
| Question the viewer has asked | "Why does your stock count never match Tally?" | Recognition |
| Named objection | "'We already have a vendor.' Fine - here is what that costs you." | Speaks to the sceptic already scrolling |

## Hooks that lose

- A logo animation. Nobody has ever stayed for one.
- "Hi everyone, welcome back to the channel."
- A slow push-in on a building with music.
- Text that takes four seconds to read before anything is said.
- A title card explaining what the video will cover.
- Any sentence starting with "In today's video".

## Duration by platform and intent

| Intent | Platform | Sweet spot |
|---|---|---|
| Reach / cold audience | Reels, Shorts | 15-25s |
| Reach with a story | Reels, Shorts | 30-45s |
| Consideration | LinkedIn, YouTube pre-roll | 45-90s |
| Direct response ad | Meta / Google | 15s and 30s, both |
| Testimonial | Website, LinkedIn | 40-75s |
| Explainer | Website, sales follow-up | 60-120s |

Cut to the platform's sweet spot, not to the length of the footage. A 42-minute webinar is not a 42-minute asset; it is eight 30-second ones and one 4-minute cut.

## Turning one recording into many

| Clip type | What it needs from the source |
|---|---|
| The strongest claim | One sentence that stands alone without setup |
| The story | A beginning, a turn and an end inside 40 seconds |
| The objection answer | A question asked (or implied) and answered |
| The demo | A screen region that is legible at phone size |
| The quotable line | A sentence that reads as text on screen |
| The contrarian take | A statement a reasonable person might disagree with |

Rule: **each clip must work for someone who has never seen the full recording.** If it needs the rest for context, it is not a clip, it is a chapter.

## Text on screen

- **One idea per card**, six to nine words, large enough to read at arm's length.
- **The first card carries the hook** even if the audio also does - muted viewers get it too.
- **Never repeat the spoken words verbatim** as on-screen text; the two tracks should complement, not duplicate.
- **Keep the top 12% and bottom 20% clear** on Reels and Shorts - the interface covers them.

## Anti-patterns

- **Front-loading context.** "So we started in 2019 with three people..." is a documentary opening, not a short.
- **Burying the payoff at the end with no promise at the start.** Nobody waits for a reward they were not promised.
- **Cutting for length instead of for rhythm.** A 20-second cut with three dead beats is slower than a 35-second cut that moves.
- **One video for every platform.** A 16:9 with black bars in a vertical feed reads as reposted, and gets treated as such.
- **Asking for a follow, a like, a share and a comment** in one breath. Pick one.
- **Ending on the logo instead of the payoff.** The last frame is what gets remembered and re-shared.
