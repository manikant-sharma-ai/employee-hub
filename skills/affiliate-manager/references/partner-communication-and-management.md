# Partner communication and management

Most programmes do not fail on rate. They fail because the partner never heard
from you again after approval.

## The cadence

| Audience | Frequency | What |
|---|---|---|
| Top five partners | Monthly call, quarterly review | Numbers, what to promote next, exclusives |
| Active partners (10-50) | Monthly email | New launches, best-performing angles, top three links |
| Long tail | Quarterly email | The same, plus a nudge to activate |
| Dormant (no sale in 90 days) | One re-activation email, then stop | A reason to try once, not a chase |

## The monthly partner email

Four blocks, under 250 words:

1. **What sold last month** - the top three products and the angle that worked.
2. **What is new** - launches, offers, the one thing worth promoting now.
3. **Your numbers** - top performers by name, with their revenue. Competition is the cheapest motivator.
4. **One ask** - a specific post idea, a specific code, a specific deadline.

## Managing a top partner

- **A quarterly call with their numbers on screen.** What they sent, what converted, what returned.
- **An exclusive code and a better rate for a fixed period** - not a permanent raise.
- **Early access to launches**, so their post is not the fourth one their audience sees.
- **A content brief, not a script.** They know their audience; you know the product.
- **A named human** who replies within a day. This is the whole retention strategy.

## Reactivation, honestly

Send one email. If there is no reply and no sale in 30 days, move them to dormant
and stop. A partner who is chased becomes a partner who complains.

```
Subject: still worth doing?

Hi <name> - no sale through your link since <month>. That is fine; audiences
change. If it is still a fit, here is what is selling now: <one line>. If not,
reply "stop" and I will close the account and keep the door open.
```

## Reviews and rate changes

| Situation | Move |
|---|---|
| Partner asks for a higher rate | Ask for the numbers behind it; trade rate for volume or for new customers only |
| Rate cut needed | 30 days' notice, with the reason, and a grandfather period for the top partners |
| Partner underperforms for two quarters | Move to a lower tier rather than removing; keep the relationship |
| Partner breaks a rule | Follow the published ladder, in writing, with the order IDs |
| Partner wants exclusivity | Price it; exclusivity is a cost and should be paid for by volume |

## The partner-facing dashboard

What a partner should be able to see without asking: clicks, orders, conversion
rate, commission earned, commission approved, commission paid, and the date of
the next payout. If they have to email you for any of these, they will stop
promoting before they stop asking.

## Anti-patterns

- **Silence after approval.** The commonest cause of a dead programme.
- **A newsletter that is only a list of links.** It gets filtered within two months.
- **Negotiating rates individually with everyone.** The rate card becomes fiction.
- **Public leaderboards with revenue figures.** Partners talk, and the numbers leak.
- **Removing a partner by silence.** Say it, in one line, with the reason.
