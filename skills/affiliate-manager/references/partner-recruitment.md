# Partner recruitment

Affiliate recruitment is not outreach at volume. It is finding the twenty people
whose audience already wants the product, and making the offer easy to say yes to.

## The three lanes

| Lane | Who | Where to find them | Expected response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customers | People who bought twice and left a review | Order data, review platforms, NPS responses | 5-15% |
| Content | Bloggers, reviewers, newsletter writers in the niche | Search for "best <category> India", YouTube reviews, Substack | 3-8% |
| Comparison and coupon | Deal sites, cashback apps | They will find you; also CouponDunia, GrabOn, CashKaro, IndianCoupon | 20-40%, low quality |

Start with lane one. A customer advocate converts five to ten times better than a
coupon site and costs the same commission.

## Finding content partners

1. **Search the buying question**, not the category: "best protein powder for beginners India", "which accounting software for a small firm".
2. **Read page two of the results.** Page one is often affiliate-farm content; page two is often a real site.
3. **Check whether they already run affiliate links.** A site with no links anywhere is either new or not commercial.
4. **Look for a contact page and a name.** A site with neither will not reply.
5. **Check their last three posts for accuracy.** A partner who gets facts wrong costs you refunds.

## The recruitment email

Four lines, no deck:

```
Subject: <Product> - a 12% commission for <their site> readers

Hi <name> - I read your <specific post> and the point about <specific detail> is
the one our customers keep making.

We make <product> for <who>. We pay 12% on the first order and 5% on repeats,
30-day cookie, monthly payouts on the 7th.

If it is worth a look, here is the programme page: <link>. If not, no reply
needed and I will not chase.
```

What makes it work: a specific reference to their work, the rate in the email
rather than behind a form, and an explicit permission not to reply.

## What to send once they say yes

| Asset | Why |
|---|---|
| The terms, one page | So the rules are not a surprise at payout |
| Their link and a code | A code catches offline and WhatsApp sharing |
| Three creative assets | Banner, product shot, a 30-second clip |
| A swipe file | Three emails or posts that already converted |
| The dashboard link | A partner who cannot see their numbers stops |
| A named contact and a reply time | The difference between a partner and a signup |

## Qualifying before you approve

| Question | Disqualify if |
|---|---|
| How do you plan to promote this? | "I will run ads on your brand name" |
| Where does your traffic come from? | Cannot say, or "social media" with no account |
| Do you disclose affiliate links? | Refuses |
| Have you promoted a competitor? | Currently exclusive with one, unless disclosed |
| What is your site or channel? | No site, no channel, just an email address |

## Volume expectations, honestly

| Effort | Approvals | Active after 90 days | Revenue |
|---|---|---|---|
| 50 personalised emails | 4-8 | 2-3 | Modest, but the start of a bench |
| 200 emails, semi-personalised | 10-20 | 4-6 | Better, more admin |
| Coupon-site partnerships | 3-6 | 3-6 | Volume, mostly cannibalised |
| Customer referral offer to the base | 20-60 signups | 10-20 | The best incremental return |

Recruitment is a monthly habit, not a launch event. Twenty new partners a month
compounds; two hundred once does not.

## Anti-patterns

- **A form and no outreach.** Programmes with signup forms and no recruiting have four partners, three of them spam.
- **Paying a signup bonus.** It attracts signups, not sales.
- **Approving anyone who applies.** Every unvetted partner is a future clawback argument.
- **Recruiting only coupon sites** because they reply fastest. They reply because they own the last click.
- **No named contact.** Partners who cannot reach a person stop promoting within a quarter.
