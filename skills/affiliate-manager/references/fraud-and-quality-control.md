# Fraud and quality control

Affiliate fraud is rarely dramatic. It is a partner taking credit for a sale that
was already going to happen, and doing it every day, at a rate you agreed to.

## The seven schemes, and how each is caught

| Scheme | What it looks like | Detection | Control |
|---|---|---|---|
| Coupon poaching | Affiliate posts your public code; last click at checkout | Attributed orders using a code not issued to that partner | Brand-issued codes only |
| Brand bidding | Paid search ad on your brand name pointing at an affiliate link | Monthly search of the brand term; check the landing URL | Ban, then clawback |
| Cookie stuffing | Cookie dropped on anyone visiting their page | Traffic-to-click ratio that cannot be real; orders with no session | Platform review, then removal |
| Self-referral | Partner buys through their own link | Same email, address, device or card as the partner | Void, with a warning |
| Ad hijacking | Their ad imitates your brand and redirects | Brand term search, ad copy review | Ban and report to the platform |
| Return fraud | Commission on orders that are returned | Return rate per partner against the base | Net before payout, 90-day clawback |
| Sub-affiliate laundering | An unauthorised network resells your programme | Partner's traffic source does not match their site | Written approval required for sub-affiliates |

## The monthly control pass, one hour

1. **Search the brand term** on Google and Bing; check every ad's landing URL for an affiliate parameter.
2. **List the top ten attributed orders per partner** and look for patterns - same pin code, same device, five-minute click-to-order.
3. **Check the return rate per partner** against the site average; anything double needs a look.
4. **Check for codes not issued** - every unexplained code is a leak.
5. **Check the traffic-to-conversion ratio.** A partner with 40 clicks and 30 orders is not a marketer.
6. **Apply clawbacks** and tell the partner why, with the order IDs.

## Enforcement, graded

| Offence | First | Second | Third |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unauthorised code | Void the orders, warn | Suspend 30 days | Remove |
| Brand bidding | Pause, written warning | Clawback 90 days | Remove |
| Self-referral | Void | Suspend | Remove |
| Cookie stuffing | Void all pending | Remove | Remove and report |
| Return abuse | Net and warn | Rate cut | Remove |

Write the ladder into the terms. Partners accept enforcement that was published;
they litigate enforcement that appears.

## What a healthy programme looks like

| Metric | Healthy | Investigate |
|---|---|---|
| Commission as % of attributed revenue | 6-12% | Above 15% |
| Coupon-site share of revenue | Under 30% | Above 50% |
| New-customer share | Above 55% | Under 40% |
| Return rate vs site average | Within 20% | Double |
| Clawbacks applied | 1-3% of commission | 0% (not checked) or 10%+ (bad partners) |
| Top partner share | Under 40% | Above 60% (concentration risk) |

## When a partner is too big to remove

The one that produces 40% of the channel and breaks the rules. Options: renegotiate
to a lower rate with compliance written in, split the code so you can see the
incremental part, or build two replacements before the conversation. Never let a
single partner learn that the programme cannot survive without them.

## Anti-patterns

- **Zero clawbacks every month.** Either the controls are not run or the rules are decoration.
- **A warning with no consequence.** The second offence is the one that costs.
- **Removing a partner without evidence.** It is a small industry and the story travels.
- **Ignoring the sub-affiliate chain.** You approved a site and got a network.
- **Fighting a clawback over ₹2,000.** The cost of the argument exceeds the recovery; the rule still applies.
