# Brand architecture

One brand, several products, and a decision about how they relate. Get it wrong and
every new product pays for its own awareness from zero.

## The four models

| Model | Structure | When it works | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Branded house | One master brand, descriptive products | The master brand carries trust across categories | A failure in one product damages all |
| House of brands | Separate brands, hidden parent | Different audiences, different price tiers, or a reputation you must protect | Every brand pays its own marketing bill |
| Endorsed | Product brand with "by <parent>" | A new brand that needs the parent's credibility | The parent's reputation is exposed |
| Hybrid | Master brand plus named sub-brands | Adjacent categories with distinct audiences | Needs the most discipline |

## The decision questions

1. Does the buyer of product B also buy product A? (shared audience → master brand)
2. Would a failure in one damage the other? (yes → separate)
3. Is the price tier or channel different? (materially different → separate or endorsed)
4. Does the new product need its own search demand and its own community? (yes → separate)
5. Can the business afford a second brand's marketing? (usually no → master brand)

## Naming inside the architecture

- **Descriptor, not a new brand**: "Acme Pay", "Acme Cloud" - cheap, clear, and limited by the parent.
- **Sub-brand**: needs its own logo, its own voice rules, and a stated relationship to the parent.
- **Independent brand**: full identity, and the parent stays out of the customer's view.

Write the rule down: which of the three a new product gets, and who decides.

## The hierarchy, drawn once

```
Master brand: promise, voice, visual system
  ├── Product family A: audience, one-line role
  │     └── Product A1, A2: naming pattern
  └── Product family B: audience, one-line role
```

Every asset in the business should be placeable on this tree. If it is not, the
architecture is incomplete.

## Migration, when the answer changes

- **Announce, do not surprise.** A rename needs a transition period with both names visible.
- **Keep the equity**: carry the old name in a descriptor for at least a year if it has search demand.
- **Update everything at once**: domain, handles, listings, invoices, GST records, packaging, and the Google Business Profile. Half a rename is worse than none.

## Anti-patterns

- A new brand for every product because a founder liked the name.
- Sub-brands with no stated relationship to the parent.
- Two products with overlapping names that customers confuse in search.
- A house of brands run with a branded-house budget.
- Architecture decided by the logo designer rather than the strategy.
