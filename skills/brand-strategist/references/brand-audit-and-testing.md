# Brand audit and testing

A position is finished when a buyer can repeat it back. Everything before that is a draft. This is how to test it, and how to check the brand is actually being used.

## The five-buyer test

| Step | What to do | What to record |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Read the one-liner aloud, once, no context | Their paraphrase, in their words |
| 2 | Ask "who is this for?" | Whether they name your buyer or a vaguer group |
| 3 | Ask "what would you expect it to cost?" | The frame they heard (cheap tool, consultancy, enterprise system) |
| 4 | Ask "what would you compare it to?" | The real competitor set, which is usually not yours |
| 5 | Ask "what would stop you trying it?" | The objection the messaging must answer |

Pass condition: **three of five repeat the buyer and the difference correctly.** Below that, the one-liner is doing too much or saying too little.

## What to test, in order of cost

| Test | Cost | Signal |
|---|---|---|
| Read it to five matching buyers | An hour | Comprehension and frame |
| Change the site header and watch enquiries | A week | Enquiry quality, not volume |
| Put it in the sales call opening for two weeks | Two weeks | Whether deals move faster or stall earlier |
| Run it as ad copy against the old copy | A small budget | Click and qualified-lead rate |
| Win/loss interviews | A month | The reason deals are actually lost |

The cheapest test that produces a real answer is the five-buyer read. Do that before spending on anything.

## The brand audit, on live material

Take ten pieces of live material: the site header, the About page, three recent emails, two social posts, a deck slide, an invoice footer and one support reply.

| Check | Question | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| One-liner consistency | Is it verbatim, or a variant? | Make it verbatim everywhere |
| Pillar coverage | Are all three pillars present across the set? | Add the missing one to the next piece |
| Banned words | Count the violations per piece | Add to the editing checklist |
| Proof density | How many claims have a fact attached? | Rewrite the unsupported ones |
| Register match | Does the support reply sound like the site? | The support reply sets the standard |
| Buyer vocabulary | Are you using their words or the industry's? | Replace with call-note language |
| Claim risk | Any claim the business cannot defend? | Remove or substantiate |

Report it as a table with a count per row, not as an opinion. "Six of ten pieces use 'solutions'; zero carry a proof point on pillar three" is actionable; "the brand feels inconsistent" is not.

## The monthly drift check

1. Read one customer-facing page, one sales email and one support reply.
2. Compare against the guide.
3. Fix one thing - the worst violation - not all of them.
4. Log it in the journal. Drift is continuous; so is the correction.

## Signals that the position needs revisiting

| Signal | Likely cause |
|---|---|
| Win rate falling while lead volume rises | The message is attracting the wrong buyer |
| Every deal is discounted | The difference is not believed, so price decides |
| Sales team describes the product differently each time | The hierarchy is not usable, or not read |
| Customers describe you in a way you did not intend | The market has already repositioned you - decide whether to accept it |
| A competitor adopts your language | The position was good but not defensible; move to the mechanism |
| A new segment is 40% of revenue | The position may be pointed at last year's best buyer |

## Anti-patterns

- **Testing with colleagues.** They know the business, so they cannot hear it as a buyer does.
- **Testing for liking rather than comprehension.** "I like it" is not a pass; "here is what they do" is.
- **Changing the position after two negative reactions.** Five buyers, a pattern, then a change.
- **A brand audit with no counts.** Impressions cannot be tracked month to month.
- **Never auditing.** The guide is followed for a quarter and then quietly abandoned.
- **Treating the position as permanent.** It has an owner and a review date, or it decays into a slogan.
