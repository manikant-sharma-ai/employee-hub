# Tone of voice

A tone-of-voice guide is a set of rules someone can follow and break. "Warm but professional" is not a guide; "sentences under 18 words, no exclamation marks, never call a customer a user" is.

## The guide's seven sections

| Section | Content | Example rule |
|---|---|---|
| Who we sound like | One paragraph, a person not an archetype | "A competent senior colleague who has done the job and does not need to impress you" |
| Sentence mechanics | Length, voice, person | "Average 14 words. Active voice. 'We' for the company, 'you' for the reader" |
| Vocabulary | Words we use, words we ban | Use: godown, dispatch, reconciliation. Ban: solutions, leverage, seamless, robust |
| Openings and closings | How to start and end any message | "Start with the reader's situation, not with our company. No 'I hope this finds you well'" |
| Numbers and claims | How to state a fact | "Every number carries a unit and a period: '11 days to 2, over three months'" |
| Hard messages | Price rises, refusals, apologies | "State the decision, the reason, then what happens next. No softening paragraph first" |
| Language mixing | When to use Hindi, Hinglish or a regional language | "Match the language of the customer's last message" |

## The before-and-after table, which is the whole guide

Rules without examples get ignored. Every rule needs one line of before and one of after.

| Before | After | Rule applied |
|---|---|---|
| "We are a leading provider of innovative supply chain solutions." | "We count stock with barcodes, so month-end takes two days instead of eleven." | Say the mechanism, not the category |
| "Please be informed that your request has been escalated." | "I have passed this to Priya in accounts. She will call you before 5pm today." | Name the person and the time |
| "Unfortunately, we are unable to process your refund at this time." | "We cannot refund this one - the order was delivered 47 days ago and our window is 30. What we can do is a credit note." | State the decision, then the alternative |
| "Our team will revert at the earliest." | "You will have an answer by Thursday." | No "earliest"; give a date |
| "Kindly do the needful." | "Please send the signed PO and we will dispatch on Monday." | Ask for one specific thing |

## Rules that are worth writing down

- **Banned words:** solutions, leverage, seamless, robust, cutting-edge, world-class, synergy, ecosystem, empower, unlock, "kindly revert", "do the needful", "as per".
- **One idea per sentence.** Two clauses maximum.
- **Contractions are allowed and encouraged** in customer communication; not in legal or statutory documents.
- **Numbers in digits**, units always, Indian grouping in anything a customer reads (₹12,34,567).
- **No exclamation marks** in B2B, at most one per message in consumer.
- **Never blame the customer** - describe the situation, not the person.
- **Say "we" for decisions and "I" for actions taken by one person.** It is the difference between a company and a queue.
- **Titles:** use "Mr/Ms + surname" or the first name as the customer uses it, never "Dear Customer".
- **Emoji:** only where the channel is WhatsApp and the customer used one first.
- **Response time is part of the voice.** "Within 4 working hours" is a brand promise; say it only if it is true.

## Register by channel

| Channel | Register | Length |
|---|---|---|
| Email to a business buyer | Full sentences, one ask, formal but plain | Under 120 words |
| WhatsApp to an SMB owner | Short lines, Hinglish acceptable, voice note acceptable | Under 60 words |
| Support reply | Direct, warm, one fact first | Under 80 words |
| Landing page | Second person, present tense, claim then proof | Under 25 words per block |
| LinkedIn | One idea, no hashtags in the body, no "agree?" | 80-150 words |
| Invoice and statutory | Formal, precise, no personality | As short as completeness allows |
| Bad news (price rise, outage) | Plain, early, with the remedy and a date | Under 150 words |

## Hinglish, treated as a register and not as a mistake

- **Match the customer's mix.** If they wrote "stock ka issue hai", answer in that register rather than formal Hindi or formal English.
- **Do not translate brand or product names.** They stay in Latin script inside a Hindi sentence.
- **Do not correct the customer's English**, in any channel, ever.
- **Keep technical terms in English** where the trade uses them - "dispatch", "GST", "invoice" are normal inside Hindi sentences.
- **Written Hindi in Devanagari** is right for formal letters and government communication; Hinglish is right for WhatsApp and sales follow-up. Say which the business uses where.

## How to enforce it

1. **Give the guide to whoever writes**, with the before-and-after table, not the archetype paragraph.
2. **Review ten pieces of live copy** against the rules, and count the violations per rule. The worst rule becomes the training topic.
3. **Put the banned-word list in the editing checklist** so it is checked mechanically, not remembered.
4. **Re-read one customer-facing page per month** and fix drift. Voice decays in the direction of whoever wrote last.

## Anti-patterns

- **An archetype with no rules.** "We are confident but humble" cannot be applied to a sentence.
- **A guide nobody has read.** If it is not in the writer's checklist, it does not exist.
- **Voice that changes per channel into a different company.** Register changes; personality does not.
- **Formality as a substitute for clarity.** "Kindly revert at the earliest" is less clear than "reply by Thursday".
- **Sarcasm, jokes about the customer, or jokes in an outage.** It reads as contempt at exactly the wrong moment.
- **A guide written for the website and ignored in support.** The support reply is where the brand is actually experienced.
