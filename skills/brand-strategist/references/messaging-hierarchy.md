# Messaging hierarchy

The hierarchy is what stops four people describing the business four ways. Each level must be defensible from the one above it, and no level may introduce a claim the level above does not support.

## The five levels

| Level | Length | Use | Must contain |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. One-liner | 5-8 words | Trade show, email signature, the answer to "what do you do" | The buyer and the difference |
| 2. Boilerplate | 25-40 words | Website header, deck slide 2, press note | The buyer, the problem, the mechanism |
| 3. Pillars | 3 x 1 sentence | Sales deck, landing page sections | One dimension of the difference each |
| 4. Proof | 1 fact per pillar | Anywhere a claim is made | Customer, number, mechanism or demo |
| 5. Objection answers | 1 line each | Sales calls, FAQ, replies | The doubt the pillar raises |

## Worked example

**1. One-liner.** "Stock counts that survive an OEM audit." (6 words)

**2. Boilerplate.** "We are a stock-count and audit-trail system for tier-2 auto-parts makers with more than one godown. Barcode counting replaces the Tally-plus-WhatsApp routine, so monthly reconciliation finishes in two days and the audit trail the OEM asks for already exists."

**3. Pillars.**
- *Counts in a shift, not a week.* Barcode-based counting per godown, one person per aisle.
- *An audit trail the OEM accepts.* Every transfer has a document, a time and a name.
- *Works with the books you already have.* Posts to Tally rather than replacing it.

**4. Proof.**
- A Pune tier-1 supplier: monthly reconciliation from 11 days to 2.
- The audit pack accepted at three OEM audits without an observation.
- Live with Tally 9 and Tally Prime, two-way sync.

**5. Objection answers.**
- "We already have Tally." - "Keep it. We post to it. The counting is what Tally cannot do."
- "Our staff will not use barcode scanners." - "One screen, one button, and a shift's training. The Pune plant trained 14 loaders in a morning."
- "What if the OEM changes the format?" - "The template is configurable; the first change is free."

## Writing a pillar that is not a slogan

| Slogan | Pillar |
|---|---|
| "Simple to use" | "One screen and one button per count; 14 loaders trained in a morning" |
| "Reliable" | "99.4% dispatch accuracy across two godowns over 11 months" |
| "Great support" | "A named person on WhatsApp during shift hours, median 9-minute first reply" |
| "Affordable" | "₹1.2 lakh a year for two godowns, against one stock-out a month" |

Rule: if the pillar could be a poster, it is not a pillar. A pillar names a mechanism and its evidence.

## The proof inventory

Before writing pillars, list every fact you actually have:

| Type | Example | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Named customer with a number | "X cut reconciliation to 2 days" | Strongest |
| Aggregate from your own data | "Across 22 customers, median 4.1 days" | Strong |
| Demonstration | A live screen share | Strong, but only in person |
| Mechanism explanation | "Barcode per bin, so a transfer cannot be untracked" | Medium - needs the demo |
| Third-party validation | An OEM audit acceptance | Strong |
| Founder assertion | "We believe" | Not proof |

If a pillar has no proof in the top four rows, the pillar is not ready. Either build the proof or change the pillar - never soften the claim into vagueness, which is how brands end up saying nothing.

## Consistency rules

- **The one-liner appears verbatim** in the deck, the site header and the email signature. Variants are how drift starts.
- **Pillar order is fixed.** The first pillar is the one the buyer cares about most, not the one the product team is proudest of.
- **No new claim may appear below the pillar level** unless it is added to the hierarchy and approved.
- **Numbers carry a date.** "99.4%" without a period is not evidence, it is a boast.

## Anti-patterns

- **A hierarchy with seven pillars.** Three is a message; seven is a feature list.
- **Pillars that overlap.** If two pillars answer the same buyer doubt, one of them is decoration.
- **A one-liner that is a category label** ("AI for supply chains") with no difference in it.
- **Proof invented to fit a pillar.** The pillar changes, the proof does not get fabricated.
- **Different hierarchies for sales and marketing.** That is how a prospect hears one thing and buys another.
- **A boilerplate written for investors** and used for buyers. They are different audiences with different questions.
