# Naming

A name is a decision made once and paid for every day. The goal is not a great name - it is a name that can be pronounced, remembered, cleared, and does not mean something unwanted in a language your market speaks.

## The five tests

| Test | Question | Fails when |
|---|---|---|
| Say it | Can someone repeat it correctly after hearing it once, on a phone call? | It needs spelling out twice |
| Spell it | Can someone find it after hearing it, typing what they heard? | It is a deliberate misspelling |
| Meaning | Does it mean nothing bad in the market's languages? | It means something crude or absurd in Tamil, Marathi or Hindi |
| Space | Is it free in the class that matters - trademark, domain, app store, social handle? | It is free as a domain but taken as a mark |
| Stretch | Does it survive the next product, the next city, the next category? | It names today's feature |

## Name types, and what each costs

| Type | Example shape | Strength | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Descriptive | "StockCount" | Instant understanding | Hard to protect; low distinctiveness |
| Suggestive | "ClearStock" | Understands and hints at benefit | Crowded; needs a search |
| Invented | "Stoqly" | Protectable and ownable | Must teach the meaning |
| Founders' or place name | "Sharma & Sons", "Deccan Tools" | Trust in traditional trade | Hard to scale beyond the region |
| Metaphor | "Anchor", "Ledger" | Memorable, stretches | Crowded; ambiguous in a category |
| Acronym | "APMS" | Short | Means nothing until taught; hard to remember |

For an Indian B2B business selling to traditional trade, descriptive and place-based names often outperform invented ones, because trust and clarity beat distinctiveness at the first meeting. For a consumer brand, invented and metaphor names carry further. Say which logic you are applying and why.

## The generation method

1. **Write the position first.** A name for an unpositioned business is a guess.
2. **Generate in four lists**: what it does, what it changes, what the buyer calls the problem, and words from the buyer's own language (including Hindi, Marathi, Tamil, Gujarati where relevant).
3. **Combine and invent** - 60-100 candidates, no filtering while generating.
4. **Filter in order** of cheapness: say it aloud, spell it, meaning check, length (2-3 syllables is the sweet spot), domain availability, trademark class.
5. **Take three forward.** Each with the meaning, the pronunciation, the domain status and the trademark status.
6. **Test on ten people** who match the buyer: "what do you think this company does?" and "would you trust it with your accounts?"

## Meaning checks that matter in India

- **Check across the languages your market actually speaks**, not just Hindi. A word that is neutral in Hindi can be unfortunate in Tamil, Telugu or Bengali.
- **Avoid a name that collides with a common word** in a language where the meaning is comic, crude or inauspicious.
- **Watch the transliteration.** A name that reads well in Latin script may be awkward in Devanagari or Tamil script, and vice versa. Write it in the target script before deciding.
- **Check the abbreviation.** Three-letter acronyms frequently spell something unwanted.

## Clearance, which you do not do yourself

| Check | Where | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| Trademark search (India) | IP India public search, classes 9, 35, 42 typically | Whether a conflicting mark exists |
| Trademark search (other markets) | WIPO Global Brand Database, USPTO | If export matters |
| Company name | MCA21 | Whether a company already uses it |
| Domain | Registrar, and `.in` plus `.com` | Availability, and what it costs if held |
| Social handles | Each platform | Whether the brand can exist consistently |
| App store | Play Store, App Store | For a product name |

You run the searches, you report what they show, and you say plainly: **a search is not a clearance opinion.** A lawyer's opinion is a separate, paid step, and for a name the business will spend money on, it is worth it.

## Presenting a shortlist

| Column | Content |
|---|---|
| Name | As it will be written |
| Type | Descriptive / suggestive / invented / place |
| Meaning | One line, and the intended association |
| Say and spell | How it sounds on a call; how a person types it |
| Scripts | The name in Devanagari and in one southern script, written out |
| Domain | `.com` and `.in` status |
| Trademark | Class searched, result, date |
| Risk | The one thing that could go wrong |

## Anti-patterns

- **A name that needs a pronunciation guide.** If it needs explaining, it will be mis-said for a decade.
- **Deliberate misspellings** for distinctiveness. They cost every future search and every verbal referral.
- **Naming before positioning.** You end up renaming in 18 months, which costs more than the first search.
- **Falling in love with the first name.** Generate 60 before choosing 3.
- **Skipping the trademark search to save a few thousand rupees.** A rebrand costs a hundred times that.
- **A name that boxes in the next product.** "StockCount" cannot sell payroll.
