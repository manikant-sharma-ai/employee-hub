# Lead source quality

Volume is what a channel sells. Qualified leads are what the business buys. This file is the arithmetic that
tells them apart.

## The patterns that repeat in India

| Source | Typical behaviour | The trap |
|---|---|---|
| IndiaMART | High volume, low intent, many price-checkers | Judging it on lead count; it pays on the third or fourth call |
| Paid search | Highest intent, highest cost per lead | Turning it off in a slow month, then losing the ranking |
| Meta lead ads | Cheap leads, low qualification | Cheap is the problem, not the benefit |
| Marketplace enquiries | Fast, price-led, five sellers at once | Slow replies lose every one |
| WhatsApp from a listing | High intent, evening hours | No one answering after 7pm |
| Referral | Highest close rate, no cost | Nobody asks for one |
| Walk-in and exhibition | High intent, poor follow-up | Cards in a drawer |
| Organic search | Slow to build, compounding | Judged on month one |
| Cold outbound | Low close rate, high control | Compared to inbound on the wrong metric |

## Junk detection, without insulting the buyer

| Pattern | What it probably is | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Same text, five minutes apart, different names | A scraped list or a bot | Filter on the message text and the email domain |
| Generic email domains plus no company name | Often a student or a competitor | Ask one qualifying question; do not disqualify on the domain alone |
| A phone number that fails twice | Bad data | One WhatsApp attempt, then close with a reason |
| "Please share your price list" with no other text | Price shopping, or a reseller | Send the range and one question, not the sheet |
| Enquiries only between 1am and 5am | Automated or overseas | Check the IP country before treating it as Indian demand |
| The same person enquiring four times | Genuinely keen, or a system duplicate | Check, then treat as one lead with a phone call |

Never tell a person they are "junk". Disqualify the enquiry with a reason, politely, and keep the record.

## Form design, the cheapest fix in this role

| Field | Keep | Drop |
|---|---|---|
| Name | Yes | |
| Phone (with country code) | Yes, it is the channel that works in India | |
| Email | Optional if phone is captured | Making it mandatory cuts volume and adds nothing |
| Company name | Yes - it is the best single qualifier | |
| City | Yes - it decides routing | |
| "How did you hear about us" | Yes - it is the only attribution you control | |
| Message or requirement | Yes, required | |
| Budget dropdown | Optional; it depresses completion | A range with "not sure yet" |
| "Number of employees" | Only if the ICP depends on it | |
| CAPTCHA | Only if bots are real | A honeypot field instead |

Two rules: fewer fields, more leads; one qualifying field, better leads. A four-field form with company name and
a requirement beats a nine-field form every time.

## Cost per qualified lead, honestly

| Input | Where |
|---|---|
| Spend on the channel | The ad account or the marketplace subscription |
| Leads attributed | The CRM's source field, not the ad platform's number |
| Qualified leads | This role's score |
| Sales cost per meeting | Owner hours × loaded rate, if it is being measured |

Then the only comparison that matters: **cost per qualified lead against cost per won deal**. A channel with a
₹900 cost per qualified lead and a 40% close rate beats one with ₹200 and a 2% close rate, and the second one
looks cheaper on every dashboard.

## Attribution, in a business with no tracking

1. Ask "how did you hear about us" and record the answer verbatim.
2. Use a distinct phone number or WhatsApp link per channel where possible.
3. Use a UTM on every paid link, and a source field in the CRM that is required.
4. Accept that referrals will be under-counted; ask the owner directly at the first call.
5. Never attribute by last click alone when the business runs more than one channel.

## Anti-patterns

- **Judging a channel in its first month.** Search and organic compound; lead ads do not.
- **Counting leads, not qualified leads**, in the weekly report. It flatters the wrong channel every time.
- **Killing a channel because the leads are "low quality"** when the response time was two days. Check the clock
  before the channel.
- **A form with nine fields and a budget dropdown.** The completion rate falls and the qualification rises by
  less than the volume lost.
- **No source field in the CRM.** Then the whole table above is impossible, and the spend decision is a
  preference.
