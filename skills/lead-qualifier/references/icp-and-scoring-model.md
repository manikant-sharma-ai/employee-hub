# The ICP and the scoring model

A qualification is only as good as the profile it is measured against. This file builds the profile, then the
score, then the disqualifier list that saves the most hours.

## Building an ICP when there is none

Do not write it from ambition. Derive it from the last twenty closed deals.

| Column | From the CRM or the founder's memory |
|---|---|
| Industry or business type | What they do, not their sector label |
| Size | Employees, outlets, revenue band, order volume |
| Geography | City, state, the serviceable radius |
| Buyer role | Owner, procurement, marketing head, IT |
| Trigger | What changed in their business the month they bought |
| Deal size and cycle | First order value and days to close |
| Channel they arrived on | Referral, search, marketplace, WhatsApp |
| Why they chose you | The one sentence they said |

Then write the profile as six lines: **who**, **size**, **where**, **trigger**, **deal shape**, **why us**. Six
lines, no adjectives. Anything longer will not be used by the person on the phone.

The second list matters more: the last twenty closed-lost, with the reason. Most businesses have a type that
never converts - too small, wrong city, only wants the cheapest, needs a feature that does not exist. That list
is where the hours are saved.

## Fit, scored on what is visible before a call

| Signal | Weight | Strong | Weak |
|---|---|---|---|
| Business type matches the ICP | High | Same trade as the last six wins | Adjacent, unproven |
| Size in the band | High | Within the range that buys well | 10x too big or 10x too small |
| Geography serviceable | Medium | Inside the delivery or visit radius | Needs a partner or shipping that does not exist |
| Buyer role | Medium | A decision maker or a named influencer | A student, a researcher, an intern |
| Requirement specificity | High | Names a product, quantity, timeline | Asks what the company does |
| Channel quality | Low-medium | Referral, existing customer, repeat visitor | A scraped list, a bulk enquiry form |

Fit is a judgement, and it must be written as one line: "Fits: mid-size distributor, Pune, matches the last six
wins. Weak: asked for a 20-unit trial, below our usual 200 minimum."

## Intent, scored on evidence

| Signal | Reading | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Names a quantity or a budget | Active procurement | Strong |
| Names a timeline ("this month", "before Diwali") | Live need | Strong |
| Asks a specific technical or compliance question | Already comparing | Strong |
| Asks for a quote or a sample | Further along than most | Strong |
| Visited the pricing page twice | Researching | Medium |
| Asks about GST, invoice, delivery terms | Ready to transact | Medium |
| Replies to the acknowledgement within a day | Engaged | Medium |
| Generic "please share details" | A broadcast, or a form-fill | Weak |
| No reply to two attempts | Not now | Negative |

## The 2x2, and what to do in each cell

| | High intent | Low intent |
|---|---|---|
| **Good fit** | Call today, owner named, deadline in hours | Nurture with a specific reason to return; do not call |
| **Poor fit** | Route to a partner, or answer once and close the file | Disqualify with a reason; do not spend an hour |

The bottom-left cell is where the money hides: a poor-fit lead with high intent often has a partner who is a
perfect fit. A referral costs one email and builds a channel.

## The scoring output, in one line per lead

`FIT 4/5 - INTENT 3/5 - Call today. Distributor in Pune, 40 outlets, asked for a quote for 500 units this month;
below our 200 minimum? No - 500 clears it. Matches the last six wins.`

That line is the whole artefact. A number without the sentence is a score nobody can act on or argue with.

## Disqualifiers, and the polite exit for each

| Disqualifier | What it looks like | The route |
|---|---|---|
| Student or researcher | Asks for data, a project, an interview | One line: not a commercial enquiry, here is the public material |
| Competitor | Asks for pricing in detail, no company, generic email domain | No pricing. Note it, do not engage further |
| Vendor selling to you | A pitch disguised as an enquiry | Route to the person who buys that category, or ignore |
| Job seeker | A CV in the form | Route to `hr-recruiter` |
| Wrong geography | Outside the serviceable area | The partner or distributor who covers it |
| Below minimum order | Wants one unit of a wholesale product | Self-serve or the retail channel |
| Wants a free sample only | No quantity, no timeline, asks for "best price" repeatedly | A paid sample offer, once |
| A genuine browser | Reads everything, commits to nothing | Nurture, with a quarterly touch, not a call |
| Already a customer | An order query, not a sale | `support-agent` or `customer-success` |

## Keeping the ICP honest

| Check | Frequency | Question |
|---|---|---|
| Closed-won against the ICP | Monthly | Are the wins still inside the profile, or has it drifted |
| Closed-lost reasons | Monthly | Is a disqualifier appearing that the ICP does not name |
| Qualified-to-meeting rate | Monthly | If it is under 30%, the score is too generous |
| Meeting-to-close rate | Quarterly | If it is high, the score is too strict and good leads are being dropped |

## Anti-patterns

- **An ICP written by the founder about the customers they wish they had.** It produces a queue of leads nobody
  can close and a real buyer ignored.
- **A single score out of ten.** It hides whether the problem is fit or intent, which are fixed differently.
- **Scoring on the form fields only.** The message text is the signal; the dropdown is what someone selected in
  four seconds.
- **Never disqualifying.** A queue with no exits is a queue that grows until it is ignored.
- **A disqualifier list that includes a whole region or community.** Disqualify the business need, never the
  person.
