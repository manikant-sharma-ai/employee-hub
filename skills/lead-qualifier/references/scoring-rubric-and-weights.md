# Scoring rubric and weights

A score is only useful if two people applying it to the same lead get the same number. That is the
whole test of a rubric.

## The default rubric

Seven factors, 100 points. Change the weights to fit the business, never the factors mid-batch.

| # | Factor | Weight | 100% value | 50% value | 0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Industry fit | 20 | In the ICP industry list | Adjacent, plausible | Outside |
| 2 | Size fit | 20 | Inside the band | One band away | Two or more bands away |
| 3 | Geography and serviceability | 15 | Inside the service area | Remotely serviceable | Cannot serve |
| 4 | Problem clarity | 15 | States the problem, the impact and the timeline | States a problem | Generic enquiry |
| 5 | Authority and process | 10 | Decision-maker or named champion | Influencer | Unknown or no path |
| 6 | Budget signal | 10 | Budget stated or already spending | Implied | Explicitly no budget |
| 7 | Timing | 10 | Needs it within 30 days | Within a quarter | No timeline |

## Bands

| Band | Score | Meaning | Route |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 75-100 | Pursue now | Owner contacts the same working day |
| B | 50-74 | Worth a conversation, not a priority | Nurture with a dated reason and a trigger to watch |
| C | below 50 | Decline with a reason | Decline log, no contact |
| D | any | One unknown factor would change the band | One question, then re-score |

Band D is the one that earns the rubric its keep. A lead at 48 with size unknown is not a C; it is a
question.

## Weighting for the business

| Business type | Raise | Lower | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| High-ticket, long cycle | Authority, budget | Timing | The wrong stakeholder wastes a month |
| High-volume, low-ticket | Problem clarity, timing | Authority | The buyer is often the user |
| Services with a travel radius | Geography | Budget | You cannot deliver what you cannot reach |
| Regulated product | Industry fit | Timing | Compliance decides the sale |
| Marketplace or self-serve | Timing | Authority | The decision is individual and instant |

The weights must sum to 100. Write the version and the date on every scorecard - a score from last
quarter's weights is not comparable.

## Scoring discipline

- **Score the evidence, not the vibe.** Every factor value carries the field, sentence or page it came
  from.
- **Unknown scores zero and is listed.** Never impute. The missing list is often more valuable than
  the score.
- **Score before reading the channel name.** Channel bias is the most common error in a batch review.
- **Re-score after any new information**, and keep both scores with the date. A band change is a
  finding.
- **Round to whole points.** Decimals imply a precision the rubric does not have.

## The three-question rule

For every lead, write the three questions whose answers would most change the band. In practice they
come from the same short list:

| Question | Factor it resolves | When it matters |
|---|---|---|
| How many people are in the business, and who would use this? | Size, authority | Almost always |
| What are you using today, and what made you look now? | Trigger, technographic, timing | The highest-value question in inbound |
| Who else is involved in the decision, and what is your timeline? | Authority, timing | Any deal above the SMB threshold |

Three questions, asked in the first reply, turn a score into a qualified conversation. Ten questions
turn it into a form nobody completes.

## What the score is not

- It is not a probability of closing. That needs win-rate data by band, which takes a quarter to
  accumulate - build it, and put it on the scorecard when it exists.
- It is not a priority order within a band. Within A, order by arrival time.
- It is not a reason to skip a human's judgement. Overrides are allowed and must be recorded with a
  reason.

## Anti-patterns

- **Changing weights mid-batch.** The batch becomes uncomparable and the exercise is wasted.
- **A rubric with twelve factors.** Nobody applies twelve consistently; seven is already at the edge.
- **Scoring on enthusiasm.** The most excited lead is often the one with no budget.
- **Treating a 74 and a 50 as the same band.** The band boundaries exist to force the distinction.
- **Filling in a missing factor to finish the sheet.** This is the single most damaging habit in
  qualification, and it is invisible in the output.
