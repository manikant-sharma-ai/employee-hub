# Qualification conversations

The first call has one job: find out whether there is a real need, a real budget and a real date, without
interrogating the buyer. This file is the questions and the exits.

## The five things to establish, in this order

| # | Question to yourself | The signal |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What are they trying to do? | A requirement, or a curiosity |
| 2 | Why now? | A trigger: growth, a failure, a deadline, a competitor |
| 3 | Who decides? | A name, a process, or "I'll check" |
| 4 | What is the budget shape? | A number, a range, or silence |
| 5 | What is the timeline? | A date, a quarter, or "someday" |

You do not need all five to qualify. You need enough to decide whether the next hour of a salesperson's time
will be well spent, and to write a handover that is worth reading.

## The questions, in plain language

| Instead of | Ask |
|---|---|
| "What is your budget?" | "Have you set aside a number for this, or are we still shaping it?" |
| "Who is the decision maker?" | "Who else would need to be comfortable before this goes ahead?" |
| "What is your timeline?" | "When would you want this running?" |
| "What are your requirements?" | "What happens today, and what is not working about it?" |
| "Do you have authority to buy?" | (Never ask this) - ask who else is involved |
| "Are you comparing vendors?" | "What else are you looking at?" |
| "What is your company size?" | "How many people would use it?" |

## Reading the answers

| Answer | Reading | Next |
|---|---|---|
| A number and a date | Qualified, high intent | Book the next step now, with a date |
| A number, no date | Real need, slow | Nurture with a specific trigger question |
| A date, no number | Urgent, budget unclear | Ask who holds the budget, once |
| "Send me details" | Usually a polite exit | Send one specific thing, ask one question, then stop |
| "Just checking" | A browser | Nurture list, quarterly touch |
| Silence after two attempts | Not now | Close the file with a reason, not a guess |
| "We already have someone" | A displacement deal | Only pursue if the trigger is real |

## The budget conversation, without asking for a number

Ask about the shape, not the amount:

1. "Is this a planned spend this quarter, or an exploratory conversation?"
2. "Have you bought something like this before? What did it cost you?"
3. "Would a number in the range of ₹X to ₹Y be workable?" - only if the price band is real and stated.
4. "Who signs off on something this size?" - a size question that reveals the process.

Never open with a price and never discount on the first call. The price belongs to `pricing-analyst`'s policy and
the owner's quote, not to the qualification.

## The polite exit, three versions

| Situation | What to say |
|---|---|
| Below minimum order | "Our minimum is 200 units; below that the retail listing is the better route. Here is the link, and I'm happy to help if you scale up." |
| Wrong geography | "We deliver to these pin codes directly, and here is the distributor who covers yours. I'll introduce you." |
| Student or researcher | "We don't take research enquiries, but this is public and may help. Good luck with the project." |
| No budget, no timeline | "It sounds like this is still early. May I check in after your next planning cycle, in <month>?" |
| Competitor pricing | "I can't share our pricing sheet, but I can tell you what we're best at. Is that useful?" |

An exit done well produces a referral, a future lead, or a reputation. An exit done badly produces a review.

## Handing to a person, mid-call

When the call reveals a technical question, a commercial question or an escalation:

| Reveals | Hand to | What to pass |
|---|---|---|
| A technical requirement or integration question | `sales-engineer` | The requirement in their words, the environment, the constraint |
| A pricing or discount request | The owner, or `quote-generator` | Quantity, spec, delivery date, the ask |
| A tender or a government requirement | `tender-bidder` | The reference, the deadline, the eligibility question |
| A support problem for an existing customer | `support-agent` | The ticket, the account, the urgency |
| A partnership or channel conversation | `partnerships` | The proposal, the scale, the ask |

## The notes that make the handover worth reading

Six lines, in the CRM, after every call:

1. What they are trying to do, in their words.
2. Why now.
3. What they are using today.
4. The number and the date, or the honest absence of both.
5. Who else is involved.
6. The next step and the date.

A call with no note is a call that has to be made again.

## Anti-patterns

- **An interrogation.** Five questions in a row makes a buyer defensive. One question, then listen, then reflect
  back what you heard.
- **Qualifying a lead you already know is bad** because the queue is short. It costs the salesperson an hour and
  the business a reputation.
- **Pitching on the qualification call.** The pitch is the next step; this call is about whether there is one.
- **Asking for a budget number before establishing the problem.** It reads as a price-first conversation and
  invites a lowball.
- **Leaving a call without a dated next step.** "I'll revert" is not a next step.
