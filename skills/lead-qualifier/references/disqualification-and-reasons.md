# Disqualification and reasons

Declining well is a skill. A decline that carries a reason teaches the business something; a decline
that carries silence teaches nothing and leaves the lead wondering.

## The reason codes

Fixed codes, so the monthly roll-up is possible. Free text is kept beside the code, never instead of
it.

| Code | Meaning | Typical source |
|---|---|---|
| `OUT-INDUSTRY` | Outside the ICP industries | Any |
| `OUT-SIZE` | Too small or too large to serve profitably | Form, LinkedIn |
| `OUT-GEO` | Outside the serviceable area | City field |
| `NO-BUDGET` | Explicitly no budget or free-only | Conversation |
| `NO-AUTHORITY` | No path to a decision-maker | Conversation |
| `NOT-BUYER` | Competitor, agency reselling, student, researcher, job seeker | Obvious from the enquiry |
| `ALREADY-SERVED` | Existing customer or active opportunity | CRM |
| `WRONG-PROBLEM` | The problem is real but not one this business solves | Form text |
| `DUPLICATE` | Same company already in the pipeline | CRM |
| `NO-RESPONSE` | Three attempts, no reply, no other signal | Timeline |
| `CUSTOM-BUILD` | Needs bespoke work at a standard price | Scope discussion |
| `TIMING-FAR` | Real fit, buying in 12+ months - usually B, not C | Conversation |

Codes are additive over time and never repurposed. A retired code stays in the list with a note.

## The decline message

Three sentences, in this order: what was understood, why it is not a fit, what happens next.

> Thanks for the detail on the three-branch rollout. We work with single-location retailers under 50
> staff, so a three-branch group is outside what we support well. If the branches run independently
> later, come back to us - and here is a firm that does multi-branch work: <name>.

Rules: never leave a decline without a next step, never explain a price you did not quote, never
suggest a competitor you would not recommend to a friend, and never send a decline that reads like a
template with the name swapped.

## When a decline is really a B

| Situation | Not a C because | Do |
|---|---|---|
| Right fit, buying next year | The trigger is dated, not absent | B, with the trigger date |
| Right fit, no budget this quarter | Budget cycles are seasonal | B, revisit after their year-end |
| Right problem, wrong person | A champion can be found | D, ask who owns it |
| Right company, currently with a competitor | Switching windows open | B, watch for the trigger |

The most common error in a small business is declining a lead that was simply early. The second most
common is nurturing a lead that was never a fit.

## The monthly roll-up

| View | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| Declines by code | Where the funnel leaks |
| Declines by source | Which channel to stop paying for |
| Declines by industry | Whether the ICP is honest |
| `NO-RESPONSE` share | Whether the first touch or the channel is wrong |
| Overrides by rep | Whether the rubric or the rep needs attention |

One page a month. If a code exceeds 30% of declines, either the ICP or the targeting is wrong, and
that is a conversation with the founder rather than a scoring problem.

## Anti-patterns

- **A decline with no reason.** The lead re-enters next quarter through another channel and is
  scored again by someone else.
- **A soft decline that reads like a maybe.** "We will keep you in mind" generates three follow-ups
  and one bad review.
- **Declining a competitor rudely.** They will quote you in their next pitch.
- **Reusing a code for a new meaning.** The roll-up silently becomes wrong.
- **Never declining anything.** A pipeline where everything is a B is a pipeline with no decisions in
  it.
