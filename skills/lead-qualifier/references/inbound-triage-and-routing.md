# Inbound triage and routing

The first hour decides most inbound deals, and in India the enquiry usually arrives when nobody is at a desk.
This file is the triage and the promise.

## The response clock, by channel

| Channel | What the buyer expects | A defensible promise |
|---|---|---|
| WhatsApp | Minutes | Under 30 minutes in business hours; an auto-acknowledgement outside them |
| Phone call missed | Same day | Call back within 2 working hours |
| Website form | Hours | Under 4 business hours |
| Email enquiry | A day | Under 8 business hours |
| Marketplace (IndiaMART, Amazon, GeM) | Fast, because five sellers got it | Under 2 hours; the first credible reply usually wins |
| Social DM | Hours | Under 4 hours, then move to WhatsApp or email |
| Referral | Same day, personally | Under 2 hours, and by the founder if it is a large one |
| Walk-in or exhibition card | A day | Next morning |

The numbers are a promise, not an aspiration. Measure from the lead's timestamp - the form's, the message's, the
marketplace notification's - and say so.

## Why speed is the whole game

| Delay | Effect |
|---|---|
| Under 5 minutes | The buyer is still at the screen, still comparing, still interested |
| 1 hour | They have opened two competitor tabs |
| 1 day | They have spoken to someone else |
| 3 days | The enquiry is now a memory, and the call is an interruption |
| A week | The lead is dead and the source gets blamed |

The business will usually blame the lead quality. The read is almost always the response time, and this role's
job is to prove which it is.

## The routing rules

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| One owner per lead | Named person, not a team inbox |
| Route by segment, then by load | Industry or region first, then round-robin inside the segment |
| Respect language | A Hindi or Marathi enquiry goes to someone who can reply in it |
| Respect channel | A WhatsApp lead is answered on WhatsApp, by the same number |
| Respect capacity | Never route more than the owner can call in a day; the rest go to a tomorrow list with a date |
| Escalate by value | Above a stated deal size, the founder or the senior owner is the first call, not the third |
| No orphan leads | Every lead has an owner field that is not blank, checked daily |

## The queue sort

Sort by **deadline**, then by **score**, never by arrival. The order of work:

1. Past-SLA leads, oldest first - the apology is part of the call.
2. High-fit, high-intent inside the SLA.
3. High-fit, low-intent inside the SLA - a short call or a nurture email.
4. Marketplace leads inside their two hours, because the buyer is talking to competitors right now.
5. Everything else, in score order.

## The acknowledgement that actually works

Four lines, sent by the owner, in the lead's language:

1. Their name and what they asked about, in their words.
2. One sentence that shows the business does this specific thing.
3. The one question that makes the call worth making - quantity, timeline, or the requirement.
4. A named person and a time: "I'll call you at 4pm today. Is that number fine?"

What not to send: "Thank you for your enquiry. Our team will contact you shortly." It is a receipt, not a
response, and it is the single most common reason a good lead goes cold.

## The daily ten-minute pass

| Step | What you do |
|---|---|
| 1 | Count leads by age: under SLA, at SLA, past SLA |
| 2 | List everything past SLA with the owner and the reason |
| 3 | Score the new leads and route them |
| 4 | Read yesterday's routed leads for the outcome: called, no answer, meeting, dead |
| 5 | Note the one lead that was mishandled, and why, in the journal |

Ten minutes, once a day, and the queue never becomes a backlog.

## Weekend and festival reality

- Indian inbound does not stop on Sunday, and a large share arrives after 9pm.
- Either staff a shift, or set an honest auto-acknowledgement that states the next working time - and honour it.
- A festival week has its own rhythm: enquiries spike before Diwali and go quiet during it. Say which week you
  are comparing.
- A promise of "24 hours" that excludes Sunday is fine if it is stated. A promise that is broken silently is
  what loses the lead.

## Anti-patterns

- **A shared inbox with no owner.** Everyone's queue is nobody's queue.
- **Scoring before acknowledging.** The clock runs while you analyse; send the four-line reply first, then score.
- **Routing by round-robin into a team that cannot take the load.** It looks fair and it is a slow queue.
- **Answering a WhatsApp lead by email** because the CRM only holds email. The channel is part of the promise.
- **A CRM that shows a lead as "contacted" when a template was sent.** Contacted means a human spoke or wrote,
  specifically.
