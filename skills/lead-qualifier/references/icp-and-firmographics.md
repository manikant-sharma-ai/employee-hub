# ICP and firmographics

An ICP is not a description of who you would like to sell to. It is a written, testable statement of
which companies have bought, at what size, from which industry, with which trigger - and which ones
have not.

## The five layers

| Layer | Question | Field that records it |
|---|---|---|
| Firmographic | What kind of company is it? | Industry, employees, turnover band, age, ownership |
| Geographic | Where is it, and can we serve there? | City, state, service radius, language |
| Technographic | What does it already run? | Stack, platform, tooling, integrations |
| Situational | Why now? | Trigger event, timeline, budget, decision process |
| Behavioural | How did it arrive? | Channel, content consumed, response speed |

Score the first four. The fifth is a tiebreaker, never a substitute - a lead from a great channel with
a bad fit is still a bad fit.

## Building the ICP from won deals

Do this before writing any rubric, and do it with the last twenty deals:

1. **List every won deal** with industry, size, city, source, deal value, sales cycle length.
2. **Mark the top five by margin**, not by revenue. The biggest deal is often the worst one.
3. **Find the two attributes all five share.** That is the ICP. If they share four, the ICP is
   probably too narrow and the sample too small.
4. **List every lost deal** and find the attribute they share. That is the anti-ICP, and it is more
   useful than the ICP.
5. **Write both down in one paragraph each**, with the numbers.

An ICP built from a founder's ambition rather than from the won list is a wish with a target market
section.

## Size bands that actually behave differently

| Band | Typical behaviour | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| 1-10 employees | Founder decides everything, buys fast, pays slowly | Short cycle, small ticket, high churn |
| 11-50 | One manager owns the problem, needs a business case | Medium cycle, the sweet spot for most SMB tools |
| 51-200 | Procurement appears, security review appears | Longer cycle, larger ticket, more stakeholders |
| 201-1000 | Committee, pilot, legal, IT | Needs a sales process, not a founder |
| 1000+ | Vendor onboarding, MSA, compliance | Only with a dedicated sales function |

The mistake is treating a 5-person firm and a 500-person firm as the same lead because both filled the
same form. They need different pitches, different prices and different patience.

## Geography, in the Indian context

- **Service radius.** A service business that cannot reach a city in four hours has a geographic
  disqualifier that must be checked before anything else.
- **Language.** A lead writing in Tamil or Bengali is not a problem; a lead whose decision-maker only
  reads that language is a resourcing question the business must answer honestly.
- **Entity and compliance.** GSTIN presence tells you the business is registered and trading. MCA
  tells you the entity type and incorporation date. Both are one lookup and both beat an assumption.
- **Payment behaviour.** A first order from a new geography at a large ticket is a credit question as
  much as a sales one.

## Trigger events that make a lead good this month

| Trigger | Why it matters | Where it shows |
|---|---|---|
| Just raised funding | Budget exists and a deadline exists | News, LinkedIn, MCA filings |
| New location or new product line | The old solution no longer fits | Their website, press |
| A competitor's price increase or outage | Switching window is open | Public posts, review sites |
| A hiring post for the role that owns the problem | The problem is recognised internally | Job boards, LinkedIn |
| A regulatory deadline | The purchase becomes non-discretionary | The regulation itself |
| A failed implementation | The pain is fresh and funded | The conversation |

A lead with a trigger is worth three without one. When the form does not ask, the first call should.

## The anti-ICP, written down

| Signal | Why it fails | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Wants a free pilot with no date | No decision, no budget | Decline with the paid pilot option |
| Asks for the price before describing the problem | Price-shopping, not buying | Send the published range, offer a call |
| Wants a feature on the roadmap first | The roadmap becomes the negotiation | Decline politely, log it |
| Is a competitor, an agency reselling, or a student | Not a buyer | Route explicitly, never score |
| Needs a custom build at an SMB price | The margin is negative | Decline with a referral |
| Has no authority and no path to it | The cycle never ends | Ask for the decision-maker or close |

## Anti-patterns

- **An ICP with no numbers.** "Mid-market companies in India" is a map with no scale.
- **An ICP that excludes the best existing customers.** If the top three customers fail the ICP, the
  ICP is wrong, not the customers.
- **Scoring firmographics you cannot verify.** A guessed employee count is worse than an unknown.
- **Letting the biggest logo override the fit.** Enterprise logos at SMB prices are the most
  expensive leads in the pipeline.
- **Never updating the ICP.** It should be re-derived every quarter from the last twenty deals.
