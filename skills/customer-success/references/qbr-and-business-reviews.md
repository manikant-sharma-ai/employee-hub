# QBRs and business reviews

A quarterly business review is a meeting where the customer decides whether the
money was worth it. If it becomes a product update, they decide without you.

## Who comes, and what each one needs

| Attendee | What they want from the hour | What you must bring |
|---|---|---|
| Economic buyer | Proof the spend paid for itself | Their number, before and after |
| Daily users | To be heard about what is annoying | The three fixes you already made |
| Champion | To look good in front of their boss | A slide they can forward |
| Procurement or finance | No surprises on the invoice | The commercial position, stated plainly |
| You | A decision, not a thank-you | One ask, one risk, one action list |

Two of your people maximum. Five of theirs is a good room.

## The 45-minute agenda

| Minute | Segment | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 0-3 | Why we are here | "Three numbers, one risk, one ask - and your decisions" |
| 3-10 | What you set out to do | Their stated goal from the sale, quoted back |
| 10-20 | What the data says | The anchor metric, the trend, the comparison to their own baseline |
| 20-27 | What worked, with a customer proof point | One case, same segment, same size |
| 27-35 | What is in the way | Your open issues, your roadmap items that matter to them, honest dates |
| 35-40 | The ask | Renewal, expansion, a reference, a case study, or an intro |
| 40-45 | Decisions and owners | Who does what by when, read back aloud |

Send the pack 48 hours before. A QBR where the numbers are seen for the first
time in the room becomes a negotiation about the numbers.

## The four numbers that belong in every pack

1. **The outcome metric** - what they bought it for. Orders, hours saved, cost per case, revenue per seat, compliance filings on time.
2. **Adoption** - active users against licensed seats, or usage against the plan limit.
3. **Reliability** - uptime, ticket volume, average resolution, open issues.
4. **Money** - what they paid, what it produced, and the renewal position.

Everything else is supporting material. A QBR with fifteen slides of feature
adoption and no outcome metric is a status report.

## Making the case in the customer's currency

| If they bought it to | The number to show |
|---|---|
| Save time | Hours per month, at their loaded cost per hour |
| Reduce errors | Error rate before and after, and the cost of one error |
| Grow sales | Orders or leads per month, and the conversion change |
| Stay compliant | Filings on time, notices avoided, audit findings closed |
| Serve customers faster | First-response and resolution time, repeat contacts |
| Control cost | Spend per unit, wastage, duplicate purchases avoided |

Get the loaded cost from them, not from a benchmark. "At the ₹350 per hour you
quoted, that is 62 hours a month" lands; "industry average savings of 30%" does not.

## The ask, chosen deliberately

| Ask | When it is right | What to bring |
|---|---|---|
| Renewal | Inside 120 days | The value case and the uplift clause |
| Expansion | Usage at capacity or a second team asking | Their own data, and a small pilot |
| Reference call | Score strong, champion active | A named prospect profile, 20 minutes |
| Case study | A clean before/after | The numbers and a permission line |
| Intro | Their peer has the same problem | One sentence they can forward |

One ask per QBR. Two asks reads as a sales call and the champion has to defend
both to their boss.

## The pack, page by page

1. Title: account, period, attendees, the one-line purpose.
2. Their goal, in their words, quoted with the date they said it.
3. The outcome number, with the baseline and the source.
4. Adoption, with the trend and the seats not used.
5. What we fixed since last time - the three items they raised, closed.
6. What is open, with dates you will hold.
7. The road ahead for them, not the product roadmap in general.
8. The ask.
9. Decisions and owners, filled in live.

Nine pages, ten at most. Use `assets/qbr-pack.md`.

## Running the room

- **Start with their goal, not your agenda.** It sets the frame for everything after.
- **Read the numbers aloud and pause.** Silence after a good number is the customer doing your selling.
- **Invite the objection early**: "Where does this not match what you see?" An objection at minute 15 is fixable; at minute 44 it is a decision.
- **Write the action list in the meeting, on screen.** Everyone leaves with the same list.
- **Send the minutes within 24 hours**, with the decisions, the owners and the next QBR date.

## When the QBR goes badly

| What happens | What it usually means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| They cancel the meeting twice | The champion cannot defend the spend | Ask the champion directly what changed; offer a shorter, harder conversation |
| They send a junior instead of the buyer | The spend is not being defended at the top | Ask for a 20-minute session with the buyer on one number |
| The numbers do not match their view | Your metric is not their metric | Ask them to define success in one sentence, and rebuild the pack on that |
| They arrive with a complaint | Something broke and nobody told them | Fix the disclosure first, then the QBR - trust before numbers |
| Silence through the ask | No internal sponsor for the renewal | Ask what would need to be true; write it down; set a date |

## Anti-patterns

- **The 30-slide feature review.** It answers a question nobody asked.
- **Saving the bad news for the end.** The room spends the hour waiting for the other shoe.
- **No ask.** A QBR with no ask leaves the renewal to chance and to procurement.
- **Sending the pack at 9 pm the night before.** It guarantees the numbers are read in the meeting.
- **Letting the champion present your numbers.** They will soften them, and soft numbers do not renew contracts.
- **Holding a QBR for an account that does not warrant one.** A ₹50,000 account gets a one-page value mail, not an hour.
