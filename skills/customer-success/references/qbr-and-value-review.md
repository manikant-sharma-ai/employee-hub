# QBR and value review

A quarterly business review is not a status meeting with a deck. It is the one hour
where the customer decides, out loud, whether the money is well spent. Everything
in it serves that decision.

## The 45-minute shape

| Minutes | Segment | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 0-3 | Their agenda first | "What do you need out of this hour?" |
| 3-10 | What they said they wanted | The outcome from signature, in their words |
| 10-22 | What changed, in their numbers | The value proof |
| 22-30 | What did not work | Named honestly, with what we did about it |
| 30-38 | Next quarter's outcome | Agreed, with a number and a date |
| 38-45 | The ask | Renewal, expansion or a referral - one ask, stated plainly |

If the meeting is 60 minutes, add eight minutes to the value section and five to
the ask. Never add slides.

## The value proof, in their units

| What the product does | The number to show | The method |
|---|---|---|
| Saves time | Hours per week × people × weeks | Time study, or the customer's own estimate, labelled as an estimate |
| Processes volume | Orders, tickets, invoices handled | System count, this quarter vs last |
| Reduces cost | Cost per case, per order, per hire | Their unit cost, before and after |
| Increases revenue | Attributed revenue or conversion | Their attribution, not ours |
| Reduces risk | Incidents, audit findings, days to close | Their record |

Three rules: **the customer's numbers beat ours**, **the method is named**, and
**an estimate is labelled**. A QBR that says "we estimate 30% efficiency gains" with
no method is the reason customers stop attending QBRs.

## The pre-read

Send two days before, five slides maximum, so the hour is a discussion rather than
a presentation:

1. The outcome agreed last quarter, verbatim.
2. Progress against it, with the number and the source.
3. Two things that did not go well.
4. Next quarter's proposed outcome.
5. The ask.

A QBR with no pre-read becomes a demo, and a demo is what the customer already
bought.

## Multi-threading the room

| Role | Why they matter | What to do if absent |
|---|---|---|
| Champion | Runs the tool, sells it internally | Reschedule - a QBR without them is theatre |
| Economic buyer | Signs the renewal | Ask the champion to bring them to the next one |
| Blocker | Can stop it quietly | Find out what they need; give them a win |
| Finance | Owns the budget line | Send the value summary in money, not features |

If you cannot name the economic buyer, the account is single-threaded and it is at
risk regardless of its score. Write that in the account review, not in a footnote.

## Questions that surface the truth

- "If this tool disappeared tomorrow, what would you do instead?" - the substitute test, and the best churn predictor there is.
- "Who else should be in this conversation?" - finds the blocker before they find you.
- "What would have to be true for you to double your usage?" - an expansion question disguised as a usage question.
- "What is the one thing we do that you would not miss?" - uncomfortable, and it is the roadmap.
- "Is there anything you have stopped telling us?" - the question that gets the real answer.

## When the QBR goes badly

| Signal | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| They sent a junior person | Deprioritised | Reschedule with the champion; do not present to the room you have |
| They cancelled twice | A decision is being made without you | Ask directly: "is this still a priority for you?" |
| They challenge the value numbers | The case was never theirs | Rebuild the outcome together, in their words, and re-measure |
| They ask about contract terms | They are evaluating exit | Stop selling; ask what would need to be true to stay |

## Anti-patterns

- **A deck of 30 slides.** It is a status report, and status reports get cancelled.
- **Value measured only in our metrics** - logins, tickets closed, features shipped.
- **No ask.** A QBR with no ask teaches the customer the relationship has no next step.
- **Hiding the bad news until slide 24.** Lead with it; it buys credibility for the rest.
- **The account manager talking for 40 minutes.** The customer should speak for half of it.
- **Holding a QBR on a healthy account with no expansion intent.** Spend the hour where the decision is.
