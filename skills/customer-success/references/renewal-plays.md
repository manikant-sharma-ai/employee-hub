# Renewal plays

A renewal is a sales cycle with a fixed end date and no new logo at the end. Run
it like one.

## The 90-day calendar

| Day out | Work | Artefact |
|---|---|---|
| 120 | Confirm the contract terms: end date, notice period, uplift, auto-renewal, signatory | Contract summary in the CRM |
| 90 | Internal go/no-go: health score, risks, the number we will accept | Renewal brief, one page |
| 75 | Value review with the champion: the outcome number, the year in one page | Value summary |
| 60 | Commercial conversation with the buyer: term, seats, uplift | Proposal v1 |
| 45 | Objections and procurement: paperwork, vendor onboarding, PO process | Their forms, started early |
| 30 | Signature path: who signs, what they need, when | Signed or escalated |
| 15 | Contingency: if unsigned, name the blocker and the decision date | Escalation note |
| 0 | Renewed, or an honest exit with a date | Updated CRM, a learning |

The most common cause of a lost renewal is a PO process that takes six weeks and
was discovered in week three.

## The uplift conversation

| Situation | What to ask | What to say |
|---|---|---|
| Contract has an uplift clause (5-10%) | Apply it, with notice | "Your agreement includes the annual revision; here is the new figure and what is in it" |
| No clause, usage up sharply | Uplift tied to their growth | "You processed 3x the volume this year; the plan should match it" |
| No clause, usage flat | Hold the price, add a term | "Same price, three-year term, and priority support" |
| Customer in difficulty | Hold or reduce, with a shorter term | "Let us do six months at the same rate and revisit in June" |
| Multi-year | 5-10% for a 2-3 year commitment | "A three-year term at today's price protects you from the revision" |

Never open with the discount. The first number spoken in a renewal anchors every
number after it, and the customer will use it next year too.

## The renewal brief, one page

1. Account, ARR, contract dates, notice period, uplift clause.
2. Health score and the inputs behind it.
3. What they bought it to do, and what the data says happened.
4. The three things that went wrong this year and what was done.
5. The commercial ask: target number, floor, walk-away.
6. The people: signatory, champion, blocker.
7. The risk and the date we will know.

The floor is decided before the conversation, in writing, by someone with the
authority to set it. A floor invented mid-call is not a floor.

## Objection handling

| They say | What is usually true | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| "It is too expensive" | They cannot see the return, or the budget moved | Put their own outcome number beside the price; ask which line item competes |
| "We are not using it enough" | Adoption, not value, is the problem | Offer a 60-day adoption plan with a named owner on their side, before discussing price |
| "We are evaluating alternatives" | Someone internally wants a change, or a competitor called | Ask what would have to be true to stay; get a meeting with the person driving it |
| "Let us go monthly" | Cash or commitment anxiety | Accept if the annual rate rises 10-15%; never trade commitment for nothing |
| "Our budget cycle is April" | True, and it needs a bridge | Pro-rate to the cycle with a signed intent, or a short extension |
| "The team that bought it has changed" | The champion left | Rebuild the case for the new buyer from scratch, with the usage data |
| "We will decide after the busy season" | A real deferral or a soft no | Ask for the date, put it in the calendar, and check the usage in between |

## What "renewed" includes

A renewal is not signed until the paperwork is done: the order form or amendment,
the PO if they need one, the invoice raised, and the CRM updated. Renewals that
live in an inbox are renewals that get forgotten at month-end.

## The exit, done properly

When the account is going:

- Say it plainly and early: "It looks like this is not going to renew. Tell me what I am missing."
- Ask for the reason in their words, and write it down verbatim.
- Offer a clean wind-down: data export, a handover call, the billing stop date.
- Ask for the one thing that still costs nothing - a referral to a team with the same problem, or a sentence on what would have changed the outcome.
- Log the loss with the reason code. Fifteen losses with reasons is a product strategy; one angry exit interview is an anecdote.

## Anti-patterns

- **Starting at 30 days because the renewal "was always fine".** The auto-renewal that worked for three years is the one that gets cancelled by a new procurement manager.
- **Renewing with the champion only.** If the buyer has never heard the value case, the champion is defending it alone.
- **Discounting to avoid a conversation.** It buys a year and teaches the account the price was never real.
- **Silent auto-renewals.** They convert a customer into a passive payer who cancels the moment someone audits the spend.
- **No notice-period check.** A 60-day notice period discovered at day 40 is a lost year.
