# Expansion plays

Expansion is the cheapest revenue a business has, and the easiest to get wrong by
selling it before it is earned.

## The five signals that an account is ready

| Signal | What it looks like | What it justifies |
|---|---|---|
| Capacity hit | Usage at 85%+ of the plan limit, twice in a quarter | A seat or volume upgrade |
| Second team asking | Another department mails you directly | A second licence or a wider rollout |
| Workaround | A spreadsheet or a manual step they built around you | The next module or integration |
| New geography or product line | They expanded, you did not | An added scope conversation |
| Outcome proven | The number moved, and they said so in writing | A case study, then the ask |

A signal is not a hunch. If you cannot point at the data or the mail, it is not a
signal.

## The expansion ladder

| Step | Move | Size of ask |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Show the limit approaching, before it is a problem | No ask, just information |
| 2 | Offer the pilot to the second team, at their scale | Small, time-boxed |
| 3 | Price the upgrade against their own growth | Their numbers, not a rate card |
| 4 | Bundle the next module with a term | A multi-year commitment |
| 5 | Ask for the reference and the intro | Free, and worth more than the upsell |

## Pricing an expansion

| Basis | When to use | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Per seat | Value scales with people | Seat counting disputes; define "active seat" in the order form |
| Per volume | Value scales with transactions | Seasonality; use a banded price, not a hard cliff |
| Per module | A genuinely new capability | Bundling that makes the price impossible to explain |
| Flat uplift at renewal | Simple accounts, small values | The customer who grew 4x and pays the same |
| Revenue share | Marketplaces and channel tools | Audit rights and honest reporting |

Never quote an expansion price that is lower per unit than the existing contract
unless the volume justifies it in writing, or you have repriced the whole account.

## The expansion conversation, in four lines

1. "You are at 92% of your limit and your peak was in October. Here is what the next band looks like."
2. "Your ops team asked me about access twice in three weeks. A 10-seat pilot for them would cost X for 90 days."
3. "If the pilot works, the annual figure is Y and it starts when they go live, not now."
4. "What would need to be true for this to be an easy yes?"

Line four is the one that gets the real objection.

## Who to sell it to

| Buyer | Cares about | Bring |
|---|---|---|
| The daily user team lead | Their team's workload | A time saved number, and a 90-day pilot |
| The champion | Looking good internally | A one-pager they can forward, with the numbers already in it |
| Finance | Budget timing and unit cost | Cost per transaction, and the renewal date |
| IT or security | Access, data, integration | The security note before they ask for it |

Expansion that skips the champion and lands on finance reads as a bypass, and the
champion will quietly kill it.

## When not to expand

- **Health score below 6.5.** Fix the account first; an upsell to an unhappy customer accelerates the churn.
- **Champion changed in the last 60 days.** The new person has no reason to say yes yet.
- **A P1 is open.** Nothing sells while something is broken.
- **The renewal is unsigned.** Sequence the renewal first; an expansion conversation inside an open renewal gives them a reason to renegotiate everything.
- **The customer is in a cost-cutting cycle.** Ask for the reference instead; it costs them nothing and keeps the relationship warm.

## Measuring it

| Metric | Definition | What good looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Net revenue retention | This year's revenue from last year's cohort, over last year's | 100-110% for SMB tools, 115%+ for enterprise |
| Expansion rate | Expansion revenue over opening ARR, per quarter | 5-8% a quarter is healthy |
| Time to expansion | Months from go-live to first upsell | Under 9 for a tool, under 12 for a service |
| Attach rate | Share of accounts with more than one product | Rising, or you are selling one thing to everyone |
| Pilot-to-paid | Pilots that convert | 50%+, or the pilot was a favour |

## Anti-patterns

- **Upselling at the QBR before the value is proven.** The customer hears "you owe us more" before "you got more".
- **A discount on the expansion that undercuts the base contract.** It reprices the whole account.
- **Selling seats the customer will not use.** Unused seats are the first thing cut at the next renewal.
- **Expansion targets set before health is fixed.** The team chases growth and loses the base.
- **One expansion conversation with no pilot and no date.** It stays a "we should do that" for a year.
