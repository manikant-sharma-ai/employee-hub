# Renewal playbook

A renewal is decided long before the renewal date. This file is the ninety days
that decide it, and the conversations inside them.

## The 90/60/30 checkpoints

| Day | Checkpoint | Output | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| D-90 | Renewal opened | Health score, multi-thread map, risk list, expansion case | Account owner |
| D-75 | Value review | The outcome delivered, in their numbers, sent in writing | Account owner |
| D-60 | Buyer conversation | Budget confirmed, decision process and date known | Founder or owner |
| D-45 | Proposal | Renewal terms, any expansion, the price and what it buys | Owner |
| D-30 | Negotiation | Objections surfaced and answered; no new terms after D-14 | Founder |
| D-14 | Signature | DocuSign or paper, countersigned, PO number if required | Finance |
| D-7 | Handover to delivery | New term's outcome agreed, onboarding refreshed | Owner |

Miss D-60 and the renewal becomes a rescue in the last two weeks, where the only
lever left is price.

## The three renewal types

| Type | Signs | Play |
|---|---|---|
| **Routine** | Green band, multi-threaded, usage growing | Value summary, 5% uplift if justified, ask for a referral or case study |
| **At risk** | Amber/Red, champion quiet, tickets unresolved | Save play by cause (`references/save-plays-and-escalation.md`), founder in the room by D-45 |
| **Expansion renewal** | Usage at ceiling, new team or region, outcome exceeded | New seats or tier, priced against the value, with a named sponsor |

## The price conversation

Four sentences, in this order, and no discount before the third:

1. **The value, in their number** - "You processed 41,000 orders through this last year, at a cost per order of ₹6 against ₹11 before."
2. **The change, stated plainly** - "The renewal is ₹18.9 lakh, up 5% from ₹18 lakh, from 1 April."
3. **The reason** - "That covers the two new warehouses and the API volume you added in October."
4. **The choice** - "We can keep it at the current number of warehouses at the old price, or move to the full scope. Which fits this year?"

What not to do: open with the discount; apologise for the increase; compare with a
competitor's list price; or offer a multi-year lock-in before the customer has said
what they need. Price policy belongs to `pricing-analyst`, and a discount needs the
founder's approval with its cost over the remaining term written down.

## Multi-threading

| Question | Where the answer lives | If the answer is "we don't know" |
|---|---|---|
| Who signs? | CRM, or the last contract | Ask the champion directly: "who else needs to be comfortable?" |
| Who can veto? | Finance, IT, security, a plant manager | Ask what a failed renewal would look like internally |
| Who benefits most? | The team using it daily | Ask for a two-line quote from that team |
| Who has not been contacted in 90 days? | The contact list | Contact them before the renewal, not during it |

A single-threaded renewal has a 50% failure rate when the thread leaves, and the
thread always leaves at the worst moment.

## Objections and the honest answers

| Objection | What it usually means | Answer shape |
|---|---|---|
| "It is too expensive" | Value is not visible | Rebuild the value number together; then discuss scope |
| "We are cutting costs" | Budget is gone this year | Smaller scope, same start date; never a free extension |
| "The team is not using it" | Adoption failure, our fault | An adoption plan with a date and a named owner, before the price conversation |
| "We are evaluating alternatives" | A real process is running | Ask for the criteria and the date; do not disparage anyone |
| "We need to think about it" | No economic buyer in the room | Ask who else needs to be comfortable and when |

## Renewal paperwork, in order

1. Confirmed scope and term in writing, in an email the buyer replies to.
2. PO number, if the customer's finance needs one - ask at D-45, not D-5.
3. GST details and billing entity confirmed against the original contract.
4. Signed order form or agreement; anything non-standard goes to `legal-drafter`.
5. Invoice raised on the start date, not before, unless prepayment is agreed.
6. CRM updated with the new dates, value and the next QBR.

## Anti-patterns

- **Starting at D-30.** The customer has already decided by then.
- **Renewal by email only.** A renewal with no conversation is a price negotiation with no context.
- **Auto-renewal as the strategy.** It delays the decision, it does not make it.
- **A discount to save a relationship that has no value.** It buys three months and teaches the customer the price was never real.
- **Forgetting the PO.** The classic Indian enterprise delay: signed in March, invoiced in April, paid in June.
- **No outcome for the new term.** The next renewal starts the day this one is signed.
