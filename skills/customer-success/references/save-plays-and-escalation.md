# Save plays and escalation

A save play is chosen by cause. The wrong play on the right account accelerates the
exit, because it answers a problem the customer does not have.

## Diagnose the cause first

| Cause | The evidence | The play | What makes it fail |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Value never landed** | Low usage from week two, no outcome defined | Re-onboarding against one outcome, 30 days, named owner on both sides | Sending more training videos |
| **Champion left** | New contact, no meetings, silence | Executive intro to the new leader, with the value history in one page | Emailing the old champion again |
| **Support failure** | Repeat tickets, escalation unresolved | Named senior support owner, a fix date, a written apology with the facts | Discounting before the fix |
| **Product gap** | Feature requests blocking their process | Honest answer on roadmap status, a workaround with a date, or an exit path | "It is coming" with no date |
| **Price** | Budget cut, competitor quote | Scope reduction instead of a discount; keep the unit price | A discount that resets the anchor |
| **Merger or shutdown** | Public news, new parent process | Retention through the transition, or a clean exit with a reference | Pretending nothing changed |

## The save plan, in five lines

Write it, do not discuss it:

1. **The cause**, with the evidence that supports it.
2. **The play**, in one sentence.
3. **The owner** - a named person on our side and one on theirs.
4. **The date** the play is judged - usually 30 days.
5. **The condition for stopping** - the signal that means it is over.

A save plan without a stop condition becomes an account kept alive on discounts for
two years.

## The five-day sequence for a Red account

| Day | Action | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Call the champion; if gone, find the new owner | Account owner |
| 1 | Internal note: cause, play, owner, date | Account owner |
| 2 | Founder-to-executive message, short, no pitch | Founder |
| 3 | A concrete fix or a scoped plan, in writing | Owner + product or support |
| 5 | A 20-minute call with the fix and a decision date | Founder |

Silence after day 5 is the outcome. Do not send a sixth email; escalate to a
decision: fix with a date, or exit well.

## When to let an account go

Let it go when all four are true:

- The cause is not fixable inside the customer's timeline.
- The value has never landed and cannot land in 60 days.
- The account is unprofitable after the support cost, or the behaviour is abusive.
- Keeping it costs a founder's attention that a healthier account needs.

An exit done well produces a reference, a referral and sometimes a return in two
years. An exit done badly produces a public review and a legal threat.

## The clean exit

1. Agree the end date and what happens to data - export, deletion, retention.
2. Settle the final invoice and any unused prepayment, per contract.
3. Hand over the data in a usable format, with a date.
4. Ask for one thing: a short reference, or an intro to the team that replaces you.
5. Write what happened into `notes/learnings.md`, with the cause, not the blame.

Data deletion and export obligations sit in the contract and under the DPDP Act -
route the wording to `legal-drafter` and the timeline to `compliance-watch`.

## Escalation ladder

| Level | Trigger | Who is told | Within |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Amber band, one bad signal | Account owner | 14 days |
| 2 | Red band, or a named complaint from the buyer | Founder, support lead | 5 working days |
| 3 | Renewal at risk inside 45 days, or an unresolved severity-1 issue | Founder, product, support | 48 hours |
| 4 | Public complaint, legal threat, or a data incident | Founder, `legal-drafter`, `compliance-watch` | Same day |

Never escalate without the cause, the play and the date. An escalation that says
"the customer is unhappy" moves the problem to someone else and solves nothing.

## Anti-patterns

- **Discount first.** It answers the one cause that is least often true, and it
  teaches the base that renewal is a negotiation.
- **A save play run by the account owner alone.** A champion who left needs a peer
  conversation, not a status email.
- **Weekly "any update?" emails.** They signal that nothing is being done.
- **Keeping a hostile account for the ARR.** The support cost and the team's
  morale are real, and they do not appear in the renewal number.
- **No written record of the save.** The next person repeats the failed play.
