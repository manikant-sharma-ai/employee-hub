# Expansion and upsell

Expansion is the cheapest revenue a business has, and the easiest to fake. This
file is the test that separates a real expansion from a hopeful upsell.

## The three-part test

A conversation is worth having when **two of three** are true, and only a
proposal when all three are:

| Part | Evidence | Where to look |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger | Something changed at the customer | New team, new region, new volume, new compliance need, a merger |
| Adoption | They are using what they already pay for | Seat use >70%, core action growing |
| Sponsor | Someone will argue for it internally | Champion with a named outcome and a budget line |

One of three is a hope. Selling into 40% adoption creates next year's churn and
this year's discount request.

## Trigger events, ranked by reliability

| Trigger | What it usually means | What to propose |
|---|---|---|
| Seats at 85-100% for two months | The team is queueing for access | More seats, priced per seat with a volume break |
| A new department asking for access | Organic pull | A department tier, with its own admin and reporting |
| New location, warehouse or plant | Volume and complexity | Multi-entity or multi-location tier |
| Volume crossing a plan threshold | They are growing into a limit | The next tier, with the unit economics shown |
| A compliance or audit requirement | A deadline, so budget exists | The module that answers the audit, sold against the deadline |
| A new leader replacing the champion | A review of every vendor | Do not sell; secure the renewal first |

## The expansion conversation, 20 minutes

1. **Their number** - what changed since the last review, in their words.
2. **The gap** - what the current scope does not cover, stated as a cost, not a feature.
3. **The proof** - one customer of similar size who did this, with a number.
4. **The price and the boundary** - what it costs, what it does not include.
5. **The next step** - a scoped trial with a date, or a proposal within 48 hours.

Never a demo. They already own the product; a demo signals you have not looked at
their account.

## Pricing the expansion

| Approach | When | Watch out |
|---|---|---|
| Same unit price, more units | Usage-driven growth | Leaves volume money on the table |
| Volume break at thresholds | Predictable growth | The break must still clear contribution margin |
| New module at list | A distinct job | Bundling it free destroys its price forever |
| Uplift at renewal | Value proven, no new scope | 5-10% is defensible with a number; 20% needs a new outcome |

The margin check is not optional: a 20% volume discount on a 40% contribution
margin gives away half the profit on the incremental revenue. Route the price to
`pricing-analyst` and the invoice to `finance-ops`.

## What expansion is not

- **Not a rescue for a Red account.** Fix the value first; selling more into a
  failing account accelerates the exit and poisons the reference.
- **Not a renewal tactic.** "Expand and we will hold the price" is a discount with
  extra steps, and the customer knows it.
- **Not a feature dump.** A list of what else the product does is not a case.
- **Not a founder's relationship favour.** A deal the account cannot adopt is a
  refund in nine months.

## The expansion forecast

| Stage | Definition | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Identified | Trigger seen, no conversation | 10% |
| Qualified | Two of three tests true | 30% |
| Proposed | Scope and price in writing | 60% |
| Verbal | Sponsor has said yes | 85% |
| Signed | Order form countersigned | 100% |

Weight the pipeline honestly and the number stops being a wish. Review it monthly
alongside the health bands, because the same accounts appear in both.

## Anti-patterns

- **Upselling a customer who has not hit the outcome they bought.** The fastest
  route to a bad review and a cancelled contract.
- **Expansion targets set without an adoption floor.** They produce pressure, then
  churn, then a lower base.
- **No onboarding for the new scope.** The expansion fails in the same way the
  original purchase would have without onboarding.
- **The same proposal for every account.** It reads as a template, because it is.
- **Forgetting the existing contract's terms.** A new module may already be
  included; check before you price it.
