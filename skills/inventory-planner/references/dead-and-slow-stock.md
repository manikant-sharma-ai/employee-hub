# Dead and slow stock

Dead stock is cash that has stopped moving and is still on the balance sheet. The job is
to price it, provision it and get it out without damaging the price of the stock that
still sells.

## The ageing table

| Bucket | Last movement | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Active | Within 90 days | Normal policy |
| Slow | 91-180 days | Stop buying, review price and placement |
| Dormant | 181-365 days | Provision 25-50%, plan an exit |
| Dead | Over 365 days | Provision 75-100%, liquidate or scrap |
| Obsolete | Superseded, expired, unsellable | Write off, dispose, document |

Always in units and in cost value. Market value is a separate, labelled column.

## The provision guide

| Bucket | Indicative provision | Who decides |
|---|---|---|
| Slow | 10-25% | This role recommends |
| Dormant | 25-50% | Accountant sets, with the ageing in front of them |
| Dead | 75-100% | Accountant, with the CA |
| Obsolete | 100% | Accountant and the CA |

This role supplies the ageing, the evidence and the action. It never sets the provision
and never values inventory for the accounts.

## The liquidation ladder

Work down the ladder, in this order. Each rung has a price floor and an approver.

| Rung | Channel | Price expectation | Damage risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Bundle with a live SKU | 70-90% of MRP, blended | Low - the live SKU carries it |
| 2 | Own channel, clearance section | 50-70% | Medium - visible to full-price buyers |
| 3 | B2B lot sale (IndiaMART, reseller) | 30-50% of cost-to-MRP | Low - different audience |
| 4 | Marketplace clearance or deal site | 25-40% | High - it sets a price reference |
| 5 | Export or institutional buyer | 20-35% | Low |
| 6 | Scrap, donate or destroy | 0-10% | None, but document it |

Rules that protect the rest of the business:

- **Never discount the hero SKU to clear a slow one.** Bundle instead.
- **Never let a marketplace clearance listing be found from the main listing.** Different
  title, different images, different pack.
- **Set the floor before the negotiation**, in writing, with the approver's name.
- **One channel at a time.** Listing the same lot in four places invites the buyer to
  wait.

## The dead-stock file

| Field | Why |
|---|---|
| SKU, description | Identify it without opening the warehouse |
| Units, cost value, MRP | The size of the problem |
| Last sale date and last movement | The age, honestly |
| Reason (season missed, spec changed, oversupply, discontinued, damaged) | Determines the rung |
| Recommended rung and floor | The decision, ready to approve |
| Cash released | Why anyone should care |
| Approver and date | Accountability |
| Outcome | Closes the loop; without it the file is a wish list |

## The five reasons, and what each implies

| Reason | Implication |
|---|---|
| Season missed | Order earlier next year; the lesson is in the lead time, not the SKU |
| Specification changed | The customer changed; sell to the segment that still wants the old spec |
| Oversupply from a price break | The discount was smaller than the carrying cost - put it in `learnings.md` |
| Discontinued line | Clear fast, it will not come back |
| Damaged or expired | Dispose, document, fix the cause |

## The recovery rate that matters

Track, quarterly: dead-stock value at the start, value cleared, cash recovered, value
written off, and the average recovery percentage by rung. A business that clears dead
stock at 22% of cost every year has a buying problem, not a liquidation problem.

## Anti-patterns

| Anti-pattern | Cost |
|---|---|
| Calling it "slow-moving" with no value | Nobody acts; the number grows quietly |
| Discounting the hero SKU to clear the tail | Permanent damage to the price of the product that pays |
| Waiting for the season to come back | It does not, and the stock ages another year |
| Scrapping without a record | The loss appears in the accounts with no explanation |
| Clearing once a year, in a panic | The lowest recovery rate of any method |
| No approver on the floor price | A salesperson clears at any price to hit a target |
