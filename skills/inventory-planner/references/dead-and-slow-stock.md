# Dead and slow stock

Dead stock is not an inventory problem, it is a cash problem with a storage cost
attached. The decision is which cure, and by when.

## The three categories

| Category | Definition | Usual cure |
|---|---|---|
| Dead | No sales in the review period, stock on hand | Liquidate, bundle, or write down |
| Slow | Selling, but cover beyond the policy ceiling | Stop ordering, promote, or reprice |
| Stranded | Selling only in one size, colour or location | Split the SKU; the aggregate hides it |

Report the cash at cost for each, because that is the number the owner feels.

## Before calling something dead

1. **Is the sales history long enough?** Four weeks in a seasonal business calls half the catalogue dead every off-season.
2. **Was it ever listed?** A SKU with no listing, no photograph or no price is not dead, it is unsold.
3. **Is it a size or variant problem?** The aggregate says zero; the size curve says one size sold out and the rest never moved.
4. **Is the stock figure real?** Negative stock and phantom stock both exist. Count before you write down.

## The cures, cheapest first

1. **Bundle** with a fast mover. Costs margin, preserves the price and clears units.
2. **Reprice** with a clear reason - end of line, last season, single size. A discount with no story trains customers to wait.
3. **Channel shift** - retail stock that will not move goes to bulk liquidation (IndiaMART is the first call for surplus in India), not to a deeper retail discount, which damages the retail price for everything else.
4. **Donate** where the category allows it, with the documentation the accountant needs.
5. **Write down or write off** - the last resort, with the accounting treatment confirmed in writing first.

## What to record

For every dead SKU: quantity, cost value, months since last sale, the chosen cure,
the owner, the date, and the expected cash released. A dead-stock list with no
dates is a report; with dates it is a plan.

## The prevention rule

Dead stock is created by a purchase decision, not by a sales failure. Every plan
this role produces should state the maximum cover it will allow on a C item, and
every order above that ceiling should be refused or escalated with a reason.
