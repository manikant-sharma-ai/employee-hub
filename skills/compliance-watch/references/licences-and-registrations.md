# Licences and registrations

What a business must hold depends on four things: what it sells, where it operates,
how many people it employs, and how much it turns over. This file is the map from
those four to the paperwork.

## By event

| Event | Registrations usually triggered |
|---|---|
| Incorporating a private limited company | Incorporation, PAN, TAN, DIN, statutory registers, auditor appointment |
| First hire | Professional tax (state), PF if threshold met, ESI if wage threshold met, appointment letter, POSH policy when the headcount rule applies |
| Opening a premises | Shop and Establishment (state), trade licence (municipal), fire NOC, signage permission, lease stamping |
| Crossing the GST threshold | GST registration per state, invoice series, e-invoice if above its threshold |
| Selling online | GST registration regardless of threshold for most marketplaces, marketplace agreements, returns policy |
| Selling food | FSSAI registration or licence by turnover, water testing, pest control, handler medicals |
| Packing goods for retail | Legal metrology registration, declarations on the pack, EPR registration for plastic packaging |
| Importing or exporting | IEC, AD code with the bank, customs registration, FEMA reporting |
| Employing 20 or more contract workers | Contract labour licensing, principal employer registration |
| Handling personal data at scale | DPDP notice, consent records, grievance officer, breach process |
| Applying for a government tender | Udyam, GST, PAN, and often a specific class of registration per department |

## By sector, briefly

| Sector | Additional |
|---|---|
| Manufacturing | Factory licence, pollution consent to establish and operate, boiler and pressure vessel certificates, safety registers |
| Pharma and drugs | Drug licence, qualified person, storage and records per schedule |
| Education | State education department recognition, fire safety, building norms |
| Healthcare | Clinical establishment registration, biomedical waste authorisation, drug licence for a pharmacy |
| Logistics | Transport permits, driver records, goods carriage compliance |
| Real estate | RERA registration for projects above the state threshold |
| Financial services | RBI or SEBI registration depending on activity - never assumed |

## The renewal problem

Most losses are not in the first registration; they are in the renewal.

| Certificate | Typical validity | Renewal lead time |
|---|---|---|
| Shop and Establishment | 1-5 years by state | 60 days |
| Trade licence | 1 year | 45 days |
| Fire NOC | 1-3 years | 60 days |
| FSSAI | 1-5 years by category | 30-60 days |
| Pollution consent | 5-10 years by category | 120 days |
| Factory licence | 1 year | 60 days |
| Legal metrology | 1-5 years by state | 60 days |

Put the expiry in the register with the renewal work-back date, not the expiry date
alone. A renewal that starts 30 days before expiry on a 120-day process is a lapse.

## What this role will not do

- **Will not decide that a licence is not needed** on a borderline reading. It states the threshold, the reading, and that a professional should confirm.
- **Will not file an application.** It assembles the checklist, the documents and the owner, and tracks it.
- **Will not certify a document as true.** Attestation and signature are the director's or the professional's.
- **Will not advise on a structure to avoid a registration.** Operating without a required registration is not a compliance strategy.

## Anti-patterns

- **Registering the head office and forgetting the branch.** GST and shop registration are per location.
- **Assuming the CA covers it.** Most engagement letters cover filings, not premises licences.
- **Renewing after expiry and paying the penalty.** Cheaper than the renewal fee, and more expensive than the tender lost.
- **A licence held in a former address.** After a move, every certificate needs the new address, and the register needs the new scan.
