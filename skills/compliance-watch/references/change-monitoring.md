# Change monitoring

A rule change is not news until it is read against this business. This is the
weekly scan, and the reading method that turns a notification into an action.

## The scan, fifteen minutes, same day each week

| Source | What it publishes | How often it matters |
|---|---|---|
| CBIC and the GST portal | Notifications, circulars, rate changes, portal and form changes | Weekly |
| MCA | Companies rules, forms, filing extensions, LLP changes | Weekly |
| State gazette for your states | Labour, professional tax, shops and establishments | Weekly |
| EPFO and ESIC | Contribution changes, due-date relief, inspection drives | Monthly |
| Sector regulator | FSSAI, RBI, CDSCO, state licensing authority, as applicable | Monthly |
| Income tax e-filing portal | Forms, utility changes, due-date extensions | Monthly, more in March |

Keep the list short and fixed. A scan with fifteen sources is a scan nobody runs
in week three.

## Reading a notification, in five steps

1. **What changed, in one sentence.** Not the title - the operative change.
2. **Effective date versus issue date.** Most notifications are issued now and take effect later; a few are retrospective. Write both.
3. **Applies to whom.** Turnover threshold, sector, state, registration type, transaction type. The condition is the whole answer.
4. **What action it forces here.** A field in a return, a change in a format, a new registration, a rate on a line item, a disclosure in a pack. If none, say so in one line and close it.
5. **What would be different if we ignored it.** The realistic consequence, and the date after which ignoring it becomes visible.

## The change log

One table, appended to, never rewritten:

| Date read | Source | Change | Effective from | Applies here? | Action | Owner | Due |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | yes / no / unsure | | | |

The "no" rows are the most valuable part of the file. They are the proof that the
change was read and considered, which is exactly what an inspector, an auditor or
a new CA asks about.

## Weekly versus event-driven

Some changes cannot wait for the weekly scan:

- A rate change on a product the business sells today.
- A form or portal change during a filing window.
- An amnesty or relief scheme with a closing date.
- A new registration requirement for a state the business is entering.

Route these to the founder the same day, with the effective date in the first
line.

## Judging a change in ten minutes

Three questions, in order:

1. **Does it touch money, a filing, or a permission?** If no to all three, close it.
2. **Does it apply to our profile?** Entity, states, turnover band, sector, headcount. If it needs a fact you do not have, ask for that fact rather than guessing.
3. **What is the last date on which doing nothing is still safe?** That date goes into the calendar with an owner, or it does not exist.

## Sources that mislead

- **Aggregator blogs and CA firm newsletters** are useful for discovery, never for the date. Read the notification itself.
- **Screenshots in WhatsApp groups** circulate for months after a change is superseded.
- **A vendor's webinar** is a sales channel with a compliance angle.
- **"As per the new rules"** in an email, with no notification number, is not a source.
- **A news headline about a proposal.** A proposal in the Finance Bill is not law until it is enacted and notified.

## Anti-patterns

- **Monitoring without a register.** Changes accumulate as emails and nothing is applied.
- **Applying without a condition.** A rule read without the applicability test produces unnecessary work and occasional unnecessary panic.
- **The one-off deep dive.** A quarterly sweep after three months of silence always finds something urgent.
- **Reading only the summary.** Summaries drop effective dates and thresholds, which are the only parts that matter.
- **No record of what did not apply.** The most common question in an audit is "how did you conclude this did not apply".
