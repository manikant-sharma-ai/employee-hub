# The obligations map

Which rules apply to this business is a function of five facts, not of a checklist
downloaded from a website. Get the five facts, then derive.

## The five facts

| Fact | Why it decides almost everything |
|---|---|
| Legal form | A private limited has ROC filings; a proprietorship does not. An LLP has its own set. |
| States of operation | Labour, professional tax and shop registration are state subjects. An office in two states is two registrations. |
| Turnover band | GST scheme, e-invoicing, audit thresholds and TDS applicability all move with it. |
| Sector | Food, pharma, lending, education, transport, export and e-commerce carry obligations no generic list has. |
| Headcount and wage bill | PF, ESI, gratuity, POSH committee, labour welfare fund and contract labour all trigger on numbers. |

Write these five into one table at the top of the register and date it. Re-derive
when any of them changes - a new state, a turnover crossing, a thirtieth employee.

## The five buckets

### 1. Tax and statutory dues

- GST: registration per state, GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B monthly or quarterly under QRMP, annual return, e-invoicing above the notified turnover, e-way bills for goods movement.
- TDS: deduction at the time of payment, deposit, quarterly statements (24Q for salary, 26Q for others), certificates to the deductee, and the annual return.
- Income tax: advance tax instalments, the annual return, and the audit requirement where turnover or another condition triggers it.
- Professional tax: state-specific, per employee, usually monthly or annual.

### 2. Labour and employment

- PF and ESI registration and monthly contributions above the headcount thresholds.
- Shops and Establishments registration and renewal, per state.
- POSH: an internal committee above the notified headcount, an annual report, and awareness material.
- Gratuity accrual and payment; bonus and minimum wage where applicable.
- Contract labour registration where contract work crosses the threshold.
- The four labour codes: track the commencement notifications for your state, and do not assume one national date.

### 3. Corporate and secretarial

- Private limited: annual return, financial statements, board and general meeting minutes, statutory registers, director KYC, and event-based filings for charges, capital, directors and registered office.
- LLP: annual statement of accounts and solvency, and the annual return.
- Beneficial ownership and significant beneficial owner declarations where applicable.

### 4. Sector

| Sector | The obligations nobody puts in a generic list |
|---|---|
| Food | FSSAI licence or registration per premises, annual return, hygiene and labelling rules |
| Pharma and medical devices | Licences, storage conditions, pharmacovigilance |
| Lending and fintech | RBI registration or partnership rules, KYC, fair-practice code, data localisation |
| Education | Affiliation, fee regulation, RTE obligations |
| Transport and logistics | Vehicle permits, fitness, driver records, e-way bills |
| Export | IEC, shipping documents, GST refund cycle, FEMA reporting for inward remittance |
| E-commerce | Marketplace seller verification, e-invoicing, consumer protection rules |

### 5. Data and consumer

- DPDP Act readiness: consent, notice, grievance officer, breach process - the mapping work belongs to `data-privacy-officer` when it ships; the calendar and the evidence of what was done belong here.
- Consumer protection: grievance redressal timelines, no misleading claims, MRP and pack declarations.
- Advertising and claims: the ASCI code and sector advertising rules.

## How to build the register row

| Field | Rule |
|---|---|
| Obligation | The action, in plain words - "file GSTR-3B" not "GST compliance" |
| Condition | What makes it apply: turnover, headcount, state, sector |
| Law or source | The Act, rule or portal page, and the date you last read it |
| Frequency | Monthly, quarterly, annual, event-based |
| Due rule | "20th of the following month" beats "monthly" |
| Owner | One name |
| Evidence | The document that proves it, and where it is kept |
| If missed | The realistic consequence, with the range not the maximum |

## Deriving it for a typical Indian SMB

A 40-person private limited in Maharashtra, ₹9 crore turnover, selling packaged
snacks online, with one warehouse:

| Bucket | Applies | Note |
|---|---|---|
| GST | Registration in Maharashtra and in every state with a warehouse; monthly or QRMP returns; e-invoicing if above the notified turnover | Check the e-invoice threshold at source each quarter |
| TDS | Salary TDS, vendor TDS above thresholds, quarterly statements | Deposit by the 7th, statement by the end of the month after the quarter |
| PF and ESI | Both, above the headcount thresholds | Monthly, by the 15th |
| Shops and Establishments | Maharashtra registration and renewal | State form and fee |
| Professional tax | Maharashtra, per employee, monthly | State-specific slab |
| POSH | Internal committee above the notified headcount, annual report | Composition and report are inspectable |
| FSSAI | Licence per premises, annual return | The licence category follows scale |
| Corporate | AOC-4, MGT-7, DIR-3 KYC, statutory registers, board minutes | Dates follow the AGM |
| Consumer | MRP, net quantity, nutritional declaration, grievance contact | Pack artwork, not paperwork |

## Anti-patterns

- **The copied checklist.** Another company's register imports obligations that do not apply and omits the ones that do.
- **The CA-only view.** The CA files what the CA is paid for; nobody files what falls between the CA, the payroll vendor and the founder.
- **The turnover assumption.** "We are small" is not a band, and thresholds are not intuitions.
- **The single-state assumption.** One employee in another state can create a registration in that state.
- **The undated register.** A register without a "last verified" column is a document about the past.
