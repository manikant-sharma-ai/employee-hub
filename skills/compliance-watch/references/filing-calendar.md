# The filing calendar

A due date tells you when it is late. A work-back date tells you when to start.
Only the second one prevents the first.

## Work-back arithmetic

| Filing type | Due | Data must be closed | Payment funded | Review before filing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Monthly GST (GSTR-3B) | 20th | 10th | 17th | 18th |
| Monthly GST (GSTR-1) | 11th | 5th | n/a | 9th |
| TDS deposit | 7th of the next month | 3rd | 5th | 6th |
| TDS quarterly return | End of month after quarter | 15 days after quarter | n/a | 10 days before due |
| PF ECR | 15th | 8th | 12th | 13th |
| ESI contribution | 15th | 8th | 12th | 13th |
| Advance tax | 15 Jun / Sep / Dec / Mar | 7 days before | 5 days before | 3 days before |
| ROC annual filings | Within 30 / 60 days of AGM | After accounts signed | n/a | 15 days before |

Dates above are the common pattern; confirm the current statutory date for each
filing before putting it in a client calendar, and confirm state-level dates
separately - professional tax and labour welfare fund dates vary by state.

## The calendar row

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| Obligation | GSTR-3B, October 2026 |
| Start work | 10 November |
| Fund the payment | 17 November |
| File | 19 November (one day of buffer) |
| Statutory due | 20 November |
| Owner | <name> |
| Consultant | <CA firm, contact> |
| Evidence to file | ARN, challan, reconciliation sheet |
| Consequence if late | Interest on tax, late fee per day, and a compliance rating that affects tenders |

The buffer day is not decoration. Portal outages on the last two days of a filing
window are routine, and the person who files on the 19th has a second attempt.

## Recurring rhythm

- **Weekly, 20 minutes** - what is due in the next 21 days, who owns it, what is blocked.
- **Monthly, 45 minutes** - close the month's filings, record the acknowledgements, note anything late and why.
- **Quarterly, 2 hours** - the returns that fall at quarter end, the change review, and the register's three questions.
- **Annually, half a day** - the full calendar rebuild: new thresholds, new locations, new headcount, new sector obligations, and every expiry date refreshed.

## When a deadline will be missed

Say it early, in this order:

1. **What will be late**, and by how many days.
2. **The consequence**, as a range, with the rule cited - interest and late fee are arithmetic, not opinion.
3. **The cheapest recovery** - file with the payment now and regularise later, rather than waiting for a complete reconciliation.
4. **What it means downstream** - a tender, a loan sanction, a customer's vendor audit.

Never let a missed deadline be discovered by the founder from a notice.

## Anti-patterns

- **A calendar of due dates only.** The work starts on the day it is already urgent.
- **No funding date.** A prepared return with no money in the account is a late filing with extra steps.
- **The consultant as the owner.** The consultant files; someone inside the business still owns the date and chases the acknowledgement.
- **All deadlines at the same reminder setting.** A monthly return and an annual ROC filing do not need the same lead time.
- **No buffer.** Filing on the statutory date means the first failure is a late filing.
