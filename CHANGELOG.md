The revenue half is not: the queue had no revenue role between #30 and #41, and since the mix is fixed while the queue is only a source, the pair was taken from #41 and #42. `voice-and-audio` (#29) and `copy-editor` (#32) were **held, not skipped** - both are operations roles sitting behind #26 and #28, and neither collides with anything shipped. They lead the next drop. This is also the first drop gated by `scripts/validate_repo_v2.py --strict`, and the first to ship through a branch and a pull request rather than a push to `main`.

- **`hiring-manager`** (`skills/hiring-manager`, v1.0.0) - the operations role the queue had carried since the morning, and the one that closes a reference that has been dead since 2 November: `hr-recruiter` ends at the offer, and nothing in the hub owned the ninety days after it. Shaped around the finding that almost every "bad hire" is a bad first ninety days - no plan, no buddy, no written outcome, and a probation date nobody diarised. So the workflow writes the role's three outcomes before it writes a single date, because a 30-60-90 built on a job title is fiction. `onboarding-first-90.md` carries the pre-boarding sequence (the silence between offer and joining date is where the candidate is lost, not the first day) and the hour-by-hour first day, including the small real task that a new joiner finishes before 5pm - the single cheapest thing that makes someone believe they can work here. `role-scorecards-and-goals.md` gives the four tests an outcome must pass (observable, dated, bounded, sourced) and the probation decision rule written in advance: all three criteria met - confirm; one shortfall - extend once in writing with a named gap and a date; two or more, or any conduct issue - exit properly. `difficult-conversations-and-pips.md` puts the PIP's support column in, because a plan with no support is a termination with extra steps and reads that way to anyone reviewing it. `retention-and-attrition-signals.md` reads attrition by window rather than as one number - a 25% rate made entirely of month-two exits is an onboarding problem and no bonus fixes it. The role never confirms, promotes or exits anyone: it produces the evidence, the recommendation and the draft. `scripts/probation_tracker.py` turns a joiner sheet into check-in dates, expiry and the confirmation deadline, clamping month-ends correctly (31 August + 6 months is 28 February), because a lapsed probation is both a legal problem and a management one.

- **`compliance-watch`** (`skills/compliance-watch`, v1.0.0) - the second operations role, and the gap between what `bookkeeper` files and what actually applies to this entity in this state. Built on five facts rather than a downloaded checklist: legal form, states of operation, turnover band, sector and headcount - those five derive almost the whole register, and a register copied from another company imports obligations that do not apply while omitting the ones that do. `obligations-map.md` refuses to answer without an applicability condition per row, because the most expensive compliance work in an Indian SMB is done on a rule that never applied. `change-monitoring.md` is a fixed fifteen-minute weekly scan and a five-step reading method that separates issue date from effective date and ends in a written "does not apply, because" - the line an auditor actually asks for. `evidence-and-inspection.md` treats the acknowledgement as the deliverable, not the filing, and specifies the numbered folder that sorts into the order an inspector reads it. `risk-register-and-escalation.md` puts a range on exposure instead of quoting the maximum penalty, which ends the conversation instead of informing it, and fixes S1 and S2 as phone calls to named people. `india-filing-calendar.md` is the shape of the year with the caveat printed on it: dates move with notifications, state obligations vary by state, and the source is the answer when a date is challenged. The role does not file, pay or reply to a notice - it prepares, indexes and escalates, and says once, plainly, that this is not legal advice.

- **`chief-of-staff`** (`skills/chief-of-staff`, v1.0.0) - the founder-leverage role, and the work a founder does personally and badly: the Sunday-night status rebuild, the decision taken four times because nobody wrote the first one down, the commitment nobody chased. It is the hub's first L4 - it owns a cadence rather than a document, which is a different kind of employee and the reason the level scale exists. `weekly-operating-review.md` fixes the page at five rows with a source per row and makes reading it in silence the highest-leverage rule in the meeting, because it removes the twenty minutes usually spent narrating a slide. The three questions end with "what are we saying no to this week" - a review that answers only the first two becomes a reporting ritual. `decision-log-and-reversibility.md` makes two fields do the work: options rejected and the reversal test, which needs a metric, a threshold, a duration and a date, or it is a promise to remember. `followup-enforcement.md` writes the escalation ladder by age (due today - one nudge; two weeks late - a decision) and states the rule that keeps the system alive: the founder is swept first and separately, because the first exemption ends the register. `founder-time-and-delegation.md` audits from the calendar rather than memory and gives the five-rung delegation ladder, since skipping from "do exactly this" to "own it" is how a founder is surprised by a decision and blames the delegate for judgement they were never given. `scripts/commitment_tracker.py` sweeps a register and names what is overdue, undated, unowned and over the cap of five per person, and exits non-zero when a decision is needed today.

- **`growth-marketer`** (`skills/growth-marketer`, v1.0.0) - the first revenue role, split from `performance-ads-buyer` on the only axis that holds: this one decides where the next rupee goes and designs the test that will prove it; the buyer builds the account, tests the creative and paces the budget inside that decision. Shipping them together is deliberate - either alone would have re-created the collision the queue rule exists to prevent, and both descriptions now say so. Built on the arithmetic that the constraint is the stage where a 20% improvement is worth the most rupees, not the stage with the ugliest percentage, which is why the funnel table has a revenue-value column. `experiment-design.md` carries the power formula and its two consequences for a small business: detecting a 2.0% to 2.4% move needs about 19,600 users per variant, which most Indian SMBs will never have, so test a new offer rather than a new button colour. `channel-mix-and-cac.md` makes payback the decision rule and prints the margin constraint honestly - under a 25% contribution margin, paid acquisition is structurally a loss and recommending a channel is a way of spending the founder's cash on a lesson. `measurement-and-attribution.md` puts four numbers side by side (platform, analytics, orders, gateway) and states the ratio, because every platform claims the same order and only the gateway knows who paid. `scripts/experiment_power.py` does the sample, payback and CAC-ceiling arithmetic the same way every time.

- **`performance-ads-buyer`** (`skills/performance-ads-buyer`, v1.0.0) - the second revenue role, and the one where money moves, so its permissions are written narrowly: it reads the account, drafts structure, and hands over a pause/scale list for a human to execute. Nothing is launched or paused without approval. `account-structure.md` does the arithmetic nobody does - a ₹30,000 month at a ₹1,500 CPA produces about 20 events, which cannot support five ad sets, so consolidation is not a preference but the only structure that can learn. `creative-testing.md` treats the first three seconds as the cheapest intervention in paid media and gives the diagnosis order (hook rate, then hold rate, then CTR, then landing-page CVR), because a high CPA with a strong hook is a page problem and no amount of new creative fixes it. `budget-pacing-and-bidding.md` defines the allowable CPA for the remaining period and lists India's CPM realities - Diwali, IPL, exam season - with the instruction to judge creative outside those windows. `readouts-and-decisions.md` writes the decision rules with a number and a duration attached so anyone can apply them, and gives the seven-cause diagnosis order for a blown CPA that puts "the algorithm" last, where it belongs. `scripts/pacing_calculator.py` projects spend, conversions and the allowable CPA, and exits non-zero when the ceiling is broken.

**The new gate.** `scripts/validate_repo_v2.py --strict` is the drop gate from today. It runs `validate_repo.py` first, then checks what v1 never asked for: description under 1024 characters, the ten sections in the fixed order, three to six references of 50-160 lines each, an asset template, `evals.json` with the five required cases, a `notes.seed/` that is schema only, and at least one deterministic script whose `--self-test` exits zero - which it runs rather than trusts. It also gates the registry: level L0-L4, category, permissions, cost band with a driver, connector availability with at least one connector actually connected, status `probation` and reliability `unverified`. And it checks the batch itself - the 2-2-1 mix, the backlog rows marked shipped, the changelog block, the router's role map, and the three manifests agreeing with the registry version. `--strict` makes warnings fatal, which is why the five new skills carry no warnings at all.

**Probation, declared.** All five ship with `status: probation` and `reliability: unverified`, and none of them has a `notes/` folder full of invented lessons - each carries `notes.seed/`, which is schema and an empty format with a comment saying so. The v2 gate fails the build if a dated entry appears in a seed. `validate_skill.py` now accepts either a live `notes/` folder or a schema-only `notes.seed/`, so the older skills are untouched.

- **`pr-writer`** (`skills/pr-writer`, v1.0.0) - the revenue role the queue had held since 3 November, and shaped so that the hook is tested before a word of prose is written: if the sentence a reporter would say to their editor cannot be filled with facts, the answer is "this is not news yet" and a proposal for what would make it news, not a better-worded release. The list is treated as the strategy - a named reporter with three recent pieces and the line of this story that is theirs - because a perfect release sent to a scraped list is a week of work and a burned sending domain. `news-hooks-and-angles.md` carries the editor test and the ten hooks that are actually news, plus the timing map that matters here: budget week, Diwali and the last week of March are where announcements go to die. `press-release-craft.md` fixes the block order and the 350-500 word length, and bans the quote that could belong to any company - the one test that separates a press note from a brochure. `media-list-and-outreach.md` is the India half: business dailies versus startup media versus regional-language editions, the embargo as a written promise, and the observation that a Marathi note to a district edition often out-earns a national mention. The role never claims coverage, a reply or a send that is not in a system it can see, and it routes anything with legal exposure to `legal-drafter` before a founder says a word.

- **`partnerships`** (`skills/partnerships`, v1.0.0) - split from `sales-outreach` on the only axis that holds: that role sells to the buyer who pays us, this one recruits the reseller, distributor or integrator who sells for us, and the boundary is now written into both descriptions so "find us distributors in Gujarat" cannot land on the outbound seller. Built on the two places partnerships actually fail, neither of which is the signing: selection, where the partner had no reason to sell this on Monday, and activation, where nobody trained the sales floor and nobody noticed for two quarters. The fit test is six questions in writing and the first three end the conversation. `deal-terms-and-economics.md` makes the arithmetic explicit - a 30% reseller discount on a 44% contribution margin leaves us 20% and leaves the partner ₹950 net per deal, which is a line they will never push - so the fix becomes a smaller cost to sell rather than a bigger discount. `partner-activation-and-scorecard.md` writes the kill date at signature and makes "zero at day 30" information and "zero at day 60 with no pipeline" a decision, because the alternative is a dormant partner kept on the books for the logo.

- **`procurement`** (`skills/procurement`, v1.0.0) - the first operations role, and the one where a small business loses money without noticing. The workflow starts at the specification rather than the quotes, because five vendors quoting five different things is not a comparison and the cheapest one wins by accident. `quote-comparison-and-tco.md` normalises to landed cost and three-year TCO with the assumptions named - freight, installation, GST credit, the cost of payment terms at 12%, consumables and downtime - and it is the file that most often reverses the ranking. `vendor-due-diligence.md` puts name agreement first: GSTIN legal name, bank account name and letterhead must match, because that mismatch is the most common advance-payment fraud against an Indian SMB, and a bank-detail change by email gets a phone call to a number from the original contract. `negotiation-prep.md` replaces "what is your best price" with a sentence containing the benchmark, the number and the thing being offered, and it orders the levers volume, terms, scope, then price. The role stops where the money starts moving: it raises the PO and sets the three-way match, and `bookkeeper`/`finance-ops` pay, `legal-drafter` contracts.

- **`analytics-instrumenter`** (`skills/analytics-instrumenter`, v1.0.0) - the second operations role, and the one gap that could not be repaired after the fact: `data-analyst` answers questions from data that exists, and nothing in the hub decided what to capture before the code shipped. So it is built backwards from the questions table - every event names a Q number and an owner, and an event with no question is deleted in review - because instrumentation that starts from "track everything" produces volume, app weight and a privacy surface. `event-taxonomy.md` fixes `object_action` naming, one string across web, Android, iOS and server, and the rule that no personal data and no user-typed text goes into a property. `tracking-plan.md` treats the plan as a contract in the repo with a release gate attached: no release until every event fires once in the debug view and `order_paid` reconciles to the gateway within 2%. `qa-and-data-quality.md` exists because the failure that matters is not a wrong chart but a launch that cannot be measured at all - `payment_failed` is the event everyone forgets and the one that finds the leak.

- **`pricing-analyst`** (`skills/pricing-analyst`, v1.0.0) - the founder-leverage role, and deliberately not counted as revenue: it sits closest to the money of any role in the queue, but the work it removes is the founder's own - setting the number, defending it, and saying no to the discount. Built on the arithmetic that ends most pricing arguments: on a 40% contribution margin a 10% price cut needs 33% more volume to hold profit, while a 10% rise survives a 20% volume loss, so the table in `margin-and-unit-economics.md` is the whole case against discounting. `willingness-to-pay.md` is an evidence ladder with the bias of each method labelled, and it says plainly that most Indian SMBs should run the first three rungs and stop rather than decorate a decision with a 40-person survey. `discount-and-leakage.md` starts by comparing the win rate at full price with the win rate at 20% off - if they are close, the discount buys nothing - and then writes the ladder, the give-get table and the quarterly audit that finds the renewal discount kept for five years. `price-testing-and-rollout.md` grandfathers existing customers, forbids testing on renewals, and requires a reversal trigger with a number and a date before the change ships, because a change without one becomes the baseline by default.

**Standard wiring:** all five link `core/connector-preflight.md` before the first substantive step and `core/memory-and-notes.md` plus `core/performance-tracking.md` before the output contract. Each carries five craft references, one asset template and seeded `notes/` (learnings, contacts, decisions, journal for 2026-11), and each routes to `scripts/validate_skill.py` at the root rather than carrying its own copy. `scripts/validate_repo.py` passes with zero errors and zero warnings.

## 2026-11-03 - audit fixes + five employees + the hire desk (v1.5.0)

This drop is not a queue drop. It is the repair of the four things the repo promised and did not have, plus five employees.

**The four missing parts, now built.** (1) *The 06:00 IST cadence had no mechanism.* `registry.json`, `README.md` and `docs/daily-drop.md` all claimed five employees every morning, and nothing in the repo could fire at 06:00 - no workflow, no script, no scheduler. `.github/workflows/daily-drop.yml` now runs at `30 0 * * *` UTC (06:00 IST) and opens an issue with the day's five roles pulled from `BACKLOG.md` by `scripts/daily_drop.py`, with the procedure as a checklist. It deliberately does not write skills: a scheduled job that commits five half-written employees is worse than no automation. (2) *Nothing validated a push.* `scripts/validate_repo.py` existed and was run by hand; `.github/workflows/validate.yml` now runs it, plus a new `scripts/validate_manifests.py`, on every push and PR. (3) *The manifests had drifted and nothing noticed.* `plugin.json` and `marketplace.json` said 1.3.0 while `registry.json` said 1.4.0, and the publisher name differed between them ("Pawan Sharma" vs "Manikant Sharma") - so `/plugin marketplace add` was serving a version that did not exist. All three now read 1.5.0 with one publisher, and the new manifest validator fails the build on any future drift. (4) *Scheduling was a table row in the README and nothing more.* `docs/scheduling.md` is new: the four ways to fire a job, the exact cron and Actions lines, the weekly and monthly review layer that `core/performance-tracking.md` assumes, and the rule that a schedule does not grant connector access.

**The hub had twenty employees and no hire desk.** Every request arrived at twenty descriptions and the person chose by guesswork, which is how a caption job lands on the graphic designer. `employee-hub-router` reads the job, names one owner, states what must be handed over and which connector is missing. `references/role-map.md` carries the twelve pairs that actually get confused - content-writer vs social-media-manager, graphic-designer vs ui-designer, finance-ops vs bookkeeper, sales-outreach vs email-marketer - and the five sequences that recur, because "we are launching" is never one employee.
